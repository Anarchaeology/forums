Don't count on a Linux version ever happening, actually. Even if they get the market share.

The rewrite needed to make AO capable of running natively in that OS would be a huge undertaking and, quite frankly, a massive money sink and one more support problem.

Being that Linux doesn't support DirectX (Which really isn't all that bad), AO would need to be written to completely support all features of OpenGL. Not a small undertaking in any regard.

Going along with that, the filesystem in Linux is horrendously slow. Even the fastest journaling ones still can't hold a candle to the hack of a filesystem known as Fat32 and definately can't keep up with a well-laid out NTFS partition.

The market for Linux will never reach that critical point that would make Funcom decide to create the client. Most linux users have ZERO interest in gaming, anyway.

Fact is, most games for Linux just plain suck, or are 3 year old ports that most people play for amusement once every few months between ranting about how Windows Sucks and Linux Is Great.

To bad that fact is backwards when it comes to a desktop machine. Serverside, well.. Linux still stinks. Give me Solaris or one of the BSD flavors. Linux just can't stand on two legs when it comes to comparisons on the desktop.

The majority of this thread seemed to be about a native port. Not a hack of WineX attempting to run it.

In any case, I've yet to see a benchmark of Linux box doing multiple processes that can keep up with a Windows box of the same exact hardware.

While Linux is GREAT running 1 or 2 small graphical applications.. you start creating the same activity and usage that AO will do and I bet you dollar for dollar that X freezes itself solid.

One of the biggest drawbacks to Linux <or almost any UNIX> is X itself. The worst being X11. OpenWin is passable. X11 is slow in performance, terrible with handling high intensity graphics (without severe lag-locks/redrawing problems).. and that's WITH DRI supported drivers installed.

I say it can't compare filesystem wise due to the fact you actually DO need to sit down and tweak it before it'll run to any functionally sane degree. Joe User (AKA: AO's userbase) isn't going to do that, or know how to do that. AO will, on that note, suffer horribly. This is the price to pay for being a backwater "down in the mud" OS.

I'd be willing to sit down and tweak a box to fiddle with AO, but in the end, Linux still can't hold a candle to the simplicity of using a Windows machine at home.

That's my opinion. Also the opinion of a lot of the real UNIX geeks I know. Windows on the desktop. UNIX on the server. It's just right that way.

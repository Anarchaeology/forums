Avec Leet Art - Sunday 30 19:00 GMT
RUR, Rubi-Ka Universal Robots, cordially invites our friends and customers to a private guided tour of the "Avec Leet" Collection.
This is a key retrospective of Rubi-Kan painting between 29,415 - 29,450.

The event will be at the The Modigliani Art Gallery, in Omni-Trade
High Rise 18 (near the exit to Lush Fields) on Sunday 30, 19:00 GMT. Please note, once the tour has started late-comers will not be admitted.

The tour will be conducted by Modigliani himself. If you more info please send Dabblez or Modigliani a tell or just show up on the day in Omni Trade, HR 18.

Below is extract from the exhibition's catalogue.

"Between 29,415 and 29,450 a group of Rubi-Kan painters produced a series of works collectively known as the 'Avec Leet' school. The goal of this artistic movement was quite simple, to revisit the works of the Old Masters in Rubi-Kan key by introducing leets. In essence this can be seen as the Rubi-Kan artists taking possession and control of their cultural heritage. While this premise has often been dismissed by critics as 'shallow' or 'provincial' what really brought this movement to life was the sheer genius of its main exponents, like Tinka and J.J. Eek."

/ooc
I have not done an in-game tour of the Avec Leet gallery in quite some time, mainly because Modigliani and his gallery were on my second, cancelled, account. Although the "paintings" have been available on the SRS site for a year or so, I've always felt the interactive tour was a lot of fun, well, fun in a slow, wordy sort of way.

Since I belong to the Clan faction I regret to say that I'll not be attending the exhibition. Trade District police tend to be overzealous in suspecting that all Clanners who visit the city are conducting espionage activities. I have seen prints of the collection, however. My personal favorite is Tinka's Le Dejeuner sur l'herbe avec Leet. Leets in a wilderness setting are the most visually appealing and this painting expertly portrays that setting. The leet is shown in a lonely posture, and when juxtaposed with the three closely grouped humans in the center this is particularly evident. We can also see a fourth human in the background, separated from the other people; there is a sense of loneliness in this figure as well. Perhaps Tinka is trying to indicate that solitary humans and solitary leets have something in common and can coexist peacefully in the wilderness.

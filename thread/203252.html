*Two Days ago in Hope*

It looked sturdy. Yeah...Probably sturdy as granite. Solid through and afixed with alloy hinges. The surface was bare, umblemished and gleaming with a polished sheen. So smooth and shiney that she could actually see the reflection of her booted foot as it approached contact near the bolted lock. Her short athletically toned leg shot forward as she thrust her hip toward the door, turning her heel into a battering ram.

The thud of connection blended with the sound of splintering wood as the inner framework exploded around the inadequate lock.

Her blue armor coat billowed as a breeze came from behind. Wind caught her collar-length red hair and blew it forward, whipping strands across her face and eyes. She lowered her extended leg slowly and brought a gloved hand to her face, brushing the waving hair aside, tucking ropes of red behind her ear.

The inside was dark and silent. There would be things to be seen and smelled inside which the Homo-Solitus breed would not detect. Her mind triggered the Nano-technology which would give her the skills she needed for this task. Combinations of thought patterns and hand gestures brought forth a swirl of energy before her. Powerfull forces built between her hands climaxing in an inward burst. She arched her back and extended her arms as she absorbed the effects, causing her body to alter its shape. She triggered a second nano-program to enhance her senses.

Senses suddendly became acute as she padded forward into the building. Brown fur brushed the edges of the open doorway. Becoming the hulking two-headed wolf was always met with an adjustment period. Smells, sounds and sights became over-powering. What struck her first was the steril scent of disinfectants. Perceptive eyes scanned the sparsely furnished room noting a table, two chairs and a bed.

As her senses began to filter, sort and categorize the different scents, her heart rate excellerated. Her prey had been here and not long ago. She was getting closer.

Other than furniture, the room was bare with the exception of a datapad lying on the table. She padded toward the table and canceled the morphing effect. She stood at the table staring at the pad for several seconds. A soft green light blinked on and off, compeling her to reach down and activate the "play" feature.

Static played across the small viewscreen, giving way gradually to the clear image of a man dispised.

"Hi baby!"

The man grined sadistically and spoke in a near whisper.

"It's taken some time, but it looks like your past is catching up. Now before you get all vindictive, let me set down some rules for the game. First, you won't tell anyone about me. If you do, I'll know. Second, when you catch me, and you will, there won't be any coming home. Our last meeting proved that you're beyond help, and must be put down like an animal. Third, you will sever all ties with anyone and anything familiar to you. You will hunt me alone, and as a pariah. Fourth, you'll follow these rules or someone you haven't seen for a very long time is going to pay the price." The screen panned to an open doorway, and through the open doorway a teenage girl with flaming red hair sat on a bed reading a holobook.

"She means nothing to me anymore, and frankly I grow tired of being a parent." His eyes widened in madness. "Oh...grrr....can't control....must....strangle......daughter........" His eyes snapped back to lucidity and he laughed softly. "Hurry up, baby. Destiny and payback's waiting."

Hattie "Ditzie" McCallagher stood in stunned silence as the viewscreen faded to black.

Mist hung heavily over the grass covered hills. Under normal conditions, it would be the perfect time and place for a morning run. She liked early mornings. A cup of coffee, then a long run to get her head clear and keep her tiny body fit. But these weren't normal conditions, and the hills of Lush represented the first stage of a sick twisted game.

As her short legs covered the terrain at a quick pace, she recalled the events that had transpired only two hours earlier.

Her instructions had been to wait for contact, and she had chosen a solitary yet familiar spot. Baboons was empty at 3:30 in the morning, so there was little chance of having to explain herself to friends or acquaintances.

The upper level held rows of tables and benches and she picked the furthest one back. During her early months with Devil's Advocate, it was here where she would usually find Valaik.

As her memories of friends departed drifted through her mind, a buzz sounded from the datapad she had found in Hope. She looked around one last time to make sure she was alone. Satisfied in her security, she activated the device.

"Good Morning, Sunshine!" came the excited voice emitted from the over animated charicature of a man on the screen.

"Good Morning, Michael," Hattie breathed with contempt seething through clinched teeth.

The man smiled widely. "Hey!" He blurted out. "Wanna see somethin' cool?"

Hattie stared back refusing to say any more than she absolutely had to.

"Okay," he said with complete joy in his voice. He stepped back a little so she could see his upper torso and head in the small window. He stretch out his arms and wiggled his fingers. "Nothin' up my sleeves." His right hand raised and his left hand formed a cup in front of this face. His right hand smacked the back of his head hard and from his eye socket dropped a prosthetic eyeball. "Wait, baby," he grinned. "It gets better."

His left hand brought the orb to his mouth and he popped it in. Then, stepping absurdly close to the camera, he opened his mouth. Hattie stared in disgust as the eyeball moved and seemed to watch her from the monster's open mouth.

He spat the eye back into his hand and fluidly replaced it into the gaping socket in his head. "You know what's really cool? I can see out of this sucker even when it's in my mouth," he cackled. "How cool is that?" he practically screamed. "Thanks for poking your knife in my old one, this thing's a hoot!"

Settling down, he took on a more serious demeanor. "Time for business, Katie."

"My name's Hattie," the short red head breathed.

"Nah kid, that's a dream you've been having for the past few years. Time to wake up." His grin sent a cold shiver running the length of her spine. "Daddy's home, and it's time to play."

"We're going to be playing a little game called....Wear Out the Junkie," he said this last part in the demented voice of a Holofeed Game Show host.

Hattie's heart skipped a beat, then began to pound furiously. She hadn't used a single stim for a year and a half, since not long after her wedding, when a binge nearly sent her marriage into a tragic nose dive. Forcing herself to breath out the words, she practically growled "I don't use."

"Oh! You will, Kitty Cat. But that's for later. We'll do this step by step, and add more and more as we go along. I'm going to be sending you some coordinates, and all you have to do is run there, then watch some movies." His cheerful voice made Hattie want to shatter the datapad against the nearest wall. "No vehicles allowed, and you'll be keeping your distance from any roads" he continued. "And the movies are going to be a really special treat. If you reach your coordinates in time, you get to share in the last nine years of Elizabeth's life. Birthdays, Holidays, Vacations, a whole mess of stuff." His tone turned more serious. "But if you're late, I'll be filming and showing some new, less enjoyable memories with our little punkin."

Silence consumed the air until Hattie spoke slowly. "What happens to her when this is all over?"

"Awwwww" her crooned. "Warms me heart to hear the words o' the protective momma. I'll tell you what, Katie. If you give me some amusement, and properly scream in terror when I peel off your skin, pluck out your eyes, spend weeks causing you pain, then take you into orbit and end your missery.....I'll dump the kid here and go on my merry way. You know, that kid's a hassle. I'm going broke keeping her in worthless, no stamina Nannies." The man sighed heavily. "But first, the game. I'm sending you some data. See ya at your first stop."

"Wait!" Hattie screamed at the screen in panic. "How is she even alive! Where is she! Is she.........."

"Easy, baby. Don't wear yourself out yet." He grinned. "You have lots of stops and lots of movies to watch. You'll get the whole story before you catch me!"

"OH! And don't forget to pack a few stims, baby!"

The top of the hill glowed with light as Hattie ran upward. Nearing the crest, the first of the suns was visible, and already warming the air. The woman ran on, knowing with pristine clarity what her fate would be. But clinging desperately to a hope that she could somehow save the life of the child stolen from her.

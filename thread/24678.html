Absolutley
In the best roleplaying games, which AO purports to be, the bulk of experience comes not from defeating monsters but from other aspects of the game.

By way of example:

Earthdawn-
1 experience award: monsters
1 experience award: session
1 experience award: special treasures (trophies and such)
1 experience award: role-playing
1/2 to 1 experience award: heroics

AD&D-
Each class (per DMG) was awarded experience based on accomplishing things their class was supposed to do. Wizards earned experience for making magic items (not finding them), Rogues for stealing treasure, and so forth.

Shadowrun-
Awarded experience for just surviving a mission.

and so on...

How much experience is awarded for what actions varies from game to game of course.

However, in most RPGs the experience awarded for a monster depended on *defeating* not necessarily *killing* the monster. This could be emulated in AO quite easily with sneaking. Sneaking past a mob (without detection) nets you experience for that mob (limit to missions let's say). If you then later kill that mob you lose the experience for the sneak but game experience for killing. Eitherway you have earned the same amount of experience.

One of the many problems of all MMORPGs (save UO) is the designers feel the only meaningful way to evolve a character is to fight and kill things. That is not the core play mechanisms of PnP RPGs upon which these games are based.

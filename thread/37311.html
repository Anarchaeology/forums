Bounty?
(scene: SPARTA Office, Omni Administration building, Blue District, Rome)

Over steepled fingers Director Silvestrini looked out across a Rome bathed in the orange light of yet another glorious Rubi-Kan sunset. The city below had already quietened from the days business, and most employees were at home by now. The air was still, and the last of distant birdsong twittered into his office from the large open window occupying most of the wall.

He remained reclined in his high backed leather chair staring out to his beloved Rome, before absently letting a hand drop to the window controls on the armrest, closing the wall-window to seal his office, before spinning his chair about to return to his desk.

On sensing this, his terminal flicked back to life and he reread the still open report from one of his Phoenix Team agents a final time, chuckling ruefully at its content. Tapping forward he detailed it to go out to the rest of SPARTA. Team - please read Chris Ardmans report below regarding the bounty on my head. he typed. Whilst Im taking this discovery seriously, I dont foresee it affecting any of our ongoing operations, nor affecting my way of life. Standard security operating procedures. he finished, tapping send.

He leaned forward and activated the intercom to his secretary, Janet, can you please have Adan Ganes sent up to my office.

Yes sir. she replied.

And who was trying to contact me earlier? he questioned further.

It was Lou Bannister sir, his wife has just given birth to a little girl, he wanted to tell you first himself sir. She answered.

Damn. he felt guilty he did not take the call. Damn. Janet listen, can you send Claire over some flowers and a card, you know the type of thing. Ill give him a call myself in a minute.

He actually said he was just going to bed sir, hes been up all night. She prompted.

Of course, of course. Ill call him later then. Arrange the flowers and the card anyway. he clicked the intercom off. He had become so preoccupied with work  and in particularly Rivers  that he had forgotten his best friend was on maternity leave, and now felt rather ashamed of himself.

Stirring him from that thought a knock at his door. Come in. he called. The door opened and in walked one of his senior intelligence officers, Adan Ganes. The nanomage was casually dressed down, as if having come back to work after dinner for some after-hours catch-up; Garret had obviously forgotten the time.

Ganes sat down on the other side of the desk, You wanted to see me boss? he asked.

He nodded. He was always slightly disturbed by Ganes' brilliant blue eyes, the lack of an iris or pupil was weird enough on its own, but it was the way they almost pulsed with notum, lighting the skin blue around the sockets that freaked him out a little. Yes, where are we with ensuring the reclaim insurance for tonights operatives is safe.

I have locked down the whole of SPARTA, and the individuals you have indicated from the South Rubi-Ka Society. Ganes answered very matter-of-fact. Aside from being one of SPARTAs more senior intelligence officers, he was also the departments Gridmaster.

Are you sure? I wouldnt put it past Rivers or The Mockers to hack into something. The director underlined.

Yes boss Im sure. Everything is one hundred percent safe. As you know I cannot keep it that secure indefinitely, the resource overhead is too great, however consider it all tamper-proof for at least the next 48 hours or so. he smiled in a rare moment of his own humour, I mean consider them more tamper-proof than the normal tamper-proof way they already are.

Silvestrini smiled at that himself. And Rivers? Have you hacked his insurance yet, were running out of time.

No boss, not yet. Theres no extra protection, Im just having problems getting into it.

Come on Adan, we must have that insurance down and invoked Silvestrini trailed off.

I know boss, Im working on it, and I wont let you down.

Okay. How are we sorted for the suppression field alteration? the director asked.

Thats already been arranged boss. Itll be localised on you with a radius of exactly 5 metres. Call me when you want to drop the level, and again when you want to return it to the zone norm. Ive got it programmed this time so I can turn it on and off like a light switch.

Does anyone know about this? I mean any of the other Omni departments? Silvestrini questioned.

No boss, Ive done it in an untraceable way. Were covered from that angle.

Garret Silvestrini nodded, leaning back into his chair again, steepling his fingers once more and looking conspiratorially over them to his colleague, Good. Good...

Later that evening...
(scene: SPARTA Office, Omni Administration building, Blue District, Rome)

Midnight. Garret Silvestrini walked tiredly into his darkened office and slumped down in his chair, forcing his pistols to dig annoyingly into his ribs. He breathed out, shoulders sagging with the effort, and leant sideways pulling each out of their shoulder holsters and placing them rather heavily on his desk. As if in time, the door shut itself automatically, dowsing the room in complete darkness.

Eventually his eyes began to adjust, and he swivelled about in his chair to look out of the large wall-window to sleepy Rome below. Lights twinkled, the odd air car drifted quietly along, but largely it looked as if nothing were any different from any other evening. Peace and quiet.

Nothing to compare with this evenings chaos in Omni-1 anyway. Terrorists, bombs, explosions, destruction, innocents murdered, and death. And Lev. Lev Rivers.

Garret rubbed his jaw line and just stared out into the city at nothing. Lev. He breathed out and shook his head, rubbing his eyes. Lev. Why?



2 am. He roused from his thoughts as a knock came from the door. Who at this hour? He swung his chair about to face forward again, Come.

The door opened and Adan Ganes stood in the doorway, not entering. He was dressed as earlier, as if he had not left the building. Adan? Questioned the director. What are you still doing here?

I thought Id monitor things for a while, and tidy up the lose ends on the grid etc. replied the nanomage. Are you alright boss? He noticed the director looked weary, and was still wearing the undercover plain clothes he had been wearing for tonights mission.

Garret tried to ignore the question, Meister seems to think his people got Rivers reclaim insurance revoked.

Adan smiled, Yes I heard that over the channels. He shook his head. His people were trying to wipe it for about half an hour before they realised the reason they were failing was that the insurance wasnt there in the first place. Mind you, its a bit rich they told their C/O they did it.

Do they know it was you, I mean us? Garret pressed.

No, as usual I made sure nothing lead back to us. Were clean. Theyve no idea who did it, and are just taking the glory. Omni-Pol, you know what theyre like. Same old, same old. Ganes replied.

Good work Adan, as always very good work. Garret said before nodding to his Gridmaster, then swivelled his chair about to face the window again. The nanomage turned in the doorway to leave, when from behind him his director said rather quietly, I shot him Adan.

Ganes paused and turned about again. Silvestrini was still facing the window. I know boss, I watched it all from the security camera feed.

Garret breathed out heavily. I mean I wasnt alone; there were others shooting, but I was one of them. He paused, then saying even quieter, Hed probably still be alive if I hadnt wiped his insurance.

You didnt boss, I wiped it. The nanomage suggested.

Yes, but I told you to. Then I shot him. Three times. Added the sullen director.

Adan Ganes did not know what to say, his director was having a rare moment of regret and self-doubt, and he could not think of anything supportive to say.

You know, he was a damn fine Omni-Tek agent, damn fine, back in the day. Garret recalled with half a smile, sounding as if said for his own benefit.

Boss Adan stole up some courage, was he any more a fine person than those innocent people he had murdered tonight?

Garret Silvestrini closed his eyes and reclined into his chair further, I know. I know.

(scene: CEO Philip Ross' Office, Omni HQ, Omni-1)

Afternoon: "A headless snake?" Philip Ross smirked at The Rivers gang report on the data pad and looked up, questioning his security intelligence director opposite, "Garret you're getting creative and wordy in your advancing years."

"Yes Sir." Garret Silvestrini grimaced; the wince in his eye barely hidden behind his InternOps issued sunglasses, a memento from earlier in his career. "Without Lev Rivers they appear to have not only gone to ground, but also become largely directionless."

"Let us hope they are largely toothless as well then." Ross paused, and then continued. "You say here The Mockers and quite surprisingly our own Devil's Advocate are still a threat to our interests?" Ross waved a hand at the data pad.

"Yes Sir." Garret nodded slightly. "The Mockers are just criminals who will simply go where there is money, so whilst they're a threat, it's a mercenary type threat. They'll be bought, and will follow, but they won't instigate. There's no profit in dying for a rebel cause."

"And so how do Devil's Advocate differ from that, surely they're driven by the same profit principal?" Ross did not understand the difference, but then he did not know much about these infamous party people either.

"They are Sir, but there's more to it than that. People like Valaik and Lupoh are driven by greed as much as the next criminal, however they are both" Garret searched for the right word, " subversive individuals. Our investigation about how their application to join Omni-Tek from the clans got through is still proceeding without result; however my guess would be a hacked application server somewhere."

"Both of them are fixers, correct?" Ross glanced at the section of the report detailing the wanted men.

"Correct, Sir. It's not beyond the realms of possibility they even hacked their own applications through." Garret suggested.

"Well keep me advised." Ross ended the briefing, tapping the data pad off and handing it back to Director Silvestrini. "And"

Garret stood up taking the data pad from his CEO. As Ross' sentence trailed away before it began, Garret questioned a wordless 'what?' with a raised eyebrow.

"Well done over the whole Rivers affair. I appreciate it cannot of been easy. Pass on my regards and congratulations to your team." Ross' tone was somewhat lighter and more reassuring than usual. "What's the term your team usually closes it's communications with again?" he tried to recall.

"Spartans, I salute you." Garret answered.

"Ah yes, well please tell them I salute them." Ross nodded, smiling.

Garret simply nodded without comment, turned on his heel, and walked purposefully from his boss' office.



(scene: SPARTA Office, Omni Administration building, Blue District, Rome)

Late evening: 'In closing, on briefing the CEO on the results of the Rivers operation, Mr Ross passes on a personal 'Spartans - I salute you.' So well done team.' Garret typed into his terminal. Selecting 'SPARTA - All' from his packetmail distribution list, he tapped 'send' and leaned back in his chair.

Steepling his fingers in front of his mouth, he mused for a moment before reaching down for the communication controls on the chair's armrest and activated it. "Secure anonymous channel. Trellame, Mockers." He ordered the system.



(scene: somewhere on Rubi-Ka)

Trellame's communicator blinked at him with an incoming call and he frowned. There was no caller identity listed, which technically speaking was impossible. He answered it anyway, curiously. "Yes?"

"Trellame, this is Silvestrini, director of SPARTA. We need to talk." The unfamiliar voice said curtly.

"Okay." Trellame answered cautiously. "Where?"

"The roof of Reet Retreat. Come alone." Garret instructed.

"10 minutes?" Trellame continued to answer cautiously.

"On my way." Garret replied, and then killed the channel.



(scene: Roof of Reet Retreat, Upper Stret West Bank)

15 minutes later: It was a sticky uncomfortable summer evening in the quiet neutral town. The sun had disappeared behind the horizon some hours ago, though between the moon and the roof lighting Trellame could see well enough without augmenting his vision with a night lens or torch. A gentle breeze washed over the roof of the large club situated close to the west bank of the Stret River, cooling him.

He looked about the roof; there was still no sign of this Omni-Tek director, and no matter how curious he was, it annoyed him to be kept waiting like this. He walked around the roof, looking over the edge to see if Silvestrini was below.

Then behind him, the air crackled and he turned just in time to see the air shimmer, warp slightly, and seeming to appear out of nowhere was an Omni-Tek agent. At least he assumed he was an agent from the black InternOps uniform he wore, eyes hidden behind sunglasses at this time of night, and that smug 'I've just appeared out of nowhere' look on his face.

Trellame wondered how long he had been there.

So, this was 'Bonefish'. He had not expected the mostly bald head, or the ponytail. Nano based cosmetic surgery was available to most people these days, for the right price, let alone for an Omni-Tek departmental director - and an intelligence one at that. Personal vanity was obviously not one of his vices.

"Well then" Trellame spoke first, "What can I do for you Mr Silvestrini?"

"We should talk about events the other evening." Garret replied curtly.

"The other evening?" Trellame feigned ignorance.

"Yes, Wednesday." Garret said in a very matter-of-fact way. "Don't play games how many other evenings in recent memory am I going to be talking about."

"Hmm I seem to vaguely recall something. What about it?" Trellame questioned.

"Quite." Garret smiled almost sarcastically at Trellame's little game. "I think we're both agreed, apart from Lev's mad designs, a somewhat stable peace has existed between 'the underworld', and Omni-Tek intelligence." He paused to let it sink in. "'Your' underworld." He emphasised.

"I tend to agree with that statement." Trellame answered, his voice betraying obvious curiosity where all this was leading.

"Whilst neither Omni-Tek, nor any of the more reasonable clans actually wants war, and whilst we exist in a state of neither technically war nor peace nor even ceasefire we are somewhat happy to maintain this co-existence." Garret continued. "However any more sh*t like Wednesday and the gloves are off." His voice carried obvious threat. "I appreciate it was largely River's grand mad design but your people were implicated and involved."

"I'll take that under advisement." Trellame cut him off mid-rant.

"It goes no further, or we will start to retaliate." Garret stated. "And believe it or not, I don't particularly want to go there. I hope you feel similarly."

"I'm a simple business man, Mr Silvestrini." Trellame said flatly.

"It would affect profits for both my corporation and your own 'business' dealings." Garret closed on that subtle point - or was it another threat?

"I understand thank you for your time." Trellame replied.

"Thank you for coming." Garret said and then waited, making no move to leave first.

"I hope we won't meet again, too soon." Trellame said in parting as his body was consumed in a brilliant swirl of dancing lights, the result of thousand of tiny nanobots taking him apart cell by cell, and transporting them to the Grid.

Garret thought he saw Trellame grimace at the significant pain he knew the nano programs of that type generate, but he could not be sure. Then nothing. The lights faded away and he was standing alone on top of Reet Retreat, the smell of the Stret River dancing overland to his nostrils.

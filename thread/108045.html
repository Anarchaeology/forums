Enemies (again)
From Emily, edited from another thread as a anti-hijacking measure :-)

"Two things trouble me right now: the OT side, and as someone pointed out to me last night (), the lack of true "enemies". In the old days (gawd! SO nostalgic) of OTSEC and the CoA, there were always clear sides. Always people yelling "IC" at each other and LOVING IT. I have to admit, it's nice seeing orgs like Div 9 and OP openly recruiting again, but what we need is some animosity. Angriness. Blood."

As someone who has concentrated on roleplaying outside of the clan-omni conflict, perhaps I am not really qualified to comment on this, but that has never stopped me before so here goes.

1. My feeling is unless provoked, Omni probably doesn't care a whole lot what clanners get up to. Even in a fascist state, the cops are usually more than happy to spend their days munching on their doughnuts. A crime of some sort must be committed to make them react. So it might be you need to do something to specific to upset Div9 or Omni-Pol if you want them to come after you.

2. I come from a pen & paper roleplaying background, which means that normally all the players are on the same side. Even the GM isn't really against you. In a mmorpg, if I need a priest, lawyer or journalist character, I will seek around and see if someone already plays one. That way we both get something out of it. But if I need an enemy, a fall guy, someone to hate and despise, my instinct is just to create one like the Neo-Luddite Movement of the World Leet Fighting Federation. This makes the enemies disposable and egoless. As with an p&p rpg the bad guys might score a few points, but it's the heroes that usually win in the end. I really have a no clear idea how two real organisations can roleplay against each other and not end in some sort of stalemate or going for PvP which does not necessarily require any roleplaying skill. Of course ymmv.

3. Don't you find, from a roleplaying perspective, that the emotions of anger and hatred are hard to sustain month after month after month?

Just a few thoughts for discussion.

(/me is someone who never really pen & papered, buuuuuuuuuut

The AO storyline is about conflict. It's a struggle between the clans on the one side and OT on the other, with poor little neuts caught in the middle. Or at least that was how it was initially envisioned .

Now, this conflict isn't necessarily military. Pre-NW, and still to a great extent, the conflict is fought with words. It is fought in the political arena, be it within a faction (i.e., the Council of Truth and Sentinels debates) or among factions. Granted, NW (and the silence from "officials" like Silverstone, Ross, Shogren et al) has dimished the impact of all of this, but it is still prevalent - it underlies and drives the storyline.

Now, to point 1 that Savoy makes, there is one problem with that, and it IS the NW. We are in a de facto state of war on Rubi-Ka, and oddly enough, the most vocal areas of "social" conflict have been:

(a) the neutrals (specifically the NLF and DW), who have been quite good in their militancy; and

(b) the ongoing war WITHIN Omni-Tek. Yes, within. Right now, there's a HUGE conflict going on between Name Withheld and OP (both IC and OOC), while the clans are mopping the battlefield clear of OT mines.

With (a), you have two groups who were originally classified as not caring at ALL about the war between the clans and Omni-Tek being the MOST vocal on the planet. With (b), you have more war within what is supposed to be the best organized, best operated, faction in this war.

The reason I see this as a problem (without going back to the hayday of interfactional conflict RPing that I mentioned in the quote above), is that it is a lot harder for people to RP in when there is no clear "us and them". Savoy mentions in her post that with P&P RPing the people you play with, and even the GM, aren't your enemies. The monsters you fight your way through, the tasks you have to battle against, are. That is STILL a very clear environment in which to RP. You know who the "us" is, and who the "them" are.

On Rubi-Ka, it just seems like more people are making MANY more enemies within their own factions, rather than focusing on the war ahead and the overall conflict that will (whether you RP within the storyline or not) shape the world in which your characters live. Everyone was affected by the Sents in Tir and the Mercs in Newland, and it will, at some point, impact your RPing.

Emily,

There is no clear cut 'Us' and 'Them' becasue, that is just a natural consequence of how tha game is. To a degree Funcom have stirred us in that direction. It was actually a lot worse in EQ where dark elves and paladins teamed up regularly without a second thought, to the point is was actually rude to roleplay your alignment.

The NW is not a open war. To be honest it doesn't make a whole lot of sense ic, but the mines are private property of individual factions/clans; not a communal resource for a side. OTRK has its own mines, the ones we see in 4 Holes and Clon****. I don't imagine they particularly care if Chimera or Legion set up their own private mines somewhere. In a meta-game sense, yes the mines represent territorial control and indicate who is winning, but ic, its more like gangland wars, not an all out military conflict.

As for internal dissent within OT, it really is not the reason omni are losing mines. NW is the playground for the powergamers. The reason clan is winning is that they are stronger. Both on Rimor and Atlantean clans have more and higher level players. Sure OT can mount a counter-offensive here and there, but it's in the day after day after day attrition that the superior numbers count.

I don't know if we've reached the point on RK1 in which OT players start switching to clan in numbers becasue it is more efficient way to level as they did on Rimor, but I can certainly see it happening eventually. In the scheme of things, the quarrel between Omni-Pol and Name-Withheld does not really amount to much.

With regards to the neutral taking the political spotlight, more to power to them! Between the BORPAC and the merca in NLC, the neutrals guilds are doing bold, fresh, imaginative things. In fact there are quite a few enterprising nuetral orgs at present. It might not fit in Funcom's original script, but I think it's just great.

OOC: With regards to Savoy's initial post. I do think that blind umotivated or very "simple" hatred is hard to RP for a long time (for example "I hate all clanners because they killed me parents")
You need something more complex and preferably evolving motive.

I to remember the (what was in my mind at least ) "glory days" of global political RP. Encouraged by the whole Sabulum event (still what I consider the best ever event conducted by FC with a week long conflict and many updates and twists) players started to see the real politics of AO.

The forming of large scale alliances started (one of which, OTSEC, I was chairman of myself). It was the days of bombastic statements. Of press releases with visions of the future. Of trans-factional meetings in desperate attempts to save what was left of the fragile peace or to try to bolt together some kind of mutual front against the Dust Brigade (who made their presense felt in another brilliant series of events from FC, including attacking faction leaders of both sides and of course who can forget the bombing of the apartment block in ent (btw, bring back the movies! ) )

The large alliances raged against eachother, accusations and counter-accusations flew through the air. Events were created by the player orgs, who were motivated by the twists of the storyline and wanted to to make their mark on Rubi-ka history.
But even RP'ers who wernt a part of these orginisations benefitted from this, as it provided a changeing and fresh RP environment.

Alas, all good things come to an end. As FC, most likely due to their cornered financial situation, cut away the story updates and events to near zero, so the large alliances found it harder and harder to keep their members motivated and interested. Gradually they started disintegrating or became a shadow of their former self.

What I dream of, is FC reviving the story. Start making interesting events and updates. Provoke the players into action, like the DB inspired us to form a alliance able to stand up to such a threat.

What I see now is an RP community, which while definately not dead, has changed into a much more self isolated community. RP is mostly confied to internally in the guild, or between a relatively small number of "known" RP guilds.

While there's nothing wrong with that, I do hope that we will one day again get RP on a larger scale. I know that although I do enjoy RP'ing "locally" it is the larger scale politics that holds my real interest.

AO is my first MMORPG. I first logged in on the second day after release and was completely lost. It took me an hour at least before I figured out the chat interface and was able to ask how things worked.

My first character was a clan adv. It took him a little under a week, I think, to level up to 4 and be able to leave the backyard. I remember emerging in Borealis and being totally amazed. I explored a bit around Borealis and I remember thinking that the entire accessible world consisted of areas like this around all the cities. I started playing last Febuary when I realized I was both extremely bored and frustrated with EverQuest; I had a hard time trying to level in the game because there are virtually no mobs that I can level at level 30 and gain experience... even green or blue mobs at that point are like red ones (which doesn't make sense to me because my common sense says you should be able to solo mobs lower in level than you)...

Anyway, I started AO and was awed by the way it looked and the fact that it's a sci fi theme (I started getting tired by fantasy themed MMORPGS like EQ and UO). Smooth landscapes, strange creatures, etc..
What I like a lot was the fact that you didn't have to hunt for mobs in AO; custom missions take care of that for you. No more having your mobs taken from you (I tried hunting goblins in Kaladim but was always kicked out by a group that either was already there or just showed up and claimed that the mob was there whether or not I was first).

What I love the most about AO now is the community of players here. Not all of them go into the game to powerlevel and become "Uber". More often than not I've found players utilizing the bars and dance places of Rubi Ka, decked out in social clothes and having fun; this is something you rarely see in EverQuest if you see it at all...

Despite claims about poor customer service, I know Funcom does its best to please everyone. When I kept getting disconnected from the game I emailed support. I got a prompt response, and for a while we exchanged emails; they told me what to do, I did it, and told them the results. Now I enjoy the game without being disconnected too often. (this happens once in a blue moon now).

Overall I'm satisfied with this game, and hope to continue playing it even when The Sims Online comes out

what is the roleplayer to do?
AO was sold as the story of the struggle of clan freedom fighters against the oppression of Omni-Tek. The language and imagery to some extent reminded us of the Empire/Rebel Alliance conflict. It did not work out that way. Both the Funcoms official storyline and the cumulative actions of the players in game were to dispel this simplistic interpretation of the conflict and we were left in a state of some confusion.

Players ready to jump into a world of war and strife were instead presented with a storyline was describing the normalisation process between the two sides, tragically derailed by the Dust Brigade. The leaders of both sides were presented as competent, reasonable men.
The Tir Accord, though it may not be perfect, had pretty much removed any clear-cut justification for armed conflict. OOC we may know there is some Big Evil (tm) behind Omni, that things are not always what they appear to be and again OOC players may be spoiling for a good fight. But for roleplaying purposes, the basis for either side to conduct attacks on the other at present seemed tenuous at best and you had to wonder whether the leaders on either side would really sanction them.

The in-game realities are even more messed up. The first problem people encountered is that the world was a lot less flexible than people imagined it would be. All the raids and counter raids achieved nothing but short lived bragging rights.

Another issue is that while most Omni (myself included) chose not to play evil characters, not all clanners chose to play the good guys. When I read about clan rallying cries for unity against the enemy to protect their freedom, I think to myself, as an Omni player, "Hmmm... when was the last time I actually oppressed anyone?" I look at my friends, but no one really seems to be in the oppression business. Truth be known I have a lot of clan friends and by and large they seem pretty well off materially and contented with life.

The actions of non-roleplayers or the technical limitations of AO end up colouring our roleplaying perception of the world even when we know they are in no way story related. Guard/merchant killing is a classic example. It is done mostly be non-roleplayers and from a story point of view makes as much sense as server crashes or PvP level restrictions. Should the actions of non-roleplayer really be taken into account in a roleplayed political debate? Would that not be as dishonest (from a roleplaying point of view) as suggesting the best tactic to defend 2HO is not to defend it all, knowing full well that clan players will get bored and go home if there is no fight to be had?

The "real" bones of contention between the two sides at present seem to be: (1) clan access to Omni night clubs (2) the regular clan weekend raids on 2HO (I have this image of clanners going to the pub on a Friday night after work, getting all tanked up then heading to 2HO for a bit of fun and vandalism) (3) Omni access to Camelot Castle. These are not really issues that are going to set roleplayers hearts ablaze.

So we are left with a war where there are good and bad guys on both sides, where the legal rights and wrongs are hazy and during which neither side are suffering and real hardship. Given this context, what is the roleplayer to do?

You can roleplay stories that are not actually directly about the Omni-Clan conflict but just happen to occur on modern day Rubi-Ka. You can create a character so blinded by hatred or so simpleminded that he does not care of the rights and wrongs of the situation. Or you can create, within you roleplaying environment (guild, group of friends, whatever) events and stories that illustrate your enemys atrocities and consequently justify your involvement in the war.

If you are going for the latter I think you need to be a little bit careful as this is, after all, a shared world. Its fair enough within your group to colour events so to highlight your own roleplaying stance. However every time someone in a public roleplaying forum such as this condemns the opposition, referring to Omni oppression or Clan bloodlust, they are projecting their own world vision on other players.

Were I, for instance, to write up a story in which clan raiders took over a neutral village, gathered all the children, roasted them over an open fire and ate them while their parents watched helpless, I suspect I might get some flak from clan players who are not so keen to be grotesquely misrepresented. And indeed a recent article that suggested opulent clan leaders lived in self-indulgence and debauchery while the ordinary clanners suffered deprivations was heavily criticised. Similarly, Omni players can get quite annoyed by constant clan in-character anti-Omni rhetoric when, as far as most of them are concerned, as long as the clans stay up North they dont really care what they do or how they rule themselves.

I suspect Funcom might have dropped the ball somewhat in creating a setting that is too realistic and ambiguous where perhaps a simple black and white morality might have served us better for an action packed adventure game. As things stand neither side can really be cast as the bad guys without making certain assumptions or colouring the facts to a degree. I would suggest that if your roleplaying does require you to cast the other side as the villains, which in itself is perfectly legittimate, you should be discreet about it. Otherwise, quite understandably, someone will then stand up and say Actually, no, I am not the villain, you are. And of course, from his own roleplaying stance, he is right too.

Savoy, excellent post, deserving of it's own thread.

Truth be told, as roleplayers, we are at the whim of both non-roleplayers and Funcom. We have the daily struggle of trying to incorporate the actions of the non-roleplayer into our characters' faith in the world while trying to explain these actions well enough that we can convey to Funcom how we hope the story will go. It's a stressful combination.

I honestly feel that Funcom and the players have, by and large, done an excellent job at generating a very vigorous and dynamic political environment. I have had great fun debating away back and forth for months and months. Granted, I think things were much better back in the Amnesty days, but all in all, it keeps me happy.

I believe that as time goes on Funcom will move the story in directions which will illuminate different aspects of each side. For example, as you say, not all Omni are evil and not all Clan are good. However, as time goes on, the actions of the story characters leading each side may take actions that cause people to seriously reconsider their allegiances. This is as it should be.

Right now, a year into the story, things are still in flux as the flimsy peace between sides crumbles away. We still do not know who the major players are behind each side, but there are obviously very powerful people with motives completely unlike our own at work. How we as characters react to these personnas will be the true story and I think that story is just beginning.

Fantastic post, Savoy!

You've hit the nail right on the head with that one. The world isn't a nice, clean-cut black and white situation and I for one would be very disappointed if it were. For me, the very fact that it is ambiguous is it's strength - it reflects the kind of pen-and-paper role playing I do.

For the 'plot villain' to stand up, point at the heroes and say 'no, you are the villain' is something that I love doing in pen-and-paper games to my players. It makes them think about what they're doing, not just following the rhetoric of 'save the princess' or whatever. It's not hollywood storytelling, but it's my kind of fun.

AO gives me the chance to do that here. I'm a great fan of characters that break the mold (hey, I play an Atrox 'Crat!), but at the same time I try (as a player) to think about how other people would interpret my actions. And if they can misinterpret them to mean something more violent or more angsty, then I consider that (as a player) a victory. Why? Because it makes the story richer.

AO is - again for me - a game about intolerance. It's about intolerance of belief, of rights and of freedoms. It is about putting the blinkers on and charging forward weilding your idiology and your nationalism like a big stick without stopping to think.

And in that I think it does something that all computer games should strive for - it carries a moral.

Lately I've been eavesdropping on some of the neutral boards and discussions just to see what is happening (as a player - don't worry, Mister_Unknown - I'd never use any knowledge IC), and I've also been following the world summit in Johannesburg.

I could easily draw some parallels between Drtiny and Thabo Mbeki in what they're doing. I could easily draw similar parallels in the dissent of neutrals who think their council is a good idea or a bad. Most importantly, however, it gives a personal perspective on what is happening in our world for people involved in a virtual one.

(apologies for any imagined slur against your good name, Drtiny. i mean no disrespect to your real-world politics and views, whatever they may be)

If one person plays AO and from it realises that just becase someone has different views on a certain topic they're not necessarily wrong then the world is made a better place.

We all learn better if we're having fun. Please - continue with the ambiguity! The propaganda war that crumbles at the edges is fantastic. Witnessing a war not just as 'red team, blue team: fight!' but as a whole dynamic event is just too valuable to pass up.

I do think that it makes AO a more 'adult' game than what EQ was or what SWG will be. The fact that it isn't so simplistic and has a thought-out setting is one of its biggest strengths, and for that alone I for one won't be defecting to SWG when it comes out. Even Neocron doesn't look like it carries the same backdrop.

A war is a backdrop - like any piece of scenery. I believe Savoy has the right idea of setting her stories against the background of the conflict, but remembering to not make the conflict her story. The conflict is everyone's story and because of that it is either hard or incredibly cookie-cutter to make it everyone's personal story.

I'm interested in the lives of the people - who they are and how they are affected. The relationships that form and break, the familes that are torn apart and others that are formed. War is a powerful thing and many writers and movie-makers in the past have made fantastic stories against it. We're given the opportunity to do the same.

I'll get down off my soapbox now.

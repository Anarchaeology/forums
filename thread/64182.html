- Make it so that the brown cave missions aren't so damn long .. or have one of the mission sliders affect mission size.

- Track where missions are being completed and have the game automatically reduce rewards in frequented locations and increase rewards in less-frequented locations. Your theory of geographical distance is FLAWED. There are many more important factors than that (obviously), like the grid and wompa systems.

It is time for this issue to be discussed and addressed!

I'm talking farther back than this. I'm talking a few mission code iterations ago and Broken Shores completely out of the equation.

In fact, originally, even camps and things like the Bully weren't a factor. The first "camp" was probably out at Rhino Cockpit where you could just fight Rookie's all day. One day the GM's actually spawned in a group of really high level Omni guards that wasted all the players there because they regarded the camp as a "cheap" way of getting xp. The next patch they permanently stationed Omni guards at the camp...and the guards are still there now. However, the game has camps like this all over the place, so eventually, FC seems to have accepted players as camping them as inevitable.

Regardless, leveling at camps was not your best option. It seems that people who came over from EQ popularized this one. There were still plenty of people who joined hunting groups. Provided you had a skilled and well-balanced team, hunting was the fastest way of getting xp. Camps were safer and "brainless" but slower in most cases.

Missions really weren't an option. They were too far away and it took too much time to gather a team and get them all the way out to the mission. You'd lose people constantly from buggy /follow code and if anyone died, they would rarely want to come back. Also, the cramped quarters made everything a headahce and pet classes useless. And then if you finished the mission, you had to head back to town and get another and repeat the whole process again. You were much better off heading out to a hunting team gather point (the crossroads in Varmint Woods, the Avalon OP or the Mort OP, etc) and finding a forming hunting team. You could be out there spanking stuff in minutes and you could stay out there as long as your supplies would last. I would spend entire Saturday's out around Rhinoman Valley hunting Rhino's...once you were out there you could stay out there forever.

All this went away when you could suddenly get missions in Broken Shores. It's only a grid away and even has local save terminals. This was better than hunting or the camps. It was less fun, generic, routine and repetitive, but it was easier and so everyone did it...and still does. Team missions just made it even easier.

This may be true. However, it doesn't change the fact that ClickSaver entirely removed a major aspect of the game. I agree it was annoying that you could not pick your reward, and some people would spend hours, literally, clicking away at the terminals looking for one particular thing. But people still got those items...some through luck, some through a lot of clicking. And if you didn't get them you just had to buy them from other players. You rarely, if ever, got anything of use to you from a mission other than credits, but you could often sell what you got to other players and get good money for it. And you could turn around and buy something you did need from someone else. There was a real economy. The amount of time you would spend trolling a terminal was far greater than the amount of time you would spend earning the credits to just buy the item from someone else so you were best off spending your time doing more enjoyable things.

ClickSaver eliminated this. Suddenly, anyone could get anything in no time and at virtually no cost. Nobody wanted to pay a dime for anything "missionable" and so everything became worthless.

Sure, I'd agree that things weren't that great originally, but ClickSaver certainly didn't "fix" anything. I just got around the problem using a 3rd party tool. Funcom should have made the effort to fix the mission system instead of letting a 3rd party tool just eliminate this part of the game entirely.

The other side of Customer Service
My apologies for cross-posting, but this is a response of mine in one of the profession forums to someone that thought that Cosmik and Cz were not working particularly hard (slightly edited since I am a hopeless rewriter). Since I have seen similar postings (including my own before I saw the light), I thought I would give another viewpoint:

I think we are just confused about what their job is. It is not, in fact, customer service. It is bureaucratic obfuscation, the other side of 'handling the customer', shall we say. And they are doing it very well.

For instance, when the boards were wiped for the third time, Cosmik laughed and said that it was not a wipe and the proof was in the fact that the archives were accessible. This is a classic hegemonic tool to first make the information you do not want out there in a harder to get to form and then to discard it altogether claiming that it wasn't being used. In other words, you set up a system with as many disincentives to access as you can and then get rid of it because not enough people were accessing it. Predictably, the archives were closed a month or so ago with no announcement or explanation whatsoever.

Likewise, they have taken up a tried and true method of getting out of their promises. Remember the much-heralded 'Answered Questions' and 'Unanswered questions' lists? These were announced by Funcom's then-advisor on customer service Savant. The trouble is that at some point along the way someone at Funcom realized that this would mean the 'A' word: Accountability. *gasp*

Therefore, after a couple months of no 'Unanswered Questions' list and a couple of weeks after the unannounced and unexplained death of the 'Answered Questions' list, Cosmik finally says (after losing the question twice) 'what would you rather have us spending our time on, putting together that list or answering questions' (not a direct quote, I'm afraid, but I believe you will find it very accurate)?

This is an old mantra not living up to promises, used most often by politicians needing relief from campaign promises, because there is always something that needs to be done more. So they will wheel that out, dust it off and point to it saying, "wouldn't you rather us do this than what we had promised we would do?" At that point they will wheel that back into the closet and safely lock the door. Thus they continue answering a couple of questions a week and use that as an excuse why they have not taken any time in the 3-4 months since that initiative was announced to actually follow through on it.

This is not to mention all the work they have done shielding Dev from the need to answer weeks and months old questions! Losing questions, missing questions, delay after delay after delay, insubstantial answers with no timetables, telling multiple professions 'you are next to be looked at'. You don't not answer as many questions as they don't answer without working up a sweat. They are working and working hard.

I may not like their tactics, but as a bureaucrat I have to respect their abilities.

Scorus

Can you guess? I know that a persons view of what was/is the best is subjective and varies from person to person...

Let me describe to you what i think was the best RPG ever made to date.. EVER! It's ahead of the curve on AO, EQ, DOAC, etc.

Here's how it worked....

In the game world there is a council of elders who have the last say in how the land is ruled. There are two major factions that try to curry favor with this council. As a player, you have 3 options for faction. Princess Katrina, Duke Kailor or go neutral.

Each faction has perks. THe princess, if in power with the council; will grant you the bonses of faster skill improves, and hitting more ofthen in combat. The Duke; if in power, favors a better ecomony.. thus better prices in shops, and youll hit harder in combat. Neutral has no bennfits.

So you want to get your faction leader in power with the council do you? Well each councilman has a token, which for some reason, vanishes on occasion somewhere in the game world.

So maybe you just happen to be hunting someplace, and kill a monster.. all of a sudden, a system wide message goes out "Ducimus has found a jade token in border of badlands!".

Hmmm roh roh.. i better get this token to my faction leader so they may gain favor.. thus getting my bonuses working. How now thogh, EVERYONE knows i have the token, and where im at. So.. i have two groups of ppl come looking for me.. people in the same faction, and people not in the same faction who want the token. If i'm PKed before i reach my faction headquarters, i have nothing to fear, as so long as i hold the token, i will suffer no death penalty, however shoul i die, the token will drop from my inventory where i died at for anyone to pick up.

What we have here is PvP with a purpose that effects how the game is played.. not just some lofty story we only read abount.

What's this? Not everone wants to have the same faction leader in power? Guilds tended to collectivly favor one faction or another. The Knights watchman may prefer duke while KBO prefers princess.

IN this game guilds have alot of power. They can formally declare allies, which is showed in a system message such as "KBO has offically declared your guild The Readers as allies!". What does ths do? In this game were conquerable GUILD HALLS. Varying in location and desierablity with chests to hold guild loot. An allied guild has free access to your guild hall.

What now? "The knight watchman have offically denounced you as their mortal enemies!" So what does this mean? It means that normally you cant attack anyone in town.. UNLESS the player is guilded and those guilds are at war.

Uh oh .. random PKers.. what now? Well the noterity system was like this.. a white named player was innocent.. they cannot be attacked without you becomeing an outlaw. An outlaw is someone who was an oranged name player, who had attacked another player. While under outlaw noteriaty, you can be attacked by any white named player , and they will not suffer any reprocussions from the game. If your a red named player.. its no holds bared, your branded a murder, and anyone has free reign to kill you without any ingame recourse.

OH .. whats this... a system wide message
### Ducimus has been murdered in cold blood!

instanlty i get tells asking me who did it and where..... 15 mins later...

###The notorious murder Lim Dul has just met justice at the hands of Dartoth!


It gets better.. once a month thers an election.. to elect a justicar or judge to preside over murder trials.. this player elected player has the power to grant reprives for people in RED status, and no one else. Once a pardon is issued, a player is returned to white status.. there is no other way. Outlaws by the way return to white status once killed. Red is permenant.

Guild polics, larger guild involvement, PvP with a purpose, interactive game mechanics taht respond to the players actions, and not just some lofty thing you read about. not to mention ingame bulliten boards and e mail.

This game is NOT a MUD. Its game engine and world construction resembles EQ to some degree;' but its gameplay social engineering and mechanics remain the best; bar none.

A virtual chocolate chip cookie to anyone who can guess which game im talking about.

Why do i yap about this? Online RPGs have fallen into a rut.. and my hope is that maybe.. just maybe.. people will start looking, and take a few lessons from games past.

This game was ahead of its time, can you guess what its name was?

What is Meridian 59?

Meridian 59 was one of the first commercial 3D graphical online RPGs. Released in September 1996, Meridian 59 was one of the first games in the new era of graphical online games. Using a proprietary graphics engine similar to id Softwares DOOM engine, the architecture of the world was rendered in 3D with sprite-based objects within the world.

Meridian 59 remains one of the best PvP (player vs. player) and social games even today. Thousands of the games players agree that the balance in PvP combat has not been matched by subsequent games, and that the communication tools are still the most developed in any graphical game. Many important aspects of todays online RPGs, such as customizable chat groups; buying, defending, and conquering player owned buildings; and player selection of guild emblems were an early part of Meridian 59.
--------------------

A new online game with merdian's mechanics, AO's graphics, and AC quest and loot system... what do you think?

Meridian 59 was vastly ahead of its time. There's still been nothing that beat it game mechanics-wise. The reason it failed was due to 3DO maliciously and intentionally trying to scare players away so they could shut it down with a good consciounce because the president of 3DO believed that online games were a trend and to invest any more money in M59 would be foolish. (Big mistake, eh?)

Other revolutionary game mechanics included (these were apparently added after Ducimus left or something):

Territorial control; ANY place in the entire game world could be conquered in the name of your faction.

Rentable rooms in inns that are infinitely customizable (yes, more so than AO's).

Mana is acquired by questing for mana nodes, and they must be defended from regular attacks by mana-stealing Xeochital Elementals.

There are 6 schools of magic and combat, each with a different theme, and each named after their respective god.
Riija - God of Illusion and Deception (spells such as summoning monsters from another realm, making it appear as though a player is taking less damage than he really is, or minor teleportation)
Shal'ille - Goddess of Healing and Blessing (Buffs, Ressurection, self teleports, healing, purification, and marks on evil players that hinder then in battle)
Qor - Goddess of Evil and Suffering (blinding your opponent, cursing them or their weapon, bringing darkness over the land, attacking with unholy acid attacks, turning poeple to stone, etc)
Faren - God of the Earth and Nature (Various elemental attacks, walls of fire and lightning, entangling brambles, trapping people in giant spider webs, shattering earthly weapons, and channeling mana to another player)
Kraanan - God of War and Battle (Various buffs, creating weapons and armor, some attacks, night vision, and other utilitarian spells)
Jala - Goddess of Song and Enchantment (Various enchantments, dispelling of illusions, creation of magic potions, etc)
Weaponscraft - The way of the sword and every other weapon imaginable (weapon skills, skills to allow you to specialize in certain weapon types, disarm, skills that gave you a second wind of energy in difficult times, etc)

ALL spells were unique there were NO upgrade lines. None of this same spell for 50 levels only more powerful crap. There were no two spells alike.

The game was NOT level based. Your skills, spells, hit points, and mana were all acquired independtly through different avenues of advancement.

Hit points were acquired like levels in battle.
Mana was acquired by questing and joining with the mana nodes and defending them.
Skills were acquired by their use in combat.
Spells were acquired by casting spells in the chain of advancement to acquire certain spell levels with certain spells. There were also complicated ways to handle advancement in more than one spell school at once.

A third faction was added later, Jonas D'Accor's rebel faction.

Archery is actually useful.

The only game that even rivals M59 in variation in terrain is EQ, as M59 has everything from jungle to desert to arctic tundra to inside a spider web, to the UNDERWORLD where you go if you die and there is a quest down there too, to sewers, to cities of the undead, etc.

Many puzzles and minor quests, including the ability to sway the balance of power between good and evil in a forest that can either be an angelic utopia or a demonic ruin.

M59's entire world is hand crafted. All the NPCs and items are hand drawn with great detail, and the world is truly interesting and unique, especially considering its graphical limitations.

Deep and complex fiction with heroes and gods described in great detail if you wish to learn about them.

Arenas and endless avenues for controlled PvP combat as well as all out war.

Many minigames including an original card game, chess, and even an elaborate assassins tournament.

An ongoing PvP battle between the Necromancers, holders of the Amulet of Three, and the Hunters, holders of the Sword of the Hunt, over who controls the land.

The other cool thing about M59 is that the way advancement worked, you could become a player in the fray fairly quickly, but the truth is to truly max out your character in every way possible took *Years*. It was an excellent balance. It took you maybe a month or so tops of regular playing to get to the point where you are able to defend yourself somewhat efficiently, but the other avenues of advancement abounded.

It was way, way, way ahead of its time. I'm looking forward to its new release/update.

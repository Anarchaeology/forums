>>What do you expect from the game in a roleplaying sense ?

Not much. I guess I hoped I'd be able to stay vaguely in-character while doing the levelling and exploring thing. Maybe even meet one or two likeminded people for the occasional ic chat. You must understand the low expectations, I was coming from EQ.

>>As other points did AO meet these goals ?

Meet? AO has surpassed my expectations 100 fold and it keeps getting better. Over the last ten months I've meet more and more great roleplayers, we've got more and more organised. We've graduated from 'roleplaying' as socialising in-character to 'roleplaying' playing out full player written scenarios, many of which have as much depth as most table top roleplaying game I've played.

>>From an RP point what are the good points AO has and what >>do you see are the areas that need more work.

Good
1. Continuity: I like the fact the I don't have to change the way may character acts, looks or lives every few levels. For a gameplay point of view the fact the AO changes little as you level may be a flaw, but I think this sense of continuity helps establish one's characters. I always hated how in EQ every few levels you had to change hunting ground and never really had a reason to go back. Very hard to have a sense of roots in EQ.
2. Social Support: Few games have given us as much support for social interaction in terms of places, clothes and emotes. This in turn has help foster a roleplaying climate in the game.
3. Setting: I think its a good setting. The fact that it is a little vague helps. It means than both sides can see themselves as being in the right. Clan players can see themselves as noble heroes fighting repression, Omni players can view themselves as on the side of law and civilisation fighting off the terrorist extremists.

Not so good:
1.Lack of Props: We could so use in game notepads we can write on and pass between players. We also need items that can be dropped on the floor, be picked up by anyone and do not decay so quickly.
2.Suppression gas zones are too clumsy from a roleplaying point of view. Its a pain to have to move the action into a pvp zone anytime you think the player generated story might descend into violence. Likewise its a pain to have to cross a PvP zone when you are not looking for a fight. PvP, like all interaction between players, should be consensual. Why should someone force me into PvP, do I force them to roleplay? But if it were consensual, then we could stage roleplaying fights in more logical locations among player involved in the story and only the players involved in the story.
3. Ten Item Limit in Furniture: Come on, how bad could it be to lift this? Most people don;t even have an apartment.
4. The Professions: In concept, rather than playability, I've always felt they got list of existing professions a little wrong. Not much that can be done about it now.

>> If you could have one RPing feature added to AO what would it be ?
The props mentioned above.

My thoughts...
I'm so glad you asked that question!

My feeling is that we can no longer expect Funcom to have the will or the resources to provide roleplaying opportunities for us. If they did, it should take the form of:
- Meaningful, fun events in game.
- Web site press releases that summarize what happened in order to advance the story.
- In game changes to show that Rubi-Ka does change to reflect the ongoing storyline.

It has been my experience (and yours too, I hope) that the best roleplaying scenarios come directly from the players. So, how can Funcom help? They can help by recognizing the value added to this world by the roleplaying and entertainment guilds and offering their support to us in the form of game improvements that help us continue to bring quality roleplaying scenarios and experiences to the world of Rubi-Ka. I feel that our community is being neglected as all major game improvements have had a bias towards improving professions or providing mission content.

My hope is that positive change can be brought about through the actions of roleplayers interested in advocating for game improvements that benefit our community.

My thoughts on this issue are summarized in a post on the Neon Zebra titled Roleplaying Advocacy

What do you expect from the game in a role-playing sense ?

From what I heard about AO, I expected plot advancements and world changes at least as good as what AC offered. I’d heard about there being GMs and thought that meant in game plot changes and not just customer service reps.

I didn’t necessarily expect that a new player on the GMT time zone would get to be involved, but I imagined talking to people who were and reading about people who were.

I heard that AO had a lot of support for personal RPing. Apartments, lots of great locations clubs and bars, and a good range of decent social emotes and social clothing, and a good variety in faces and body shapes and sizes. Also it had a great background story that was easy to fit your own story into.

I knew from playing in AC and DAOC that you don’t get actual RPing in day to day interactions unless you put in a lot of work yourself. I read the background material, made up a backstory that fitted in with the world and that other people shouldn’t have trouble accepting. I looked on forums and read stories and emailed those who seemed to enjoy the type of RP I did. I met them in game. I joined in with their stories and tried to tell them mine.

As other points did AO meet these goals ?

AO does have a lot of support for RP; the clothes, emotes, locations, and variety of faces. AO does have a deep backstory that makes it easier than most MMORPGs to come up with a background that is within a stones throw of someone else’s.

In my short time here the AO world has not changed, but Henry Radiman has returned. I hope more things will happen so that my IC conversations can speculate as to the doings of the NPCs running the major political factions.

I enjoy RPing in AO.


From an RP point what are the good points AO has and what do you see are the areas that need more work.

As I’ve said previously, and said by a couple of other people in this thread the social support is great; clothes, apartments, emotes, locations.

The setting is good for creating characters with interesting stories.

Areas that need more work:

1) More of the good stuff would be nice…its just something nice to look forward to a new club/bar, a new cool emote, a release of a new fashion range, an interesting social accessory. I would love FC to run a competition where they got people to sketch new fashion ideas and the winners outfit got added to the game. Those are things that I would like to see being added each month rather than a new gun, but that’s just me.

2) Please, please can you make the graphic for White Wine a yellow/straw colour rather than red. My character drinks white wine…but it just looks red when she is out posing in bars…

3) Keep on filling out the background in more detail. Tell us about the ICC, Sol Banking Corporation, some of the history behind things on the world. I understand this is already happening from reading the Story Forum. Yay.

4) Create and expand the trivial details of the world. Periodically run competitions and ask for write ups and stories to explain some building, event or anything that will expand the fluff content of the world, pick the ones you like best and add them to the story site in some lexicon or glossary of things about Rubi-ka. That is one way you will reward the writers and RPers of Rubi-ka. You might not have the time and energy to make their characters heroes, even if that is what they wanted, but you could create controlled circumstances under which periodically they got a chance to have bits of their world made into official world story. Tie it up in whatever legal stuff you need to protect yourselves, but I think that would be something people would welcome.


If you could have one RPing feature added to AO what would it be ?

In game media.

Note pads, data pads, notice boards. That is the one thing that would help the most in diversifying the amount of cool stories and plots you could create for other people. These media need to be able to be left in the game for a few hours before they dissolve so that they can be found by players and interacted with. It would be good if all players were allowed a few items they could up the drop duration on, or if you could get a ‘Story Creating’ item that you could use on any other item that increased its drop duration from a couple of minutes to a few hours.

(This would really have helped an outdoor party we tried to light with lamps once. The lamps could have lit the area and looked really nice for a few hours.)

It would also be good for leaving notes for a paper trail type adventure, or the journal by the corpse mystery type adventure, or the search the room after the suspect is out to find evidence type of adventure. Or we could just write poems and stories in game for people to read.

Another great item would be the note/book duplicator which could copy the contents of a notebook/datapad across to another empty one. We could make out own in game publishing industries.

Give RPers pen and paper and they can do wondrous things in and out of a game world.

Hirunda, the concept of in game media is something I have been screaming for for a long time. I'm glad to see that other people think this idea has value. My thought was that if we had media that could be written on, it could serve as a substitute for customizable objects. This would open up a world of possibilities where a "writable" object like a notepad could be a substitute for something like a vial of toxin or some other object passed around by players that as a roleplay object. I've had some experience playing live action roleplaying games (LARPs), and this is exactly how they do it. Of course, the object would contain the signature of the person who placed the information in it, and this would offer it legitimacy in the context of a RP scenario where the person who imprinted the information is the (player) GM.

One idea I had is that of a (player) GM avatar. Something that has a minimal physical appearance, like a glowing ball. I think that this would offer a better RP experience where the player is providing narration. It would have a different mode of communication than emotes and says, because its a narrator. For example, you step into an abandoned house, and the narrator says, "This house is barren accept for a thin straw mat, and a pillow". The player does "/me looks under the mat". The narrator says, " you find a key, and a note that reads ..", and trades the key. I hope that made sense. It just sort of seems lame and confusing to have a GM with the physical representation of an ingame character providing narration when no character is present.

Use the sneak code that makes you turn invisible now for the narrator? I'm not sure if you can speak and stay hidden, but if you can... voila, no physical presence.

My estimate is that writeable objects isn't going to happen. Coding a system for persistent, tradeable written objects that is workable without gumming up the servers with tons of player-written notes just doesn't pass the return-on-investment test. It would take some significant modifications to FC's client software. Basically the way I'd see it, FC would have to store the text for the note on the client side (storing it on the servers would be impractical for this number of people) and somehow have that 'point to' an object ID in game. If you trade or show the object, they'd have to send the text from your client to the receipient client(s) and if traded, remove the text from your client.

It's doable, it's just probably not considered worth the amount of time and testing it would take to do. Given the many, many other problems AO faces I am inclined to agree - the latest optimizations to reduce lag, for example, seem to have made it worse. It's hard to play in any sense if you can't stay connected

EDIT: You can, however, use that reference editing trick to make something along the lines of a written object. You'd either have to run in windowed mode or pre-make these references, I guess, and I don't know how or if they could be traded... but there may be some potential there.

Origin story of Sylikon, Meta-Physicist



Anger.

Guiding his senses to focus within, the nanomage pries deep into his empty soul, his metaphysical hand stretching and grasping, trying to find the hidden and buried emotion that he had torn from him so long ago. His chin tilts upward slightly, straight black hair spilling down his shoulders and pooling between his shoulder blades like nanobots coalesced into some black viscous oil-stuff. His brow knits in concentration, causing the slightly off-color membrane that covers his eye sockets to crinkle faintly. As his will to find the deeply hidden emotion grows, the gridiron of notum that frames his face begins to glow softly, matching the bluish tint to his skin.

One by one, all of his senses fade into the nothingness he plumbs. The first to go is his sight, in which nanobots directly interfacing with his defunct optic nerves are allocated to sustaining his vital functions. The desert mesa appears as it would to any other man or beast, although interspersed amongst the vibrant orange and yellow sands and the giant hazy sky is various numbers and characters spilling past in the peripheries of his vision detailing his vital functions and running nanoprograms. Next, his sense of touch goes, as his consciousness is drawn completely inside him. Fading next is the faint but serene chirping of the nearby reets attempting to peck amongst the cracks in the desert for seeds and thrashes, as well as the distant croaks of vultures circling some rotting carcass. Sense and taste he never had, being deemed as unnecessary by his creators.

Rage.

His creators. He still remembers, although it took him quite a long time to purge the anomalies causing the recurring nightmares. He still has the nightmare in its many forms catalogued in the back of his cyber-memory, carefully locked and nested deeply to prevent his subconscious accidentally coming across it during his rest cycle. Lingering in the periphery of his flesh-memory, however, are brief flashes of recollection. The maturation vat. His forced education. The datafiles streaming past his consciousness into his cyber-memory, hard-wiring his purpose and his loyalties to Omni-Tek and Falchion Corporation. The extraction procedure, and his acclamation amongst the other vat-born.

He can sense the emotion now, a tiny pinprick of red at the pit of his very being. Lip curling slightly, he pushes further into his soul, seeking that speck, so that he might bring it forth.

The punishments, when performance was a fraction of a percent beyond acceptable tolerances. As the human mind can make predictions based on intuition, or fuzzy logic, grown and conditioned nanomage statistical minds were the prize technological cornerstone of Falchion Corp.

And there can be no room for error, even with a partly biological system. Calculations must be precise and made in a timely fashion. Predictions and statistical interpretation must be accurate. In a system that allows for an answer that may be somewhere between zero and one, there must be a definite answer, only. Zero, or one.

Wrath!

Like a throb of a fleshborns pulse, the red speck begins to pulse. His consciousness fingertips brush against the speck, and the electricity shooting up through his body is undeniable. Soon, the pulse becomes a solid beacon of red in the hollow blackness, which his senses begin to caress as if it were the body of a lover  if he were capable of such an act.

It empowers him.

Another flash of recollection leaks from the hidden recesses of his brain, a leak that his cyber memory has not yet patched with its army of gray-matter masons. He remembers, being strapped to the computer chair like so many of his brothers and sisters, forced to do the seemingly infinite calculations through the interaction of the coarse cloud of nanobots that sprayed forth from his corporate prison. And he remembers the sharp klaxon of error, as his NCU failed to accommodate the vast volume of nanobots barraging his notum-enhanced vat-grown brain. He simply couldnt keep up. It was impossible.

As the Atrox keepers, dressed in the stark crimson of Falchion Corp., came to get him to deliver him into the hands of the Recalibration Team, a faint chirrup of processed information flashed before his consciousness. Rate of data transfer and his core processing speed were horribly out of balance with one another. It was as if

as if they were all supposed to fail.

Frenzy!

He can sense the pulsing red light become more, and his minds hand grasps around it controllingly. The cold metal half of his mind idly calculates the rate of how his vital signs are becoming affected by the growing emotion inside him, while he no longer struggles to delve deep, but rather focuses on containing the release of the upwelling.

He feels his cyber memory glitch and a warning flashes across his minds eye. Another recollection spills forth, and the leak in his data vault becomes a slow trickle.

Coordinated through the quietly exchanged nanobots that the young Calculators shared, the nanomages planned an escape. An escape that would dump them, hopefully, straight into Rubi-Kas grid system from Omni-HQ but it required that they access the NCU of the Head Recalibrator. He was known to have grid ability like how Fixers do. The plan was simple and inefficient  and they would never expect the type of confrontation they had planned.

They concluded that there was a projected 79.37% chance of failure in their plan, and approximately 66.67% of them would die in the attempt regardless of success.

Just as they were bred and instructed to do on a daily basis, they determined it was an acceptable risk.

They began their calculation session the next day, and purposefully introduced errors into their NCUs so that the error klaxons would fire off repeatedly. Soon, they were all being ushered by the burly Atrox orderlies to the stark white-paneled Recalibration Center, and one by one, the Head Recalibrator ordered them to allow access by the system. Of course, they complied  resisting access to the system would be more painful than the neural re-pathing would ever be.

Enduring tremendous pain, they all focused on fighting back the onslaught of nanobots attempting to re-path their neural pathways. As they predicted, most of them died in horrible agony, but there were three of the nine that managed to create enough feedback in the system to cause the Recalibrator to become involved himself. The Recalibrator tapped on a nearby console, and the nanoswarm allowed him to interface with the procedure directly.

And then, the attack of emotion was initiated.

Lashing out at him in waves, the seperation of mind and NCU became larger and larger, and soon the three living Calculators  Sylikon included  tripped the Recalibrators Evac nanocode, and in a surge of pure pain and power, the three nanomages were scattered to the corners of Rubi-Ka via the grid system.

Pure pain, power, and enmity.

ENMITY.

The mental dam breaks, and surging through his body is the forgotten pain, anger, and loathing that is the only emotion his biology and cyberware allows him to remember.

With a brief flash of static across his consciousness, causing his vital readouts to fluxuate wildly, the pure emotion wells forth from his soul, only barely contained by the cloud of nanobots that he summons at the last split second. Uttering a small, anguished cry, the nanobots fly forth from his hands up into the desert sky, and a coalescent shape begins to form. Roughly spherical, with leathery brown skin and whisker-like tentacles, it manifests from the raw hatred the nanomage feels. And in an eerie, grating voice, the manifestation speaks.

//My powers are at your disposal, master!//

Slowly, Sylikons senses return to him. He runs several diagnostics in the background as he moves to stand, slowly, dusting off his nano armor cloak. It seems that he was in meditation for quite some time, as the Rubi-Ka suns are now sitting low on the horizon. His diagnostics complete; he was in meditation for 7.32 hours. And as he looks down, spotting where he left his augmented nano armor helmet, a roughly spherical shadow casts across it from above.

Lips drawing into a thin grimace, which could be considered a smile, Sylikon says to the air, The emotion is gone, again, but you  my friend, Enmity  will help me. You will help me purge Rubi-Ka of Omni-Tek. A beat. Do you understand?

//Blood!// Intoned the manifestation.

Crouching down to pick up his helmet, Sylikon nodded slightly. Perhaps, he murmurs, I will find my lost vatkin.

Placing the helmet on his head and securing the chinstrap, Sylikon takes a cleansing breath of nano-cleaned air, and then raises his arms to the sky. Tiny pink motes of nanobots swarm around his head and shoulders, and soon, the nanomages feet lift from the desert mesa.

And perhaps I will find my lost humanity, the Meta-Physicist says, his voice sounding stale and electronic through the nano helmet interface. And then, with another breath, he was gone into the darkening sky, with his hatred of Omni-Tek behind him

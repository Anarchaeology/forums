Making Stuff Should Be a Viable Career
When you make items, you get experience. Not much experience, but some.

Now, not only would it make certain professions more viable, but it would really increase the realism factor in the game if you could advance as quickly in rank, and as quickly in cash, if you could make items and sell them. OK, you can do that now, but what you need to be able to do is (a) gain a lot more experience for it than you do now, and (b) make a lot better profit on it than you can now.

Correct me if I'm wrong; I haven't played high-level characters yet. But I keep getting the impression that the parts to make a weapon end up costing about as much as the weapon itself. That's just Wrong. Given the IP that have to go into raising the trade skills to make them, at the very least the parts cost should total out at a reasonable wholesale price, say about half off.

Obviously vendor terminals will never pay you list price, or anything profitable, for manufactured items, and that's cool, that's reasonable. (Diablo II spoiled me here.) In fact, it's great: the constraint on gaining XP through making stuff would be how fast you can sell the stuff and still make enough profit to (a) buy more and (b) keep buffing your character.

Remember, Funcom, this is a frontier world, a mining world. During past "gold rushes," whether in California, the Yukon, or even in Africa and South America now, the people who ended up owning everything, running everything, and getting and keeping the money were not the miners, the soldiers in the forts, or even the gunslingers. It was the people who made the stuff that was useful.

Now, I saw a post on the 15th that Funcom is reworking the shops for trade skill items altogether. I'll wait. With any luck (OK, a lot of luck), that could be really, really cool. If in that same patch they also significantly up the XP reward for making items, that'll be even cooler.

(And it would be really, really cool if they would add tradeskill items and recipes so that we could also have in-game clothing manufacturers. Maybe even fashion designers; as much money as people are pouring into Miir, that could be really cool. Remember, the only lasting fortune to come out of the 1849 California gold rush was the Levi-Strauss company.)

quote:
Originally posted by Brad, STL/MO
Selling stuff to the shop terminals should NEVER pay more than diddly squat, not if they're going to let us make it at even potentially profitable prices.
I have to disagree there. Sure, selling to players should be more profitable than selling back to the store, but why should we not be able to make a little money by buying the parts, making the weapons and selling them back? It's not going to wreck the economy, and it's not going to allow people to power-level.

Allowing something like this would indeed allow engineers and other characters with IPs invested in tradeskills to generate money and XP with no risk, it's true. But I can't see anything wrong with that, myself: this is simply the lowest extreme of the risk-reward scale. The price is simply the player's time, and the sacrifice of combat abilities in favour of raising trade skills. Remember that death isn't particularly serious in this game: a cautious player, who saves whenever they get the chance and never fights anything they think they can't defeat easily doesn't really risk anything either, and they'll be making progress far more quickly. A weapon maker who hunts and runs missions on the side will easily outstrip one who just stays in the shop all day, but some of us actually like the idea of being able to do that. It works for other games, so why not this one?

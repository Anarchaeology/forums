I wish I could take credit for words of wisdom, but really all I have been doing is posting quotes from developers who made the game, out-of-game references like the timeline, information from the novels, etc. These are facts from AO's design and history. There's no opinion at work here on my end. This is simply Anarchy Onlines story.

I have said repeatedly that I have both Omni and Clan characters. My first character was actually Clan, but I wanted to start off as Omni because they had the more 'sci-fi'ish' cities. But I didn't want to play on the "bad-guy" side. Whether Omni-Tek was the bad-guys in this game or not wasn't my opinion, it was common knowledge passed from the developers all through the products development.

When the game started and my choice of which side to join needed to be made, it was all very black and white then because players hadn't populated the world yet. And let's face it, most players in general dont even remotely try acting out the part of anything, much less a "good guy" or a "bad guy" role. Most of the time you're just dealing with "jerks" or "nice people" who act that way because it mirrors their real life persona.

When the contents and background of Shadowlands started getting discussed during its development, I didnt bat an eyelash when word came down regarding the alignments of the Unredeemed and the Redeemed, and their ties to Omni-Tek and the Clans. It was always obvious to me who Funcom intended to be the evil and good because they have never made any attempt at hiding that information. I say intended, however, because despite whatever backbone the story is built off of, you have players playing the game but not role-playing the game.

Now, that isnt to say that Sue the Omni player cant behave like a really nice person, or Tom the Clan player cant act like a jerk. Everyone can obviously act however they want, and they can even tie it into the story if they tried. The problem, however, is when other players around these people start to tie those actions to the story incorrectly. Tom the Clan player is a jerk who camps the reclaim terminal, so the Clans must be bad, because Ive never seen anyone on the Omni side do that., someone will think. No, Tom is just a jerk who likes to grief people and is most likely making no attempt to interact with the story at all. But a lot of people dont seem to get that, and instead they look at players who are making no attempt to interact with the storyline, and they turn around and paint everyone on their side with that brush.

This kind of reaction is not exclusive to AO. I see it in all kinds of games. Take Star Wars Galaxies, for instance. Everyone knows the Empire is evil. The Emporer is a twisted, evil little man, and everyone working for the Empire is furthering his goals in some way. And yet, in SWG we have Rebel players acting like jerks, and Imperial players acting very nice.. and other players look at these behaviors and questioning which side is good and which side is bad. Argh.

Players in AO have always debated as to who were the good and bad guys in this game. Up until recently I always thought they were just pushing in-character propaganda. But now Im beginning to realize that the people sitting at their desks behind their monitors are the ones who are actually debating which side is good or bad. That makes no sense to me at all, because the layout of the game has been clearly defined. Yes, you can shape the world.. within the confines of the framework of the game. But dont expect to be able to just flat out ignore the concept of AO and expect everyone else to understand where youre going or acknowledge it. There are people playing who understand what the game is about and are here to play it that way.

Play the heroic Clan member, play the power-hungry Omni-Tek employee, play the fanatic overly-aggressive Clan leader, play the naïve innocent Omni-Tek worker.. I dont care. They all fit. But respect the fact that youre playing your story within a predefined world that encompasses everyone elses story as well.

Oh, and why do Omni players always bring up Simon Silverstone of the Sentinels and use him as the Clans posterchild? Thats always amusing. Im curious why they never go on to mention that Simon Silverstone was a peaceful farmer who arrived at Rubi-Ka without Omni-Teks help and without signing any contracts with them, whos wife and three young children were murdered by Omni-Teks troops when they came down on his homestead to set an example. Omni-Tek created that fanatic, not the Clans.

I have to admit I never thought that the IC/OOC distinction was difficult to grasp, but apparently it is.

Perhaps we need a new term here.
IC, In character: Speaking as if you were your character
OOC, Out of Character: Not speaking as your character
IG, In Game: Not speaking as a character but still speaking as if the in game world is real.
IRL, In Real Life: Speaking about the world we live in.

Perhaps people are getting confused because they are not speaking as a specific character and so dont believe they are speaking IC, so lets define that as IG instead.

(Most Roleplayers don't differentiate between IG and IC as the differances are rairly important)

In this case you can substitute IG for each time I've said IC in my previous posts if it makes more sense to you.

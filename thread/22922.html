New? Know what you want to do in the game but not sure which class to pick? Come here
This is a very simple guide listing all professions strong and weak areas.

I will grade each class on a scale of 1 to 5 ( 1 being useless, 3 being average, 5 being the best out of any other class).

The areas they will be graded on are these:
A. Their ability to solo.
B. Their usefulness in team hunts.
C. Their viability in Player vs Player.
D. How good they are with tradeskills.
E. How easy money comes to them.
F: Difficultly to learn and master (1 being easy, 5 being hard).
G: How fun they are to be, or roleplay with.

Agent
Solo:1 Team:2 PvP:4 Tradeskills:1 Money:5 Difficulty:5 Fun:5

Fixer
Solo:1 Team:1 PvP:2 Tradeskills:3 Money:4 Difficulty:2 Fun:4

Engineer
Solo:4 Team:3 PvP:1 Tradeskills:4 Money:3 Difficulty:4 Fun:3

Metaphysist
Solo:5 Team:3 PvP:2 Tradeskills:3 Money:3 Difficulty:3 Fun:1

Soilder
Solo:4 Team:3 PvP:5 Tradeskills:1 Money:4 Difficulty:1 Fun:3

Enforcer
Solo:5 Team:4 PvP:4 Tradeskills:1 Money:4 Difficulty:1 Fun:1

Doctor
Solo:2 Team:5 PvP:3 Tradeskills:2 Money:1 Difficulty:3 Fun:2

Trader
Solo:3 Team:4 PvP:4 Tradeskills:5 Money:3 Difficulty:4 Fun:3

Beaurocrat
Solo:3 Team:4 PvP:1 Tradeskills:2 Money:2 Difficulty:4 Fun:4

Adventurer
Solo:4 Team:3 PvP:2 Tradeskills:2 Money:3 Difficulty:2 Fun:4

Nano Technition
Solo:2 Team:3 PvP:4 Tradeskills:3 Money:3 Difficulty:4 Fun:3

Martial Artist
Solo:5 Team:4 PvP:4 Tradeskills:1 Money:4 Difficulty:1 Fun:3

Obviously I could not have compiled this list based on first hand expierence because I don't have 12 characters above level 100.

I compiled this based on what I have seen and heard of the other professions.

If you think something on this list is off, and you are that profession, please correct me.

Doctors can make excellent soloists; much better than a rating of "1". A.) Are trying to play some kind of poor bastard child that they call a 'team-only' Doc (i.e., typically, a weaponless Doc... I wouldn't recommend doing this, as it is viable to max out your heals AND a your weapon-related skills, if you're careful with IP),

Beg to Disagree
I beg to disagree with a few of your ratings.

Any trader who can't solo is just plain doing it wrong. Hint: Buy a bigger gun. Anybody can implant, buff, and wrangle their way into a gun. Traders get to implant, buff, wrangle, ransack, and deprive their way into a gun. And it's a good gun. And along about level 20, they get calms. Playing a trader is easy - and potentially quite lucrative, too.

Any fixer who can't solo ... has my sympathy, but not until around level 75 or so. Again, buy a bigger gun. For the IP, there isn't any weapon in this game that hits for more damage than a Mausser. Max combat skills, max body dev, buy just enough nano skills to use HoTs (to make up for the lack of AC or hitpoint buffs), and pour enough points into B&E (not for lock picking, but for the Hacker Tool) and trade skills to make a living.

Considering how late in the game they get calms, and how fragile they are, and that their only useful team member buff is Humidity Extractor, I'm at a loss as to what, if any, use NanoTechs are -- to themselves or anyone else, for that matter.

I'm not sure, though, for someone who's totally new that these are even the right questions. I would add "Easy to Learn" (how hard is it to learn to get good at) "Popularity" (how badly other people want you in their guilds and on their teams) and "Entertainment" (how much "chrome" they have, cute gimmicks and tricks) at the very least. (Again 1=bad, 5=good.)

Agent: Easy Learn 1, Popularity 1, Entertainment 5
Adventurer: Easy Learn 2, Popularity 2, Entertainment 5
Bureaucrat: Easy Learn 4, Popularity 3, Entertainment 3
Doctor: Easy Learn 2, Popularity 5, Entertainment 1
Enforcer: Easy Learn 5, Popularity 4, Entertainment 1
Engineer: Easy Learn 3, Popularity 2, Entertainment 4
Fixer: Easy Learn 1, Popularity 1, Entertainment 2
Martial Artist: Easy Learn 5, Popularity 4*, Entertainment 2
Meta Physicist: Easy Learn 3, Popularity 4, Entertainment 3
Nano-Technician: Easy Learn 1, Popularity 1**, Entertainment 1
Soldier: Easy Learn 4, Popularity 2, Entertainment 1
Trader: Easy Learn 3, Popularity 5, Entertainment 2

Easy to Learn: Nothing in this game is easier to learn than playing a martial artist, which is why they're so over-represented (check the player stats at antiguardians.com). Enforcers come awfully close, though, and so do soldiers. 'Crats are almost as easy, because their nano lines are so straightforward and their IP allocation so relatively easy. Once Engineers & MPs master the fine art of pet herding, they're about median in difficulty. On the other end, it takes expert advice to put together a doctor who can solo as well as be useful in teams. NanoTech software selection is so complex that allocating IP isn't the problem, it's allocating money so that you actually end up with a useful character. Allocating IP for Fixers, though, is bloody difficult, as there are so many unprofitable dead ends. IP reset will make playing a fixer a lot less unforgiving, fortunately.

Popularity: Everybody, and I mean everybody, wants at least one high level doctor on their team. For similar reasons, everybody wants at least one high level tank, a soldier or enforcer. MAs are fairly popular, but once they lose their most powerful critical-hit-buffs, that'll drop a notch. MPs are well liked. Crats are more loved for their calms and charms than for their 'bot. Adventurers and Engineers are tolerated, but not enough that anyone will pull aggro off them when they're in trouble. Traders are tolerated. Fixers, Agents, and NTs before about level 40 are red-headed stepchildren.

Entertainment: If you can't have fun with shape shifting and "face in the crowd", you have no sense of humor. Once an Engineer gets the first bot that can do social moves of its own, the entertainment possibilities are unlimited. Crats and MPs can have vaguely similar but lesser fun, making good use of pet names and pet chat scripts; Crats have also been known to play entertaining games with "long charm". Traders and MAs don't have much fun to do with their characters, but at least have plenty of money to use for costuming and entertaining (the former because they're useful, the latter because their expenses are so low). About the limit of fixer fun is playing with B&E and trade skills; whatever. On the other hand, while being a doctor is hard, popular, and emotionally rewarding work, it's not like you get much in the way of fun nanos or have any money left over when the day is done. And unless weapon collecting really floats your boat, there is nothing for soldiers and enforcers to do for fun but kill more stuff.

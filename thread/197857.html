Dissociative Notum Recombination
Hello,

My name is Dr. Verson, I am a chemical physicist specialised in DNR (Dissociative Notum Recombination). Unfortunatley my grant has come to an end and with the Council of Truth no longer in operation, I find myself without a sponsor for my research. Let me explain, in lay terms, what me research is all about.

In notum dissociative recombination, a molecular notrinuium particle captures an electron and fragments into atoms, molecules or ions. Dissociative notum recombination plays an important and often dominant role in many low to moderate temperature plasmas found only in the planetary ionosphere of Rubi-Ka.

The process has been studied for over 50 years but many of its mechanisms and those of competing processes have only been elucidated recently. Indeed, the study of dissociative notum recombination has been a challenge for both experimentalists and theoreticians and many areas of modern chemical physics have been employed to unlock its secrets.

My recent paper 'Dissociative Notum Recombination of Molecular Notrinium with Electrons' covers the important role of Rydberg states and the use of wave packets and quantum defect theory to deduce cross sections, rate constants and quantum yields. Dissociative recombination studies of other molecular ions in the interstellar medium and in cometary and planetary atmospheres are covered. Ionization is an important competitive process to dissociative recombination and its competition with predissociation and its role in the reverse process of the association of neutral species is presented.

Dissociative attachment, in which an electron attaches to a neutral molecule, has many similarities to dissociative recombination. The topics covered include the accurate calculation of electron affinities, attachment to molecules, clusters, and to species absorbed on solid surfaces and electron scattering by a molecular ion.

As I said I am looking for funding from one of more Clans to pursue this line of research further. Please get in touch.

Thank you for you attention,

Dr. Verson

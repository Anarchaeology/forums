The poor quality of some players and the resultant effect it has on teams is amazing. I have been in teams where ONE person decided to do things his way, even though everyone else is busy working together, and the entire team is ruined.

That's why I only play in teams I assemble, and in teams I assemble, only two people are allowed to be unknowns. That means, in any team I make, three people and myself have teamed before, worked well together, and enjoyed the experience. The other two new members are welcome to join me again if the team goes well.

I also have created macros containing rules the team must follow. These aren't nasty, hard rules. They're stuff like:

1.) No looting of chests while in fights.
2.) No looting of chests without warning, so we're all ready for a rat.
3.) If the doc says slow down so they can recharge their nano, slow down.
4.) Team loot is alpha, boss loot is leader. All loot will be posted from boss.

And so on. And I enforce these rules. If someone loots while we're in a fight, I boot them. If someone pulls a rat and gets us killed without warning (unless its a door, then we deal), I boot them. But, guess what, by stating these rules up front, I've found that NO ONE takes issue with them, and the whole process moves smoothly. People actually have expressed appreciation that I took the time to make up rules for the benefit of the team, and those who don't like them, don't stay.

Since I started doing this, I have not had a single bad team experience related to the actions of another player, and I have assembled a sizeable list of good people who are worthy of consideration in anyone's team. It's not hard to do, really, if you or the team leader is willing to act the part. As I say when I state the rules, "We're all in this together, to have some fun, level, and get some nice toys."

There have been missions where I've saved loot specifically for a player who LDd while boss fighting, with the OK of the team. There have been missions where I've split the boss loot evenly between two players who wanted to sell things for credits, with the OK of the team. I've also been in missions with people who refused to slow down so the nano-classes could recharge, and then left the team to steal our mobs because he didn't want to slow down. I've also been in teams where a player doesn't do anything related to his class (e.g. calming/nuking from an NT) but then complains about his share of loot and the poor XP. It's all about the people and whether or not they understand that the TEAM is working to complete the mission, not a single player.

As for limiting levels per day, I don't know about that. I've been playing for a while, and have never leveled a character past 100, until now (I should cross 100 this week), even though I've played and built many classes and characters. This has been largely because I wanted to play and experiment first. I've earned the right to level 10 levels in a day if I can, because I've learned how to do it and still keep myself ahead of the curve. Believe it or not, I think you'll find many people have done similar things, or have been mentored by some of the better high-level people (I include myself there as well) and are playing the leveling game well.

opinion
My $.02 on bad mission looting habits... slow day at work:

IMO, all loot until the boss should be alpha, and if mission is high above the team, boss loot should be only "all" (alpha otherwise) , never "leader". In fact, I can think of no defensible position justifying setting boss loot to leader in a mission ever. Everyone should either have a chance to loot the boss if possible or understand that if boss dies while someone/leader is at reclaim or on way back to mission that the chance to loot the boss will not be squandered ( it has happened to teams I have been on) due to the boss disappearing while a team member is on their way back or LD. Pet peeves in boss looting:

1) The lowest level/least contributor is often the greediest (just my exp) and will request the best piece of loot the minute it is posted, sometimes requesting the two best pieces of loot, sometimes requesting the two best pieces of loot AND stuff to sell, sometimes spamming if they don't get it the second they post for it. These people are very easy to remember and I absolutely will not team with them again. These types will totally sacrifice the team for their own benefit. Lots of combat chest poppers go into this category also.

2) Players who request boss loot that they can not really use to give to a friend, alt, guildmate or sell, especially if there is someone in the team who can use the piece. This is even lower than basic greed because at least the person in 1) took the risks of being on the mission, unlike the intended absent beneficiary. It also shows complete disregard for the other members of the team. These folks are even easier to remember... LOL, there are a couple of names that are literally etched on my brain, i.e. an enforcer who requested the augmented OT ring after my crat had looted the boss and persisted even after I told him that my character would USE the ring. I was so taken aback that I ended up giving him the ring, but will always remember, and will never team with this person again.

3) "Selling" loot should be divided among 1) The doc, who prolly spent 100k in nanochargers in the mission 2) Classes that don't solo well 3) No one else.

Mid level and above, Fixers, Crats, NTs, Traders, MPs and Agents should NEVER request loot "to sell" unless they are in an immediate cash crisis. There are people in the team that need it more and the aforementioned classes can create credits hand over fist with minimal effort.

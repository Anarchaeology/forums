Best Advice I Can Give To New Players.... (and not so new players)
Stop listening to high level players!

OK. That seems totally stupid, right? It's true. 90% of the advice being thrown about comes from old school PvP players, or can be traced back to them. If you don't intend to PvP, and someone is giving you some advice about a gun or whatever (and there's plenty of such advice) ask them if they PvP, or plan to. Ask them about alternatives. If they claim there aren't any, then they're lying or ignorant. Probably both if they insist on adhering religiously to the old school PvP dogma.

FC has intriduced items and other things to this game which are very powerful and make characters plenty strong when fighting MOBs, but the standard advice is to resist change, and make every single character into a carbon copy of someone else's good character. If you're going to PvP, then go that route. I don't really care. If you're going to be teaming with people to kill MOBs, then you DO have options, despite what you may have heard.

My highest level character is "only" level 102, which may seem high to newbies, but all the really high level people will shun me, and label me as some newbie moron running my mouth off without real information about the high level game. Whatever. I've basically decided that when I'm looking to team with people to do missions (in Broken Shores, for example) I'm simply going to refuse to recruit people with PvP titles.

This has NOTHING to do with the way that PvP disgusts me in this game. This IS brought on by spending the last few days teaming with people that are twinked on the surface, but only insofar as they've been outfitted with lots of NCU and a ridiculous weapon, but offer NOTHING to a well built team.

The best advice I can offer is to remember that when out killing MOBs, EVERY profession is a casting profession. Take a look at your chosen profession's nanos (in a store, or at www.antiguardians.org) and make a conscious decision which lines (those that do the same thing at increasing effectiveness) you want. Figure out which nano skills you need to get the best coverage of the lines that are important for your profession. Implanting those nano skills is AT LEAST as important as whatever weapon you might be using, regardless of profession. Agents? Soldiers? I'm talking to you. (And many others, but agents and their whining are driving me insane.) Those chosen nano skills MUST be maxed every level. If not, you are doing a disservice to your team mates. Period.

Like I said, people are going to stone me now, but I don't much care. Those that would burn me in effigy for my advice have no place on my team anyway.

-Frizton

My, this is turning into an argument, isn't it?

Well, Frizton, since you missed the memo allow me to enlighten you. In order to gain money and experience on Rubi-Ka, you need to be able to hunt humanoid and mutant mosters. In other words, to advance, you need to kill. According to Trond Ivar Hansen (aka Jim Salabim), "In general terms, there are three ways to survive a battle in AO: You may have a good amount of hit points and AC, you may have good firepower (either with weapons, nano programs, pets or charmed pets) or you may have good healing powers" (16 Feb. 2002, 'Fixers' from the official news).

By using attack pets, the Metaphysicist has good firepower, and also has good healing thanks to their healing pets. You are probably maxing matter creation, time & space, psycho mod, and sensory improvement, with a non-trivial investment in bio-metamorphosis. That's a lot of skills, but it doesn't rule out utilizing other skills as well. Now Metas might be good, but they can get better. With the rest of your IP, you have some choices to make. Just about everybody does ther best to keep their AC as high as they can get it, since it is also cheap (requires investment in two attributes, usually). Then there is weaponry, health, or healing. Do you want first aid and treatment, high body develoment, or a solid weapon to back up your tank? A lot of metaphysicists choose the solid weapon. The reason is simple, faster kills means less time invested to earn experience and money. As a nano-mage Meta, you can probably do just fine using attack programs to back up the pet as needed. However an Opifex of an Atrox is very likely not to have the nano-pool to get away with this exclusively. Then, why should they waste precious nano energy on dealing damage when a good shotgun, blade, or support beam can do just as much, just as fast, without any real effort on part of the player?

The point is, different people have different styles. While the IP system may seem restrictive, Funcom designed it so that unique characters can continue to be unique without becoming unviable.

Beam-wielding Atrox MPs, are starting to gain popularity simply because the combination works. If you aren't going to attempt to Nuke a target in fights, the metaphysicist's role in combat is to do nothing except order their pets around. Therefore they don't need a lot of nano pool. By balancing one's IP budget correctly, one can still max out your pet, while spending a good ammount on a token weapon, evades, and body development. This is done by micromanaging one's skills. (Don't overspend on nano-pool if you're going to try this. Instead pop nano-rechargers as necessary.) Beam wielding Metas are not going to be using the same quality of beam wielding Enforcers. But with the attack pet there, they don't need to.

Some of the "old school" class players may really hate the idea of "tank-mages," but in this case it just plain works really well. These combat MPs aren't really any worse off in a group than a "pure" metaphysicist, however they're role in a group does change slightly.

Good groups will talk to each other and establish what each member can and cannot do. It is a big mistake to assume each member of a certain profession has the same skillset trained. Good groups also establish communication macros to let each other know what is happening and what needs to be done. That means, for example, if your Fixer is capable of Instantaneous Encoding, don't leave it to the Fixer to decide when to use it. Instead, let the doctor make the call.

Even when teammates don't quite have the high quality formulas you would like, don't underestimate the power of an extra gun in a group. The more damage your group can do to a target, the less danger that group is in. So if that MP or crat has a BFG 2000 available, let him use it. He will be happier, and you will be happier. Trust me.

If you have any questions about character building and tactics using non-standard character choices, please feel free to ask. To best break the rules, one needs to know the rules first.

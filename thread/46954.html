Roleplaying starts with you.
Role playing begins in the first room. You remember, the one where you select a race / gender. Why did you select an Atrox? And dont say, Because they make the best enforcers. Thats not roleplaying, thats rollplaying.

Room two, body shape and looks. Why is your female Opifex short and slim? Were her parents that way? An aunt or uncle? Did OT specifically make your Atrox tall and heavy for a reason? Where did you get your tattoos? Why dont you have any hair?

Room three, profession. So you want to be a Martial Artist. Who trained you? Why did you receive the training in the first place? Did your nanomage study to be a Nano-Technician with the OT or with the clan? Why isnt your doctor continuing to care for others in a hospital? What made your adventurer choose a life of travel and danger?

Room four, faction. You are now registered as a full citizen of Rubi-Ka. As a citizen, you have a choice to make Dont choose to be Omni because they have a steady income and access to specific gear. Dont choose the clan just because your best friend has a lvl 150 clanner MA waiting to give you money and equipment. Choose your affiliation for a reason.

Training grounds. Where do you put those initial IP? You have an opportunity to decide for yourself what skills you want to specialize in. Maybe your MA doesnt use any weapons, pure hand-to-hand only. Go ahead and make a doctor that dual-wields pistols. If you want to power-level, there are plenty of resources to tell you exactly how to turn your character into a perfect killing machine. How about, instead, you spend some time deciding why your Fixer is specializing in SMGs or why your enforcer only uses one-handed weapons.

First interactions. Someone sees you step off the blue pad and says, New here? The proper response is not, Yep, just rolled up a new Solitus MP. Hopefully this alt will do better than the last char I genned up.

I had a great IC conversation with a young Opifex engineer last night. She was just starting out. And even though I, Katpaws, am only a few weeks in the struggle myself, I remember what it is like when you dont have the cash for the things you need to buy. So I donated my old Bio Comminuter (had to upgrade to a higher quality item) to the girl. We sat and chatted for a few minutes about why we were in the fight and then went our separate ways.

Roleplaying begins with the players behind the characters. If you dont know who your character is, and why they are doing what theyre doing, you wont ever be satisfied with the RP aspects of any game.

Certainly there are improvements that could be made to AO to make roleplaying easier but I dont believe there is anything that needs to be done to make it possible.

Certainly my post was meant to be a suggestion to new players just getting started with the idea of roleplaying. I have been doing this for over 20 years now, so this type of thing comes as second nature to me. I'm also a writer and, according to my writing workshop, one of the strongest parts of my writing is being able to create believable characters.

Despite the attempt by FC to make the character creation process seem like part of the world, I actually look at it as being seperate and distinct. I look at the movement from level to level in the creation process as me adding layers onto the characteristics of my avatar.

If you read my backstories for my characters (The Girls of West Athen ) you'll see that I assume they come from families with parents, siblings, etc. I don't have an Atrox character. They would be more of a challenge since they clearly can't procreate. But the others are all divided into male and female and they make a point of the Atrox not being neuter and not having children. So I extrapolated that the others (Opifex, Solitus, and Nano) can have children of their own.

Anyway, this process works for me. Maybe others will find it helpful as well. I find it much easier to play a role when I know some details about the character. Not everything may ever be disclosed, but knowing it allows me to determine how the character's personality, etc. was formed.

Like I mentioned above, I've been roleplaying for over 20 years now. I've almost exclusively played D&D. And I've almost always been the DM. That means that nearly every night I have to play dozens of characters. Making each unique and interesting is a real challenge. But as Jaxxas points out, the most fun is when the other players got into the story and take it in directions I'm not ready for. Two examples...

The PCs were supposed to go find this farmer who had information about where the local Orc camp was. When they got there, and had the information. One of the players decided to ask the farmer about fertilizer. We had a 20 minute conversation about the best type of excrement to use for fertilization. The other players were holding their sides they were laughing so hard. (In case you're wonder, Shambling Mounds make the best poop).

The second was when I had the PCs trapped. They were trying to break into a prison to free a political prisoner. The guards had them surrounded and each PC was grabbed by two guards each. Then a player looks at me and asks...

PC: "How long since I went to the bathroom?"
DM: "Wha?"
PC: "How long since I went pee?"
DM: (Making a huge SWAG .. there are no tables for this) "2 hours"
PC: "Okay, I pee down my leg. Which way does the urine flow?"

Anyway, we ran a brief encounter where the urine flowed toward one of the guards, who dropped the right arm of the character. This allowed the elf to draw a sword and the fight ensued.

Anyway, I realize these examples aren't totally appropriate for this forum, but that is the type of roleplaying that I enjoy. Decorating an apartment and playing dress up is fun, but it's too easy to keep that type of thing shallow.

And as I said in my original post, for me, it's all about the character. Whether that character started with the role in mind or because I just thought it would be cool to play a Homo-Nano Meta Physicist, I still work out the backgrounds for my characters.

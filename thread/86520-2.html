OOC:

One thing that hasn't been made clear is whether this was loot from the boss or not. If it was boss loot, then the only fair thing to do is roll since there is only one boss and the entire team made it possible to get to that point.

Now, if the disk dropped from a regular mob within the mission, you get into some grey areas. As Tamiyana pointed out, Alpha means that everyone gets a turn at looting and there's not much point in setting looting to alpha if everything looted is subject to debate over ownership. Granted, a GA disk is a big deal (Ask any Fixer, Arila, it's a very big deal. To someone else, it sells for a lot of credits) and I think the nice thing to do would have been to give the disk to the Fixer in the group if that player didn't have it already. But what would the story be if there were no "Player looted <item>" messages? There wouldn't be a story because the player just would have looted the disk like any other piece of loot and the team would have moved on to the next mob. Frankly, looting off a regular mission mob when looting is set to alpha is about the same thing as rolling - everyone had a fair chance at getting the disk, whoever gets it depends on whose number comes up at the time, so rolling after the fact is redundant and is asking the player to reroll for an item that he has already "won" in the first place.

So, I guess my point is that you can't expect everyone to play by the same set of implied or assumed rules. To one player, a GA or NS disk is always open for rolling; to another, when it's alpha and your turn to loot, it's your loot since everyone else has the same chance of getting loot in the first place.

No one is actually wrong in all of this (either for posting their opinion or otherwise). The fact remains that the team didnt agree beforehand what they would do in the event an item was looted by someone that someone else in the team wanted or needed. The fact that it was a GA-III disk makes little difference  the situation would have been the same at its basest level even if it was a 100-round clip of bullets.

Did Crat00 do something wrong? In an absolute sense, no. Was he honourable or respectful? We dont really know that. Crat00 was obviously intent on retaining the disk to reap the rewards of selling it. For all he knew, the fixer who he was with could very well have had several in his bank, or even in his inventory, and was asking for the disk so that he could realize those rewards himself. None of the team members knew each other well (if at all) in advance, so how could anyone trust another person in the team? Plus, we are only getting a second-hand account of these events; I am by NO means accusing anyone of lying or distorting the truth, but there are always two sides to every story (or potentially six, in this case).

Since there were no Alpha loot rules established in advance, it is FFA. Exclusively within such dynamic, the team basically agrees that whoever gets the loot keeps the loot, and when that is challenged, the decision is left to the looter as to whether or not he/she/it will do the honourable thing and either roll for it or make other arrangements (i.e., selling it to the fixer below market price, selling the disk at auction and splitting the profits, trading the disk for items/services of comparable value, etc.).

But honourable is defined by both history and situation. Perhaps there was bad blood between the two before the disk was looted? Maybe the fixer looted something that Crat00 wanted, but for whatever reason, Crat00 didnt speak up and bottled the anger up? Maybe Crat00 was intent on gifting it to a fixer member of Omni-Pol? These are only some of the possibilities.

The point here is that all of this is being judged far too moralistically and subjectively. There is no clear right and wrong in this, because it will inevitably depend on the side of the fence on which you sit.

This should quite simply be taken as a lesson learned  if you truly are principally concerned with looting equality, establish rules with your team before you run into the mission.

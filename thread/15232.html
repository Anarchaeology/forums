My humble idea for increased Rp'ing
I was just thinking about an old text-based rpg that I played online several years ago called Gemstone 3. The actual game sucked (imo) and there was no storyline to speak of...however one thing that I remember best about it was that almost everyone Rp'ed to some extent. I really wish that more people would Rp in AO cause imo it just makes it more fun and the game comes more alive. So to that I end I propose a new in-game command taken from Gemstone. The ability to *thump* lower level characters. In Gemstone once you got to lvl 20 (which took a long damn time) you gained the ability to thump characters lower level than you who weren't Rp'ing. In AO I think the level to be able to start thumping lower level chars should be around 100. When you are thumped you can't move, can't talk, can't do anything for a few minutes (think of it as a time-out for you to think about the bad, bad things you did ) I don't think everyone should Rp if they don't want to but hey guess what...they have this nifty command /tell you can talk ooc all you want on there if you want and they even have ooc channels.

NOTICE This is not a joke and I really do think it should be implented only change the name to like enforced statis or something.

Deljin Blaed,

AO's designers have stated upfront that you should be able to play the game without RPing (if you can even define exactly what it is to begin with). It is not enforced by design. And fiscally, it shouldn't as the RPers are supported in good part by those that are here just for the game mechanism. A separate server for RPers would be nice but that's a whole other can of worms.

And I haven't even mentioned yet that there's absolutely no correlation between a good roleplayer and their level. With the *thump*, you'll have level 200's who may be bored and decide to thump everyone they see just to grief them. That's hardly roleplaying, or even enforcement of it.

Instead, I suggest you try to find roleplayers and start hanging around them (short of being annoying). That's the simplest solution and the most empowering. Ignore the rest of the people that run around ooc. Heck, if you think about it, most people in a real city run around thinking and saying things that might not pertain directly to your view of the world anyway. In the real world, you just mentally filter it out, and I suggest you do the same here.

It torques me when people say 'Aye' in response to a yes/no question.

One thing I like about AO is that, even roleplaying, it makes sense not to talk in "The King's Good English," which is good, because other roleplayers seem more natural in plain English. People who didn't know when to use thee, thou, and you properly in games like UO and EQ ticked me off.

For the record, and I'm not even sure of it, I think 'thee' is the singular 'you' as an object, like "I will bring thee a bucket of water," or "I shall send my lackeys to thee for information."

'Thou' is like you, but singular. "Thou dost not know the danger that lies before thee (ahhh! see, the danger doesn't lie before 'thou', because 'thou' isn't an object!)."

And you is plural, "You (a group of guys) must eat pie." 'Thou' doesn't eat pie, because thou is only one person.

Present Day... Present Time
January 29469

The girl is running across the street, running as if her life itself was depending on it, slightly frowning in concentration. She's not really used to maintain the Phase yet, and it somewhat still makes her suffer a little, her nerves put to agony every time the nanobots course through her body to help her achieve the Connection. She doesn't care, though. She has always known how to run, and won't let such an opportunity escape her. Not when her whole future is depending on the choices she's making in this period of her life. Not when she desperately needs this job to survive, even though delivery isn't such as glorious a work as people of her kind can manage to handle.

She's not very tall for her age, and so thin that one could think the wind itself would easily break her frail silhouette; in her eyes, however, eyes the color of deep, brown earth, the fire's always alight, making them appear even too big in her pale face framed by dark hair hastily cut at her shoulders. Not completely grown-up yet, and however not a kid anymore; high cheekbones that make her look a little older than she really is; plain black clothes under the cloak covering her shoulders and most of her body, hiding her moves, also. She isn't really aware of the fact that if she was clad in a more feminine outfit, she could actually look beautiful, and not only like a sewer rat escaped from her hole in search of better days to come. The girl simply went through too many ordeals to really bother about this.

At this time of the day, the streets of Omni-1 are even more crowded than usual, the flow of the mob forcing her to slow down a little upon reaching the Whompas and the huge Bronto Burger stand. There on the platform, above her head, people rush and run to the terminals, retrieving possessions from the bank, sliding credit cards into the insurance machines, silently looking, with understanding gazes, at the unlucky ones who have just seen their bodies being reconstructed at Reclaim, and are still sitting on the pods, dizzy and terribly pale. She doesn't like to think of Reclaim and insurance, because it still reminds her of Mommy; of her own death; of many, many other deaths. Instead, she slides in the crowd, between the people, and take a few seconds to stare fixly at the eastern Grid terminal.

She's able to use it now, and how proud she is of it! How much she loved her first Dive, when her body was desynchronized from the external world to be restructured - 'metaphorized' - into the vast blue structure. Deep inside her memory, she knows that there was a time when very few people were able to travel through the Grid, and that she was one of them. At present, of course, she's just another street brat, escaped once again from the ugly Lush Fields orphanage, on the banks near the walls of Trade District, and trying to live all by herself.

All by herself? Not completely, though.

"The last one fer today. I promise, Heiji", she whispers, a slight smile on her lips. And her fingers hit the controls on the terminal, entering the right sequence... making her disappear from the passers-by's eyes in the blink of an eye and a very slight blur of the air.

A Child With The Ghost
October 6th, 29466
Clon****, end of afternoon

"Tomorrow, the hand of justice will crush the Omni-Tek war machine!
Ashes to ashes, dust to dust!"

The little girl slightly frowned over the old terminal, her hands playing on the keyboard with an agility betraying years of experience - an experience that didn't match her current age and seeming, years of practice that only an older being could possess. But of course, that was when memory was the same age as the body, which wasn't Deannah's case. Anyone observing her at this very moment may surely have found her easiness with the machine odd at the best, and felt su****ious at the worst... and yet, she just couldn't prevent herself from acting differently. This had been her life. And you could never forget such a whole lifetime.

No - not Deannah. I'm Jen. Must remember this. I. Am. Jen!
And Deannah Lane's dead. Dead dead dead dead dead!
But I'm Jen, ain't I?
But I'm Deannah too...

She couldn't stop here now. She had to know. Further, deeper into the data storage bank of the Omni-Tek Herald, her skills bringing her to various streams of information. Here and there, the information was always flowing; the Grid was the vector for all that needed a digitalized form or equivalency, and for her mind, the Grid had no secrets anymore. Deeper, always deeper, avoiding ICE programs, passing unnoticed through firewalls, entering highly restricted areas - she was getting better and better at this game, day after day, week after week, finding out that she was in fact still holding more of this knowledge than she would have thought, though technology itself had progressed during those few years of... sleep. She knew as well how to sneak unnoticed in the streets at night, when all was so dark and the only people outside were those who would never report you, because they would then have to explain what exactly they were doing outside. She knew, also, how to escape averted eyes and trained people, not because she was stronger, but because she knew the town by heart, and could walk eyes shut in the dark passages and alleyways.

Cyrus would be very angry to see that she had used one of the street terms to hack into Newland's network, and from there, back into Omni-Tek's. 'Uncle Edwin' - Cyrus' false name at the moment - never liked her to come back to her old Fixer tricks, though they were what proved the most useful when both of them needed information. Cyrus was living in daily fear of being spotted and discovered again, of being related to Borealis' events, two years ago, of being recognized and tracked by new spies and assassins sent by 'the Organization'. In fact, the former Engineer didn't like anything that could remind him of his past, and in a way, he was very right. They had already taken so many risks by coming here in Clon**** - but once again, Heiji had been right: it was deep into Omni-Tek territory that they still were the most safe.

If Cyrus had known that one day he would be back in the South, even under a false name and a false profession...

"Whassat, Hei?" Jen whispered for the voice in her head. Heiji was staying quite thoughtful for a few minutes already, and it was scarce for him to be so silent.

"...I don't know", the young man finally admitted, and Jen could almost picture him frowning too. "A warning. That looks like a warning, but none that I've ever heard of. How much you bet they won't release this to the public yet?"

"But if it's a warning, they should..."

"Omni-Tek's medias follow certain guidelines, remember? If they're told not to release it, then they won't."

"Hmm. Better warn 'im, I think.." Jen whispered again, closing the connection without leaving any digital footprints behind her. Discretly, her gestures hidden by the large brown cloak she was wearing, a piece of cloth almost too big for her, she took her 'special' card out of the data-term. Once again, the Engineer would be very angry if he were to discover the nifty little tools she had begun to craft behind his back. 'If' being of course the word here.

"He won't be glad", Heiji answered, slightly at unease - he really didn't like when Cyrus was angry at Jen.

"Mehbe, but if it's some kind of danger and he's warned, then he has a chance not to die."

Without any other word, Jen began to make her way again through Clon****'s dark and half-empty streets, turning at several more corners that would have been necessary, avoiding eyes and ears, until they reached the little house in which she was living with Cyrus, on the northern outskirts of the town.

The threat she had discovered in the Herald's well-kept files could be a hoax, for all that she knew, but it still sounded like something she wouldn't be very happy to see happen.

I know it's a dream but I can't let go...
January 29469

She wakes up all of a sudden, barely managing to back off a scream of sheer terror. Once again, he had been plaguing her dreams. This man. Always this man. The one that had killed her a first time, then destroyed the fragile hopes she had finally be able to place in life again. And one more time, attempting at killing her, though she didn't really know how she could even have kept his memory - wasn't she supposed to have crafted the disc before her death? Unless the last data of her personality had travelled through the Grid, just as Heiji had done, unwilling, unconscious, unaware, when death had hit him too.... or had the recording been processed in an even more different way, a way she had forgotten, lost in the corrupted sectors of the disc...

...Trying to kill her, again and again. Lips curled in a nasty and mocking smile. The gun in his hand, hard and cold, pressed against her temple, just before the Atrox bodyguard takes her away to.... And Heiji. Men threatening to kill him as well if she doesn't speak the truth. Something wicked about this thought, because she knows it's only a decoy... she knows Heiji's already dead.... and she wakes up, her mind automatically switching to this security mode that allows her not to drown into her own memories. Into Deannah's. Into Heiji's. Deannah is so strong, too strong for her, even after all this time, and she often gets the feeling that one day, she will wake up not knowing who she is anymore. It has already begun. It will always go on.

The night is so dark, at the hour of the wolf, and she's alone in the narrow cell, alone and panting, her body covered in sweat, her breath ragged and oppressed. Yes. They locked her in, when they took her again, and she remembers... she's supposed to stay in there for the whole week. A punishment for having run away once again. Slowly sitting on the uncomfortable metallic bed nailed to the wall, she wipes her brow with the back of a hand, in a gesture that almost seems too adult for her age - 'as usual', many people would say. Not giving up to the fear is hard, and how she hates narrow rooms like this one, always reminding her of the sewers and of the dark closet in the hovel she was sharing with... But the sewers are far behind her now? She is confused, confused and nervous and afraid, trying not to throw herself out of bed and pound the door with her fists. Nobody would get her out anyway, and she doesn't even feel anger against them, she always put them into trouble too when their superiors learn that another of the 'students' has escaped. One day, she'll get out of here for good. She doesn't need their diplomas or their approval to find a job - people of her kind always get jobs sooner or later. But she simply cannot stand being locked in.

"Shhh... Dee... It's allright. I'm here."

Heiji. There, deep inside of her, his presence so strong, so frightening at times. One moment the Heiji she loves, the other the one she fears, cold and angry whenever he feels she is threatened or simply unhappy. Yet he still is her only friend. None of the other kids at the orphanage can understand - none of them has the right to know!

Besides... Jen feels like she will never be able to make friends with anyone anymore.

Not after Burt, Jonan, Destiny and all the other children.

Two For Tragedy
October 6th, 29466
Clon****, late in the evening

"Jen? Jen! Will you come here, you little...?" Cyrus slightly angry voice called.

Jen raised her head, defiantly looking at the old Engineer who was now quickly walking toward them. She had been talking with Burt and Jonan, two elder boys whose parents were working at the Mines, near the door of the closed tailor shop; she knew very well that her 'Uncle' hated to see her roam the dark alleyways in the southearnest part of Clon**** City, especially at night. But there at least, the children usually could be alone, without anyone to watch what they were up to. "Yer uncle's mad at ya again, eh Jenny?" Burt said jokingly with the corner of a smile.

"Aw, shuddup, guys", Jen simply muttered, getting on her feet and wiping her ragged cloak from the dust gathered in the streets of Clon**** main mining center. "Ain't doin' anythin' wrong, Uncle Edwin!" the little girl protested a second before her 'Uncle' seized her by the wrist. "Ain't..."

"You come here, and you don't argue with me, damn you!" Cyrus snapped at her on a harsh tone. Shrugging at her friends, Jen followed without a word, frowning. When the Engineer was in such a mood, the better thing to do was to shut your mouth, and wait for him to calm down a little.

"First, you tell me what the hell you're doing outside at such an hour - it's past ten, in case you haven't noticed. Second, you tell me what exactly the message you left was supposed to mean", Cyrus immediately begun as soon as they were alone in the house they shared in the camp just outside the west gate, door carefully locked behind them and all windows closed. "I told you, Jen - I told you not to use the public terms out there. No damn public term, is it so hard to understand?"

"I didn't -"

"You did. Shenkel's wife told me she's seen you this very afternoon, goofing around the terms near the shops. Stop lying, please", Cyrus answered, very seemingly trying not to scream at her. All in all, this dangerous calm was even more frightening than a violent anger. "What's this message? And where did you find it?"

Jen kept silent, not knowing if answering was a very good idea. She could almost picture Heiji chuckling at her uneasiness, the Fixer playing with her one more time as he used to do when she had completely ****ed up. "I had warned you, Dee. Cyrus ain't a very funny guy now."

"Screw ya, Heiji, I know it!" she angrily muttered, but she was actually angrier at herself - at her own impulsivity - than at him.

"What?" Cyrus frowned.

"Ahh... Errm, nuthin', Cyrus!" Jen hastily sighed. "I mean... Well, it comes from tha Herald's private files. Tha ones they received this morning."

"From the..."

This time, it was Cyrus' turn to drop silent all of a sudden, needing a few seconds to realize what she had said. What she had done, the risks she had taken, and what kind of problems it could bring them if she had been discovered, and tracked to Clon****.

"But they haven't followed meh!" Jen added on a dreadly serious tone. "I was careful, Cyrus. More than ya can think! Now I dunno what it means - I dun even know where it came from. But I thought I should warn ya, 'cause..."

Cyrus was still silent, and for a moment, she really wondered if this time, she hadn't gone too far. The Engineer had after all already done so much for her. The shock she had received at Heiji's death had somewhat blurred her memory, to the point to where she was never sure if the thoughts and memories crossing her mind were hers or Deannah's, but she could still vividly remember that if she was alive today, it was entirely due to Cyrus' and Heiji's work. And the Fixer had died for her. This, she would never, never forget.

"How dared you..."

Jen took a step backwards when she met Cyrus' eye, full of a contained anger she had never seen him express yet - anger as well as fear. His fists were clenched with such a strength that they were now convulsively shaking. An image quickly crossed the little girl's mind - Mommy, standing above her, her tired face distorted with hatred and despair, then Mommy's face blurring, merging with the man in black outfit, when he had been about to kill her. Only flashes. Cyrus would never hit her, or at least, it was what she hoped, because if he did, she would just hate him, and run away forever.

"How dared you?" Cyrus shouted at her, pounding the table nearby with his fist; she confusively felt that if he had done this, it was to avoid hitting her at any cost. "I didn't teach you to read and write and program to see you ending up in... in this! You died of this stupid kind of business, in case you don't remember! Heiji died to allow you to live! Don't tell me you want his death to have been a waste, please! And let me tell you that..."

But Jen wasn't listening anymore. That wasn't right. He had no right to blame her for Heiji's death. Heiji loved her. Heiji still did. Heiji already knew what to say when she was feeling sad, and when to talk to her, and when she needed him to be silent. Cyrus had no right to tell her that!

"Why the feck ya dun wanna unnerstand?" she suddenly cried, turning to the Engineer, her frail body now shaking. "I dun wanna be a feckin' Crat like yer pushin' me ta be! Ain't born ta be a Crat! I'm a Fixer, Cyrus! And if... if ya can't unnerstand this, then screw ya, and leave meh alone!"

Backing tears of anger and rage, the little girl simply turned on her heels, running to her room, leaving behind her a Cyrus that all of a sudden was feeling very, very old.

Even The Dead Cry
October 7th, 29466
Clon****, in the dead of the night

Sitting on a large branch of one of the trees, in the small park near the bank and reclaim terminals, she was absently staring at the dark sky from between the leaves, desperatly fighting not to let the tears run down her cheeks. She was sad, and angry as well, and frustrated that he wouldn't understand, wouldn't even try to, despite the fact that he should have known how she was feeling now.

"Why he never understands?..." Jen whispered in a little voice, wrapping her arms around her legs, letting her chin on her knees. She could feel Heiji's presence, in her, around her; but never would he put a comforting hand on her shoulder anymore, never would she actually hear his voice like she would have wanted to. Waiting... always waiting... that's all she could do. She wished for becoming adult soon, and have the right to do what she really was fit for. "A 'Crat... what the hell I'm gonna do in an office? Why he dun't wanna listen?..."

"... He doesn't want you to be unhappy. You know it..." Heiji said, yet she could very well feel that he wasn't convinced by his own words. She honestly doubted she'd be able to live in complete security even in one of the most well-protected quarters of Omni-1. Not when she wanted so badly to come back to the Grid. Not when she was sharing her mind and body with the only person she had truly been waiting for. Not when she knew that neither her nor Heiji could find such their existence satisfactory in the long run.

"... I know", the little girl admitted, her lips slightly trembling. "But... but I can't do like he wants me to. I can't! I've promised Deannah."

"You ARE Deannah."

She shook her head, worried at the thought that the Fixer as well could get angry against her. "I ain't. I'm Jen with Deannah's memories. I'm sorry, Heiji. I... I can't be her like ya want meh to. I'm sorry if yer dead. If I hadn't been there, ya'd never..."

"You're my Deannah. You're the one I loved, and I still do, and I'll always do. Don't worry. Don't worry for me."

"... Sometimes I'm sure he thinks I'm mad. I mean, really mad. I can never know what he thinks. Or what he wanna do. Never, Heiji. I..."

"Don't cry. Please... Please, Dee... Jen... Don't cry..."

Jen suddenly realized that it was already too late for this, and that she was now silently sobbing, hidden in the shadows between the trees and the ugly, closed stand of the Bronto Burger. All was so gloomy in this town... so dark, so old, so grimy. And the ground regularly shaking, like a very, very light earthquake, from the deafening noise of the cannons firing notum in the sky, day after day, night after night, in a sort of weird and half-forgotten ritual. Was there only one person in Clon**** that would remember a time when they weren't in service yet? She was beginning to hate Clon**** as well - to hate every place where Cyrus would decide to hide and cower. Screw him and his warnings. Screw this masquerade she was forced to play everyday since they had left Borealis. She was a Fixer in the soul. She wanted to go back to the old days, to the thrill of the run, to the dark alleyways and the smell of gunpowder on her hands, to the delightful feelings of the Grid and to the flows of data - not to a damn office in Omni-HQ or wherever else, like Cyrus wanted her to do. And if the other children of the Mines were to think that she'd one day enter shady business, then let them know. When Jonan and Burt would become Soldiers, or whatever other job, they'd be glad to have a Fixer amongst their acquaintances. But of course, if things were as simple as that... she wouldn't be sitting here, crying all her heart out, for herself, for Cyrus, and most of all, for Heiji.

"I miss ya so much!..." the little girl sobbed again, hiding her face between her hands. "I... I want ya ta come back, Heiji. Tell meh one day ya'll come back? Tell meh..."

He kept silent, but for a second, she almost got the feeling that he was there, near her, behind her, his arms around her shoulders in a protective embrace. And then the feeling vanished abruptly again; she was alone in the park.

"I'll be back, Dee. We'll find a way - you and me and Cyrus. We just need some more time."

Jen silently nodded, wiping the tears from her cheeks. Cyrus would be angry once again when discovering she had left the house without telling him, and at night on top of this, but she just wouldn't have stood staying at home when she was in such a mood.

The night was quiet... very quiet. She would stay here a little longer. Just a little longer.

A narrow chin and wide forehead that, in a way, make her face look oddly seducing. Pale eyes calmly looking back from behind half-glasses - this must certainly be another artifice on her part, for who still needs glasses when eyes implants exist? Light-brown hair, gathered in a loose bun on the back of her neck, as if she would have liked to untie them, but didn't dare. She's not very old, even compared to Jen's teenager standards; perhaps thirty at the most, out of her medical school for a few years only, or even less than that. And irritated, too, at this insignificant work at the orphanage, at being forced to take care of stupid kids who always cause problems. The young girl can easily see it, this very discrete gleam in her eyes, this look she's trying to hide. She's annoyed at her. At her attempts at running away, that always cast a shadow on her perfect doctor reports. At her silence, at her fiery look, at her daring attitude. At her, all that simply.

"Jenny", the psychologist begins, in a slightly slow voice she certainly wants to sound reassuring, yet that makes Jen feel like she's talking to her like she would to a retard. "Jenny, Jenny... why do you always act like this, tell me?"

She doesn't want to give her satisfaction by playing the nice puppy, for she knows that even if promising that she won't do it again, Dr Wallone won't believe her. She doesn't want, also, to allow the tears out, let her guess her fears, show her this stiffening terror that seizes her every time they lock her in the cell. Of course, she cannot tell them about Heiji and Deannah. They would think her mad - send her to more psychologists, even - destroy her mind in order to find him, or simply put her in hospital until they would be sure of her 'sanity'. She doesn't want this to happen, and yet, playing the puppy any longer just disgusts her.

Dr. Wallone lets out a slight sigh, and Jen can feel her irritation slowly growing. Perhaps, if she goes on looking and keeping silent, perhaps the doctor will let her go away. Although it doesn't seem to be the case, for Wallone suddenly pulls a drawer of her desk open, taking what seems to be a report. Her report. The one where they have been taking note of every observation about her, from the day she arrived from Harry's to her more recent escapes in Omni-1 and her underground little jobs. Surely Wallone will carefully note, too, her refusal of cooperating, and her stubborn looks.

The next question, however, completely takes her aback.

"Tell me, Jen. Since how long exactly are you able to enter the Grid by yourself?"

Need To Understand
October 7th, 29466
Clon**** - Mining Center

"Where is she? Oh God! Where has she gone?" Cyrus was muttering to himself, mad with worry. How he regretted now. If he had came to her room just a few minutes earlier, maybe... if he had decided to apologize sooner for his reaction... maybe she wouldn't have gone out, breaking through the window as if it was the most natural thing in the world.

The Engineer sat heavily on Jen's bed, crossing his hands on his knees to prevent them from shaking. He hadn't any experience in raising children, and above all, the little girl wasn't just any child. Her mind was Deannah's - a mind way older than what she pretended to be, though she most often seemed to really think like if she was only twelve years old. An odd mix - a weird combination - the mind of a child, filled with all the memories of a grown-up woman whose life had been kind of hectic, to say the least. She could talk of 'the good old days' for long minutes before even realizing how much her words could be scary to him... and when she turned to him with those theories of hers about the new terminals, already planning on how she could proceed to hack her way through the systems without being caught... These were Deannah's words through Jen's mouth, but Jen's personality definitely wasn't that of the Deannah he had known. In fact... there were times she reacted just like Heiji would had. And times when Cyrus wondered more and more seriously if what she said wasn't true. That Heiji's mind had been somewhat contained withing her own memories, and that maybe, one day, he could revert the process back, just like he had done for her.

On the other hand, maybe she was just... a little deranged. It was already a miracle that she hadn't lost her mind after what had happened in Borealis; he couldn't have really hoped for more, in fact. But he had made a promise to her as well. That he'd build his machine again, that they'd try once more when she'd be a little older, and physically able to stand such a strain. And when the moment came, if she still agreed to do it, then they'd try to bring Heiji back as well. Jen was aware that this would certainly kill her in the process, but so far, she hadn't said anything about renouncing to this project.

Maybe.... Maybe if I let her act as she wants to do... maybe she'd find a way to transfer the data as well... as she had already done... She's the only one to know how to do it now, but does she still... remember?

"Where are you, Jen?..." the Engineer whispered again. Then, getting back on his feet, he simply went down to his own private little workshop, and took his gun from the peg where the holster was hanging for long months now, half covered with dust. He just hoped she hadn't left the town; she was the kind of child able to run and run for hours before realizing she had gone too far, and though Omni guards were always patrolling around the mining center, the whole Clon**** region wasn't safe for a person travelling by herself - let alone a child. Don't trust your former abilities too much, Cyrus had already told her. You may remember the right formulas, but your body can't take such a charge of nano-bots yet. If you try to reach the Grid this way, you'll tear your own cells apart in the process, and I'm not sure your organism could cope with it as it used to do. Don't forget that you're still too young to be able to Reclaim. So, I don't want you to touch a Grid term again for the moment! Understood?

He knew she understood, and that she was really trying to refrain herself from attempting to use the Grid again for physical transportation. He also knew how hard it was for her. Despite all his efforts, Cyrus knew, in his heart of hearts, that he couldn't make her in whatever else than what she had already been... but this thought was always painful. She was preparing herself to die a lot of times through such an existence.

With a sigh, he walked to the smaller room near his workshop. In the darkness, sleeping mass of metal and circuits that over the years had become more of a friendly presence than a simple machine, Maru-Ichi was patiently waiting for his master to activate it again. How odd it would have seemed to anyone else but him and Jen; to Cyrus, it was like Maru had almost developed a kind of soul... a conscience, constantly hidden behind those glowing eyes and silent frame, but a loyal one. This couldn't really be possible in itself; it had been destroyed several and several times already, the only core to its existence being the program and general AI that every Engineer used to compile to create this kind of Droid, all of this making it impossible for such a creation to become 'alive', in a word. Yet in the same way than every Engineer had different habits of conceiving and building their robots, every core still had something that made it... different from all the others. Slayerdroids all looked the same on the outside, yet Maru-1 had its own identity now.

"Let's go, old friend", Cyrus whispered to the Droid while beginning to reactivate it, NCU by NCU, re-compiling the algorithms that would help the nanobots to take shape more quickly. "We have a little girl to find, and you need some more exercise..."

Maru's glowing eyes simply stared at him, unmoving, unbliking, red LEDs fixed upon the one who had created him.

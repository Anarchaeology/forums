The definition of the word rule is as follows: 1 a : a prescribed guide for conduct or action b : the laws or regulations prescribed by the founder of a religious order for observance by its members c : an accepted procedure, custom, or habit d (1) : a usually written order or direction made by a court regulating court practice or the action of parties.

No Im telling you a contract and a law are two different things, there is your straw man. I said nothing about a rule, thats your word(straw man). Im speaking only about the binding agreement digitally signed by all of us when we click on accept.

Its not semantic, its two seperate studies of law. What holds true in a criminal proceeding, or a civil matter since you mention it, is completely different then handling a contractual matter. Rules/Laws are enforced by police, hence the name "Law Enforcement." Contracts are enforced by the entities agreeing to the terms, and in cases of dispute are handled in a court, which in most cases acts as an arbitrator.

If you need further clarification on what differentiates contracts and rules/laws then I suggest contacting an attorney who deals with contractual matters.

Law - implicitly enforced, with several entities created specifically to enforce them.

Contract - Binding agreement that is enforced by the terms laid out in the contract. No entity other than the agreeing parties are set asside to enforce this agreement.

One With The Grid
"You sure this is gonna work?"

"No. My formulas are right, but the ****ing engineer you hired has no talent at all... just look at this mass of wires lying here. This can't possibly work."

"Ease up, man. He knows his business. If you fed him the right nanotech programming, he'll make it operate."

"Yeah. Sure. We'll see. It's your ass on the line, not mine."

"That's right. And if it goes wrong, my friends know where you live and will be extracting some customary vengeance from your pocketbook and your insurance file."

"I know. Why else do you think I did this?"

Staring at the pile of wires lying in front of me, one side jacked into a standard (well, standard before being stolen and modified) insurance terminal, one side running into a conduit I had pried open a few months earlier, I almost have to agree with the nano-tech I was extorting work from; this can't possibly work. Nonetheless, when Omni-Pol is on your tail and the clanners won't let you near Tir after that incident with the treasury, you don't really have a lot of choice, right?

Last chance to change my mind.

"Everything working OK?"

"Green across the board, boss. System is booting up now."

"Good."

Guess not.

I stare at the insurance terminal, the mess of wires, the gridfeed I hijacked from one of the major "adult video" database services in Omni-Ent. Figured this might take a lot of bandwidth, and nobody has more than those guys. Hell of an unholy crossbreed, this, and it could only work on Rubi-Ka; a strange combination of nano-tech, insurance technology, and some new grid interfaces I'd been developing.

"System's green, boss. You ready?"

I look at the nano-tech, who's been staring into one of the little toys they all seem to like to carry around.

"What do you think?"

"I think if you back down now, you're still going to owe me my fee, and you're too cheap to pay me for useless work."

Cheeky bastard.

"OK. Let's go."

I stand in front of the scanner. The wires hum.

Data floods my brain. From multiple sensors, cameras, microphones, scanners, satellites, gridfeeds, I watch the engineer turn to the nano-tech.

I hear a thousand different versions of the nano-tech's question:

"Did it work?"

"How should I know?"

I flex a couple of new muscles. The apartment's datascreen drops down and displays a little message, from me to them.

yeah, it worked. it worked all right. your money is in the promised accounts. your insurance records have been restored to their former pristine state. thanks for the assist.

I watch them look at each other. I turn away, and begin some of the operations I had planned for this moment. I watch data slide through the grid as if it were flocks of birds flying through the air in front of me.

I'd smile, if I had a body.

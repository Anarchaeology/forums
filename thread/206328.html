Unforgiven
(( Historical Reference: here ))

Do not forgive us our trespasses, as we do not forgive those who trespass against us.


Ravvyn "Sundai" Div had seen this before. She had only been in this Omni-Tek department for a short time but this was becoming a weekly ritual. A request from InternOps and a subpoena from Omni-Pol was not to be taken lightly. As naive as she was with Rubi-Ka politics and ICC bylaws, some things are entirely logical. Covering ones tracks was logical if that person wished to survive.

This was a long way from her original home in Elysium. When the portal first opened and she saw the "goatheads" for the very first time. She was as impressed with their brightly covered armor as she was equally unimpressed with their choice to wear dead animals on their head. How they could move and fight in any of that stuff was beyond her comprehension.

When the goatheads spewed their love for the environment while invading hers, spewed their love for life while wearing dead animals on their head, spewed their desire for peace while making war... it was obvious to her who she should side with.

When she met the unredeemed fighters it seemed like a natural fit for her. They were impressed with her penchant for blood and and she was impressed at their desire to secure their future regardless of the cost involved. Pure logic defined by self-survival.

The small motor whirred quietly, as the history of three perma-killed clanners were shredded into small strips. The dilapidated structure she worked in was a clear sign to her of the organizations role. She watched in Elysium as the most noticeable things were killed first, the beings hidden in shadows were almost always safe.

It was good that logic from her world applied here as well. Her skills would serve her and the "dark beings" well.

"All records and history files deleted?" she heard someone ask, answered in the distance with an "affirmative". They had moved her org to two different Omni-Tek departments in the same number of months. A liability perhaps, but a necessary one. This time it was different, she could tell. Someone in power had decided to split Unforgiven away from the other divisions. That's probably a good thing considering the amount of publicity that daily broken reclaim terminals might have caused whenever a "mistake" happened.

In reality, the only mistake was publicly tormenting their enemies after striking at them, but the org leaders had insisted that a formal notification be released. A lesson she was sure the Omni-Tek propagandists had learned now.

Some things are better left hidden away from the light of day and she was confident that the moons would bring more work for her.

In fact, another target had been assigned but the leaders were still deciding how to handle the situation. The internal secrecy was different compared to her previous targets. This time she had only been given a location. High stakes? High risk? Perhaps no death involved this time? Maybe it wasn't a "goathead" this time? Puzzling, but she knew her questions would be answered in time...

Some crimes against OT are intolerable and unforgivable. A mantra which brought spirit and life to the organization in the will of destroying others.

Life was so fragile; it could be destroyed in so many ways, sometimes without even killing someone. Rubi-Ka'ns with their ego were such easy prey. She laughed quietly to herself and began putting her items in her backpack in preparation for her next assignment.

She smiled. "Sundai" always smiled in times like these. She had discovered that Rubi-Ka'ns were always so willing to accept a smile. A smile comforted them, sometimes attracted them, like spirits to the darkness.

The memories played through her mind like an old favorite song. The chorus of screams, blood, and violence; her favorite parts - but for some reason the verses in between escaped her.

She heard him continue, "You know sexy... I love that outfit. You look great in it." She felt his rough dirty fingers run across her arm, tracing the outlines of her tattoos. She sat patiently on the barstool but she wouldn't be able to withstand her desire much longer. She playfully and unwittingly rubbed the straw of her drink against her bottom lip and felt him move closer to her.

He gazed at her, admiring her beauty. He had seen few shades in his travels, but he believed they were naive creatures. He smiled to himself thinking about how silly they were in RUbi-Ka with their revealing armor and strange customs. He wasn't sure if it was her beauty, the alcohol, or just the fact he had spent the last few weeks adventuring in the Shadowlands but he definitely wanted her. "Who knows... maybe I can get her to join the clans" he thought to himself.

"I don't come to Reets often but I am so glad I did tonight..." he paused momentarily and continued, "When I first saw you, I knew I had to talk to you." She felt his hand move cautiously and slowly along her upper back, where it rested behind her neck. He pressed his fingers into her, massaging her muscles. He struggled to manuever his hands around her backpack. His body was dangerously close to hers, brushing against it in rhythm with the music.

The music, it seemed to be almost hypnotic. It penetrated her thoughts and memories like a dagger through flesh. She labored to smile. Her memories, her desire, this music - it was overwhelming her senses. He pressed his masculine fingers into her neck and the crevice of where it met her shoulder. She felt herself laboring, breathing harder, losing her control.

She uncontrollably let out a soft moan and pulled away from him to rub her fingers against her temples. "Hey baby, are you feeling ok?" he asked her with apparent concern in his voice. "Yes. I'm fine. It's just this mus..." he interrupted her apparently knowing what she was going to say; or had she said it? She wasn't sure anymore. The music was a tidal wave crashing against her memories and desire. It was becoming harder to focus and withstand it. "How about if we go back to my place where it's quiet? I have a great luxury apartment in Jobe." he said.

He gently lifted her off the barstool. "You can't hold your liquor very well, can you lil' lady?" he said as she leaned against him for support. She wanted to take him then and there but she knew the consequences would be far too great for such activities in a public place. He half-dragged half-carried her to the exit. With each step, the music lost its hold on her. With each step, she managed to regain a little bit more of her senses.

"Not now... not now... not now..." desire was beckoning her and she struggled against it. "Oh... I think right now is the perfect time." he said in response. A small amount of alcohol couldn't effect anyone like that. He was reasonably certain she was faking being drunk. He almost told her he'd still respect her in the morning, but he didn't want to be cliche', or be a liar.

The warm breeze of Rubi-Ka felt good on her face. The music was gone and her memories began to clear. The screams, the blood, it was all back. Her memories seduced her with outstretched arms of torment. She fell into them, oblivious to her surroundings.

The torment was interrupted by the sound of a door opening. She pulled herself away from the arms of her lovers and gathered herself; focusing her attention on him. He carried her into the apartment and laid her down on the bed.

He was tearing at his clothes. His muscles and skin revealed to her with each pull. She had seen his type when the portals opened. Creatures that could turn into unfamiliar and sometimes even glowing animals. They had run past her in the frenzy to pillage her land.

He laid down on top of her. "Now?" she asked him as he attempted to remove her leather armor to no avail. "Yes now" he grunted. She moved her hand behind her head. He wondered if he should help her undo whatever it was on her back. Her hand slid between her armor and her backpack. She smiled at him and when he smiled back... she grabbed her crystal claw and quickly thrust it into his neck before he could react.

She reclaimed her dagger from her victim and pushed him away to look at him - to capture him in her memory. He was having trouble breathing through his blood. He turned away from her struggling to reach his stims or crawl to his weapon. He was half dead and she knew it. It was time for the climax of her desire. She climbed down off the bed and grabbed the back of his hair. Pulling forcefully she prevented him from moving any further.

Slowly she began to perform her totemic rights, devouring his soul. Her rituals drained him of his life force and right before his final moment of life she syphoned his spirit; luxuriating in her quenched desire of forever capturing a new lover.

Like the others, his spirit would be used to strengthen her will, provide for her existence, quench her desire. She had damned their souls to perpetual torment, knowing it was their essence that furthered her purpose.

The body lay motionless on the floor. She pulled herself away from it to lay on the bed.

She closed her eyes and allowed the memories to return. The blood, the violence, and the new lover in her memories.

The only hard part about quenching her desire was arranging the perma-killing but Unforgiven and Omni-Tek had provided that for her, unbeknownst to them. A minor inconvenience but she would worry about the next victim later.

She was still waiting on the directives and information on the "special case", but she relaxed to revel in her small moment of pleasure before business.

"Oh for Shere's sake", "Sundai" mumbled under her breath.

Moving dead bodies was always so tortureous. They contoured and twisted into whatever position was the absolutely most troublesome and stayed that way. She growled and pushed harder into the sky. The upper portion of the body fell against her and she almost screamed in frustration. "In the name of Shere please go..." she said while straining her muscles against the unforgiving dead weight. She manuevered her legs underneath the corpse's torso using her back to leverage the stiff body up into the air and closer to the edge.

"I love this apartment, please go..." she pleaded in futility while pushing her feet and hands harder against the broken form. The apartment was beautiful. It was such a far cry from the wooded home she grew up in. Elysium shacks with their view of the old rivers were nothing compared to the Jobe skyline in a red sunset.

She exerted herself to manuever the body a little more to the right. Once the waist was over the rail the rest of the body would soon follow. She intently looked into the dead eyes of her uncooperating "Lover"? "victim"? Who cared what Rubi-Ka'ns called it. It had to go.

The body finally slid over the rail. Delivered to some unknown destination in Rubi-Ka. Sundai remained laying on her back; she looked up at the Jobe sky from her new apartment's "patio?". Yes patio. Such a strange word. Such strange places and such strange people these Rubi-Ka'ns were.

"I really need to do this before the body gets stiff". Such a trivial lesson but one that would make her life a little easier in the future.

While growing up in the Elysium tribe, Sundai would often see other weaker "shades" sacrificed to save food for the winter. Mountains of dead "friends" (at least that's what Rubi-Ka'ns called them) buried among the remains of various creatures. At times, the dead would wholly litter the landscape.

Rubi-Ka'ns would relate it to a "spring cleaning" of sorts, dragging the corpses to the edge of elysium and pushing them off. She couldn't remember if the bodies were soft or hard; Regardless, getting rid of them was the best way to fend against hungry monsters that would show up at your refuge in the dead of winter, seeking anything digestible.

"Did all those carcasses show up somewhere in Rubi-Ka for all those years?" she wondered, is it possible that somewhere there was a mountain of shade and monster corpses? The remains of her past, decomposing in the same abyss that sheltered those who opened her home to the pillagers and ravagers?

"JAME". "The ones in white". She would slowly run her knives through their insides, of that she had no doubt. Suppression gas, Insurance, reclaim... it was unwelcome to her. Life was so much easier when people and things stayed dead. JAME brought all of it here and opened those portals.

They called to her again. Memories and desires quickly pulling her down like a setting sun. "No!" she instinctively yelled outloud. "I have no time for you now." I must plan for....

She was startled back to attention by a sound coming from the apartment door. A purposeful knock that ended abruptly. She didn't know what to do. Should she answer? Should she ignore it? Should she tell whoever it was to "go away"? What would a Rubi-Ka'n do? It struck her immediately. "Who is it?" she yelled loudly from her recently acquired new patio.

There was no answer. They couldn't be visitors for her. No one knew. Not yet. The rapping continued, it seemed to get louder and louder. Her body almost fell into the rythm of the knocks... but no... she had to concentrate.

She reached behind her head with both hands and pulled her crystal claws out from behind her. Whoever it was, they wouldnt live long enough to take her new shelter away from her.

She moved quickly and quietly to the door. The calm before the storm. She slowly moved her finger to the control that would open the door; methodically she planned her attack. A quick strike to the heart. Kill fast - kill clean. A simple lesson that was always applicable. The person would reincarnate but by then she would have a good idea of what she was up against and depending, she could fight, run, or hide.

Besides, Who really cared? JAME had very little enforcement of any type, same for the "goatheads" and Omni's. In fact, neither had declared control over any portion of the "shadowlands". "Shadowlands" as the invaders called them, "what a ridiculous name". No world could exist without shadows. Shadows were as necessary as light.

The invaders certainly didn't presume to maintain the law here. Only one law was necessary in her lands; the law of survival. They were all learning that now. Nothing is free here - every bounty has its task and every hunter is prey to something.

She tightened her muscles and pressed the button in excited anticipation.

The door opened.

She meant to strike fast but a blow quickly landed across the side of her face. It was a weight that carried a painful sting; it seemed to immediately shoot throughout her body. As she fell to the floor she watched the man pull his hand away from her. Her knives fell away from her. She whimpered and attempted to shield herself, but the blow was not followed by a continued attack. She cautiously peeked up at her assailant.

"Where is he?" he yelled menacingly into her face while quickly clutching her shoulders. His eyes burned into her and then he quickly gazed away; judging his surroundings. She wasn't sure if he had asked her a question or if it was a battle cry. She instinctively didn't say anything and pressed herself closer into the wall. He pushed her away in anger... or frustration.

She watched the orange tinted scythe streak across her vision. The three foot blade was attached to a long, curved stick. A simple weapon weilded by a jealous mate looking to butcher someone in retribution...

She wanted to melt into the wall, to hide in the shadows; but there was no refuge in the lit hallway. No shadows to hide her, comfort her, or protect her. She heard his footsteps against the floor. He moved purposely around the apartment looking for an enemy that didn't exist, at least not anymore.

"Blood everywhere" he yelled at her. "I swear... I cannot fathom what goes through your mind sometimes" he exhaled deeply in frustration.

"Aslon... there's no one else here... it's done."

It had been three long weeks since that fateful discussion with the Shadow, yet despite the passage of time, the haunt of a melodic voice and an ethereal presence still lingered in his mind. Fate, it seemed, was not allowing the memory to fade.

And fate indeed was at play. Beginning with a routine internal security memorandum detailing the Shadow's movements and sabotage of the ICC cell-scanners, to reports of the ruthless, yet oddly poetic, manner in which she dealt with her victims, to the matter which now lay before him. A matter which was slowly coming to a crux in its timeline; a point of action, where fate would inevitably decide whether the traitor lived, died, or endured torture no Opifex could possibly fathom.

Night had fallen over Rubi-Ka and Omni-1, which suited Nathan "Ammicus" Age just fine. Though the incessant hum of the machinery which drove the engines of Omni-1's infrastructure persisted, the city slowly crept into bed, choosing to hide under a cloak of false comfort rather than greet the darkness which decended. Twilight ensued, a time in which Omni-Tek's glory resounded boldy into the pervasive darkness, as the lights of the most glorious city in the galaxy burned as brightly as a star, as the only star on this curious little planet.

Why was it that people feared the onset of night so? Even in their own city, protected by the transparent eyes of Omni-Pol, and the invisible eyes of InternOps, they nonetheless retreated to their homes. Was it that they feared the relative silence that was ushered in along with the night? Perhaps. Was it the absence of the warming comfort of Rubi-Ka's twin suns? Another possibilty. Yet, for all the merit one could create from these possibilities, Nathan knew the one true reason why people hid from the night. They feared the shadows.

With that one thought, Nathan smiled ever so thinly at the night. Fear was the most powerful of all emotions; the most pervasive and defeating. Omni-Tek was losing the Notum Wars sideshow (for that was indeed what it was, nothing more than smoke and mirrors), not as a result of its lack of firepower, or lack of unity, or lack of purpose. It had all of those, and far more so than those filthy squatters to the north. What it lacked was the ability to generate fear. Its firepower was being held at bay by the forces of the confounded and impotent Interstellar Confederation of Corporations; its unity was being kept muted, its leaders ever mindful of reprisals both from the ICC and the other predatorial Hypercorporations; and its purpose...that was another story entirely.

And though the destruction of the Clans - of everything about them, their lives, their homes, their families - remained an ever-present goal, matters closer to home required more immediate attention.

Ammicus slowly brushed back his thining blonde hair, and set the report down on his desk. Legal wranglings aside, the traitor had lingered in their midst long enough without any reprisal taken against her for her foolish actions, and while he wished he could deal with this matter himself, it would be politically sensitive for a Director of an Omni-InternOps division to be directly associated with what he was contemplating. Nathan tossed the datapad displaying the report down on his desk as he moved swiftly across the room, heading for the door. To anyone who may have had occasion to see the screen of the datapad, they would have seen this:

Press Release - SCHUEMANN & ASSOCIATES TO RELOCATE

Instilling fear was required, and who better to turn to than fear in its most primal form - the Shadow.

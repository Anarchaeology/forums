Interview with Organizer of Clan Fashion Show (FNN Exlusive)
I had the Honor of interviewing
Roberto "Nasar" Houston, President of Vengeance Theory and Organizer of the 2nd Rubi-Ka 2 Fashion Show/Contest after the Show was over. Here is what he had to say.

FNN:This is the second Fashion Show you have run for the clans, however, last time you were a judge. Why didn't you judge the event this time?

Nasar: "I didn't judge the event this time for a few reasons, one, being the organizer of the event it's hard trying to judge as well as keep the show moving along so the contestants and the audience don't become bored."

FNN: Why wasn't there a competition for armor?

Nasar: "I decided against having armor as a category this time because of a lesson I learned from the previous Fashion Show. It takes to long and is too difficult to judge. Armor comes in so many different Quality Levels and some are only available to use once you obtain a level of existance on Rubi-Ka. That would give those who are more experienced an unfair advantage over our younger citizens who are trying their best to compete."

FNN: Are you going to host more Fashion Shows and if so, will you ever judge again?

Nasar: "Yes, there will be more Fashion Shows for our fellow Clanners on Rubi-Ka. Currently, some of my fellow Guild Leaders and myself are discussing a show for the Christmas Holiday Season as well as some new categories to ****e up the event a bit. I'll never judge a show again however, not because I don't want to, but because I have more fun organizing the event and watching the event take place."

FNN: What do you feel was the most successful part of the event?

Nasar: "You mean other than the swimsuit competition?", laughed Nasar. "I think the most successful part of the event was the support I had in getting the event organized and bringing it to light. With the CoT falling apart and Omni-Tek looming like a shadow over everything we do, we need to take a moment and just smile and enjoy ourselves as Clanners in our ability and right to be diverse and, more importantly, be free."

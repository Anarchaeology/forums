Amidst the chaos of battle, you may not be able to find the engineer who's bot is attacking you, or the MP who's meatball is beating on you. You may be attempting to duel some soldier when an agent suddenly appears and lands an aimed shot on you while an NT hits you with a nuke. When it's no longer one on one, it gives an opportunity for typically weaker players to help out in battle effectively without fear of dieing too quickly, especially since players tend to disregard weaker players as being harmless. The point I'm trying to make is that all the stat-tweaking and number-crunching munchkins do won't help them in Notum Wars the way it does in their pitiful duelling. Roleplayers will be perfectly fit to participate.

As for Zeroframerate's comment, the problem here is that Munchkins in AO have repeatedly condemned RPers as "not playing the game right" or being "gimped" and refuse to associate with them. In many scenarios, munchkins even insult and harass RPers. The fact of the matter is, AO is a ROLEPLAYING game whether you like it or not. Saying munchkins are simply playing the game differently is like saying exploiters and griefers are just playing the game differently and there is nothing wrong with them.

It appears that there are several routes a game can take to either eliminate the plague that is munchkinism. The first way a game can do that is by hiding almost all statistics from the eyes of the player. Of course that kind of change is not possible in any way in AO. First off because it would require a skill system in which there are no equip requirements or use requirements; instead the effectiveness of the equipment is modified by the players statistics, which are of course hidden from the player. Explaining such a system though is pointless since as I mentioned, it's not possible in AO.

The way AO can be fixed to rid itself of munchkinism to a degree is to slowly incorporate concepts into the game which assimilate munchkins into the roleplaying community over time. Basically this involves first of all to create dynamic content which gives the players a better feeling of immersion. Munchkins lack any and all imagination and so in order to get them to feel immersed in a world you have to drag them kicking and screaming. One required aspect is already coming in Notum Wars. Another thing which needs to be done is to eliminate the weapons from the database which are truly useless. Also, making changes to the game system so that minor differences in stats don't mean much would go a long way. Currently nanos require a player to meet the requirements in order to cast. A better solution might be to simply require the player to be within 20% and increase the fumble chance if the player doesn't meet the requirements. This means that someone who is one point short of having the skill to cast a particular nano will be only slightly less powerful than someone who meets the requirements fully. The way weapons work at the moment isn't too much of a problem since they implemented the OE changes. Another step needs to be made however with implants. While implants are a great source of fun for most munchkins (since they are basically a munchkin's dream come true), a limit needs to be put on them much like the OE rules which exist for weapons. I don't have time to come up with a viable solution to that right now, but since this is pure postulation, let's assume it can be done effectively. Basically the end result is that you narrow the gap between with the casual player and roleplayers on one end and the munchkins and powergamers on the other end. Powergaming and munchkinism would still make their character more "powerful" but it wouldn't give them such a drastic edge that roleplayers and casual gamers wouldn't be able to make a difference in Notum Wars. Another example of dynamic content that would really help to bring munchkins back from the dark side would be to add the dynamic missions suggested in:

http://forums.anarchy-online.com/sho...961#post393961

Trust me, there isn't anything you could possibly object to in that thread since it would be ADDED material, not changed. Were they to add those missions it would create a whole new level of immersion in AO.

Another thing they could do would be to modify the way boss spawns work so that they have more life to them. Munchkins view mobs as things to target and kill. If these mobs were to seem to have more personality it would add yet another level of immersion and perhaps a few munchkins might be won over to the RPing side. How could this be done? It involves several steps.
First off, all mobs should have their spawntimer to reduced to no longer than 30 minutes. Whether or not the loot is always on the mob is not really important to most RPers, merely that they can have a chance to actually meet and kill the mob. Munchkins who camp seem to be split on whether they'd prefer to wait a really long time and be guaranteed the loot or to wait less time and not be guaranteed. RPers like confronting the mob more than the actual loot they get. This makes the choice obvious to Funcom and that is to reduce the timers and lower the chance for each piece of loot.
Second, static mobs should not spawn in the same place over and over. To give an example I gave in another thread, TIM should sometimes spawn inside the foreman mine in certain rooms, and other times spawn in various locations all over Rubi-Ka. When TIM spawns outside of Biomare, he should be surrounded by lab testers who would be monitoring his behavior. The Lab Director should spawn sometimes in his office, sometimes in various places in Biomare, sometimes other places in Rubi-Ka. He might appear at the Bronto Factory in Galway to get fetch some brontos to run some tests on. He might appear in Omni Ent or Omni HQ to visit another office. In any of these zones that the static boss can appear, it should never be the exact same coordinates. This means that even if you know the zone you will only know the general area and will have to do at least SOME searching to find him.
Third, there should always be clues which can give you some information on where any given static boss might be. For example, the Director's office might have a non-aggroing assistant (really low level carrying no loot). If you have a high psychology you can ask him where the director is. Or you might try asking some of the guards outside of Biomare. Or you could try using a hacktool on the Director's computer. Each of these would give you a zone and a general location. It would then be up to you to find where that location is and find the mob yourself.
Fourth, some static bosses should sometimes spawn inside 100% suppression zones. When they spawn in a 100% zone, it should be possible to talk to them. How many folks know that you can actually talk to Zuwadza? It's true, unfortunately you'll never know what he says cus he's dead 3 seconds after he appears. Whenever a boss appears, if he does not enter combat for an hour or so, he should despawn.

If those changes were implemented into the static boss spawns, it would almost completely halt camping since you can't just sit in one spot expecting a mob to come. It would also breathe some life into these static bosses since you actually have to interact with the world in order to find them. The simple fact that they would be engaging in seemingly everyday activities would also go a long way towards this goal. Since there would always be a way of finding out the location of each boss, you can't argue it would be like going on a wild goose chase either.


In any case, AO isn't totally lost yet, and Funcom is soon going to add something that has the potential to take a leap in the right direction to making it a roleplaying game (instead of Donkey Kong). The players are for the most part munchkins with a minority who actually roleplay, but there are many among the munchkins who would love to roleplay but simply lack the imagination or incentive. Additional content and camping changes like I suggested are IMO necessary steps towards making this game enjoyable for all.

Now that we have that aside, please STOP bi***ing and moaning about this or that insult and look at how AO could be made into a more immersive game. Other than Stercoral, people have yet to either look at the suggestions already made in this thread or provide their own.

Keep in mind when analyzing suggestions and/or posting your own, that the goal of this is to help AO evolve into a game which is less conducive to behavior which is pure stat-pushing (complete non-immersion) and more towards behavior motivated by in-character goals. The reason for this is the simple reason that any game based on stat-pushing and number crunching will always have an innevitable end. Games with ends cause ALL players to lose interest eventually. Munchkins are always complaining that there is nothing to look forward to anymore, now that they are level 200 with 1K tokenboards and all the best items in the game. Funcom's answer to this has been with more munchkin oriented content; namely new loot. The new mobs themselves are inconsequential because no matter how much a munchkin may claim they are killing the mob for the challenge, the fact is they are doing it for the prestige of gaining that mob's loot. The problem with these kinds of additions to the game is that they are always finite. Finite to the most extreme powergamers can mean as little as a day before they've experienced ALL of the "new content" of a given patch. Knowing all this, we see that all pure munchkin-oriented games must innevitably fail.

So how do we keep the game interesting for all sides? The answer is immersion. Games which have a high level of immersion make the player truly feel like part of the gameworld (by the very definition of the word). Game environments like that make activities that in other games would seem mundane, in this game actually quite interesting. Immerssive games are also ones which are optimally filled with dynamic content to the point where no matter what you have done there is always something new to do. The more aspects of the gameworld that feel truly lifelike and unpredictable, the more the gamer feels the immersion and the more interesting the game is.

First off, an important aspect of immersion is the NPC element. Currently almost all NPCs are seem mindless. The NPCs that wander through towns are nothing more than simple mobs; they do not talk or otherwise interact in a non-combative way with the world. While some may argue that NPCs need not be social in a world with so many players, that argument only holds water when the player community is by majority a roleplaying one. So, in order to make the game more immersive you need more interactive NPCs. This means NPCs that say a large variety of things as well as do a large variety of things on their own. NPCs which recognize the existance of one another are especially good. For example, an NPC should be capable of walking through a city and if they come within a certain distance of another NPC they should say "hello" to each other. It's simple, doesn't sound like much, but it adds a lot to the atmosphere. NPCs which act and not just react are the best of all.

Dynamic mission content is also necessary. Currently all missions are the same. The only variance in missions is the scenary and the endgoal. The differences between the endgoals are negligible at best. View item, use item on other item, pick up and return item, view mob, and kill mob are the different mission goals. They all come down to something that can be accomplished with zero thought and roughly 2 or 3 clicks of a mouse. The rest of a mission is always the same: kill mobs, unlock chests. Dynamic missions are missions in which the goals are drastically different, the settings are different (sometimes having multiple mission areas), and the content within the mission is also different. It would be impossible to summarize all of the ideas right here, that is why I highly recommend once again that you view the following thread:

Dynamic Mission ideas

Another aspect needed is what I mentioned before about camping changes. If static mobs were to spawn in different locations every time it eliminates the possibilty of camping. Adding ways in which you can gleem the location of each static mob in different ways adds a certain amount of content to the game. The more that the information requires the player to pay attention, the better it will be towards getting their mind actually thinking. The more they think the more immersed they become in the gameworld. With that addition, it is also important to reduce the spawntimes on these mobs so that they may be encountered more frequently. The change is to drive home the point that the encounter itself is what is important, not the loot the mob may hold. Additionally, each one of these mobs should be able to interact with the gameworld in some way other than simply aggroing everything that moves. Human mobs especially should have a variety of things to say. Basically, this accomplishes several goals at once. First it solves the camping problems associated which each boss. Second it breathes life into these static mobs, thus furthering the element of immersion and making the game more interesting.

Additionally, the skillsystem need adjustment to a degree that minor number differences should never mean drastic advantages or disadvantages. Modifying the system to bring about such a change is more trial and error than anything else, but it must be done in order to make the game at all viable for those who want to purely roleplay and those who play only casually.

Another suggestion which Stercoral mentioned is the issue of apartments, clothes, pets, etc. These can for the most part be summed up as "social" additions to the game. For all the amount that players knock "social" additions, they don't really know what they are missing until they have it taken away. A good example is the difference between Neocron and AO. While playing Neocron, one of my favorite aspects was how detailed each apartment was. Each area in Neocron has it's own style and several types of apartments per area. The Via Rosso apartments were luxuriously styled with marble floors and well-lit interiors. The Pepper Park apartments were dark, apartments with an underlying feeling of seediness. The outzone apartments were rundown hovels in unfit living conditions. The Tech Haven apartments were cramped closets filled with the blue glow computer screens. There were different sizes for each apartments, and many had more than one floor. Each apartment has a Citicom terminal (various things can be accessed through it, including ingame forums, ingame email, ingame stock market, mission terminal, as well as getting info on clans, factions, players, and outposts), a GoGuardian (the Neocron equivilent of a bank), several cabinets (other places to store items, but unliek the bank, must be accessed locally), a bed (heals the player), and a Gene Rep (the equivilent of an insurance terminal and a reclaim terminal in one). Upon coming back to AO, I miss that feeling of safety, and general hominess that apartments in Neocron have. In AO, I don't have a home. Apartments in AO are empty, small, and useless. A sense of home in a gameworld has more value then most players realize. In the same respect, when I was playing Neocron, I missed the various clothes and visible types of armor that players in AO have. In Neocron armor isn't visible and clothes are not items; they are simply the skin chosen for the character. There just wasn't enough difference in the way people looked to give a sense of individuality. Most take for granted the fact that there are so many different armors/clothing and different looks in AO. Social elements are extremely important towards the goal of a truly immersive game.

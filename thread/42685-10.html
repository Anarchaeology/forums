You know what my problem is, Firedrake.

I feel there is absolutely nothing wrong with AO right now. I've been enjoying the game since about January. The only thing I despise and detested beyond imagination is/was camping.

But, I've had to endure nerf after nerf after nerf after nerf. All of these cried out for (as you say) a few paltry crybabies. I agree that the vast overwhelming majority of the playerbase is or was having a grand old time. They get hit repeatedly by nerfs called out for by newbies or PvP'ers.

My enjoyment of the game has decreased dramatically as nerf after nerf has crept in and the sparkle of the game has been sucked away.

All the majority of the playerbase ever wanted was more quests, better monster AI, some active storyline, cool new items, better org features, guild housing, etc.

We the players could give a flying fart about the OE rules, the PvP balancing, Trader heals, bracer effectiveness, anything. We just don't care. We were having fun. So you smack us in the teeth with a baseball bat over and over to appease the whiners on the boards.

AO could have been such a better game at this point in time if all the effort that went into nerfing went into content. All those programmer hours spent beating on the players could have been making the game more fun instead of duller.

As Firedrake said, and I agree, your efforts are:

1.) Not appreciated by many players. They just don't care.
2.) Not even known by many players. Wasted effort on their part.
3.) Detested by many players. Myself included.
4.) Time that could have been spent adding to the game.

and yes finally:

5.) Appreciated (perhaps) by those few who screamed out for the nerf and gave the game designers the opening to hopefully retard the player's progress. I say perhaps because often those with little forsight don't realize that the ramifications of their nerf-cries often make the game less fun for them in the long run as well.

To sum it up, a ton of people were having fun. Every time you nerf, you don't make if funner. Who did the OE rules make the game more enjoyable for? Honestly? That 1 out of 200 who tried PvP and got his butt kicked and didn't want to learn how to gear up. Who did it make the game less fun for? Those 1 out of 5 who enjoyed the process of squeezing every last drop effectiveness out of their characters in order to stand apart from the crowd. Who could care less? The other 189 out of 200. Sounds like a waste of programmer resources.

They could have been working on guild housing or alliance chat during all that time.

Calming Guide- A work in progrEss
Calms - The NT's moneymaker

This subject has been covered before, and it'll be covered again.

Calms are probably the most powerful aspect of being an NT. When I started my NT I thought nukes, as my primary damage source, were going to make me outshine other professions and make me a unique and wanted profession. Unfortunately this is only true at around level 200. At level 200 we can have similar weapons and attack rating as any other profession if we so choose. Add our nukes to this and we outdamage, over time, any other profession in the game. It's not easy to do, but it's possible. Unfortunately, due to having the worst defenses in the game (No pets, horrid AC buffs, evade buffs etc) and some of our nukes carrying an attached taunt it is hard, and sometimes frustrating, to get to higher levels. We visit reclaim often. Enforcers and Soldiers have to work harder in a team with an NT than they normally would. They have to work to hold agro so we don't die and they don't have to wait for us to get back from reclaim. There are many strategies an NT can employ to not be one of the 2 crit quit mage types. These will not be discussed here. I will try to stay on the subject of my strategies for success in calming for credits. I am a monster psychologist. I get them stoned and make then stand in the corners.

We have four calms: Bewilder ql 30, Project Calm ql 60, Discourage Involvement ql 129, and Peaceful intentions ql 159. I would suggest implanting and buffing as much as you can to get your next calm. The higher ql calm you use, the longer its going to last. Nothing effects how much damage a calm can take before breaking.

What to calm for: You can calm for anything your heart desires. You may or may not get it. I calm for full sets of armor that I can wear in another level or two, all my nanos, high priced items (ql 199 Snakemasters are always good), NCU, belts, etc On occasion I do the same for my friends, or someone who wants to hire me. I have proven that I have the same chance of getting a mission completed as a sneaking agent. The up side is that I get to loot all the chests, where as an Agent can only loot chests in rooms where there are no monsters.

What you can expect your calms to do: Well, the higher PsyMod and SenseImp are the better chances you have of landing your calms. I have always implanted for these skills and have always been able to calm the hardest missions I can take. Its not easy, sometimes I die. Sometimes I have to delete a mission because the mobs in it are too hard. That is no problem, if you find (as I do) that some of your missions are full of high level mobs (very hard to calm) and some of your missions are full or low level guys and the mission ql and reward are the same. I would rather delete a mission and role another in hopes of getting easier guys than spend four hours dieing in the same mission. Your calms have a much lower chance or working when you are wearing reflect bracers, shielding bracers (which reflect damage as well), or reflect shields. I would not suggest wearing any of those while trying to calm for credits. Calms can take two hits before they break. I would suggest keeping your agro slider at 45% so you dont auto attack, yet its high enough to not totally nerf your cast time. If you have a mission for something you really want, go get Masterys, Infuses, or Mochems for SenseImp and PsyMod. Also get Essence from an enforcer. Fixer runs speeds can help as well. At level 112 I calmed numerouse ql 200 missions. I ended up with six ql 200 NCU, and some spare cash.

Mission Terminal Settings: I go for Bad and Money settings. This produces Find Item missions. These are the best to calm due to the fact that you not only get a reward, but you get to keep the item. Sometimes both are valuable. I am still trying to figure out how not to get Real Means in my missions. As I mentioned before it seems to be random, sometimes I get them, sometimes I dont. The setting right above Money seems to effect mission size to a certain extent. Move it to ~30% to get smaller missions. The setting right below Bad seems to effect how many monster types are in your missions. Set it to 0% to avoid monsters such as Blaster Beetles. I dont do this often. BBs are easy to calm and cant make it through doorways. When a monster type cant make it through a door way you may just want to blitz the mission cause if you are running fast enough the monsters arent going to get enough hits on you to kill you.

Zones: Different zones have different mission attributes. Clondyke has small missions and a high level of White Caves missions. White Caves are always tiny missions, thus easier to complete. Brown Caves are always enormous. The city of Home has save terms near its missions. Also, the farther the mission is from the mission terminals, the more money they are worth. Zone mission terms give the items you want, others dont. Tir grid gives more NCU, pillows, and beams than other mission terms. Newland City is good for NCU. This little place above West Athens is good for armor. Id suggest experimenting with different terms so you know where to look for certain items. Also, FC has been known to change things to keep us on our toes, so Id suggest experimenting with things every patch.

Roots: Sometimes you may want to root mobs before calming them if they have a high chance of resisting your calms. TEST your roots to make sure they work on that mob type and in the mission type. Dont allow for surprises. Will want to root them before entering their room. That way if your calm doesnt work, and you are getting pounded on, you can just strafe (Z and C on your keyboard) behind a wall so you are out of their line of sight (LOS). Id suggest testing pathing in different mission types and mob types as well. Sometimes LOS works to our advantage if we work it right, other times we end up getting mobs stuck in walls or tables or mobs can just shoot though those types of walls. Successfully rooting melee types is always nice. They seem to hit the fastest and hardest so its nice to keep them at a distance. My calms dont break my roots. I would suggest testing this for your self though; we all know that some things in AO work automagically.

Pulling: Pull one out of the first room unless you feel like tanking while calming all three. Every five rooms pull one mob into another mobs room to create a safe room for your self. Keep in mind that most mobs return to their original rooms after un-calming, but not all of them.

Buffs to Run: Layers, Essence, PsyMod and SenseImp masteries, infuses, or mooches, fixer run buffs, evade buffs, nano init buffs, Ac buffs that dont do any reflect damage, Enfraams, Breaking and Entering Expertise, B&E profession buffs (ask fixers for these), nano cost reducing buffs, HoTs. B&E buffs are great, if you can buff to around 400 B&E you can bust ql 200 chests easily. Etc

Buffs not to Run: Reflect damage buffs, matter creation buffs (cause you are not going to shoot anything remember?), um cant think of anything else, I normally self buff.

The Joys of B&E: I recently raised Breaking and Entering skill to around 400. I can bust open all chests in ql 200 missions. I get crazy rich off this. I cant believe I have been calming missions for so long unable to get locked chests In one day I found the best XP ring in the game and the best Physics ring in the game, not to mention tons of armor and other goodies. Currently my mission rewards are around 120k, but I make 500k++ selling my loot to shops. I would make more if I sold it to players, but I just give away anything useful. People appreciate that. Also, if you calm missions often enough youll never have to buy treatment kits again. Be careful though! Exploding chests will wake up calmed mobs! I dont think is damages them in anyway, but it does un-calm them.

Money: If an NT works it right he can always calm his way through the hardest missions he can take. He will never have to buy nanos, armor, NCU, et al. You can calm for items to sell. QL 200 NCU, Implants, etc go for a bit of change. If I ever need a yalm for my alt I do a missions for any item that sells for over 4mill. It may take me 30 mins to find the perfect mission, but itll only take me 15 mins to calm it.

I think thats it for now fellow NTs. I hope this helps. Keep in mind that this guide is written for all level NTs as soon as they get their first calm, Bewilder. This guide is written in the full knowledge that FC can, and will, change the rules of the game at anytime to suit their needs. If you have anything to add please do so, I rarely play with other NTs so I am mostly unaware of their strategies.

Calming and team missions
Thought Id have a crack.

Calm is one of the most useful nanos that we have in Team missions.

This will be especially true after the next patch.

When the team is in the pre-mission stage get the puller to realise if he is possibly going to get an add to say before the pull.

Get the team to call adds, yes it can be annoying, but sometimes you might not have noticed a different door open, as they can do in Team missions.

Remind the team that if your calming adds, to concentrate on the pulled target, /assist been a good command for them.

Also remind them, if they get aggroed by an add DONT attack it back, as it will break any calms you try on them. Thats why the next patch will be so much help to us on this score, the don't attack buttons will be the way to go on this. The ammount of times I see my NT drop into funky MA mode is quite amusing. I have attack still set up on my shortcut one bar so I can see it turn red, so I can hit Q quickly not to break my own calm attempts.

Then calm those adds.

Another tatic that I have used when the puller states he is going to get two is to go with him. I start the fight with a calm, and trust the group to get the other off me. Yes this is MUCH more dangerous (to the non-NS ones of us) if you havent got a aggro getter in the group, BUT does have the advantage that unless you fail your calm its not going to get broken by accident.

Oh one last thing, do tell them how long your calms last for and point out that AoE will breka them, such as mongo. I am unsure if crat/eng symps/disruptives break them, someone comment?

Advanced calming techniques
Fingers mention an important technique for NTs that cant tank --

when you are in a team, you can calm to shift the aggro -- Calm invloves an aggro wipe, so you can "share" the mob with your teammates.. BTW you can actually hold down a mob almost completely using just calms...

For soloing, one of the most effective techniques (if not the fastest) is to nuke calm nuke calm. Mobs will not resume attacking after the first nuke post calm (they will attack once, then go back to a neutral state) -- you can also use this to treat periodically.

Expert tips for masters

The key to expert calming is moving during recharge. At higher lvls, when your calm is instant cast, you should be able to move the instant after you initiate the calm -- this allows you to avoid getting hit at all in most cases, once you get good at it, even if the calm is resisted.

Separate mobs by pulling -- This is critical when you cant tank, and need to deal w/ 2-4 mobs. Let the mob see you. Wait until it orients on you (the Stare). sidestep, or move. run around the corner. If you pay attention to where they are looking, and proximity, you can separate 3 mobs walking side by side with this tactic

Calming in teams

Some teams respect calms, others do not.
If everyone in the team is using reflect shields, then the calm will frequently be broken when the mob take reflect dmg

Solution: 1) Pre-calm the mobs -- basically calm every mob within one room of where you are located

2) Keep calming the mob, and shout at the person attacking it

3) if the team is very strong, or completely ignores the calm, my recommendation is to forget about calming, and just AOE nuke instead -- if all the mobs are being attacked, you will rarely get aggroed, and your dmg output is awesome. Usually you will know this in about the first 5 minutes. Often, if you can finish a mob off quickly, this is even better than calming an add.

4) for reflects -- draw the aggro using taunt nukes, then calm -- OR, time your calm so that it goes off after the mob has just attacked

If you are the team's crowd control, always position yourself so you are looking in the direction of the likely source of adds.

There is more, but I cant tell ALL my secrets hehe

Hope this helps

Re: Help, What's Blitzing?
quote:
Originally posted by Crow_T_Robot
WHat's Blitzing?
Blitzing = when you run as fast as you can to the mission objective (MO) and finish the mission without fighting. Works with all mission types except "kill" (obviously). The purpose of blitzing is to get the reward money and/or item, or even the pick-up item, not XP.
Blitzing will allow you to get a things like a full set of armour with all parts the same QL, or rewards much higher QL than you'd be able to get if you were to fight your way through a mission.

At level 112 you'll be able to "blitz", or "run" as it's sometimes refered to, QL 200 missions. As a fixer you'd have a hard time trying to fight your way thru a QL 200 mission solo at that level.

Blitzing missions is lonely and time consuming work, but it could get you rich fast. If you know what to missions to get. For details see the link in the original post.

Now you might ask yourself "what items should I go for?", the answer to that is simply "Whatever people are paying for". The first thing on your list, since you're already too high level for the QL 1 stuff should be this in QL 10, no higher, no lower QL. A pair of those will get you something like a couple of 100.000 credits, up to half a mil. (Or more. Paid 750k for a pair myself once after some problems with the bank. I've also sold a pair for 5 Mil, but that doesn't happen every day.)

Keep your eyes on the Rubi-Ka exchange message board and the shopping channels and you'll soon see what items that are worth picking up.

Just one more thing. It says in the blitz thread (link in the original post) that the MO always is in the corner. That is no longer true. The MO can be in anywhere a chest can be, including under crates and in walls or water.

I think that should about cover it.
Good luck.

Reposted to newbie tips thread by request:

----------
Okay - let's cut to the chase here. If you're considering GA, the first thing you need to do is determine whether or not you actually want to go that route.. You do NOT need grid armor, it is not a must, and it has both benefits and detriments. Regardless, I wouldn't worry too much about getting it until you are a bit higher level and can actually use it.. somewhere around level 55 if you don't want to EXCESSIVELY gimp yourself in the process.

Con's of using GA at low levels:
Serious ip investment. This will cause you to be unable to raise some of your skills as high as you normally would - as a GA user I have always been able to max the skills I want to max (all abilities minus psychic, body dev, SMG, burst, treatment, all three evades (duck is only needed high for pvp)). My nano skills were gimped a fair bit, hell, still are, as a result of raising MC to use GA. My RI was gimped for a while, not too big of a deal because I use a Sol Chironis, not an MCS. All my misc. skills like first aid, comp lit, run speed are heavily gimped.

Need for outside buffs - to get the full benefit of GA you're going to have to get masteries/infuses/mocham's every 6 hours until you can self-cast it. I wouldn't bother even buffing into it until you are within the OE limit (20%) because GA turning red on you, really, really sucks. If you can get it not red by casting expertises, go for it.

Low AC - can't wear anything else - GA is considered a social armor - this means, you can't wear much of anything with it - no armor, no shoulder pieces, no bracers.. you can wear your token board and two rings, and that's it. GA by itself has low AC's which means when you do get hit, you're going to get hit HARD. Maxxing evades is a must, imho, due to the fact you need to evade as much as possible to avoid getting smacked around with your low AC's.

Debuffs - trader/MP/NT players (and mobs!) are much, much more deadly to you as a fixer in GA - if you get drained, or hit with nano shutdown/dominate/etc, you're going to be in for a world of hurt as your GA turns red and often 0% effective. This means you are fighting NAKED when this happens. Ouch. Nukes, DoT's, damage shields, etc slice right through GA like a hot knife through butter. Keep that in mind.

6 hours Once you've summoned your GA it lasts six hours before you have to summon a new suit.. nothing quite like being in the middle of a mission, fighting 4 deep red enforcer mobs, to see yourself suddenly become naked! Hellllo, reclaim.. and time to go look for a friendly MP to give you mocham's so you can make a new suit.

--
Pro's of wearing GA at low levels:
PvM I'm not sure about teaming, but I do know you'll easily be able to solo deep red mobs.. you'll scoff at them, in fact, as they miss you 95% or more. I was running 100% missions at level 55 in GA MKI, that tapered down to 75% missions after a bit. Level 111 now, in GA MKII, and soloing 75% team missions, boss & all, no problems.. can still tank numerous deep red mobs at a time.. traders are a bit of a problem, but other than that, it's very easy.

PvP - While you will be pathetically weak to certain professions as a result of wearing GA (MPs, NTs, and traders come to mind), you'll gain a serious advantage against some other professions - enforcers, soldiers, etc - those who just shoot at/whack you.

---

Now, having read that, you need to ask yourself a very serious question - Do you still want Grid Armor? If so, how bad do you want it? IF, and only if, the answer is REALLY REALLY BAD, then you have two choices.

1> Camp it. I think the easiest way to do this is by soloing a whole lot of QL 55-60 bosses for GA MKI. Remember you're going to have to do this all over again around level 90 when you outgrow GA MKI, and again when you outgrow MKII, and again when you outgrow MKIII... how long will this method take? Who knows? The drop is totally random. I have found 2 GA discs by camping it, and I've bought 4. Sometimes it is easier to earn the credits and just buy the darn thing. There are also a few outdoor hunting spots you can get it at, but these are often heavily camped, and again.. the drop is random - you may be there for days or weeks before you find it. I've found GA MKII on the 28th QL 90-93 boss I killed, but I've also killed 87 bosses in a row looking for GA MKIII with no luck. It will eventually drop for you, but it may be on the 1000th boss, you never know. Might also be on the first.

2> Buy it. This is a daunting task for a low level fixer - if you're on RK2, GA MKI runs around 30 million, GA MKII runs around 60 million, unsure about the other two. If you're on RK1, you can probably double those prices. Yes, raising that kind of money at a level when you can actually make full use of the GA is difficult. It is NOT impossible. It's boring. It's not much fun. It may take a full week or longer of nothing but money runs, nonstop, for 10 hours a day. But it can be done. Is it worth it? Only you can answer that question.

---

All that said, I looked into this before I made the decision and I decided to use GA. The main reason is because I much, much prefer to solo, and GA makes soloing infinitely easier. My personal method of getting GA works like this:

Level 1-45 - solo team mission bosses for cash, make as much money as possible, blitz for money, etc etc.
Starting at level 45 - begin soloing QL 55-60 team missions. Cut pretty much straight to the boss, but stop to loot and get chests on the way. Continue until either 1> GA drops, or 2> you have enough money to buy it.
Starting when GA MKI is equipped (level 55 for me) - start soloing QL 80-93 bosses. Continue until either 1> GA drops, or 2> you have enough money to buy it.
Starting when GA MKII is equipped (level 91 for me) - start soloing QL 105-116 bosses. Continue until either 1> GA drops, or 2> you have enough money to buy it.
Starting when GA MKIII is equipped (not yet) - start soloing QL 125-140 bosses. Continue until 1> GA drops, or 2> You have enough money to buy it.

If the bosses turn grey on you in the process, it's time to either skip to looking for the next version of GA, or else just do a ton of blitzing for cash and buy. I flat out bought my GA MKI. GA MKII, I've found twice, the second time I found it I kept it for myself and that was using the above method.. took 28 bosses to get it, and by the time I had gotten that far, I'd already earned close to 50 million credits. I was level 85 when I found it (gained 30 levels in the process). I decided to skip over GA MKIII because the bosses were getting yellow and green on me, and I'm looking for MKIV now. In the process of looking for MKIII/MKIV, I have gained 26 levels, and earned around 120 million credits so far. By the time my MKIV bosses turn grey, I should be able to come up with 500+ mil and just buy it if I have not found it by then.

--

Side note: Yes, all the time I spend playing revolves around finding bigger, better GA. You can call that lame all you want, but I'm having fun. Are you?

Grouping Tips for Low Level Fixers
----------------------------------------------

I'm writing this as a level 48 Fixer and I've grouped for probably close to 30 of those levels.

There are a couple of things you need to do in a group, as a low level fixer.

1) Know who your tank is. Make an assist macro (/macro assist /assist). What it comes to combat, do what they say unless they're making some major mistakes. If they ARE making some mistakes, politely suggest a better alternative, don't sit there silently fuming - people can't grow from their mistakes if they never know what they're doing wrong. Just remember to be diplomatic - after all, one day you'll be doing something foolish and friendly advice is much better than arrogant disdain Anyway... if your tanks want to pull to the group, let them pull. If they want to storm rooms one-by-one and attack first to obtain aggro, do it.

2) Learn to pull. In a nut shell, you put on your best HoT and your best speed buff. Drop the Agg/Def to under 50%. Inch into a room and when you see one target you - bolt back to the group. Wait for the tank to pull aggro off you, then you can use burst and fixer rounds. Don't use those special attacks too early or you'll probably retain aggro. You don't want that - you're trying to bring tasty treats to the communal dinner table for mass consumption - not to overwork the doc.

3) Stay away from unexplored doors. This is probably the hardest thing to explain to some people. In team missions it seems to me that mobs aggro from further away. In other words, in they are watching through an open door and they can see you, there's a pretty good chance they'll aggro on you in a team mission, even if they won't in the same circumstances in solo missions.

4) Recognise danger areas. Some rooms are bigger than others and have multiple mobs and sub-sections. For example, there's a warehouse storage room with 4 racks of shelves and an interior room. There WILL be two or mobs in these rooms, so if you're not sure about adds get someone to pull the mob out of the room. If you attack one of the mobs in the room - ANYWHERE in the room, the other will aggro onto you as well, regardless of LoS.

5) DON"T ninja loot. I love picking locks throughout missions (I max B&E where possible) but the contents are not yours. I put them into group chat and if someone else makes a claim, they get it. If there's a conflict (hasn't happened yet) then gamble for it via Helpbot Ultimately though, most people are going to ignore those things - there have been situations where I've walked out of a mission with items no-one wanted and sold up for tens of thousands of credits. Nothing to a twink, but it makes a big difference to me. And no, I don't share the proceeds - I carried all that junk around for a reason, and you weren't it

6) Buffs. If you're planning on grouping, bear in mind people just love our NCU buffs - and more often than not, our speed buffs too.

7) Politeness. Maybe this should be first on the list? If someone gives you something say thanks. If you want something, say please. In essence, treat others as you'd like to be treated. Remember, what goes around, comes around

Soloing Team Missions for XP and Profit
Due to the growing popularity of soloing team missions, and the questions that have been cropping up recently as a result of this, I've decided to put together a little guide on how go about this.

--

Note: In my time in AO, I've soloed well over a thousand team missions. I used this method exclusively to solo to level 90ish on my agent before progress slowed to the point that I felt I needed to team.. and I have done absolutely nothing but solo team missions with my fixer, who is currently level 124. This guide is more of a "How I do it" than a "How you should do it" - take it with a grain of salt, but it's working so far

--

Why solo team missions?

There are a number of reasons to solo team missions. People solo team missions as a method of making cash, to find team mission only loot, to speed up the search for rare drops such as GA, and as a method of gaining xp at a reasonable rate without having to look for a team.

Advantages of soloing team missions: You'll make considerably more money and bring in more loot than if you were soloing individual missions or in a team. The xp is better than individual missions. It's one of the most efficient ways to find GA and other rare items. The other advantages of soloing in general apply - you won't be standing around LFG, don't have to worry about idiots in your team bringing adds down on you, or ninjalooting, or going AFK, or waiting around when half your team dies. You'll get more tokens per level than you do in a team, but less than you get doing individual missions.

Disadvantages of soloing team missions: It can be difficult, especially when there are rooms with 3-4 mobs. The boss can tough to take down for a single player. You have to find another player to temporarily team with to get the missions. A single team mission can take a very long time to complete if you clear it completely (especially true at higher levels and when you get one of those 300 mob missions). You'll miss out on the social interaction of teaming (whether that is good or bad, is up to you), but you'll get lots of socializing in by auctioning off all the boss loot you don't need. On an xp per hour basis you will probably get less than you would in a team. You may run completely out of room in your inventory & packs to carry loot

What you need to get started:

Decent gun, decent armor, lots and lots of packs, and plenty of basic supplies. You don't need to be super-twink to solo team missions, but if you're using a gun the same QL as your level, you're probably going to have one hell of a time killing the boss. Bring at LEAST 6-7 empty packs to carry loot back in. Make sure you have plenty of ammo, nano recharges, treatment kits, and first aid kits. Nano stims are probably useful as well, although I don't use them. If in doubt, you probably don't have enough. As an example, I carry 50,000 rounds of ammo, 10,000 cluster bullets, 1000 nano chargers, 500 treatment kits, and 300 first aid kits. I restock about every 6 missions. You also need a temporary teammate to get the team missions from.

Optimizing your character for soloing team missions:

A fixer with average equipment should be able to solo 50% difficulty team missions, perhaps 60%. If you're having trouble, or you want to crank the difficulty slider up further, I highly recommend the following setup, especially if you want to make soloing team missions a way of life.

Gun - from level 1-41: Kerans or Sol Chironis, QL should be at least double your level. From level 41-60: Gamma Ejector. From level 60-90: Kerans or Sol Chironis, QL should be at least double your level. By the time you hit level 110, you should be in a QL 200 MCS + TIM scope or QL 200 Sol Chironis + VE/LLTS. Damage output is VERY important when soloing team missions. The faster the mob drops, the less time it has to do nasty things to you, such as beating you up or divesting & plundering until you can't tie your own shoes without a Mocham's.

Armor - at low levels, I found armor to be of lesser importance. My fixer only had one set of armor ever - QL 50 flowers tech, that she wore from level 30ish until 55. Obviously, the better your armor is, the better off you will be, but if the choice is between a bigger gun and better armor, go for the gun. I hate to say it, but GA will make soloing team missions 1000x easier. Obviously, not everyone is lucky enough to find it or rich enough to buy it, but if you are, for the purpose of soloing, it will help a lot.

Implants - I really, really hate messing with implants. But a good set of imps will definitely help out. As for what to implant, I use triple SMG, double burst, evades, evades, evades, triple max health, and nano skills (focusing on HoT skills), along with some misc. junk in the spots that don't take anything truly useful.

Skills - Soloing team missions efficiently requires a somewhat different skill set than most fixers I know use. My brother and I share similar ideas as far as ip distribution goes, and now that he teams sometimes, he gets some funny looks. Another fixer asked him, "How do you play that way?!" *chuckle* All I gotta say is, I can solo QL 160+ team missions with no real difficulty. I have soloed level 200 outdoors bosses (and I don't mean kiting - I mean standing there and taking my punishment) while at 100% aggro. It works. Note the following setup is based on efficiency in soloing team missions/solo play in general, and probably gimped for any other purpose.. it's also a very "boring" ip distribution and not really very fixerish.

I max every level - all abilities except for psychic, body dev, SMG, burst, dodge ranged, evade close combat and treatment. RI will be maxxed as soon as I hit title cap (soon I hope). As far as nano skills go, I focus almost entirely on HoTs. Run buffs and NCU buffs are very low priority.. at level 124 I'm using Grid Surfer and still can't cast the level 75 NCU buff (Deck Recoder). I also haven't raised comp lit, B&E, first aid, or run speed in over 80 levels. I have just enough comp lit. to wear NCUs that will (barely) hold my own buffs.. if someone hits me with an outside buff I would like to keep running for a mission, I use the NCU buff. I've never killed a mob by lockpicking it to death, or running around it in circles until it got dizzy and passed out, thus I find no use for those skills. I have around 250 base skill in comp lit, B&E, FA and run speed. It's not a problem.

I also highly recommend casting expertises and int boost to squeeze into the biggest HoTs possible. I actually cast all 6 expertises as standard buffs. I also have int, str, agil, and sta boosts as standard buffs. I believe in squeezing every last drop out of my character

Anyway, the above suggestions are simply what works for me, and it's a very min/max type template. I should also point out that I don't kite at all, I don't even own a root or a snare. I'm also *terrible* at pulling, so I stand around tanking 3-4 mobs at a time very often. Obviously if you're good at kiting and pulling mobs one at a time, you probably won't need to min/max as much or twink your character out as much. However, it's a hell of a lot more efficient in terms of xp per hour to walk into a room full of mobs and tab-Q them than it is to pull them out one at a time and root, break LoS, etc. Unless you need to use those methods, in which case, go for it - remember the old adage - There is neither xp to be made, nor loot to be found at the reclaim terminal.

Getting Started:

First, you need to find someone who is within your team range, to team with temporarily in order to get your missions. The easiest way is to ask a friend or guildmate. Maybe you can find someone else who would like to solo team missions, and it'll benefit both of you to temporarily team. If not, you'll have to ask a stranger. If you're asking a stranger, don't be pushy about it and offer to tip for their time.

When pulling team missions, you should go to a different zone than the one your teammate is in. This way they will not have to delete the missions/keys. Pull them quickly to avoid wasting their time, and make sure to ask if they are also going to pull missions so you don't break up the team before they are done. Pull three missions (the max you can have at one time) so you don't have to keep going back and finding someone to team with.

The first time you pull missions, get a 50% difficulty, one of the next highest available QL, and one of the next highest after that. As an example - I'm level 124, so I would get a QL 124, a 136, and a 151 (I think, would have to check helpbot ). Do them in order from easiest to hardest - this will give you an idea of how hard a mission you can handle. If 50% is too hard, you really need better equipment, but you can drop the slider to easier if you like.

Soloing team missions for xp/loot (actually doing the whole mission):

Simple enough, kill everything, loot all the chests, loot all the bodies, kill the boss. When it comes to rooms with several mobs, it's a good idea to try and pull them out one at a time unless you're sure you can handle them all at once. Keep both HoT's running at all times and be sure to refresh your short term HoT very frequently - you don't want it running out in the middle of a fight with multiple mobs. Be picky about what you pick up as far as loot goes, or you're going to run out of pack space even if you brought a ton of packs. I pick up armor, weapons, nanos - stuff I know will sell for good cash at the vendor.

If you don't feel like clearing the whole mission, you can just kill your way to each lift to save some time.

Killing the boss:

When you get to the boss lift, stop and completely rebuff. Some bosses take a long time to kill and the last thing you want to be doing is buffing while the boss is regenning. Hit the boss lift, kill the lackey(s), then go back down to heal up, get a full tank of nano, and refresh your short HoT before you start on the boss himself. Go back in and start in on the boss - if you have LFF, use it. Keep your short term HoT running at all times, use FA as soon as it becomes available if you're below 75% health. Don't zone back down unless you're pretty close to death (unless you're fighting an NT boss), and heal up fast - be sure you're using the biggest treatment kit you possibly can. If you get stuck "in a fight" when you go down, the boss will usually not regen much. Use FA & nano stims as they become available, and let your HoT's do the work when this happens. Damage output is extremely important when killing the boss - you want to take a good chunk out of him if you have to leave the room to heal.. that way he won't heal back up completely and you'll be making progress. Be patient, some bosses are tougher than others. Keep fighting if you're debuffed or have DoT's running, if you stop to let them wear off the boss is going to heal up and you're going to wind up starting all over again. Leave the boss room if you get under 25% health and go back in when you're over 75% health (HoT's will usually heal you up the rest of the way before you get hit again). Be sure to use burst, cluster bullets, the second they become available, and be persistant.. if you made it to the boss, it is almost certain you can kill him eventually.

I know some people break LoS on the boss or root melee bosses, etc.. I've never needed to do either and have no idea how to
If someone would like to add that information it'd be appreciated.

Blitzing bosses:

If you're just in it for the loot, you can blitz to the boss without killing much on the way. Try to get as many people in the team when you pull the missions as possible for this - more people in the team means more loot on the boss. The best way to do this is to get a group of 6 people who all want to blitz bosses, and stay in the team the whole time - everyone splits up to pull their own missions and blitz their own bosses, continuing as long as the team stays together. Note that if you stay in the team and do it this way, you will get reduced xp, and you will not be able to use /quit to break aggro (see below), however it is very convenient because you can keep going back for more missions. You will also usually have to wait about a minute after the boss dies to be able to loot him. It's still much more efficient.. using this method I can blitz a boss every 15 minutes. Very handy if you are after loot for cash or you are looking for rare discs.

To blitz a team mission, buff up completely (just like you were going into battle) in the foyer, drop agg/def to 100% defensive, keep your lockpick handy, and blitz all the way up to the last floor. Blitzing bosses is much, much easier if you get a SHE/BHE from a friendly NT. When you get to the last floor, you can either kill mobs on your way to the boss lift, or you can go straight to the boss lift. If you go straight to the boss lift, things can get real hairy unless you are prepared to take on the boss and his lackeys without being able to stop and heal.

If you kill stuff on the final floor of the mission - usually after 3-4 mobs you will lose aggro from the mobs on floors below you (the "in a fight" thing). If you absolutely cannot shake aggro and you must get nano and heal, find a safe spot and /quit. Then log back in, but wait for the little "character active in world" bar to go away before you log in again.

If you do not kill stuff on the final floor of the mission - run straight to the boss lift, and click it, then immediately run into the furthest corner of the boss room (away from the boss and lackeys). Sit down and attempt to heal/get nano. If it does not work, you can (usually) safely /quit at this point unless you are staying in a team for convenience. Either way, with or without aggro, when you're ready, go back to the lift in the boss room and go down (don't take on the boss yet). In the room below you there will usually be a cluster of 3-4 mobs that followed you through the last floor of the mission as you were running for the boss lift. Kill them. If you can't take them without fleeing, you can go back into the boss room and heal in the corner, unless the boss or his lackeys aggro you, in which case you're just screwed. Once the guys in the room with the boss lift are dead, proceed with killing the boss as usual. If you're feeling really badass when you get to the boss lift, jump on in and attack the boss & lackeys without killing the mobs below This works okay if you are doing easier team missions but if you're rolling them hard and you get a nuke-happy NT boss.. don't cry when you find yourself staring at reclaim

Tips for soloing specific bosses:

Soldier and enforcer bosses tend to have higher attack ratings (OS & challenger I presume), and will hit more/crit more as a result. You may need to lower your agg/def and chip away slowly at this type of boss. If you have a soldier boss who runs TMS, it's generally a good idea to stop attacking until it wears off. They usually only do it once, though.

NT bosses will sometimes nuke for shocking amounts. This is especially true if you are in GA. I went from 75% health to reclaim at one point with an NT boss that nuked me for almost 3k. I highly recommend going down the lift when you get below about 60% health when fighting an NT boss, as they will often toss little nukes at you for a while then suddenly let loose with the show stopper.

Fixer, crat, agent, MA, engineer, and adventurer bosses are usually pathetically easy. Engineer and adventurer damage shields can be annoying, crats nuke a little bit, agents have crappy DoT's, and low level fixer bosses will LFF you, but they're pretty much laughable compared to say, traders.

Doctor bosses can be easy and can be tough, depending on just how much the doc likes to cast. They have an annoying tendency to debuff hell out of your inits (Rapid Palsy anyone?), stack DoT's on you, and stand there chain casting heals on themselves. Then they open up on you with their Ithaca and 15% ELLTS.. oh, wait, that's doctor players, not bosses. Actually, everything but the Ithaca and the ELLTS applies, be prepared for a long and annoying (but not very dangerous) fight.

MP bosses aren't too bad, unless you have poor chemical AC. However, if they debuff your nano skills, you may be in for a world of hurt. Often these debuffs will stop you from being able to recast your HoT's, and if you have GA, will OE it. If you are wearing GA and you get hit with Nano Shutdown, you may proceed to reclaim now. (You can fight through Nano Shutdown with GA, I've done it, but it gets extremely hairy. If you can self-cast your GA, and you have bad luck with MP and trader bosses, I recommend carrying a suit of armor you can self equip in a pack just for such occaisions. Any armor at all beats a 0% effective smurf suit.)

Trader bosses are a real pain in the ass. Expect to be drained until you have the skills of someone half your level. Only worse. Within 2 minutes of attacking the last trader boss I fought (a mere QL 136 mission, looking for MKIV), my nano skills were negative numbers, my attack rating was halved, my gun was 50% effective, my GA was 0% effective, and my ACs were -1011. Yes, negative 1k ACs. The boss died and I didn't, but it was a miracle. If you wear GA, see the MP section above. If you don't, trader bosses are annoying (you won't be able to recast your short HoT as needed, and your gun will probably be OE'ed), but not all that dangerous.

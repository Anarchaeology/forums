Design Discussion: Overly Generic Items?
Before I begin, let me state that I really do enjoy AO, and continue to be a paying player. I am also a professional game designer/producer (which, admittedly, doesn't give me any more knowledge than the next guy... I mention it only because I feel it is pertinent). I have played every MMORPG out there, to varying degrees (heavy on EQ and DAoC, light on others), and am currently an active player only in AO.

As many others have said, AO is a game with phenominal potential, only a portion of which is realized. The folks at Funcom are hard at work trying to upgrade the game and add content while doing continuing playbalance to address issues they find along the way. Still, with all their hard work and all the feedback from their customers, there are some areas that feel more shallow than they should. In fact, I feel that in many cases the folks at Funcom just plain "got lazy", delivering lots of generic content that is worthless because it is so generic. My intention in this short article is to discuss a couple of these areas, and start a conversation on the topic.


WEAPONS
There are a huge array of weapons in AO... from grenades to submachine guns... from chemical sprayers to pillows. Each of these weapons has some basic statistics that control the function of the weapon (Damage, Crit damage, Attack rate, Recharge rate, Ammo capacity, etc). Yet, for all the many statistics that are part of the makeup of a weapon, the weapons in the game have a very generic feel. There are fast, low-damage weapons... there are slow, high-damage weapons... there are weapons with huge crits... there are weapons without huge crits. The problem is, weapons don't really have any "characteristics" to call their own. SMGs don't fire faster than rifles or shotguns... there are some fast, some slow. Rifles don't hit harder... some do, some don't. This has led to an environment where 5% of all weapons are actually used by players, with the other 95% merely being un-converted credits that you convert at shops.

So, how would we fix this? Well, the simple answer is you go in and change stats on weapons so they matter. At least half the weapons out there should have a use to SOMEONE, in my opinion. The goal of a game designer is to have people arguing over what is really good... that means you did it right. Having everyone agree that a handful of weapons are really the only ones out there worth having means you got it wrong, and should try to fix it. Funcom did so with the Freedom Arms (clearly the ONLY pistol worth having at the time... yet now NO pistols are worth having), but have yet to really finish the job.

Suggestions:
1) Make weapons fit their characteristics. Rifles hit hard (including a much higher bottom end) but fire more slowly. SMGs have a huge variability in damage, and fire fast. Assault Rifles take more skills to use than SMGs, but are more powerful. Etc...

2) Make use of skill-boosting weapons more frequently and earlier in the game. By level 50, you should have seen many such weapons, even at lower levels. I'd probably put way more such on Pistols, to increase their usefulness.

3) Do the math. Figuring out expected damage curves on weapons isn't hard. Do it. Every weapon in the game should be stored in a huge spreadsheet for balance purposes. Having done games with hundreds of weapon types available to the player, I do understand the fun of trying to balance them all... it CAN be done, it just takes time.


ARMOR
Similarly to weapons, armor is pretty dull. As levels increase, more armor types become available with increased ratings, but if you look at the armor types out there, by and large players stick to certain armors at certain levels. Additionally, there is really not that much variability between armor types. Every armor type as a "vulnerability", "weaker" armors have more than one. Still, there is no "specialty" armor, that gives you 2x the protection for the same QL vs one type of attack, but is really weak otherwise. Nearly all characters end up with moderate protection against everything, and strongest protection against the "big three" (energy, projectile, melee).

So what do we do here? Well, once again increasing variability and allowing players a choice will help. If you create armors that specialize in one protection type, you'll have players carry armor for special situations... a good thing. Wouldn't it be nice to be able to carry armor to deal with chemical attacks when you know you're going into spider central? Allowing such will simply put more control into the players' hands, and allow for more strategy on their parts... a good thing. Add in more stat-boosting armors and you have created a matrix of options.

Suggestions:
1) Nothing wrong with the concept of upgraded versions of each major armor type. However, they shouldn't all be "we now cover one more type". Why not having improved ones that also improve core protections? Or, perhaps on a different armor line, have a set that chooses to balance out the weaker protections so they are ALL covered, but less well? Make it different.

2) Add more stat-boosting armors. Biomech is a great idea (if not great armor). Let's do more of this. If armor A is great protection and has no stats, while armor B is lesser protection but has some stats it boosts... it makes a player think. This once again increases strategies by the players.

3) Do specialty armors. Have some armor types that protect superbly well against one or two attack types, but don't protect worth beans against others. If I want to laugh at energy weapons, give me that ceramic armor... but if someone comes after me with a baseball bat, I'm going to be hating life. Once again, more choices to the players = happy players.

4) Balance armor based on the rest of the game system. Currently, you can boost the snot out of STR/STA with buffs, but you cannot do the same with INT/PSY. Take that into account when building armor types... perhaps making INT/PSY types give more boosts to make up for it.


VEHICLES
It is all about the Yalm. Getting a Yalm is a "game changing" experience. So why on earth don't we do more with this concept? Why not have lightning fast ground vehicles that don't fly? Why not have slow aircraft that let you fly, but really no faster than you run? Why not have water vehicles that let you move so fast on the water that they are actually worth having? Variability... give the players control.


SUMMARY
I could go on at length here, but wanted to touch the tip of the iceberg to start discussions. AO is a game with a wonderful potential for the "toy factor"... but currently mediocre implementation. With a couple of weeks of hard work, this could be dramatically changed. I could easily see something as simple as these suggestions bringing literally hundreds of players back to AO.

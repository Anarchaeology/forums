Ideas on Skill System Changes
I just wanted to start up a discussion of some ideas people have concerning skill changes. Please note that here I'm going to discuss the skills in particular, and be moderately blunt about it. Sorry in advance!

-- What's the point in having the Navigation menu? There are four skills there, of which only two are developed frequently (Vehicle Air and Map Navigation), one by the poorer individuals (Vehicle Ground), and one by NO ONE (Vehicle Water). Here's what I think can feasibly be done with this skill option:

1.) Move Map Navigation to the current "Spying" option and --change the name of "Spying" to "Survival", since all those already there (Breaking & Entering, Perception, Trap Disarmament, and Concealment) can really be considered Survival Skills.

2.) Consolidate the three vehicle skills into a single "Vehicle" or "Transport" skill, and place this skill under Speed. First, they aren't really "Navigation" skills per se, and the development of these skills leads to Vehicle usage, which directly affects Speed.

--Another focus of changes would have to be the Adventuring and Swimming skills. Here are my thoughts on these two skills:

1.) Swimming almost no one uses, so removing it is an option, unless we are expected to swim to Jobe. We have no reason to develop our swimming skill, since 99.99% of our time is not spent in the water, and the expected extinction of Sharks and Piranhas makes a fast swim unnecessary. However, if it must stay, move it to Speed as well. All it really does is improve our swim speed. Now, if there were underwater missions, that may be a different story....

2.) As for Adventuring, find something better to do with it than backpack usage, and move it to the new "Survival" group. Maybe let it increase rare loot chances (as someone with a high Adventuring skill could be a tomb raider type), or make special hidden areas in missions that can only be opened with a B&E/Adventuring combo.

--Other ideas can include the addition of new skills. If we remove the Vehicle Water, Vehicle Ground, and Swimming spots, perhaps we should expect to add something else in its place.

1.) One idea? Add a skill called Ecology, or something similar, and use it to make the likelihood of an animal monster aggroing be inversely proportional to the Ecology skill of the player (aggro decreases as Ecology increases). This can even be a sided skill (i.e. Omni-Tek get Cyan or Blue, Clans get Teal or Cyan), or a bonus modifier skill given to eco-minded guilds.

2.) Another idea? Make a class-oriented skill menu, where increasing skill points to a certain level allows the use of some nanos of that class. For example, you could give Advys a Green "Doctor" skill, enabling them to use doctor nanos at a certain skill level.

Just some quick ideas. Not that they're likely to occur, but, it doesn't hurt to throw things at the wall to see what sticks! Please post your ideas, too, or feel free to comment on these!

As for my idea on Profession Points (PSPs):

1.) Every level, give a player Profession Points. These can be used in a special skill window, which lists each profession and a skill bar. The profession you are to begin with is maxxed out to start, and cannot be released or changed (though this system can be the doorway to profession changes).

2.) At title level 1, you get 1 point per level, at title level 2, 2 points per level, and so on. Each profession is assigned a color Green, Teal, Cyan, and Blue, this color assigned by related profession. An example: Advys could have Green Doctor and MA profession skills, Teal Enforcer and Soldier, Cyan Trader and *maybe* Fixer, Blue everyone else (MP, BCT, NT, ENG, AGT). Total available points would be 700 (a +10 bonus given at level 200). Blue skills cost 2.5 to upgrade, cyan 2, teal 1.5, and green 1 to upgrade.

3.) When a profession reaches a skill level of 100, you can cast nanos of that profession and use their weapons up to player level 100. If you do the math, you will not be able to reach this point before player level 55, minimum (green), 104 maximum (blue). This would COST between 100 and 250 Profession points.

4.) When a profession reaches a skill level of 180, you can cast nanos of that profession and use their weapons up to player level 150. If you do the math, you will not be able to reach this point before player level 82, minimum (green), 153 maximum (blue). This would COST between 180 and 450 Profession points.

5.) When a profession reaches a skill level of 250, you can cast nanos of that profession and use their weapons up to player level 200. If you do the math, you will not be able to reach this point before player level 104, minimum (green), 189 maximum (blue). This would COST between 250 and 625 Profession points.

6.) This does not include your other IP-based skill development. Just because you've invested P-points into a profession, doesn't mean you've upgraded you're 2HE enough to use The Edge of Tarasque. All skill requirements still apply.

7.) These numbers mean you can develop either

a.) both Greens fully and a 100 point Cyan (700 points)
b.) one Blue fully, and a 75 point Green (700 points)
c.) something in between, like 2 180 Teals, (540 points)

Obviously, this is meant not to restrict an Advy from being able to summon Belamorte, for example, but to make it feasible though not preferable. Could it be potentially unbalancing to have an Advy running around with Doc heals and MA Crit buffs? Yes, just as it could be unbalancing to have an Enforcer running with Soldier and Martial Artist abilities (two likely Greens), or an MP with NT and Agent abilities. That's the reason why I've placed the level requirements fairly high, so as to delay and control any super twink-outs.

So, what do you think?

Supa Leet
Alvador, you are correct: Adventuring is used in relation to climbing. However, a high Adventuring skill is not used to enable a player to "scale the highest mountain" as it were. The ability to scale mountains is determined by the incline of the mountain wall (certain ones cannot be climbed) and by whether the mountain is one of those invisible walls, like the ones surrounding Rome. Adventuring has little, if anything, to do with it. Instead, the higher your Adventuring skill, the less damage you will take when falling from a high place.

Since almost no one I know has fun with dropping themselves from their Yalmahas from 1000 ft, or with falling deaths and damage in general, most people avoid situations that will cause severe fall damage, thus negating the value of the Adventuring skill.

I'm still in the process of thinking through some other items, but these are good start. Thank you for your positive feedback, and keep it coming!

Indeed, Kiryat, you are correct. But, that's where the issue with COLOR associated with the professions comes in. I do not consider Fixers to be a related profession to NTs or MPs. For NTs and MPs, the profession breakdowns would be:

Nano-Technician and Meta-Physicist:

Green - Meta-Physicist/Nano-Techician and then a toss up between Agents and Bureaucrats.

Teal - The loser of the toss up and Engineers

Cyan - Doctors and Traders

Blue - Fixers, Soldiers, Enforcers, MAs and Adventurers

Nowhere did I say that all of these professions would be easy to acquire. Take your example of an NT and a Fixer. Using the system below, and Fixer as blue, it will take the NT until level 153 to be able to use Fixer "stuff," and even then, can only use Fixer stuff up to QL 150, and not beyond. An NT/MP will not be able to use QL 200 stuff until level 189.

That's not to unbalancing if you ask me. Besides, the general skill colors do not change, so an NT with the Fixer line will still have crap weapon potential and so on. Will they be able to run Fixer nanos? Sure. Might they get some nanos before Fixers do? Yes... only if the NT/MP uses all their PSPs to max their Fixer stat, and even then, the secondary profession nails down a hard cap for usability.

Again, an NT using the Fixer lines would be FEASIBLE, though not PREFERABLE. That's the key. If you want to be an NT using the EoT, fine, go ahead! But you will be restricted to doing JUST that, since to be able to build the blue Enforcer profession high enough, you'll spend almost all your profession points and a ton of IPs. You won't be able to choose any other profession (unless we do something with 75 PSPs, giving a limit to level 25 stuff).

Now, if you wanted to say that the option 7a would be unbalancing, you may be right. But consider:

a.) The first full Green would be at level 104
b.) The next around 135
c.) and the Cyan at level 200.

So, by level 140ish, an Advy can use all of a docs and an MAs nanos and items. Great, wonderful. But, unless you spend all your IPs to build up MA skills, you're not going to be much of a hand-to-hand fighter. And, even though you've developed Doctor fully, which uses similar nano lines, will you really be wanting all the Advy, MA, and Doctor healing lines? Might a more valuable use of your Profession points be useful, like Trader or Enforcer?

The whole question is of customization. Would some people become uber-healers? Would some people become uber-nanomages? Yes, of course, once the secrets of good profession choices are figured out. Is it a built-in system for abuse? No. There are hard caps. There is a limited number of profession points. You still need to spend IPs to develop your skills, and a ton of cash to cross the profession lines.

Again, the focus is on making something FEASIBLE, and of introducing a system that, I feel, the players will get a kick out of learning, using, and maximizing. Your complaints are valid, of course. Balancing this type of thing is of the utmost importance, and as I've indicated, is one of my biggest concerns.

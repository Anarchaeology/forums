It's down to money. They have a contract that says "You will not make this downloadable for X amount of time."

There are parallels in the PC hardware world. The reason ATI is stopping production on ATI branded cards is because they're moving to CHIP production over board production. They're doing this to gain market share; yeah, they make a little more money per board than per chip, but if they make boards, many board makers won't make boards using their chips. They figure they'll make more money by selling many more chips to many boardmakers than they would by selling their own boards; Nvidia certainly does okay as a chip-only maker.

Similarly, why do you think AMD doesn't make motherboards?

Funcom knows they stand to make more money by letting retailers retail. If they compete with this, retailers will retaliate by not selling the product, and FC won't make as much money. Yes, money is the reason why they won't let us download it... at first. Later on, when retailers are mostly not stocking it anyway, they'll go ahead and add the option, for customers just getting the game, for whom the download option is great.

There are good reasons behind it.

edit: Oh, and the MARKETING was horrible? What are you on? The marketing was fine. Lots of press, even a couple ads, and lots of screenshots and videos. That's marketing.

The distribution was... screwy. Their primary distributor, Digital River, is pretty obviously incapable of handling a pet dog, much less distribution of an MMORPG expansion. They probably should have used better distribution channels, but they don't have access to the distribution muscle of a Sony. Small publishers have a hard time distributing. In the music industry, you have independent distributors like Caroline, who can help indie labels get their music out to stores across the country by aggregating their business to the point where they are big enough to push a record.

I know of no analog in the software industry. Funcom seems to have done the best they could.

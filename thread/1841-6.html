This thread is now far too long to read unless you have quite a bit of time to dedicate to it, but I'd like to just restate the best solutions I've seen in all the months these things have been discussed. Sorry if someone else has mentioned these already.


Weapons

Most importantly, simply cause the chance to fumble and chance to do lower damage to go up exponentially when you do not have the skills to use a weapon. Increasing the chance of fumble should be no problem at all. As for doing less damage, just make the chance of critical go negative or so low you essentially never critical. At high levels, if you don't critical regularly, you're a waste of space, so this would completely eliminate things like agents and traders overequipping weapons 2-3x their level.


Nanoformulas

I also think that nano users should be able to use any nano at all times according to the same rule. Just implement penalties to using a nano you cannot cast. You may not be able to do a damage scale because not all nanos cause damage. So make them take longer to cast and have a higher chance of fumble if you do not meet the requirements. Maybe you can do a 1k nuke at 30th level, but since it takes almost 20 seconds to cast and has a 50% chance of just not going off, you're probably better off not bothering.


Net Result

I really feel this would eliminate the most noticeable affect of overequipping. If you tweak the numbers right, nobody will want to use equipment too high, or they're likely to be so unpredictable it isn't worth it. Players would choose not to overequip even if they still could. This would also make Trader nanos work as intended. In my 5 months of playing this game, I have never once had a Trader cast Team Skill Wrangler, because who the heck needs it? Now it would be useable as it would actually make everyone a bit better.


Other Items

As for items like NCU belts, implants, and armor. Belts and implants can no longer be overequipped due to correct but far too late nerfs by Funcom. Armor is much less a concern because MOBs have insanely high minimum damage anyway. If you're really concerned about people overequipping armor (which I feel is a waste of time to consider) then why not take a look at all the items in the game which give a boost to abilities (i.e. Concrete Pillows, Carrier Ships, Flechette Rifles, etc). I'm not sure what the point of these items is other than to overequip armor. But again, I don't think overequipped armor is too big a deal, because it isn't that hard to only take minimum damage from a MOB using normal reachable armor (especially since the flood of Decus coats, nice move), and you still die just as quickly with them doing minimum damage to you.


The Real Problem

A final thing that would help greatly. Nerf the Trader class. Reduce the skill bonus of the Skill Wranglers. Make Ransacks and Deprives not stack. Make all these nanos take up far more NCU. These things would reduce the availability of unbalancing Skill Wrangers and would hopefully reduce the number of shouts we see like "35th lvl enforcer needs 70 point wrangler!" No Trader can argue that this is a severe nerf to their class, because the only thing it really takes away from them is their unfair ability to make insane amounts of credits off other people. No other class gets 70k for a single buff, and you sure as hell shouldn't either (Traders typically charge 1k per point wrangled). Also, if the skill changes I mentioned above were implemented, Ransacks and Deprives would be greatly increased in usefullness in combat, as would all debuffs, which I can only imagine was their original intended use.

Overall, great article. Nice to see a Funcom rep actually say things boldly and honestly; I think everyone is fed up with getting the run-around.

Eleet
Just came back from trying DAoC - bleh. Don't bother with it...many reasons not to.

However, there was one thing I did like about the way they handle the overequipping issue. First, each item is designed to be usable by a certain level of char (the item would be color coded as Blue in this case). If the item was lower level than the char it was another color (green i believe) to let the user know that it was wieldable, but not the "best" level of weapon for the char to use.

Inversely, if the weapon was of a higher level than the char, it was color coded (yellow, orange, red, purple) to notify the char of the fact. AO has the QL posted for the same reason...so the char will know if the item is within their "grasp" or not.

Now, in DAoC, the user can indeed equip a higher level item - however, with each level of difference between the char and the item, there is an ever growing chance that the item will not hit, break and become unusable... In a nutshell, the char is LESS effective than he is under normal level (sweet spot) item usage and is encouraged to use items at his own level because they will prove to be more effective for him and will not break (thereby rendering the char's bank account empty and his inventory full of broken items).

There is encouragement to use items at your own level in DAoC because of the fact that using items far above your own level probably will break and become useless.

Currently in AO there doesn't seem to be any reason NOT to overequip a char as much as possible. There is no chance an item will "break" if the char persists in its use. In some instances, there is a greater chance to hit or crit. There is no reduction in damage output or protection either (to my knowledge); only a gargantuan increase in overall damage. The char can continue to do ludicrous damage to anything but the hardest "reds" with no real penalties or drawbacks to worry about.

IMHO it would be primarily useful and probably a good balancing move to allow items to malfunction/break/deactivate if a char's skills are not up to the specified minimums.

EXAMPLE:
Imagine some level 40ish Enforcer, spending 750k credits on an uber QL110 sledgehammer; they go out to the Cyborg Encampment in GTC; draw back to pummel the Mob into the soft earth; and on impact, the only sound is the cracking of the hammer's head and a bright yellow note: "You have just broken your weapon. It is no longer useable and has been placed in your inventory."; the Cyborg laughs and bursts the Enforcer into oblivion! Enter...the Reclaim terminal.

While attempting to use a higher level item, eliminating the probability of a crit (or reducing the chance of even a normal hit), reducing an armor's effective AC, the amount an NCU chip adds, etc., as the char lacks the skill/experience to wield the higher level item properly would discourage folks from using items outside their sweet spot (the item is neither too high nor too low of QL).

Alternately, making the items that are within the char's sweet spot work exceptionally well (pretty much like AO does now with critical hits, etc.); perhaps giving a small, additional AC% bonus, or an NCU% bonus, etc., would be further reason to use them and not over equip. There are several ways you can apply this...

EXAMPLE:
Our intrepid level 40ish Enforcer, indefatigable in spirit, decides he will forgo the Cyborgs as they decidedly have his number (and he is now 750k credits in the hole, and without his beloved gigantic hammer). Instead, he will go up to the Temple area in GTC with his QL48ish Sledgehammer. He finds a few OT, an Alpha, and even a Bloodcreeper.

To his surprise, he is getting critical hits far more often, and his normal hits are meeting with great success. The fights take a little longer, and are a bit more anxious (running to heal a few times)...Our lumbering meatshield finds that he has MORE success with this "sweet spot" item than he ever could with the "uber" item.

So he can't single-handedly take out the Cyborgs while in the level 40's... isn't that supposed to be the case? If reds aren't difficult to impossible to kill, then why have reds? If yellow mobs are supposed to be the "equivalent" challenge, then the battle(s) with them, with similar QL items being used, will be the most balanced fighting available. Therein lies the balance...I think.

The end result would hopefully be folks WANTING to use items at their own level's sweet spot (+10%/-10% of char's level, for example) because they are far MORE EFFECTIVE that way than they are with exploits/over-equipping, etc. There would still be the incentive to continually upgrade because items would eventually become lower quality than you can use within your sweet spot.

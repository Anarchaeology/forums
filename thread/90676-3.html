quote:
Originally posted by Meister
The property comes first, since when destroyed, it is gone. The employees come second, because when an employee dies, he is simply reconstructed. So OT property first, OT employees second, lawabiding neutrals third.
OK. This is the argument I remember. I think this prioritization is badly misplaced, for a number of reasons.

First of all, whatever the cost in terms of property damage, if employees comes second to equipment, it creates resentment and huge morale problems. Human beings, whether they have the luxury of insurance protection or not, MUST be more important than equipment. As a bureaucrat, Meister, I know you have seen first hand the limitations of robots and machines. A disenfranchised workforce is one of the biggest problems Omni-Tek could face as a company!

Second, on a purely pragmatic level, it is worth looking at the cost, in terms of lost productivity, that reclaim-related absences and delays cost the company. The company stands to lose BILLIONS in credits from these delays and lost worker hours. Reclaim is a wonderful thing, but from an efficiency standpoint it is a HUGE drain on the company.

Third, it is worth noting that Omni-Tek underwrites the insurance of Omni-Tek employees, in order to keep reclaim costs manageable for the individual employee. On average, the employee pays between 10-15% of the total cost required to pay for reclaim. This translates to thousands of credits in costs to the company, each and every time an employee is sent to reclaim! Multiply that over the number of reclaims in a given day, and the costs become staggering!

Fourth, and I list this last only because I know it is not a serious consideration for you, not because *I* consider it of lesser importance, reclaim takes a huge physical and emotional toll on the employee forced to undergo the process. It creates stress, trauma, and can lead to serious mental health problems over repeated exposure. From a purely humanitarian standpoint, minimizing the number of reclaim instances an individual employee must endure is in the interest of every compassionate person!

I would like to point out, however, that there is a contradiction in several of the things that you have said: On the one hand, you suggest that property is a higher priority than people because property is uninsured, and, on the other hand, you acknowledge that insurance is a luxury that not every Omni-Tek employee/citizen enjoys.

If I am on a dangerous mission, I don't expect Omni-Pol to rescue me if something goes awry.

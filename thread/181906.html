Omni-InternOps Public Access Memorandum
From: Nathan Age, General

It is the opinion of Omni-InternOps that, at present, peace is not a viable solution with the clans and certain neutral departments.

The clans have, beginning with the rise of the Sentinels, been nothing short of beligerent towards Omni-Tek and its right to rule over Rubi-Ka. They continuously pose a security threat to the Corporation, its employees and their families, and their newly-"reborn" leader, Henry Radiman, have issued statements and conducted operations in an attempt to undermine Omni-Tek's lawfully-acquired and maintained lease.

Furthermore, terrorist organizations among the neutrals, such as the Nanomage Liberation Front, maintain a negative stance against the Omni-Tek family.

In light of the recent surge of Cyborg activity against Omni-Tek interests, it is the opinion of Omni-InternOps that the resources of Omni-Tek and its personnel are better directed at neutralizing this threat.

Any form of peace with the aformentioned parties would require the following to be deemed satisfactory to Omni-Tek:

Essential Pre-Conditions:
  • A complete renunciation of terrorism and terrorist activities.
  • A complete halt to all lawless activity and statements from the clan populace, and a recognition, in writing, of Omni-Tek's valid and overriding authority in matters concerning Rubi-Ka pursuant to its lease.
  • Mr. Radiman has proved wholly-incapable of conducting himself in a professional manner and honouring various peace initiatives in the past. He has still not absolved himself of allegations of his involvement with the Dust Brigade. Until Mr. Radiman clears his name, Omni-Tek will not consider him a valid political entity, but will not call for his ouster from Rubi-Ka.
  • The arrest, or information leading to the arrest, of any and all members of the Dust Brigade, their affiliates and assistants.
  • The ouster of the Sentinels as the de facto ruling entities of the city of Tir.
  • The arrest and prosecution of Simon Silverstone for theft, crimes against humanity, inciting war and patent infringement.
  • The return of Omni-2 to Omni-Tek, its lawful owner.
  • The immediate ceasation of the production of Notum Mining Technologies, the return of the stolen Omni-Tek patented plans and schematics, and a written pledge to discontinue and refrain from any mining activities whatsoever.
  • A written pledge signed by all clan leaders, with accompanying four billion credit bond, that no clan member will use any weapons of war against a neutral citizen or Omni-Tek employee out of an act of unprovoked aggression. Should any such incident occur, such bond will be forfeit and will become property of Omni-Tek Corporation of Rubi-Ka.
  • A written statement is to be signed by all clan members renouncing Sol Banking Corporation and recognizing Omni-Tek as the pre-eminent corporation in the known universe.
  • An immediate pledge to support either the Yuttos or the Unredeemed in their various endeavours within the Shadowlands. All other groups are to be considered forfeit.
  • The immediate demolition of any and all facilities used in the production of weaponry, weapons of mass destruction and ammunition. This includes, but is not necessarily limited to, the Sentinels' bases in Wailing Wastes, and the associated shop terminals in Free Trade establishments within Clan territory.


Open for Negotiation:
  • An immediate pledge by the owners of The Cup in West Athen to serve and cater to all Omni-Tek employees.



Anything short of the above will be deemed unsatisfactory.

Most likely General Age is using the negotiation tactic where one makes demands that he knows the other side will not accept, but demands that in an ideal world would be accepted. The opposing side then brings its on list of excessive demands, giving both sides a framework to begin the negotiations, and the talking begins. Most times, the negotiations end with an agreement to talk again, and nothing else. To the casual observer, the meetings have accomplished nothing. In actuality, both sides have learned the goals and issues from the other, and gained a better understanding of what demands are really a non-issue and unimportant. These can be dropped, and issues of contention can be retained. More often than not, only one or two demands at most survive this brand of negotiation.

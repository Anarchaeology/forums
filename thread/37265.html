Terrorism & Me, or, Why I Love the Balaclava
There are people who really, really feel strongly that the balaclava mask that was introduced for agents in Anarchy Online patch 14.2 is ugly. But there are also people who think it's worse than ugly, it's evil. Not me! I know what it represents, and it gives me a warm glow of nostalgia. I wish I could get a helmet that looked like that for my fixer character.

The balaclava is the traditional uniform of the anarchist. Anarchists and terrorists have worn it for decades, going back at least as far as the late 19th century in one form or another. The balaclava is traditionally black, even when that's not the best camouflage, because black symbolizes anarchism. Its shapelessness is meant to disguise facial features, to protect not just the wearer but his or her family from reparations.

With a global war on terrorism going on, with the last of the wreckage of the World Trade Center only recently moved to the (too aptly named) Fresh Kills landfill, how can I feel warm nostalgia for the balaclava? Because at least three times in my life, my sympathies have been with the anarchists or terrorists. And not just any terrorists, but groups actively labelled as terrorists and hunted by the USA.

IRISH REPUBLICAN ARMY

If you read the best-selling book from a few years ago, How the Irish Saved Civilization, then you got a reminder that when most of Europe was still wallowing in the Dark Ages, it was Irish monks and priests who copied most of the books, founded most of the libraries, and invented the university. If you're not Irish yourself, this may have come as a surprise to you, because the Irish are famously ignorant brawling drunkards.

How do you get from one to the other? Through one of the most brutal wars of occupation in the history of the world. Not long after the British conquest of Ireland, the British went so far as to make it a death penalty offense to teach an Irish child to read and write. British "Orange-men" were granted plantations in the north of the island, given full military backing, and the Irish who were living on that land were made their slaves. They then got away with something that should boggle your mind: they treated those Irish slaves even worse than American southerners treated African slaves.

When independence finally came to Ireland after centuries of war, the Republic of Ireland was not able to liberate the Ulster plantations. Here we are decades later, and the descendents of those slave owners still own most of the land, still have most of the money, and maintain the right-wing death squads needed to ensure that their plantation kingdom remains under English rule.

To remedy this, the world proposes a "peace process" where the votes of the hereditary "property owners" are counted as equal to the votes of the Irish. It also proposes a "peace process" where the Ulster death squads and the British Army need only move their weapons back to England, but the Irish Republican Army (many of whom have worn balaclavas during their struggle to reunite with free Ireland) must destroy theirs.

Not acceptable.

Here in St. Louis, as with many cities in the US with extensive Irish populations, there are charities that collect money on behalf of northern Ireland. The FBI believes that many of these charities funnel money to the IRA, and some went further and helped the IRA buy weapons and explosives. I have never contributed to those charities. This is a matter of some embarassment to me, as I'm a quarter Irish myself, and fully agree with their cause. I've just never gotten around to make the couple of phone calls it would take.

The FBI considers those charities to be organizations that support terrorism, and is still conducting operations against them. They are also still helping the INS deport IRA refugees who escaped to the US. Our government is on the wrong side. England out of Ireland - now, please.

EARTH FIRST!

Those of you who are in your 20s or younger can not possibly remember what the Reagan administration was really like. Not only did we have a government that was every bit as much of a lapdog for greedy corporate management as the Bush II administration is, we had one that was actively anti-environment. Not just anti-environmentalist, but openly hostile to the environment.

Why would anybody be actively anti-environment? In an interview, Reagan's Secretary of the Interior, James Watt, let the cat out of the bag. They believed that the world was going to end in 1988 (based on best-selling author Hal Lindsey's interpretation of the Book of Revelations), and that God would punish them for every ounce of metal or oil still unused or usable tree left standing (based on Jesus' "Parable of the Talents"). Besides, if the world is going to end in 1988, who cares about pollution when it's 1982? Is it going to kill you in six years? No? Then get over it. What, the world didn't end in 1988? Well, no, but it should have, and it will, any day now ... a claim that keeps producing best-sellers for Tim & Beverly LaHaye.

In the mean time, companies like our local Monsanto (now Solutia) were dumping toxic waste straight into the Mississippi and getting away with it. How were they getting away with it? The EPA scheduled their inspections. So starting the day before the inspection, Monsanto would shut down the plant and run fresh water through the pipes, until after the inspection - and they still paid some fines for the output being too toxic. Monsanto whistle-blowers went to the Illinois and federal EPA, and were told to get lost.

None of this was especially secret. Everybody who lived in the area knew it, and it made it in the papers off and on for months. But nothing was done about it - until balaclava-wearing "terrorists" (from a local Greenpeace chapter) took a boat up to the output pipe, videotaped the collection of honest samples, and then tried to use concrete to sabotage it. The concrete plug didn't hold, but the news footage of a "terrorist" attack on Monsanto, and the independently tested evidence those "terrorists" brought out, forced the EPA to take official notice, and they finally made Monsanto clean it up. Some. Briefly. It could probably stand to be done again.

In the mean time, financiers Michael Milken and Ivan Boesky were fraudulently and illegally raising money and channeling it to friends of theirs, who used high-risk loans with ruinous interest rates (which is all that a junk bond is) to buy out companies whose stockholders didn't want to sell, against the advice of company management (which is all that a hostile takeover is) - the junk bond leveraged buy out, or LBO. Despite the fact that the money that bought the companies was crooked, the new owners got to keep their companies. Owners like Roger Hurwitz (sp? from memory), whose Maxxam corporation used junk bonds to buy up a lot of companies.

Among those companies was a small timber company in nortnern California, whose sole claim to fame was that they owned almost all of the redwood trees in the US that weren't on public land. They weren't logging them, not very heavily. They held themselves to only cutting down trees that were sick or otherwise in trouble, to preserve the forest. Why would they do that? Because logging is a cyclical industry, and that redwood forest made one heck of an asset to borrow against. They put it up as collateral, however often they needed, and made sure to pay the money back in time.

Now, the theory behind junk bond LBOs is that it is immoral for a publicly traded company to have an asset that valuable and not return it to the shareholders. So Roger Hurwitz's plan was simple: borrow money at insane interest to buy out that company by hook or crook. Then, over the five year course of the loan, Maxxam was going to cut down and sell every privately owned redwood tree in America to pay back the loan. It wouldn't leave the company with any assets to borrow against in bad times. It wouldn't leave much in profits after the loan payments. And it wouldn't leave any redwood trees in those forests for the next three or four generations. But the asset would have been converted to cash, paid to the bondholders. He and his (crooked) supporters thought that that made it a good thing.

Environmental organizations had never confronted this level of threat before. The total ruthlessness was unprecedented. What's more, so was the level of contempt for the law that the corporations' owners and managers were showing.

Lots of controversial timber sales on public land or in protected habitats were clearly, by the letter of the law, illegal. (They were also blatantly crooked payoffs to campaign contributors, because there's no other way to justify timber "sales" in which the government spends more on helping the company extract the timber than the company pays in logging fees.) The traditional environmental groups tried getting the courts to enforce the law. Many of the courts were quite willing to enforce the law ... and found that they were being outmaneuvered.

High-priced company lawyers used every trick they could to sneak in "gaps" between the injunctions against logging. And every gap they got, they rushed the bulldozers in. If they didn't have time to log, it was enough to wreck another part of the forest. Why? Because if they could wreck the forest, there'd be no environment left to protect. So you might as well let them log the rest, right?

In response to these kinds of threats, and this level of contempt for the law, Earth First!ers like IWW labor organizer and pro-Choice activist Judy Bari, folk singer Darryl Cherney, and others formed a loose network of people who were willing to break the law to defend the rest of us from the lawbreakers. Call them vigilantees if you disapprove, but nothing else was working. They organized a season of non-violent human blockades, called Redwood Summer, to keep Maxxam and other companies from getting away with their crimes.

Judy Bari's car was blown up. With her in it. By a pipe bomb that was put into the car while it was under FBI surveillance. After the blast the FBI got there before the paramedics or local police did - and attempted to keep medical personnel from saving her life. They then went on to attempt to frame her as the bomber. (Recent note: And the false arrest lawsuit for that finally made it to a jury just a few weeks ago, and the FBI was convicted and ordered to pay millions to the victims.)

Why would they do that? Because the FBI had labelled Earth First! a terrorist organization, and as far as the FBI was concerned the assassination of "terrorists" was a good thing. They may have even helped, but this many years later, we'll probably never know.

But the bomber and the FBI and Maxxam failed to stop Redwood Summer. Earth First! succeeded in slowing Maxxam down long enough that they went into default on those junk bonds. Maxxam ("without admitting wrongdoing," as the clich&eacute; usually goes) recently sold the land to the government, and the Clinton administration did the right thing in making it a national monument, safe from corporate vandalism (for now).

It was during this time that I met with some people from the local chapter of Earth First!, donated money to Redwood Summer, and attempted to join. I didn't last long. We agreed on tactics, pretty much, but disagreed wildly on target priority.

At the time, Big River Earth First! was making a big deal out of the Times Beach Incinerator. As I predicted, this turned out to be a total non-event, harmless to the environment and about the least dangerous thing to happen to St. Louis, environmentally, in history. I was trying to get them to do something about bigger local problems, like the poisoning of the entire town of Herculaneum by Doe Run, the poisoning of South Roxana by Shell, the poisoning of everything down stream from here by Monsanto, and devastating clearcuts in the Mark Twain and Shawnee National Forests. (All of which I was later proven right about.)

They concluded that since I was trying to distract them from the incinerator, I was probably an undercover FBI agent, an employee of the incinerator companies, or both, and after a couple of weeks I was dropped from the phone tree and stopped getting invited to meetings and actions. Oh, well. So much for my "terrorist" career.

(They also failed to stop the incinerator. St. Louisans make lousy activists.)

On the west coat, the now-expert activists from Redwood Summer went up to Washington and Oregon, where you heard from them again in the battle over the temperate rainforests up there, which they also mostly saved. You then heard from them again after that, when as full-fledged balaclava-wearing anarchists they formed the core of the "Black Block" in the Seattle anti-globalization protests. And as with the incinerator locally, this time I think they don't know what they're talking about and are making fools of themselves ... but I recognize them as friends of friends of friends, and I still get a warm, friendly feeling when I see those masks on TV.

LEGION OF DOOM

Somewhere around the apartment, I think I still have my "membership" card in the Legion of Doom, the most infamous hacker anarchist group of the 1980s.

As a hacker, I've never been anything but small potatoes. If I get access to a system, I'm likely to test the limits of that access. Or at least I used to; I'm old and lazy now, and much more bored by computers. I've worn the white hat (network security) much more often than I've worn the black hat (probing other people's networks for vulnerabilities).

I was an early contributor to the Electronic Freedom Foundation, and it was through them that I heard about a local appearance of EFF representatives at something called Cyberview, I think it was in 1989. I bullied and bluffed my way in, because I wanted to meet them in person. I should have recognized the name, but I didn't - Cyberview was the annual gathering of the Legion of Doom. It had been happening in my own home town for years, and somehow I'd never caught up with it until then.

I found the EFF people interesting. I met Bruce Sterling there, and for all that I know people who can't stand him personally, I thought he was quite charming. I also met the Doomsters, who I found to be ... well, it won't make me popular to say it, but not just harmless nuts, but silly and foolish harmless nuts, who were nowhere near as smart or sneaky as they thought they were. Watching them try to "social engineer" escort services into sending them free hookers, and then when that failed, watching them try equally unsuccessfully to "social engineer" their way into the swingers' club event at the same hotel, was almost as much fun as I've ever had.

They gave me a Legion of Doom membership card. At the time, they were handing them out like candy to almost anyone who'd take one. They were doing this specifically to tweak the FBI and the aptly-acronymed SS (US Secret Service), who were trying to shut them down as "dangerous terrorists."

If anything deserves the name of terrorism, it would be the SS's disastrous "Operation Sundevil," in which heavily armed commandos in ninja suits staged daring midnight raids - on sleeping 14 year olds, holding whole families at gunpoint, and only belatedly long after the fact showing any law enforcement credentials, let alone warrants. In US SS mythology, the Legion of Doom all had software on hidden PCs, and could crash the entire world economy if they pushed a button, so they had to be seized with maximum ruthlessness to keep any of them from pressing that button. Not an easy thing to imagine with a straight face, is it?

Note that one home-owner heard his dog barking, and got up in time to catch one of those ninjas breaking into his kid's bedroom window, and shot the out-of-uniform federal agent point blank with a shotgun. He was acquitted, and good for him.

As part of the same Operation, federal agents raided and attempted to shut down Steve Jackson Games for the "crime" of publishing games that were pro-hacker. In those raids, the government seized a publishing house's work-in-progress, something the Supreme Court has repeatedly looked down on as un-Constitutional prior restraint on 1st Amendment rights. To win that case, the feds argued that a company with fifty or sixty books in print wasn't really a "publisher."

My part in all of this history was trivial and uninteresting. Other than showing up at one Cyberview, I supported the EFF until it went all corporate on us. I made a couple of contributions to hackers' defense funds. I cheered a bit from the sidelines for the occasional victories. I wrote articles defending them for Telecom Digest and Risk Digest. Eventually, it all drifted off into the past. You don't hear much about the Legion of Doom these days. Except for the cruel internal exile of Kevin Mitnick, they all got old and respectable, or just old.

They were superceded by the next wave of "terrorist" hackers, the crypto-anarchists, or cypherpunks. I played even less of a part in that conspiracy. Cryptography requires more math than I have, and the coding was all being done in C, a programming language I despise. But cheer them on I did, as the Bush I and Clinton administrations waged a relentless but futile war against them. You see their ideological stepchildren these days in the spamcops, using vigilante techniques to defend the internet backbone from spammers.

When hackers don balaclavas for newspaper photographs, as they often do, it's all in fun. But it's not inappropriate, because hackers both white-hat and black-hat are anarchist vigilantes, doing the right thing, with the only tools they have available, when the law is on the side of evil.

SO WHAT IS A TERRORIST?

During the Reagan administration, the CIA and the NSC were (illegally) aiding the Nicaraguan Somozista former national guard (the northern faction of "the Contras") in their attempt to overthrow the communist Sandinistas who had seized power from them in a coup. They did this despite the fact that everybody knew that the Somozistas were raping nuns, murdering doctors and nurses, and blowing up grade schools, all to keep the Sandinistas from getting credit for improving rural people's lives, and shipping cocaine to the US on CIA and US military aircraft to pay for it all (and to buy themselves real estate in Miami).

When a Congressional committee lambasted Reagan administration officials for supporting terrorism, someone (North? Poindexter? I forget) contradicted the Congressman, saying that the more accurate term for the Somozista Contras was "freedom fighters." The Congressman then famously remarked, "I guess one man's terrorist is another man's freedom fighter."

Right on.

Terrorism is a terrible tactic of war, but it is just that - a tactic. It is nobody's first choice, because it almost never works, and almost always backfires. But it works some times, and that is why desperate people, who believe they are fighting a last ditch effort, who believe that if they fail then all is lost, who are fighting against an overwhelming enemy, some times turn to terrorism. As I think it was Andre Breton who said, "Terrorism is the 'surgical strike capability' of the oppressed."

I'm not saying that there's such a thing as good terrorism and bad terrorism. I am, however, saying that there's such a thing as bad terrorism and worse terrorism. And there are causes worth engaging in terrorism to defend, and those terrorists, I enthusiastically praise and even occasionally support. I'm saying that when only choice left is between powerful criminal alliances of government and corporate power, versus criminal anarchist terrorists, I side with the anarchist terrorists.

I say this not because I'd ever want the anarchists to win, not definitively. But I will say that they scare me less than equally lawless corporations or equally lawless governments do.

So pass me a balaclava.

I'm acquiring a deep cynicism about "peace processes" in general. Ever since the term was coined, there's been a model for a "peace process": Set the leaders down face to face. Use a 3rd party to find things on which they already agree, and get them to jointly issue a statement on that. Then find the things on which they can each easily concede, and get them to do that. Make both sides live up to their concessions. Then show both sides that the other side lived up to their concessions. If any issue looks impossible to resolve, put it off for a later round of negotiations. Keep going with these "confidence building" measures until both sides are familiar enough with each other, and trust each other enough, to negotiate the hard issues.

The problem is, in most conflicts in which this has been tried, it hasn't worked. Why? Because no matter what else they can agree on, the hard issues are usually flat-out non-negotiable, or there wouldn't have been a war in the first place. So all a "peace process" does is store up resentments even longer, and give both sides a period of relative peace in which to continue arming up and continue spying on each other.

In the middle east peace process, they knew what the hard issues would be: full Palestinian statehood (more specifically, whether or not the Palestinians would be allowed to have a modern army) and the status of Jerusalem. The only thing I've been wrong about was the level of restraint with which Israel would fight it; I was pretty sure there wouldn't be a single living Palestinian in the occupied territories by now.

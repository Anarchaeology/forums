Hmm. You're right. I should have addressed the original topic instead of responding to CaptFallout's post. It must be frustrating when trolls get all the attention and the serious players with the constructive criticisms get nada. My bad.

Kirrana, your frustrations are quite understandable. I'd probably feel the same way if I were you, and it's good to see dedicated players taking the initiative and asking difficult questions. AO's story was overpromised and underdelivered. I'll take responsibility for the delivery-part. I don't mind saying that, yes, I'm definitely at fault for what's wrong with the story. It's certainly been my responsibility to ensure that Rubi-Ka has a real history, and that every part of the story fits into the larger arcs. This hasn't happened to the extent that you, the players, expect and deserve. My bad. Again.

Personally, I do honestly believe that AO's done a better job than any other MMOG when it comes to story...but then we did promise a lot more than any other MMOG has ever done, so in that (if little else) CaptFallout was correct. Yet we're only five months and, uh, nine days down the road from where we started. It's still early, and we have absolutely no intention of abandoning the storyline. We just have to get our bearings right before picking up speed. We don't want to end up in the ditches. Again.

So what's happening with the story? It's still there, though things have definitely slowed down a bit. Events take place on a regular basis, though of course not everyone is affected by them. There's lots of stuff in the pipeline, but (frustratingly enough) I can't really talk about it. Our plan for the story was to have it impact on the world to a much larger degree than what's currently the case. But priorities continuously change, resources are reassigned, and we have to take all player feedback into account, not just the story-related feedback. While I do agree -- as does game director Gaute Godager, the real creative force behind AO (and someone who must be credited for echoing a lot of your concerns to me) -- that the story is what makes this game unique, there are a lot of other demands and wishes that the team must accommodate. Like balancing. Like new content. Like bug-fixes. Should this get in the way of the story? No. It shouldn't. Does it? Yes, sometimes it does.

Will things get better? If the game in general is any indication, then yes, absolutely. We had an awful launch. The game crashed every five minutes. Now, nine months later, AO is (in my completely unbiased opinion, of course) the best darned MMOG out there. When we initiated the story, five months ago, a lot of you were disappointed. Since then, it's had its ups and downs, but we're learning -- from my myriad mistakes and from the feedback you give us -- and hopefully that will enable us to do better. Much better.

Oh, and before I go: Keep in mind (and I'm sure some of you will be happy to hear this) that the AO story ain't Ragnar's story. It never has been, not by a long shot: It's Funcom's story. There are a bunch of people who contribute to the story in a bunch of different ways, and chances are that the elements of the story that have been the most successful had absolutely nothing to do with me. The Dust Brigade attacks, for example, were very effective in creating a sense of fear and uncertainty on Rubi-Ka, and the entire credit for that should go to the events- and support-teams. As for the story videos -- while opinion appears to be somewhat divided on the topic, I thought they were a cool bonus, something very unique and beautiful...and I had very little to do with their success. They were created by a bunch of amazingly hard-working artists, animators, and audio personnel, who put together almost one hour of top-quality CGI in two months.

I could go on. The point is, disregarding my input, there's still enough talented and dedicated storytellers at Funcom to make AO's story kick serious arse.

Thanks for listening,

-Ragnar

Flame me for posting. But a thought came to me while I was off in some other game.

These changes in the way they are implemented have nothing whatsoever with with 'over-equipping'.

What they did with weapons armore has a basis in 'reality'. You no longer have the skill to equip a weapon, so they penalized you when you were 'under-equipped'. I didn't like that because it took away from the 'game'. But I never said they didn't valid reasons for attempting _something_. They had to address the failure of the poorly implemented QLVL system that made an item's quality more important than the player's skill.

Same for the pet Nerf. They pulled off an amature fix in that an Engineer's skill in creating a robot should have nothing to do with his ability to control it. If there were checks put in that required a certain amount of Electrical Engineering, Intelligence, or other skills that made _sense_ in terms of being able to control what you created while still being less buffable, I could deal with a Nerf.

Nerfs aren't what bring about my vitriol. It's putting in artificial game constructs that have absolutely nothing whatsoever with the inherent strengths and weaknesses of your character in relation with what they can or can't do.

Put an Adventuring requirement on charming Leets and, if I were an Adventurer, I could understand why I could not charm the most grand of SupaDupaLeet. I have neglected my 'outdoorsy' skills and suffer in kind. It's all about our hatred for the friggen 'level'. All level should ever be is a way to gain 'skills'. Not some way to 'unlock' some treat that some pasty-faced programmer in some country across the pond had deemed we are now 'worthy' of.

Let's assume that I buff an Enforcer with Mocham BioMet and MatterMet. He can now execute a real nice Essence. If FunCom had decided that when the Mocham's run out that his Essence would fall, I could understand that he no longer had the SKILLS to maintain that essence in his NCU. Still a Nerf. But it makes _sense_. He was able to take advantage of temporarily increased skills to execute programs in his NCU that he was not able to before. It'd be a hell of a Nerf. But, I'd guess that the playerbase would accept the fact that if you wanted to get the advantages of an MP, you needed to have him around to maintain your increased skills and probably have a Fixer around to provide the additional needed NCU. A Nerf. But, one that makes sense and doesn't destroy the beauty of the skill system nor eliminate the benefits of bringing varied professions together to do things they couldn't do alone.

An even more severe Nerf I could accept: If I as an MP were not able to execute Infuse: BioMet in a Doctor's NCU unless he had the PsyMod and SenseImp required on the Nano for the Infuse, I could understand that. In order to maintain the Infuse in his own NCU, he needed _skills_ to keep it going. Only I as the MP could run the program. But, the Doc needs the skills to keep it going. Another Nerf. But, another that makes _sense_ in terms of keeping Skills and character build at the forefront and maintaining that 'levels' are only ways to earn IP's that you distribute to build your character.

We only rail out against artificiality and the not-so-slow death of the Skill system.

I won't play a level-based game. I'm sorry to see the poor skills your game-designers are equipped with. You send out signals that your game-designers have poor skillsets themselves. Your moves in both dropping this on us without exploring valid alternatives and ignoring an outcry the likes of which I've never seen before paint you as completely inept in nurturing a community.

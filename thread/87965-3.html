Crattyjack sat at the coffee table to lunch with Harry, his sales bot assistant. He should have been hungry, especially as Harry had prepared his favourite Leet and Reet shaped Bronto meat, with his favourite ‘Mothers milk’ Milkshake. But the inspection that Omni-Pol had requested still rested in his mind. It had happened earlier; Dabblez, Speedydude, Limona and their pro bono Lawyer Alexmis of the Law Society were present for it, as well as Omni-Pol’s Eternalforce, Kemansky, Techblast and Templus.

Cratty recalled praising the Officers at every opportunity, and playing on their graces. But they seemed awfully paranoid about the Rebels on the World, even though a sort of War existed, was there really a need to fear what the Clan could use R.U.R. bots for? He didn’t know how could he? More concerned for the dream that he and the others started to make real, of money glory and recognition.

He dipped a Santa Leet piece of bronto meat into his milkshake, sucked it and then chewed on it, slouched at the low coffee table. Harry looked down at him and looked concerned, Crattyjack confused him. His Master seemed so belligerently confident to all those other ‘humanoids’ outside of the doors of the apartment, but in here he was as if a boy of 10, lost and confused. Maybe he missed his Mommie ‘Gum Gums’, (as Cratty called her)?

“Im sorry Master, but I had to leave before the kind Officers would pry into my mind.” He said with his bot eyes reflecting an ‘apologetic’ expression. Crattyjack, stopped chewing for an instant, ‘my mind?’ he thought quizzically. He’d never heard Harry refer to his self-consciousness in that way before. He was impressed, as he never believed what his techie colleagues claimed; that in time with enough learning, personality and self-awareness can become as is in biological humanoid life.
“Harry, don’t worry about it, they were just concerned with our consumer line of bots, so its all okely dokely”. He put the thought aside for a moment and moved aside his giant sized teddy bear, brought his keyboard close and wrote a message…


MEMO

Today (19th of January 2003) we attended the request from Omni-Pol to interview and inspect our line of Bots we produce. It all was rather amicable I think, bar a few ‘minor’ points. Firstly, as Speedydude and I were being questioned by a General of Omni-Pol and as Dabblez demonstrated the Vacuum bot; it scared the General as it ran to our table to suck up dirt from under the table that she was standing near to, making her slightly err ‘fearful’. Also the PHEWY Type 6 ‘Milly’ bot model that we supplied did rather well in answering the questions that Techblast asked of it, except for some sensor readings that the Officers found. As to what they translate to is yet unknown.

But what concerns me is that of the Generals reaction to the vacuum bot that was to be sold to the Clan Fair Trade in Clan territory. I hope that hers is not the general feeling of our products, but if, and only if it is, what should we do to correct it apart from Milly dolls?

I also heard that there are concerns of the ‘hunter killer dust’ algorithm being modified for larger particles, such as err, people. I believe that the 10-megawatt suction should be reduced to only suck up smaller particles… Of course I may have read it wrong, as my tech specs are somewhat worn out, but that kind of suction could be a bit excessive?

We should get the results in a few days, and I think it will just reinforce what I think so far, that is, its easy sailing from here.

Crattyjack, (RUR Sales).

Hopefully she'd be able to meet with Mr. Age later this week, and draft all of this. The last thing RUR needed was another lawsuit from another idiot who hoped to make a quick buck off of his own stupidity at the company's expense. She had a list of questions already prepared for him, as well as notes of things they should probably include in the warranty.


******* a couple of hours later**********


She pulled up an interesting case, a class-action suit against one of the oldest existing corporations in the history of man, McBurger's. Here was a company that, time and again, had weathered one consumer-stupidity suit after another. She read through one of the more recent ones. It involved a lawsuit with several dozen people who had suffered hearing loss after inserting potato products into their ears. She read through, fascinated, but was ultimately discouraged by the ruling. In addition to settling a credit-amount on the plaintiffs, and paying a fine to the ICC, McB's now had to label their notoriously delicious and highly addictive french fries with a label. French fries were intended to go into the "mouth only", with instructions to "chew first, then swallow". It included a disclaimer that McBurger's was not liable for anyone putting the fries anywhere except in the mouth. The packaging now included a hologram of a person picking up a fry and placing it into their mouth.

Good lord, why was it necessary to protect a person from his or her own dim-wittedness?

She read on, increasingly horrified... it would appear that after McBurger had complied with the judge's ruling, several pro-nasal societies had filed complaints, saying the labelling and diagrams were distinctly prejudiced in favor of the "oralistics", citing that McB's was discriminating against those who inhaled food through their noses and down their throats. The complaint was still awaiting a preliminary hearing...

Dixie leaned back in her chair, shut her eyes, rubbed her temples. She had a new respect for Alamexis, who not only did this for a living, but apparently liked it. She opened her eyes again, glanced at the clock, did a double-take. Oops! The Rome Zoo Board of Trustees had a meeting in about half an hour, and she was doing a presentation. Time to go.

She hastily messaged Alamexis, requesting a meeting later in the week, to go over all this warranty stuff.

Chance Find
Hookslaw sat at the terminal in his workshop. Staring at the slowly scrolling list of contacts in his address book. The hunt of the RUR Type six parts was not going very well. He’d put the word out to his usual component suppliers and also to a couple of Fixers who he had used in the past. Nothing second hand so far, but at least he had all the info on how to get OEM stuff if he ever had call to fix a Type six.

“Enough” he thought, and with the wave of a hand the terminal shut down. He stood and grabbed his coat as he made for the door. He needed air.

Outside his workshop the unique odour of the Omni 1 Entertainment sewers filled his nostrils, he took a few deep breaths and looked around. The sun was just going down, and there was a small flea market forming at the other end of the complex by the looks of the glowing torch fires in the distance. “Worth a look I suppose” he thought as he set of to wander down.

The Neut kid selling the junk was a stim head, he stood beside the make shift stall, but never quite stopped moving, in between sniffs he twitched and coughed. His Lifter girlfriend was crouching against the wall behind him, asleep or unconscious.

“ Hey Man, what do you need? I got a lot of good stuff “said the kid grinning.
Hookslaw picked up a shattered NCU belt from the neatly laid out junk on the ground in front of him.“ I’m looking for robot parts, NCU’s and CPU’s…... Is this all you have? ” He replied, nodding at an android NCU module on the ground, as he began to idly fiddle with the broken belt.
“ That’s a blue 32, and a fast one, it’s a bargain at 25k, I got someone else interested in it too, so better take it now if you want it” said the kid, as he rushed round to pick the unit up.
“Yeah, I’ll take it, but I was looking for a yellow 64, anything will do, it doesn’t even matter if its working” said Hookslaw, as he produced a screwdriver from his pocket and continued working on the belt. The kid paused and pointed at him,
“ I got a melted yellow 64 and matching CPU, but they are junk man, I was going to strip them for the nano sensors, still got programs in them, wont take a reset... I could let you have them for 50k”
Hookslaw snapped the cover back onto the NCU belt and hit the power button, the belt hummed into life.
“ I think you would be getting a good deal on the working 32 and the broken 64 and CPU for 30k. “, he said handing the repaired belt back to the kid with a grin,
“Take it or leave it, you wont get 5k for the sensors you can strip from them”.
The kid took a moment to check the NCU belt, before offering his hand to Hookslaw. “ You got a deal, man, ” he said.

Walking away from the stall, Hookslaw removed the yellow 64 NCU unit from his pocket and looked at it for the first time. Turning it over in his hand he found the data plate and waved his mech ID scanner over it.

“ RUR-00013-64-A-NCU-PHEWY-VI ”. Read the screen.

Smiling, he slipped it back into his pocket and made the call.

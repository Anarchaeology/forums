With all due respect to the esteemed Doctor in the corner, DNA is available in blood samples, provided an adequate DNA yield can be effectively sorted from the the aggregate sample of blood (which is inherently high in protein - hence the confounding nature of the process).

DNA is present only in white blood cells (which account for approximately 2% of any given raw sample of blood). The reason not all blood cells contain DNA is quite simple - red blood cells, the largest and most plentiful, have no nucleus.

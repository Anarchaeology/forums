Anyhow, I think that's what Zero was alluding to. It certainly is doable within the framework of game mechanics and would actually be something to get people excited rather than elicit the muted apathy they receive for the rocks and trees.

I think the rocks and trees are actually kinda 'Agenty' and anything that differentiates the playstyle of one profession from the other is a welcome addition. However, I still can't shake the image of a bunch of Agents hearing this news and twirling one finger in the air with an exclamation of 'big whoop'.

Finally, I gotta say that I'm kinda against making almost any item profession-specific. I know that game designers do this because it makes 'balancing' easier on them and provides the opportunity to 'throw a bone' to specific professions. But, honestly, if the character has adequate Electrical Engineering (to a degree), Perception (to be able to see through the 'duck-blind') and Concealment (for argument's sake) why does he also have to be an Agent to use this item.

The purpose of the skill system was to allow characters to be able to be customized. It's pretty much been a failure after several adjustments by FunCom. The limiting factor was supposed to be the cost in terms of IP for any particular skill by any particular profession. An item such as the Hologram Project should never have been targetted at 'Agents.' (note the period). It should have been targetted at characters who specialize in Concealment and Perception. This would of course drop it squarely in the Agent's lap as a no-brainer. The Fixer or Adventurer might find some limited use for it as well.

The problem is that FunCom has either no balls or no skill. Not sure what it is. They have no balls if they continue to make 'proffession'-specific items because they are so terribly afraid of the effect that having a lvl 185 Enforcer in that Rock who spent the IP and raised the skills to use it (How's that for getting people to come and melee you up close and not be able to be rooted?). See the backlash from Fixers when they see some character who raised dark blue B&E open chests in a qvl200 mission.

I say they have no skill if they make it profession-specific because they are unable to balance the value of this item to anything other than a ranged profession that probably has low body development. That's a sign of less than top-notch game designers. (Example: the lower the qlvl of the projector, the smaller the item, hence an Atrox will appear sticking out from all sides of it unless he gets real good in the appropriate skills). The easy way out is to simply continue the degradation of the value of the skill system that marches on with each patch.

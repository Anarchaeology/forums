My Perspective
Clear your mind and imagine you are a writer. You slave for hours and hours day after day to compose a majestic tale of overcoming various challenges to obtain a victory that few others are capable of accomplishing. Your efforts are dear to you, with every page the product of your valuable time; every sentence a carefully prepared flow of words representing your skill and style. You approach the end of your story and feel the rising excitement of the pending completion.

Then as you finish, someone walks in and throws your work into the fire.


Now imagine you are a software developer. You slave for hours and hours day after day to develop a software library. In this library you have found the ideal balance between functionality and performance. Each block of code a carefully prepared function representing your skill and style. You approach the end of your development process and feel the rising excitement of the pending completion.

Then as you finish, someone walks in and deletes all of your work.


Pretend you are an artist. You slave for hours and hours day after day expressing your creative vision upon the canvas. The painting comes to life with each cautious and deliberate touch of the brush. It represents a momentary genious that you have never experienced and may never experience again. You feel the rising excitement of the pending completion as you place the final touches on your creation.

Then as you finish, someone walks in and destroys your work.


Yes, the process of creation is enjoyable on many levels. But to lose the sense of accomplishment flushes all enjoyment out of every aspect. This tragedy manifests itself in anger and frustration; and hate.

Spending hours and hours day after day to find and assemble a boss pattern (or several) only to have the mechanics of the game take away the reward.

Spending hours and hours day after day to work your way through a quest only to have the mechanics of the game block you from completion.

Spending half an hour getting to somewhere in SL only to fall or die due to buggy game mechanics and having to do it again; and again; and again.

Spending hours and hours getting a raid together on Hollow Island, progressing through the tiers, and killing the weed only to have the mechanics of the game reward one single participant out of a hundred.

et cetera.


I've demonstrated many times that I am not interested in walking the easy path in AO. I've done every part of every quest I have completed, from tagging silvertails in Nascence to trading in mirrors to the imps to spawn Tchu. I am not looking for a carebeared game. I am not looking for freebies. All I want is for my time investment in AO to have a return. I cannot enjoy the process if I cannot enjoy the completion, whether it is for myself or for others. And if I can't enjoy the process, this game is an utter waste of my time and money.


If a person takes the right actions, he or she should receive the appropriate compensation.

Stop adding content that hasn't been well though out and finished, including a thorough evaluation of the ratio of time and effort to reward.

Communicate with us so that we don't spend our valuable time working toward a goal that is predestined for failure.

Reduce or remove the luck factor that permeates the game. Luck punishes the explorers and rewards the lemmings.

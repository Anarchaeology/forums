My Idea to Solve Gameplay Balance in PVP, and PVE
I have been thinking of various ways that both PVP and PVE could be addressed so that both the normal everyday player who is properly equipped and the players who choose to equip very high weapons and armor etc. could still be able to coexist without a lot of programming nightmares, and obvious upsets to both player types.

It seems both player types are up in arms regarding eachother. The regularly equipped players are terrified of PVP due to the highly equipped players who claim they need to be that way in order to survive in the PVE environment. I am still curious why these highly (or over) equipped players always seem to have a PVP title in front of their name, are seen frequenting 25% and Mayhem zones, and have a nasty reputation of "people to avoid in PVP."

But aside from that, there are a few good ideas on the table now. I do like the idea of weapons and other items "adjusting" to the skill level of the player, which will balance the power or defenses of characters, but it does leave a couple of things to be desired. The biggest problem will be that those people who have already attained their PVP titles could then sit back and relax because they already have their weapons, etc. for the next several levels, while the others must still go on shopping every few levels, or now must spend a lot of money (as the demand for high QL items will go up) to equip their characters. Secondly should it really be fair for those who already have items 50-100 lvls above them to be able to just up their skills each level and just automatically become stronger? Hint: If someone buys items every 5-10 levels like normal, and another person just buys one really high item, which person will spend more overall?

My solution is very simple, it does not incorporate any of the current ideas I've seen. Instead of changing items, and skills and letting people reroll their IPs, when they sould just start over if they really screwed up, and throwing the whole economy out of balance, hurting the regular players, you just need to change the lvl rating system itself.

(EXAMPLE)
Currently I am level 54. If I "consider" another lvl 54 she/he is yellow. Can we both get the same level PVE missions? Yup. Can we PVP eachother? Yup. Could one of us have a QL120 weapon with a full set of around QL130 armor, while the other has a QL66 weapon with QL60 armor? Yup. Well then I ask you, why are we compared by character level? Really it's a duplication to have your character level, AND put a level on everything else in the game. Which is more accurate? Obviously the item levels are far more accurate to actually stats. If I am lvl 54 and naked, you are gonna kick my butt. If I am higher equipped than you, I will most likely win.

(SOLUTION)
The mechanics are already there. The armor you wear, the weapon you wield and the nano programs and pets you use, all have quality levels assigned. Lets keep our character levels as guides for us to track our character's progress, and to attain new title levels within our class, but NOT for matching up opponents, as it is totally inaccurate. A simple formula which considers your weapons, armor, etc. and assigns a "power" value would be right on the money.

Back to my example, if this were in place, the highly equipped player would con red to me (his power level could be around say 125, whereas mine would be around 64), and therefore we couldn't fight. However a normally equipped say level 120 character could come along and fight the level 54. This would force people to engage only in fair fights, versus being able to squeeze into the highest possible weapons and armor and go kill everyone you see in your range. It would also force those who "over" equip to make sure they balance their entire character, to make up for HPs and other areas they neglect when when using big guns and armor as they do now. It will also let those who feel they need all that extra QL to get through PVE.

After all you are fighting equipment v. equipment not level v. level. Players would win or lose based on their real life strategy and the tactical skills they employ during their confrontations.

I welcome any comments, suggestions or ideas. I do think this would be the most fair way yet to please both play styles, and still allow the extremely important PVP aspect of the game.

Thanks!

The problem is not that some players over-equip. The problem is that not all professions have an equal ability to over-equip. So you get the situation we have now where an agent could aim shot an NT to death in a few seconds, but there is nothing that the NT could do NO MATTER HOW MUCH TIME they spent to equalize the situation. Then you get the added problem that Funcom tries to balance the game so players dont get to level 200 in a couple of months, and all hard to over-equip professions suffer even if they dont PvP. They suffer because the mobs they have to face are geared to all the over-equippers.

Professions need to be balanced against one another. That is the fundamental problem with this game. Until it is solved, this game will bleed a slow death.

Another problem with over-equipping is that it gives disproportionate returns. Once you have improved your equipment to the point that you can kill another class in a matter of seconds it becomes an absolute power. It doesnt matter if the other class has great healing or escape abilities because once you are dead none of that matters. You get fights that are decided not by who uses their classs abilities the best, but by who has the best equipment and shoots first.

Over-equipping should be allowed in the game, but under some constraints. First, all professions should have an equal ability to do it. Second, the benefits of over equipping should follow the law of diminishing returns. The character that has spent the time to build up their equipment should be given a bonus to damage or defense in a fight, not guaranteed victory.

In the end, fights should be mostly decided by the skill of the player not by the one who could stomach the tedium of getting the best gear. Most people who over-equip would admit that even though the end results are very satisfying, the process involved to do it is not very fun. Its just like going to work. I like getting a paycheck, but if I had a choice, Id rather win the lottery. Games are supposed to be fun, not work, and any game mechanics that would require, or even encourage doing anything not fun should be removed or mitigated.

Yes Yazule, basically this is an example of what i'm saying.

example:

A level 45 soldier who's Assault Rifle (AR) skill is at 328 wants to equip a QL100 Flamethower which is %100 AR AttSkill. At the moment, because the Flamer is 100% AR, his Attack skill is exactly equal to his AR Skill level so it is at 328 as well.

The Soldier has full QL70 Implants. He gets AR Expertise for +20, AR Mastery for +60.... now the soldier has an AR Skill lvl of 408, his current temporary attack lvl is currently at 408 as well.

Since the QL 100 Flamethrower has an AR Req of 505 (for example) he finds a trader and gets Sophisticated Wrangle for +109 which puts him well over the req and gives him 517 AR, and once again his Attack Skill is temporarly 517.

The soldier equips his new weopon, his buffs wear off and now his attack level is back at the original base 328.

My idea is that if the effectivness of the QL 100 weopon is dropped down to the stats of a QL45 Flamethrower because the soldier is lvl 45, then a bonus, equal to a given percentage or programming value of the difference between the base AR skill (328) to the weopon requirement AR skill (505), should be applied to the Attack level as reward and incentive of equiping a better than average weopon.

In this case, as an example, lets say that the programmed value or percentage worked out to 1 point of Attack Skill Per every 2 points that the QL of the weopon Req exceded the players base AR skill Level. So now the soldier is carrying a QL100 Flamethrower that is effectively a QL45 when it comes to it's damage stats, but his Attack Rating bonus for equiping a QL 100 weopon (approx 83) gives him an attack rating of 411 (unbuffed) instead of his base 328.

This would:

a. Let all the Carebears breath a bit easier. There would be greater balance, but not total balance. If it was all equal I could play Duke Nukem/Doom/Quake/Wolfenstien.... pick any FPS, for free - it would be the same thing.
b. Let the over-achievers (myself included) equip to thier hearts desire. The effectiveness of the weopon would be lowered to current player lvl, but with the attack level bonus you would hit for more crits and higher end damage, that would be the incentive for over-equiping.

The same rules would apply to armor and all other equipment as well.

At the same time, if your going to lower the stats of an over-equiper than you have to do the same caluclations for an under-equiper. If a lvl 45 soldier is using a QL 25 Flamethrower, his base AR stat is 328 and the required weopon stat is 202 then he would be assigned a penalty (using the same calculation would be -63) for equiping a weak weopon so that his attack level would be 165 instead of his normal base of 328.

This way, say these two different soldiers who are both level 45 meet in an arena, The both will have effectively QL 45 Flamethrowers.... but one will have a 411 Attack Level and the other would have an 165 Attack Level.... Like wise if the later soldier (underequiped) was standard equiped with a QL 45 Flamethrower his Attack Level would be the same as his original base of 328.

Now... If both are equal in skill and technique then the guy with the higher attack level is obviously going to win. Which is how it should be. But if the guy with the higher attack is a retard and the guy with normally average equiped weopon is intelligent, the lesser equiped guy could still win.

I know I rambled on here, I hope I didn't lose anyone... I might have lost myself somewhere along the line. These are just basic concepts, let me know what you think.

George the Reet: A Comic Hunt
I have faced many challenges in my day, but perhaps none so great as the victory I achieved today. Some time ago, I was resting in the Cup and enjoying my Sake by myself. I could not help but overhear the couple sitting at the next table discussing their recent hunting exploits. One, a young nurse, mentioned an ancient nemesis of doctors all around Rubi-Ka. She proceeded to tell her enraptured audience, an equally young adventurer, and, unbeknownst to her, an old Atrox doc called the Olympian, me.

The legend of it goes that back when the first colony ships were coming to Rubi-Ka, one of the dropships was coming down full of medical supplies and a single passenger, a surgeon from Earth by the name of George McCoy. This was the last ship to come down to the colony site but on its way down something happened. No one is sure what happened, exactly, but they think it was pure mechanical failure. The autopilot crapped out or something.

I heard another story though. This doc, George, was one of the personal physicians to some Omni big-wig back on Earth. He used to do yearly skin jobs for both the exec and his wife. A touch up here, a tuck there, you know. These are the luxuries we left behind by coming here. Anyhow, he was running a standard augmentation scheme on the execs wife and apparently left the machine running while he went off to somewhere with one of the nurses. Now, these things were automated so this wasnt a problem, usually.

Somebody picked that day to hack into the Terran OT MedCenter and changed the settings for the augmentation scheme. Word is, the lady came out bigger and dumber than an Atrox. So naturally, the bigwig gets the whole story and flips out. The doc, meanwhile, boards a colony ship in lieu of facing an OT tribunal. Apparently the bigwig had one last trick up his sleeve, though.

Doc Georges dropship went down in flames far from his destination. Now, no ones sure what happened next, but years went by. No one really gave the old crash a second thought. But, when they were scouting out the territory and marking the site for building Tir, the team was attacked by a mad reet that came charging out of the desert. No one had seen anything like it. Reets were peaceful creatures and none fought so viciously as this strange bird. After the initial surprise, the team rallied and shot it down in a matter of moments. It was just a reet after all.

When they examined it further, they saw the bird was carrying things. It was carrying an old Medical Epaulet, devices from back on Earth that let docs analyze their patients, and an old pair of dogtags. The dogtags were so beaten and charred that the only word they could make out was George.

True story, I swear. She said and took a swig of her beer. Weird thing is, that bird keeps coming back. Damn thing attacks everything in sight. Still, that old epaulet thing sure has come in handy.

Her companion looked on, shocked. You mean youve actually seen it? And fought it?

Damn straight. First time I heard about him had to go see for myself. Found him straight away out by where the new borg outpost is. She leaned back and took another swig.
Needless to say, I was entranced. It didnt sound that bad and she didnt look that tough. I grabbed my hammer and headed for the door to go hunting for a reet named George.

I soon came to rue that hasty decision. I found myself out in the desert west of Tir surrounded by enemies and encamped with a pair of guards in a small outpost. We were under fire and I am ashamed to admit I relied on those guards to save me from the marauding scorpiods and spiders. After a few trips to the reclaim terminal there, we fought them off, and I bid my guard friends farewell and took off through the desert. I managed to slip between a pair of scorpiods into the safety of some woods at the edge of the dune.

I paused there to reassess my position and my gear before striking out east, in pursuit of the legendary George. I soon found a rocky encampment, crawling with OT assassins. I didnt know what I had stumbled upon but I skirted around it, trying not to attract too much attention. I became involved in a skirmish with some minor patrols, but they must have radioed for help and I soon found myself outnumbered and outgunned. Remembering the old adage about the better part of valor I turned tail and ran.
I ran like mad without sense for where I was going or how far I ran. Soon, despite my size and my clumsiness, I lost them amongst the dunes. I paused to patch myself up and catch my breath. As I scanned the area, I saw him. He was perched atop a dune and preening himself idly.

I set my sights on him and approached, slowly and cautiously. Finally in range and still unnoticed, I hit the bird with a rapid succession of nanoprograms, weakening him and poisoning him all at once. This battle should have been over in minutes. He squawked loudly and charged at my head. Momentarily stunned in the onslaught of beating wings, I fought him off with my fists and blows of my hammer. Luck was not with me that day, as a patrol of OT soldiers came charging over the dune, alerted by Georges cries.

Moments later, I was reeling on the reclaim pad outside Tirs gates. Furious, I tightened my grip on my hammer and set off at once to put an end to George once and for all. Three more times I found the bird, only to be beset by an ambush of OT patrols. Three more times I left that reclaim terminal to exact my vengeance on George the reet.

Days had gone by before I found the bird again, hiding like a coward in the dunes. I had done thorough patrols of the area and there was a single omni soldier in sight. I had searched far and wide. I was equipped, ready, buffed, and livid. I would not be shamed again by this meanest of prey. I would unleash my wrath on the bird. I would have my victory.

The bird sighted me as I crept over the dune and unleashed his assault again. He fell that day to three blows from my hammer. I looked down on his crushed body and yelled for joy. I waved my hammer in the air like a fool and danced like a madman. I picked up the birds body and held it aloft for all to see my victory.
Much to my chagrin, the only ones who noticed were a pair of OT assassins who decided they wanted my ass for lunch. My victory dance cut short, I grabbed the epaulet and ran for it.

I have faced George the reet and have lived to tell the tale. I bear the scars from his talons with pride. My shoulder carries the badge of my victory, an ancient relic from Earth. One step at a time we move against our oppressors, whether they be reet or omni all shall fall before the tides of change.

Money...
It seems to me that this game requires one's character to have a ridiculous amount of liquid assets (i.e. Credits). As I read through some of the in-game help files, I was stunned when I read that a character should have both a weapon and various pieces of armor of a quality rating approx. equal to her level. Are they kidding? I have yet to see a decent weapon below 25,000 Credits (likewise for armor). Granted one can set the mission reward bar all the way toward cash, but the yield is still not even close to sufficient. Am I missing something? All comments and suggestions are appreciated.

Micro-Economics on Rubi-Ka
Yeah. Let me repeat, amplify, and/or attempt to clarify some of the above.

  • Armor at your own level is available as mission rewards. Stop paying for it. Mission booth camp for it.
  • Weapons at your own level are available as mission rewards. If all you need is a weapon at your own level, stop paying for it. Mission booth camp for it.
  • Software at up to about 4 levels above your level is available as a mission reward. Stop paying for it. Mission booth camp for it.
  • Where ever it is that the villains are shopping for their loot these days, that place obviously had a sale on first aid supplies lately. Pay for these, but only if you absolutely can't find enough in mission loot.


Things you absolutely must pay for with cash, or get from higher level members of your guild:

  • Anything you need at well above your level. For soldiers, enforcers, traders, agents, and fixers, that's your weapon. For puppeteers and other nanocasters, that's your software. Eventually you'll want armor way above your level, too, but by then you'll be running missions in teams for it, or getting it from your guild. Or you'll do without.
  • NCU belts and NCU memory, because you pretty much need a complete set at twice your level, and there's no way you can get that as a mission reward.
  • Most tools, because they're not offered as loot, like Advanced Hacker Tools, Bio-Cominutors, and so on. Fortunately, you don't need to buy or upgrade them very often.
  • Ammunition. Which is mostly cheap, and about to get 75% to 90% cheaper.
  • First aid kits when you're running low.


Ways to make money, even as a low level character:

  • As soon as you get to level 6, it starts offering you missions in other towns. Travel the whompa network and try various mission terminals. There are some very profitable runs where you get paid good mileage bonus just to take one whompa jump, like Rome to Omni-Ent or Avalon to Athen.
  • Keep track of what items are selling for big bucks at a QL you can run missions for. Mission booth camp for those items, and sell them on the for sale channels. Nobody wants to tell you what those items are, though, because if supply rises too fast, the bottom will drop out of the market. But here are a few that any character below level 10 should know about, because not enough starting characters know this and demand is nearly inexhaustible: Creviced Concrete Cushions, Tear-Stained Pillows of Important Stripes, and Second-Hand Old English Trading Co. pistols, which are all worth more money at low QLs than at higher ones. (Especially those first two.)


If all else fails, and you have fallen so far behind that you can't complete a 50% difficulty mission, run lower-difficulty missions repeatedly for the cash. Then buy the items you need to get back up to speed. Expect this to take some time.

Finally, if you have the people skills to talk your way into a large guild with lots of people in it from your profession, life becomes a much easier place. People with higher level equipment who no longer need it might as well give it to a guild mate, because the sales terminals pay pennies on the dollar to recycle it. Your average soldier guild has entire bags of E-Beamers and Nova Flows to hand down; same for trader guilds and Vektors, fixer guilds and MCSs, enforcer guilds and hammers, and so on. And trust me, free is the price you can't beat.

As with all games, if other people don't find it difficult but you do, they know something you don't. In an ongoing massively multi-player game like this, it may also be because they are more popular than you and have more friends, more helpful friends, and richer friends than you do. Ain't that just like life?

Here are my suggestions:

1. Most of the time, armor you find on missions will be sufficient. I don't think I've ever payed for armor and I have ql38-42 stuff. (I'm level 33, I believe)
2. Weapons can usually be found as mission loot.
3. I see most team missioners ignore chests with treatment and nano kits in them. In my opinion, this is a waste of money. Usually the kits you find on missions are actually fairly nice, and it's always good to have backup kits.
4. As Brad says below (GREAT guide, by teh way), you will need to buy NCU belts and memory, because they are invaluable.
5. You need to buy ammo, but I wouldn't hesitate picking any you find in missions up.
6. Do team missions with people a few levels higher than you. Not only does it give great experience, you get lots of money.
7. Don't buy useless items. If you're not sure whether something is useful or not, you can
a. shift-leftclick it to see if it looks helpful
b. Ask someone higher-level than you
8. Oh- lastly, sell anything you can't use. If you use blades, you can get some nice money off higher-ql guns.

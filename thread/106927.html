Race/Breed/Species-variant differentiation
This topic deserves its own thread. Within the discussions of BORPAC, there have been charges of the NLF being anti. Certain people have expressed a superiority, and that has incensed some caustic debate.

I'd like to put out this perspective:

If you read Rubi-Ka timelines, its clear that of all races/species-variants, the Solitus was the least adapted to living and mining on Rubi-Ka. They therefore, created different species better adapted to living on Rubi-Ka. Whether you consider that superior is a matter of interpretation. The fact is, some are better adapted. But I believe that this argument is a real diversion from the real conversation that needs to be had:

Omni-Tek created the atrox, opifex, and nanomage as service (or slave) race/species-variant. As a nanomage who once worked in the Omni labs as a measly peon, I had no will but the will of the Corporation. And that's a fundamental violation of all human-based species. Unless this inequity is addressed, and until all regular, non-combat employees of Omni-Tek have a real choice, can make an informed decision that will not jeopardize their livelyhood, my opposition to Omni-Tek stands.

I'm loyal to the clans ideologically. But I withhold my membership because of the unstable nature of clan politics.

I think we are getting mixed up when talking about patents and such. Omni-Tek owns ( or owned, how long are patents good for here?!?!?!!? ) the patent for many species. They have or had the sole right to use them. The products of this however are not neccesarily property of Omni-Tek. Like a pharmacuetical company that creates a drug for a consumer. The company divests it's interest in the drug after it is manufactured and sold. It now has a new owner, the reseller or consumer. Now the question of leets and bacteria. You could argue that Omni-Tek did own the first generation, but without showing due interest in it's investment and allowing it freedom to live and procreate for more than a few years clearly shows abandonment under the law. No one has the right to deprive any other citizens of this environment now. It has become the public domain. Now in the case of Omni-Tek they can chose not to help maintain the environment but they are responsible not to overtly destroy any of its creations at this point.

Then the question of Sentient beings comes into it. Clearly law states that by simply genetically altering a being doesn't give you any kind of ownership of it. All of the breeds on Rubi-Ka today originate from the same genetic line that started on earth millions of years ago. Each breed started from genetic material taken from a cell of a being. None were wholey created. So Omni-Tek may hold a patent on the process of their genetic engineering, but not the products.

I find this question odd...

Animal ownership has been common since the dawn of man. Humans have bred and owned animals as far back as history goes.

If you genetically design a new species in the lab...or even a variant of an existing species...you might 'own' the rights to that DNA sequence but you don't necessarily own all individual organisms of that species. If you sell me a breeding pair you might, in that sales contract, negotiate a royalty for all resulting individuals but you don't 'own' them...I can do with them as I wish within the confines of the contract I made with you.

As for sentient species in a non-slavery society you simly can't own any individual even though you might own the rights to the DNA sequence. In other words OT may own the DNA sequences of my wife but they don't own her any more than if I had artificial insemination I would own the resulting offspring. I might own the sperm I had perchased but I certainly wouldn't own my children.

As to the moral and ethical rights to own sentient life...I think it goes about like this: You can TRY to 'own' them and maybe suceed. On the other hand you may yourselves get 'owned'...and would have little room for complaint at that result. Morals and ethics are relative. Does might make right? Perhaps not...who is to say but those that have the might? Might certainly makes reality though. And eventually a large mass of downtrodden sentient beings finds that there is nothing to lose in gaining that might...a foe with nothing to lose is the most dangerous kind. And I might add here that NLF feels like that foe with nothing to lose as they 'see' their lifeblood notum streaming off-planet in ever increasing quantities.

I think this entire arguement is silly...

As for the questionn of 'superiority' I consider that just as silly.

Are nanomages 'superior' as a species capable of living on Rubika? No...their survivability is no greater than any of the rest of us as long as our weather control system is operable.

Inately, they have no better survivability than any of us. Without technology their tie to the notum of Rubika offers them no additional survival benefits. Even with technology they are not afforded additional survival benefits since the same technologies can be used by non-nanomages to 'level the playing field'.

Now...in CERTAIN off-planet environments nanomages have a VERY significant survival disadvantage. Could technology be used to negate that? Yes...but at a rather steep expense I'd say. In other off-planet environments, such as space ships and envronmentally controlled areas, notum could be made as much a part of the atmosphere as oxygen. Do nanomages have one additional requirement for survival off of Rubika? Yes, of course, but it is not an insurmountable problem as long as the supply of notum is maintened to them wherever they are.

Sorry but once again this 'superiority' thing is a silly and meaningless arguement.

Do nanomages have the right to secure and maintain their only known source of notum? Yes...in the same way that if OT decided to pack up their weather control system and leave Rubika leaving many of us to perish in an environment they brought us to. We would have the right to see to our survival at any cost; ie. demand, and if necessary secure, the technology used to support the biosphere of Rubika.

Unfortunately NLF has come to the conclusion that their very survival is in jeopardy. Honestly I find the arguements for the "infinite" supply of notum ludicrous and and bereft of understanding of basic physics. It is easy for the greedy to turn a blind eye towards reason. It is this greed driven ignorance that the NLF feels forced to fight with violence.

As to the environmental claims of DW I am a bit perplexed by the lack of understanding. There is NO self-sustaining balanced ecology to protect on Rubika. There is NO indication that the mining efforts unbalance the artificial 'ecology' of Rubika...other than as a threat to the long-term survivability of the nanomage breed. There is NO evolution on Rubika that is not continually being swamped by the artificial introduction of species or the hunting to near extinction of existing species. Furthermore the introduction of 100% gas around mining bases in areas that where previously 75% has served to PROTECT species in these areas.

So while I find NLF motives based on a potentially real problem I find DWs involvement less than altruistic and their rush to violence over their issues disturbing.

Ok, let me tell you how I would fix Camelot:

1. Remove the PvP zone, it's just annoying and doesn't serve a purpose except to say "this dungeon is made for camping". It's there to let one team kill other teams trying to make it through - it might be fun if PvP was balanced and there were no mobs in the dungeon except a few bosses but as it is now...no.

2. Adjust the mobs in the dungeon. Make the dungeon itself hard for a normal team, based on the minimum level req to enter. Not just normal mobs with 10x HP, show some imagination here. Traps, labyrinths, buttons that are placed so that the team would have to split up and meet later on etc. Not just a big hallway with a bunch of mobs, which is frankly a bit pathetic for a dungeon.

What kind of defenses would there be if Camelot was used by real people? Would they keep Tarasque unguarded in a big room with lots of smaller mobs around him? Why are there a bunch of skeletons down there? More information needed.

3. Tarasque should be a lot smaller and easier to kill. What it drops would of course be less uber than now, but why do you feel the need to restrict these items to a few guilds? Let the items be nice (not uber), good looking etc. Let everyone who's higher than a certain level (min req to enter dungeon) get a chance to kill it if they bring a good team (and not their entire guild - 10-15 people maybe, preferably less) and get whatever it drops. Also let the players find out more about the dungeon and it's inhabitants (lore) throughout the quest. Tarasque should be on a 3-5 minute timer. Long enough to get ready, but you won't get bored.

4. Don't let people log back in inside the dungeon. If you log out and back in you'll be placed at the entrance. If you want to camp, you have to run through everything and actually stay online.

Everything in the dungeon should be there for a purpose. A huge dragon hidden in a tiny dungeon doesn't exactly sound logical, and why - assuming this is high-end content - did you make something that just screams camp me in big, bold letters? A short dungeon, a big room, phat loot, and a looooong timer. Bleh.

As for restricting loot access - WHY? Don't make quest/camp drops so darn good, or do you want 1000+ member guilds to rule the game? Why can't I walk up to someone using say Tarasque's sword and say "niiiice" and not feel like a total gimp with the "normal" sword I found last night?

Quests shouldn't be rated on their rewards. Getting nice items is part of the fun yes, but how about making the experience fun as well?

AO version now:
"I camped X mob 40 hours this weekend and finally got my X item"

Future?
"My group got split up at the jump and we had to fight the dungeon keeper with fewer people than we thought, but luckily we had a few bots for shields so we survived. Then we went the wrong way and hit the poison trap along with a few skeletons, I managed to keep myself healed until the doc could get to us. Holding the end room with just 7 people (1 doc) was hard with the fast spawn, but we only had one death and he got his X item before he died. I can't wait to go back and try the other way in a week." [some traps, not too hard bosses, fast spawn, 1 week timer for getting another X item (reward)]

Well, all that could be said about how we hate camping has been said it looks like...

All I'm going to say is this:

FC, take a clue from Asheron's Call. You don't need to make quests an impossible camp fest and frustrating expierence to make the loot worthwhile, valuable, or a status symbol.

You can remedy your quest situation by taking a few simple steps:

1. All unique mobs drop their loot every time.

2. Unique mobs respawn within 5-10 minutes.

3. Each person has a personal quest timer on the items they pick up. They cannot pick up another "sword of uber slaying" until a day, week, or month has passed.

4. Quest loot should never be the "best" and it should never be made to "balance" a class or skill. Quest items should unique and worth your time to get them, but they should never be the absolute best.

The best stuff should drop randomly out in the open. Anything that you know exactly where and when to get should not be that powerful. It just caters to the uber guilds.

These simple rules of design are what AC has followed, and it has worked wonderfully. Everyone gets what they came for, and gets out fast so the next group can enjoy the dungeon as it was meant to be seen.

Dungeons in AC were a great expierence, not something you had to force yourself through in order to obtain that phat uber lewt you can't live without.

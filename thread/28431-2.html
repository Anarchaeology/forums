If one waded into the SWG official forums they would find, to this day, daily posts on why the Jedi Profession should be available to all. Many people can simply not understand the concept that they will not be able to load the game go to the character selection screen and pick Jedi out of a list of professions. The Development Team has stated that only about 1 in 200 players would be able to become a Jedi. The obvious question here is, Why? Why is it that the Jedi Profession is it not like the others? Many other Star Wars computer games have let a person play a Jedi. The entire Jedi Knight/Dark Forces line celebrates the Jedi powers and fighting style as well as Obi-Wan and the upcoming Knights of the old republic. So why cant SWG be like this? The easiest answer is, because Lucasarts said so and they call the shots with the Star Wars extended universe. It is no secret however that the Development team at Sony agreed with this judgement however. Simply put, SWG is a representation of the Star Wars universe. So to is a game like Jedi Knight but in that game, only YOU are playing that instance of that game. In SWG there are more then just yourself in the game you are playing. To keep with what the films have set up, Jedi must be rare. In a single player game this is easy because its just you, even multi-player would cap the number of Jedi to 8 or so. Massively multi-player games however attempt to create a virtual world with a virtual population of interacting players. If you want the world you are making to really be virtual then you must make the Jedi rare to make it feel like Star Wars.

So that covers why but now the question is how? It is certain, there will be Jedi in the game and it is possible to become one. In fact the Development Team listed a whole page of FAQs just on the topic of Jedi. They do want them to be in the game but to make it real they must be rare and the people playing them must play the character like a Jedi is shown in the films. There have been some clues that the Development Team has let slip and some even sound like they contradict themselves. The first thing to understand is some Jedi/Force concepts. Before becoming a Jedi, a character will become Force Sensitive. This means that the character has the ability to feel and work the force. An FS character will not always become a Jedi but a Jedi will always be FS.

An FS character will then need to find a tutor/mentor to teach him Jedi skills and this will be a PC who is currently a Jedi Knight. This also brings up the question of how anyone will become the first Jedi if there are none to teach but most assume that GMs will take this role in the beginning of new servers. After the character has a teacher he can begin to learn Jedi skills just like any other skill tree. This will be done in the form of Jedi Trials. Your teacher will put you through these missions and this is where the randomness of becoming a Jedi will come from. The Jedi teacher will most likely not be able to choose the entire make up of the trial himself as this might invite collusion by players. The student will begin as a Padawan and progress through a whole series of titles and might even become a Jedi Master. This exact process of how the character will progress through these skills is also a mystery. It has been said that the path to becoming a Jedi will NOT be the same for everyone. There will be no duplication in the process as to prevent any sort of guide or walkthrough to be written to show someone how. The other exception to this profession is that a Jedi must follow the Jedi Code or risk loosing his powers. How the Jedi Code will be implemented into a MM computer game is unknown. Logic would assume that either the server or GMs monitors the actions of characters. So even after one completes the long and challenging process of becoming a Jedi, they are continually tested. It will be possible to become, loose the status and then become a Jedi again. Jedi will be powerful, there is no doubt of that, and adherence to this code will ensure that there will be no Jedi doodz ruining the game for everyone else.

The question you are still probably asking however is how then will one take that first step and become Force Sensitive. Most importantly (to many of us) is that it has been stated that it will not be random. At the same time however there will be no exact way to become FS. This may seem like a contradiction but the Development team assures us that this is true. What advice should one have if they wish to pursue the Jedi path? To take a quote from Yoda, do or do not, there is no try. This may seem a bit existential for most but maybe the best advice is to simply follow the Jedi Code as you play. Get into the role-playing of the character and act the part. If you want to be a Jedi, start acting like one. Work on the skills that you see Jedi using in the films even if they dont seem to head toward a particular profession. The Development Team has stated that not all branches and skills will be viewable until you qualify for them. Talk like a Jedi, act like a Jedi, get a normal sword and practice sword fighting (normal melee weapons will be in the game). Also, travel and explore when you can, the universe contains many secrets of the force and you may just find one that can help you. This may come in the form of a Jedi artifact or it might come in the form of a person who can help you find what you seek. It may put you behind other characters, it might make you poor but keep the prize in mind.

Force sensitive is a requirement but this doesn't stop mentors to pick their friends in anyway. Unless of course if the Mentor may not choose his padawan is better,

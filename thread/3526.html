A Modest Proposal: Event Space for Rent
It should be even easier than it is now to schedule in-game events. I've put some thought into this, and I'd like to offer my Modest Proposal. I'd like to make it easier to schedule all kinds of events: lectures, live theater, parties, debates, boxing matches, church services, fashion shows, live comedy, flea markets, recruiting expos, political rallies, and more.

And here's how I'd do it. Note that I have broken this down into phases of increasing complexity. It should be possible to implement this as increasing functionality, over a series of patches.

Executive Overview

The basic idea is to let players create their own special mission keys. Said keys will operate only during a specific range of times. If someone holding that key approaches that door during that time, they get into that event. I suggest a simple user interface for creating such keys. Everything else derives from that.

Phase One: Room Reservation Objects

Create a series of new items to be sold in the trade skills booths. Each one will be called "Reserve a *name of map goes here*." A character can buy one and take it to any locked mission entrance in the game. Once there, they pick it up, and right-click it on the door. It pops up a dialog box that asks the following questions: What date? What time ( UTC), and how long to let people in (up to 3 hours)? What event name? What description? It will then make a mission key with those attributes and drop it into inventory.

This will require that a "starting time" field be added to mission keys as well as an ending time, if it doesn't already exist. Oh yeah, and it should definitely be created with no objective, so the mission can't be "completed" before the doors close. Similarly, there should be no mobs. The default indoor suppression gas level, 75%, is fine for now.

The coordinates should, as with all mission keys, be the coordinates of the door. That way people can get directions to the event with a right-click to upload coordinates to their map reader.

The art department has already created some lovely rooms, suites, and maps that could be offered. In particular, the "classroom" map would be nice; you could even offer the two different versions in the Omni vs Clan terminals. The "apartment" and "lunch room" maps from the Omni missions would also be nice for "party suite" and "banquet room".

Phase Two: More Easy Rental Facilities

Auditorium: Three rooms. Small entry room. Large room with sloped floor, bench seats, facing a raised platform. Small room behind it; access to the stage should be from that room. Add restrooms off the entry room if you want.

Unused Warehouse: Large, dimly lit, empty room. Bare walls, exposed ceiling beams. Probably one restroom, maybe even as dingy as the men's room at Reet's Retreat.

Convention Center: Smaller version of the auditorium set, plus two of the existing classroom rooms, plus an exhibition hall. That latter is just a big room, better lit than the warehouse space above. Divide it up into open-fronted cubicles, each with a table and a stool someone can sit at. Optionally, put a Snack Bar (an existing Food Vendor) in the hall.

Note that these would also make perfectly acceptable mission settings, and could be re-used. These are also pretty easy sets; I imagine the art department could knock them out pretty quickly, and this phase could possibly be combined with phase one. Other people may have more ideas for simple sets to add to this thread.

Phase Three: Easier Distribution, Ticket Prices

Note that up to this point, the only way to make this work is to find a volunteer player to stand outside the door, hand-duplicate duplicate keys for people, and endlessly trade for them. Boring. (Although for exclusive enough events, it does let the doorman take bribes to let people in.)

To make it easier to distribute keys:

  1. Make it possible to stack identical keys.
  2. Make shift-right-click on the key duplicator bring up a simple dialog box, asking for how many to make. Alternatively, shift-right-drag left and right can work a slider, like the split stack command does now.
  3. At this point, the person who makes the key can hand out stacks of them to people to distribute.


Now for the incentive: modify the mission keys so that there is an optional field for "admission price." It should be displayed in the info window if there is one. If someone uses that mission key to enter a building, their account should be debited and the money transfered to the person who made the key.

This gives people a financial (well, in-game financial) incentive to come up with entertaining ways to gather groups together. It even becomes a gamble: assuming there's a charge for the room reservation, can you make money or not?

You may want to sanity-check the prices, limiting them to, say, around 5000 credits a head. (I'm guessing that you guys think of one Rubi-Ka credit as being about equal to one yen or one cent?)

Phase Four: More Complicated Sets

To make it possible to control who gets the microphone/stage, make some rooms produce two keys: a regular ticket key, and a backstage pass key. Inside the set, there will be a door that only the backstage pass can open.

Enhanced Auditorium: Modify the auditorium set, above, so that only people with the backstage pass key can get back stage, and therefore to the stage. Turn off sending to vicinity chat in the main auditorium room. Rig the stage so that anything said there is also heard in the main auditorium on vicinity chat.

Warehouse Party: To the warehouse set above, add a high pedestal for a DJ booth. A door in the base of pedestal should teleport people to the top of the pedestal, but only if they have the backstage key. If there's anyone on the DJ booth, then as with the auditorium, vicinity chat should be turned off below (music's too loud). "Mic" the DJ booth so that what gets said up there is heard on vicinity chat on the floor.

Also on the floor, add an ugly, improvised-looking bar with one or more bartenders -- with marked up drinks, with the organizer getting the markup. (May want to do this to the enhanced auditorium, too.)

Add another side room, a "chill space," with the ugly couches from the Tir mission sets, low lighting, a lava lamp or two, and allow vicinity chat in there.

Fashion Show: Large room. Tall, shallow stage at one end, only accessable from "green room." Stage includes a models' catwalk that runs out into the room. "Backstage pass" key lets you into green room, with two models' dressing room sets off of it. Area around the catwalk has benches facing it.

You could really make this a game event generator by taking about half the clothes out of the Miir boutiques. Make them, plus any new clothes the artists create, only available after their every-so-often fashion shows. Set a one-hour window to let people in. Only after people are no longer being let in do you run the fashion show. Only after the fashion show do you instantiate a sales NPC, offering the items just shown. That way, only people who were at the show could have the "exclusives," that and anyone they sold it to. (You could do the same thing with armor sales, maybe even gun sales.) How much would people pay for those tickets? How much money would the resellers make?

Professional Fight: Large room. Bleachers all around. In the middle, another pedestal, a bigger one, with a door in the base like the one from the enhanced auditorium. For this room, vicinity chat should definitely be on, all across. At the top of the pedestal, suppression gas should be ZERO.

Don't enforce any rules up there (too complicated for the software). After all, the organizers can set their own rules and prizes, and appoint judges who'll decide if someone cheated.

Probably wants restrooms (probably modeled after the bus-station restrooms from the subway sets) and at least one food vendor and/or party mixer.

Council of Truth: Looks just like the existing set, which is kind of nice. Can only be reserved by GMs. Works like the enhanced auditorium above. I don't know where you'd put the door/teleporter to the central platform.

Phase Five: Automatic Ticket Booths

Finally, to eliminate the need for that poor schmoe out front handing out keys, create another vendor booth, a ticket booth.

You don't really need a new graphic for the booth; the existing "generic sales booth" will do.

When you right-click a ticket booth, it should offer you tickets for all events currently running and anything starting in, say, the next two hours. Price on each item should be zero, money will be collected at the door. Descriptions should be available via the usual method. You could keep the key icon, or make up a ticket icon; it's all one to me.

Put one of each of these booths in each major city, preferably in a high-traffic area like the banking center in Tir, near the Bronto Burger in Newland, on the platform between the wompas in Omni Entertainment, near the center of Omni Trade, near the gate in western Old Athen, and maybe somewhere in the park in Rome. Alternatively, put one in each of the existing bars.

Phase Six: Event Control

Add a command to the command-line interface, only available to the person who registered the event, and only while they're at it. The "/evict" command makes the target "/terminate" -- that is to say, "kills" them and resurrects them at their last save point, but doesn't give the organizer "credit" for the kill and doesn't let anyone loot the body.

Ideally, once this has happened, the ticket booth should no longer sell them the key, but that may not be necessary. Or maybe do both, and have a second command, "/ban."

They should get a message (maybe everybody should see it), "Event-organizer evicted So-and-so." (With the real handles filled in, of course.) Or else, "So-and-so evicted by Event-organizer."

To make abuse of this feature self-correcting, ticket descriptions should include the handle of the person who registered the event. If someone gets a reputation for abusing their guests, people will stop showing up.

You may also want to put three "pet peeve" checkboxes on the create-an-event dialog box: Allow Weapons, Allow Armor, Allow Swimwear/Lingerie. If someone has an offending item equipped and tries to enter the event, it won't let them, and displays a message telling them what kind of thing(s) to de-equip.

If you implement the pet peeve checkboxes, you may also want to then give the organizer a "/warn" command that checks the target for offending items equipped, and then issues a warning if there are any. That way an organizer can /warn, wait for compliance, then /evict -- without worrying about language barriers.

Note that the pet peeve boxes will, if events are popular enough, go a long way towards reducing twinking.

Phase Seven: Chrome Plating

OK, now I'm going to be completely silly, and give a personal wish-list item to go with the above. I don't know if this one's even plausible.

Backstage at the enhanced auditorium, there should be a Furniture sales terminal. It should include a few extra pieces of furniture, especially a podium or pulpit. The game should allow furniture to be unpacked on the stage. There should also be a "curtain" button that raises and lowers a curtain between the front of the stage and the audience, to allow for scene changes.

People would have to be warned that anything they leave on stage will go away when the event is over, but that's cool.

It would also be even nicer if there were a series of generic backdrops, flats, available, with buttons for them backstage. (Woods, desert, ocean, castle exterior, castle interior, town exterior, apartment interior, maybe one or two more.) Presumably that'd be on the same panel with the curtain control.

Oh, and of course, that same panel should have the lighting switches: stage left, stage center, stage right, and house. If the coders are feeling really generous, make all four on, dim, and off. If they really want to show off, allow gels.

With a podium, you could run religious services. (Well, pagan services would probably be better off in a warehouse set, but you know what I mean.) With two or more podiums, you could run a debate. With the whole shebang and some volunteers, you could do The-a-tuh.

Come on, as much as "Boss" Ross supposedly cares about the arts, you know you want it ...

The Scottish Play, anyone?

More Chrome Plating, and Why Any of This Matters
Take the auditorium or enhanced auditorium sets from my proposal. Add another class of items that can be equipped as right hand: musical instruments. Add emotes to play those instruments. Even if you only start with a few instruments, you've got enough to fake a musical performance. (Add the larger instruments, like a drum set and a piano, to the furniture booth.)

Sure, there's no way for the band to actually play music. What you've got is the equivalent of music in a book: text descriptions, lyrics, and in this case the "musicians'" icons acting out the parts.

Can you say "battle of the bands"? Do you really think Anarchy Online has no customers or potential customers who wouldn't set out to be the most popular band on Rubi-Ka? Has there ever been a revolution in human history that didn't have its own music?

- - - - -

See, here's where I'm coming from: I honestly don't give a rollerrat's patoot whether or not combat is balanced between 150th level characters and above. And honestly, I'd get ungodly bored with the combat, myself, long before I will ever get to 150th, let alone 200th, level.

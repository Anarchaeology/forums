Cops and Robbers - A RolePlay Conundrum
Disclaimer: This is not intended to criticize or judge any techniques of roleplaying. At any point where you could read something as an insult...please read it the other way, as that was it's intention.

I may not be able to make the (in planning stages) RP community meeting due to personal issues with those who may also show up. I will, of course, advertise for it, as it's entire intention is definitely the right way to go.

But I do have some thoughts that I would like to lay on the table. You can discuss them here, or you can save your thoughts for the meeting. Either way, I would like it put on the table if possible, whether or not I am present. (That precedes a please-and-thank-you, of course)

Role-Playing a Cop: I've never roleplayed an Omni-Pol or any policing unit in AO, so I'm merely taking conjecture from what I've read in Meister's posts about how he handles RP in his Omni-Pol. It's my assumption that while the task of defining the law may be difficult, the actual rules behind roleplaying a police officer are relatively simple. It basically falls into two major steps (both able to be broken down into many others, but I'll spare you since I don't know):

1. Learn the Law (the difficult part, learning ranks and structure, and your place in Omni-Pol. This is where the actual hardcore brainpower comes in.)

2. Enforce the Law (the easy part. You are told to enforce the law while remaining inside it's boundaries, to roleplay it as you see fit, and to make sure that it is always the law of Omni-Tek that comes above all else.)

Role-Playing a Robber (or any criminal): I have roleplayed this one, so I can say that I know by experience. The training:

1. Learn the Crimes (the easy part, learning that you do crimes, what they are, and why you do them)

2. Do the Crimes (the hard part, where you must seriously think and plan a structure of how to perform a crime within the standards of a roleplaying environment that really holds no possibility to actually "commit" a crime...you must find ways to correctly and appropriately roleplay out the crime).

Now please analyze that. Notice that the "Cop" must focus more on mechanics, and less on strictness of roleplay, because you really don't need to fall too strict on roleplay to enforce a law that is already pretty much universally binding.

Now notice that the robber really doesn't have to focus on any mechanics, but must roleplay like crazy in order to actually create and administer a crime in an otherwise crimeless world because of the game mechanics. Any crime must be thought out and pre-planned.

This is where the MASSIVE problem takes place between those who want to roleplay the cop, and those who want to roleplay the robber. And that is where serious OOC "pre-role-play" rules should be somehow agreed upon. There must be a way to go OOC to tell the cop that you are going to commit a crime, and do so WITHOUT the cop using that knowledge for IC purposes. Otherwise, every single pre-planned crime has absolutely no counterbalance, and the unenforced roleplay of the cop is pretty much given free reign to destroy a potentially intriguing story of Crime and Punishment before it even starts. Also, the unenforced mechanics give the robber the ability to simply act out things that the cop would consider impossibilities, causing the cop to wonder just why the heck this robber thinks he's actually doing anything at all. And the worst part is that both sides would be justified in their ideas about the situation.

That being said: Pre-Planning a crime is a good idea. Pre-Planning the crime with strictly-OOC information conveyed between the Cop and the Robber is an absolute neccessity.

Otherwise (as has been the case to date), everyone goes home VERY angry, and absolutely convinced that the other side was fully in the wrong.

To add some more, a lot of it does have to do with communication between the two groups involved.

When Don, Valaik and I sent Lehx to reclaim after torturing him and Templus, Maxflier and company burst in on us. we were actually surprised since we didn't expect that to happen. So, they handcuffed us and led us to their station in Trade where we had some more fun until - again, we didn't know this was going to happen ahead of time - some members of Global Underground burst in, killed the Pols and freed us. They had spoken to Templus OOC through /tells and asked her if it was okay do so and she gave the okay.

Lehx was also great since he went along with the whole part of us dragging him out of Rompas and torturing him. He played his part excellently.

Anyway, my first point is that it actually is possible to have some spontaneous rp as long as at least 1 member of each group keeps in contact with the other. It really makes it interesting for those who don't know what's going on as long as the ones doing the arrangements take some lead to keep the others in their group moving in the direction that is agreed upon. For instance, Don was about ready to try and shoot his way out of Baboons when the Pols burst in, but I said to him, IC, that we were outgunned and would die so he "dropped his weapon."

The second point is that each side has to be willing to be the one to take the fall now and then. You can't always be the one who "wins" over the other side. Everyone wants to be the one coming out on top, but one side has to "lose" so the other can "win." In our situation, we "won" with Lehx when we killed him, "lost" with Omni-Pol when they arrested us, then OP "lost" against Global Underground (who "won" by killing the OPs) and we "won" by being rescued. It's give and take.

Maybe we just got lucky with this experience, but I have to say, it was one of the most fun times I had in RP in AO and the spontanaity had a lot to do with it.

quote:
Originally posted by Meister
Actually ENFORCING the law is heavy roleplaying which only very few people know how to do, since if we had an easy time enforcing law, we would use our weapons more often to shut criminals or clanners up, which does not work in AO.
That is the center of the conundrum. If a criminal does not believe that the officer taking against against him or her can escalate to more serious means of enforcement, the entire fear factor that precludes people from committing overt acts against the law is lost.

Think about it differently: if you knew that, if Meister or another member of OP caught you committing a crime, they could imprison you pending trial or indefinitely, and that you didn't have the option of simply walking away or typing "/ignore", you would certainly think twice about kidnapping, say, Templus, and celebrating afterwards at the same booth in Rompa with the head of OP.

This isn't an easy matter to bypass, either. In order for this problem to be mitigated, RPers would have to:

- recognize that OP are the POLICE of RK, and have the right to arrest ANYONE they want at ANYTIME; what precludes them from doing so is the threat of disciplinary action from their superiors (Sergeyevich or the higher ups within the OP org)

- realize that they are not invincible; if you are a level 20 character, wearing no armour and are walking through OE after robbing Rompa bar, and a level 150 OP officer decked out in full gear approaches you and tries to arrest you, you should be scared and should comply; no one likes a butt-whooping, let alone being splattered all over Omni-1. (Note: I know that game mechanics render this point moot, but even still, unless those mechanics can be explained IC they are best dismissed for the sake of operation efficiency)

- laws would have to be agreed to by everyone beforehand; people would have to know that they actually broke the law, what their rights are, etc., which would minimize the amount of OOC communication between parties. If you know you just broke a law, and that you did it in front of everyone at the OTSEC meeting, know that Meister WILL arrest you

If we are to agree to OP's absolute authority, there would also have to be a complaint process within OP (just like internal affairs in RL) that would give someone who thinks they're rights have been violated (i.e., false arrest) a route through which to pursue recourse.

THE BIG BOLD NOTE: THE ABOVE DOES NOT MEAN THAT OOC COMPLAINTS OR PERSONAL HARASSMENT OF MEMBERS OF OP GOES THIS WAY. IT'S ALL ABOUT CONTEXT, AND KEEP IN MIND ITS PURPOSE

Ending now, will post more thoughts later.

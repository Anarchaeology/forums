Taunt - Or Die
TAUNTING

If your team is going to survive in combat, you have two choices. You can stick with light green mobs and hope to pull small numbers of them, and never run Team Missions above 40% difficulty. Or you can use a healer. And the healer does not have time to heal you if they're fighting for their life. What's more, a healer can heal you more often, and for longer, if they themselves are not in combat. Why? Because if they're going to assume responsbility for the whole team, they need to be able to sit down and recharge while the fight is still going on. That's equally true whether they're a trader using Treatment Kits to recharge their Health Haggler nanos, or a Doctor using Nano Chargers to recharge their Healing nanos.

That's why Funcom made it possible to Taunt.

A Taunt is any kind of attack that does only one thing. It convinces the target to switch its attacks onto you. Why would you want to do that? In solo combat, you might not. (But read further.) But if your only chance to stay alive is to keep the Doctor alive, and they're in trouble, and they have fewer hitpoints and less armor than you (all of which are likely to be true, sooner or later, unless you're a Nano Technician), then you need to be the one getting hit, not them.

Now, here's the good news: Taunting is cheap, to the point of being almost free. It involves a trivial outlay in Improvement Points (IP) or cash. And that's in the worst case. For many of you, it's actually free.

CHEAP AND UNIVERSAL TAUNTING

Anyone can use an Aggression Enhancer. Almost everyone should have one.

Aggression Enhancers are often found in chests or on mobs in missions. They don't have to cost you anything, except the trivial few credits you'd get if you sold it to a sales terminal.

They're also fairly cheap. List price ranges from roughly 500 credits (at QL 1) up to 50,000 credits at the theoretical high end of 200. (Not that you'll ever find QL200 gear in a store.) Why ever pay that if you can get ones at your own QL for free, every so often, in a mission? Because that way, you can over-equip them. The skill they use, the Psychology trade skill, is green for Agents, Bureaucrats, Enforcers, and Soldiers.

What's more, Aggression Enhancers don't use that many IP. You can equip one at your own QL by raising Psychology by 2 per character level. (QL1=10, QL200=551) For those of you for whom it's green, that's trivial. For the overwhelming majority of you, for whom it's a light blue skill, that's still not that much to ask. The only people for whom Psychology is a dark blue skill happen to be Doctors, Engineers, and Nano Technicians - who, by coincidence, happen to be the only three professions who should NEVER taunt mobs onto themselves. Funny how that works.

Here's how you use one. You install it in your Util3 slot, under Weapons in your Equipment view. Once you have it there, you pick it up and drop it onto your toolbar. Now, with any mob targeted, type that number. It taunts the selected mob. It does this almost instantly; at 0.1 seconds, it's the fastest "attack" in the game. It doesn't spend any nano energy. You can do this once every 5 seconds. The only skill it locks is Psychology, which isn't used for any other kind of attack.

Even lower level ones work, some of the time. For those of you who make up the second-line combat units, like Traders, Fixers, and Agents, really, how much is it going to cost you to put a few points into Psychology and equip a free Aggression Enhancer that's at least somewhere near your level? If some day it saves your Doctor when you really need it to, it will save you a lot of lost XP and a trip back from reclaim. Isn't that worth a few IP and keeping a piece of common free equipment?

EVEN CHEAPER FOR ATROXES

Soldiers and Enforcers are the ones who need to be taunting the most. Too many of them were refusing to spend even the modest investment in IP it would have cost them to raise Psychology. (After all, if you're an Atrox, Psychology depends on two dark blue attributes, Intelligence and Sense.) So Funcom recently made taunting even cheaper for Atroxes. "Da Taunter!" works just like an Aggression Enhancer - but it uses much, much less Psychology. At QL1, it requires 5 - about what you get for free. All the way up at the theoretical limit of QL200, it only requires 56. Given that you're going to put at least a few points into Intelligence and Sense by then, that's probably few or no IP, too.

Unlike the Aggression Enhancer, Da Taunter even does damage! Not much, but some. So it's not as if you were actually wasting that 0.1 seconds, either.

Da Taunter does require Biological Metamorphosis. But not much. It uses less BioMet than nano buffs of the same QL, buffs you're almost certainly racing to get. (QL1=5, QL200=750) So again, given that Da Taunter is commonly given away free in missions, there's no excuse why every Atrox isn't wearing one.

TAUNT NANOS

Enforcers, Soldiers, and Martial Artists are all offered nanos that taunt, as well. Soldiers get them the earliest, as low as QL4. All of them use Psychological Modification (PM). Given that your combat skill buffs all require PM, there's no reason why you can't have these, too. OK, nano software isn't free - unless you pick it up from mission booth camping.

There's even one General taunt nano, Enmity, at QL30. Not only is it available in stores, from around level 25 to 35, you won't be able to go near a mission terminal without it begging you to take one, for free, as a mission reward.

But taunt nanos aren't as useful as Aggression Enhancers and Da Taunters are. They cost nano points. They depend on your Nano Init. But you can use them more often than once every 5 seconds, if you have to. Unlike the first two, I agree that these are a bit much to ask. But if you want to stay alive under tough circumstances, they're worth it.

General: Enmity (QL30, PM155).

Enforcer: Taunting Glare (QL7, PM 46), Aggressive Instincts (QL27, PM 137), Baleful Stare (QL57, PM 268), Roar of Aggression (QL116, PM 547), and Primal Hatred (QL139, PM 645).

Martial Artist: Bad Blood (QL24, PM 117), Distracting Nuisance (QL53, PM 262), Encourage Hatred (QL86, PM 403), Seething Resentment (QL136, PM 626), Eternal Enmity (QL162, PM 752).

Soldier: Ego Taunt (QL4, PM 31), Draw Attention (QL14, PM 83), Enraged Mind (QL30, PM 151), Annoying Presence (QL70, PM 336), Aggressive Captivation (QL90, PM 428), Only You, Only Me (QL152, PM 706).

MONGO

Enforcers get another line of taunt nanos, the Mongo series. These are taunts with a twist. First of all, unlike everything above, you don't have to target the particular mob you want pulled off of the healer. That's because the Mongo line pulls EVERY mob that's currently in combat onto you! (Note: Some say, "in the room," whether in combat or not. I'm not sure which.) Sound risky? Not as much as you'd think. Mongo also buffs your hit points by about 30% to 50%. That's why it uses both PsychMod and Biological Metamorphosis. To be fair, though, they also require a lot of extra NCU space, which is expensive.

Mongo Slam! (QL17, PM/BM 95), Bash! (QL66, PM/BM 321), Smash! (QL132, PM/BM 610), Crush! (QL149, PM/BM 696)

TAUNTING ROBOTS

Middle to high level Bureaucrats get three nanos that make their robotic pets more likely to taunt. They only need to be cast once per pet, when each robotic shell is activated. They use Psychological Modification and Time & Space, two skills that every 'Crat is already maxing out. But they don't start before QL44. And there are only three of them; for most of the Bureaucrat's life, they're not going to be that terribly effective. They're better than nothing, but not guaranteed to work.

On the other hand, given that 'Crats get long charm, short charm, sleep, and fear nanos, it's hardly like a taunting pet is the only way they have to get a mob to stop attacking the healer.

Bureaucrat: Mud Slinger (QL44, PM 220, TS 196), Defamation 101 (QL130, PM 609, TS 548), Character Assassin (QL173, PM 821, TS 745)

Engineers don't get nanos to make their robotic pet taunt. They make their own. They can make them at any QL they choose, from QL30 up to QL200. And unlike nanos, they can actually give their unused lower level ones to somebody else! They're not easy to make, as they use fairly high levels of both Chemistry and Mechanical Engineering skill to manufacture, and Chemistry is an otherwise mostly-useless skill for Engineers. They use three parts (Chemical Impact Injector, XU-11 Serum, Trimmer Casing, in that order), all of them commonly available in the Trade Department of any Ordinary Supermarket. Two of those parts are cheap and the third is mostly affordable. The result is a "Trimmer - Increase Aggressiveness". (Thanks to Virral and Makarov for this information!)

Do NOT mistake a "Trimmer - Postive Aggressive/Defensive" for a "Trimmer - Increase Aggressiveness." The positive agg/def trimmer makes your bot attack faster while lowering its dodge, evade, and duck skills. That's not the same thing at all.

It is not clear at this time if a Bureaucrat can use an Engineer-made aggression trimmer on his or her robotic pet. On the other hand, since using such a trimmer requires MechE skill, it's unlikely that one would want to.

At QL30, an aggression trimmer requires CompLit 116 and MechE 101 just to use it. A QL200 one would require CompLit 551, MechE 751 - and that, I admit, is outrageous on both counts. You only need to use it on your robotic pet once, and it lasts as long as the robot does.

It should be said that neither the Bureaucrat robot-taunt nanos nor the Engineer aggression trimmers work as well as the other forms of taunt. Having even high-level ones running on your pet does not guarantee that it will successfully taunt at all, let alone taunt every time. But they're worth having, because even if they only work once in a while, that's one less Resurrection Shock.

Sneaky Taunt Tricks
How do you keep a team alive with no healer?

Take two or more "meatshields" (Atrox Enforcers, or failing that, Soldiers) with you, both of whom have maxed out their Da Taunters or Aggression Enhancers, both of whom preferably also have the best possible taunt nanos.

Whichever one starts getting low on health first yells "Taunt for me!" The other one pulls the mobs off of him. As soon as no mobs are currently fighting him, he then stops ("Q"), drops ("X"), and heals (toolbar button for Treatment Kit). He then pops back up ("X"), and taunts at least half of the mobs back onto himself. If it's only one mob, they can actually take turns beating on it, one fighting while the other one heals!

How do you keep a team alive with no meatshield?

Almost the same way.

Nasty Funcom prank at everyone's expense: Team Heal taunts everything in the room, almost as effectively as a Mongo. This was an evil thing to do to healers. But oddly enough, it can be taken advantage of under some circumstances.

Make sure you have at least two, preferably three people with Team Heal (Doctor, Trader, Adventurer). If one of the healers is under attack and nobody else can pull the attacker off of the healer, then one or both of the other healers should fire Team Heal. This will switch the mob off of the healer who's almost dead, and onto one of the ones who's still healed up. The healers can take turns using Team Heal to pull mobs off of each other.

I don't recommend this! It's better to take a real tank in with you! But in an emergency, it may save your life.

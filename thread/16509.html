Armor Stats, Requirements, Calculations... The Whole Shebang!
First, credit where credit's due: http://home.rose.net/~tnix/aoarmor.html for the initial AC calculation stats. With a few minor corrections, this is the same set of data I used (with a few minor changes, such as Leather being sense/agility, not agility/strength).

Anyway, I have an unhealthy fascination with math, and thought I'd look into figuring out how the database calculates various things for various QLs of armor, such as AC per QL, and requirements. I've collated it all into a nifty spreadsheet so that you, too, can geekily min/max your armor. Heh. Whatever you plan to do with it.

First, let's look into some facts:

FACT 1) Not all armor is created equal. This should get a collective "duh" from most of you. Obviously, some kinds of armor are better than others. This is most obvious with various "lines" of armor. For instance, Bau is better than Cyber, Waitt is better than Bau, and Nano is better than Waitt (interestingly, these are the only four Int/Psy lines in the game, that I got data on). And of course, Raven's is better than Energized, and Link's is better than Raven's.

BUT, did you know that Metaplast is, in terms of basic stat calculations, worse than Leather? Did you know that Chilled and Heated actually have slightly different requirements (Heated is harder to equip)? That Biomech, Nano, OP Elite, and Obtru Steel Ribbed are all tied for overall stats and have the exact same requirements? That all forms of the Cyber line (not including Nano) are superior to other armors on their 'tier'? Is Organic better than Vito's? You bet. Urban Battle Armor is better than Waitt. Note that this is in terms of overall stats... all armors have their nuances, but those are obvious; OP Elite and Nano have bad chemical AC, whereas Biomech and Obtru (and to a lesser extent, Flowers Tech) have very good chemical AC. Anyone can eyeball that.

However, the stats can be misleading. Not all armor sets, at least early on, have full suits available; thus, while Leather has higher stats (marginally) than Metaplast, Metaplast will be better as a full suit because it has a helmet. The same is true of Kevlar and Tertius Decus; not only is Tertius better, is has a helmet where Kevlar doesn't. Most of the top armors have full suits, plus a cloak or coat to boot; again, not all coaks/coats/tank armor/battlesuits are created equal, another one most people know already.

FACT 2) There are basically five categories of armor in the game. These are listed by the stat which is always the highest in requirements:

Psychic/Stamina - Has 5 armor lines. The best armor in this line is Biomech armor; the worst armor in this set is Light Combat armor.

Intelligence/Psychic - Has 4 armor lines. The best armor in this line is Nano armor; the worst armor in this set is Cyber armor.

Stamina/Strength - Has 10 armor lines, 2 of which are Omni-Tek exclusive (the #1 and #3). The best armor in this line is Omni-Pol Elite; the worst armor in this line is Flak armor.

Sense/Agility - Has 7 armor lines. The best armor in this line is Obtru Steel Ribbed; the worst armor in this line is Nail armor.

Agility/Strength - Has 11 armor lines, 2 of which are Omni-Tek exclusive (OP Forest and Desert), and 2 of which are Battle Suit only (Battle and Urban Battle). The best armor in this line is Flowers Tech; the worst armor in this line is Metaplast.

Obviously, Stam/Str and Agi/Str have the most gradual progression of armor types. Fortunately for the Psy/Stam and Int/Psy lines, they reach their top type early (~QL60-90ish, though some of the other lines top out there too), so it's less of an issue.

FACT 3) Armor can basically be categorized into a series of 'tiers.' Each 'tier' is better than the previous in terms of ACs per QL, number of pieces available, and general coolness (let's face it, OP Elite and Nano look slick). They are worse in terms of cost (of course) and requirements. They are, roughly, as follows:

Tier 1 - Nail, Low-Tech, Flak, Metaplast, Leather, Light, Heated Plasteel, Chilled Plasteel, Cyber

Tier 2 - Energized, OP Forest, OP Desert, Sid's Leather, Kevlar, Raven's Energized, Tertius Decus, Steel Ribbed, Titan Plasteel, Vito's Plasteel, Nomad, Bau Cyber, Waitt Cyber

Tier 3 - Martial Artist, Tech, Organic, Omni-Pol, Secundus Decus, Nadir Steel Ribbed, Graft, Flowers Tech

Tier 4 - Obtru Steel Ribbed, Omni-Pol Elite, Nano, Biomech

Tier 5 (Back-Slots) - Tertius/Secundus/Decus Coat, Nano Cloak, Tank Armor

Tier 6 (Battlesuits) - Battle, Urban, Plasteel, Omni-Pol, OP Elite

Unsorted - Primus Decus (Tier 3? Tier 4?), Link's Energized (Tier 3?)

Thus, if you want to move up in terms of general armor, either look for a superior armor in the same tier that uses the same stats, or preferably, if you can get it, move up to a higher tier. Note that Tiers 5 and 6 are supplemental, or in the case of Battlesuits, replacements; they all have disadvantages (except Decus Coats and Nano Cloaks), but the advantages can be huge in terms of AC (for instance, Urban Battlesuits are HUGE, AC-wise... but is the nano hit worth that? Maybe you're better with Flowers Tech... in fact, you *are* better with Flowers Tech, look at the stats).

Anyway, the link below will, I hope, suffice to help you look for armor yourself. It's not complete (missing at least Primus Decus and Link's Energized, and many starting QLs), but if you feel like helping out, send me some feedback or just post it here.

To use the Comparison page, copy and paste the stats of one armor type into the "Current" line, and then input the QL in the top right box. You can do the same with the other one if you'd like. Make sure to save first, or make the spreadsheet read-only. This will show you the overall ACs of two particular armor sets at two separate QLs... and yes, you can use the same armor type twice to compare different QLs at a glance.

The By Type page is just a list of armor by the five types, in order of stats.

The QLs page is incomplete, but will eventually list the starting QLs for each particular armor set.

The Set Calculator lets you determine your AC from a mixture of armor pieces, which may not be of the same set. To use this, copy the stats for each particular armor PIECE into the appropriate lines into the topmost box, and input the QL. The lower box will automatically fill in the AC-related details.

Notes will eventually contain explanations of which sets are missing pieces of armor, and also contains requests for things I'm missing. It will also, eventually, explain the disadvantages to things like battlesuits and tank armor.

Note: Freeservers doesn't allow external linkage, so you'll have to go to the URL below and right-click to save the file. You can also left-click to preview in Internet Explorer, if you have both IE 5 or greater and Excel 2000.

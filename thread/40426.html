- Direct /tells (ok, done)

- Vicinity chat from that character

Like a Hamster on Cocaine.....
Most of the so-called "Griefers" that I have dealt with while trying to carry on a conversation or roleplay meeting have been of the "hamster on cocaine" variety.

They never sit still. They interrupt conversations with childish outbursts. They generally do anything and everything they can to try and get you and those you are interacting with to notice them.

They tend to do these things in 75% or 100% zones so that you have no choice but to confront them or leave. Either of these choices is just what they wish. You have made them more important than yourself and to them, this makes them more powerful.

To some, "griefing" is more fun on a Saturday night than tipping cows or hitting on the babysitter.

Just as all Roleplayers are different so are all "Griefers".

Some can be brought in line by simply asking them to go away or please refrain from interrupting your event.

While others are like the 5-year old in the grocery store whose had waaaay too much sugar.

And like the 5-year old sugar addict you cannot reason with them nor can you make them behave.

For these types I tend to do one of 2 things:

1. Ignore them. Plain and simple, they don't exist. They can scream and rant and rave and jump up and down and I will not respond. Eventually they will find someone else who is easier to bait and hump their leg for a while.

2. Turn the conversation into a critique of their abilities as a communicator and performance artist up to and including suggestions on how to improve their abilities so that in the hopes that one day they can become a truly brilliant buffoon. Most "griefers" only think they want the spotlight but when you turn that 500 watt bulb on them in its fullest glory, they tend to wilt.


My choice of reactions depends on my mood.


If all else fails, then simply move the meeting/RP event somewhere else in the area. If the "griefer" follows you then summon an ARK and let them know that this person has been disruptive.



Personally, I have found that most of the RP "griefers" are loners who seem to think that the only way to have fun is to ruin the enjoyment of the game for others. Thankfully people with this attitude have the shelf life of uncooked meat in the July sun. Once a new game comes out, they will take their toys and foul mouths and head to greener pastures.

We didn't do much beyond your leaving, Keredwyn - aside from just reiterate things, wave our hands about a bit and eventually walk off

I've got complete logs floating around somewhere, and am in the process of writing up the stuff I mentioned myself.

Basically, in summary, this is what we discussed and decided on:

Grief play comes in roughly two categories:

* short-term grief play, such as /shouting, /mooning, following people around and throwing up all over them constantly...

In that case the only thing you can reasonably do is /ignore, don't feed the troll and if it persists send a /petition about it and remember to take screenshots.

Remember, if it makes you (player) feel uncomfortable then it's harassment. Ask the person policely and OOC to desist and - if they don't - /petition.

* long-term grief play, such as character impersonation, direct character assassination attempts and other more long-term activities. This includes organised and consistent kill-stealing or ninja-looting of a particular person, spreading rumours about a person and similar activities.

This gets messy because in some cases this can be RP. If it is targeted at a *character* then it is. If, however, the attacks start being directed at a *player* then it has crossed the line into harassment / grief play.

Two pieces of advice came out of this.

1. If you intend to engage in what would best be described as 'antagonistic RP' of a social nature - character assassination attempts and what-have-you, then it is only polite to OOC introduce yourself to the victim and let their player know that you intend to be their nemesis. Like s*x, RP is great if both parties are consenting. After all, they're playing too. And while there is a certain amount of 'if you can't stand the heat, don't stay in the kitchen' there is also the most important thing to remember: people come to the game to have fun. If you're ruining someone's fun because of your style of play, then you're grief playing.
And while it may offend some of you purists out there, keeping your arguments entirely IC while maintaining an OOC friendship is more fun than attacking someone in both environments. And can lead to some more entertaining stories to boot.

2. Keeping the RP community together and keeping people aware of possible character attacks/impersonators/whatever makes sense. If you see someone who bears a rather striking resemblance to a prominent person, take the time to let that person know. By letting people know what is going on and what is planned, it is easier to deal with such issues.


Of course, as Ghump has already stated, there is no magic bullet overnight solution to this kind of thing. The best we can do is remember it's a game, remember to keep each other OOC informed of what is going on and - above all - remember not to feed the trolls.

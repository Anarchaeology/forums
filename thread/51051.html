Funcom is certainly looking for more players. Here are a few examples:

7 day free trial, download everything you need off the net. While some of their competitors are also giving away their product (e.g. AC: DM, free (or get $2-3 back) after rebate), I'm not aware of any of the other big players making their client available for download. Most sell for at least $20.

A while ago Funcom kept spamming their ex-customers who had cancelled and tried to entice them to come back with a similar free "re-trial" period (which may have been a month, I forget). I'm not sure how well this worked; at the time I was still so upset that those emails had the opposite effect on me (I had cancelled one or two months after release).

There's the recruit a friend program and the affiliate program. The former rewards current players with a free month for each friend that enrolls; the latter pays the affiliate somewhere between $3 and $10 for each new subscriber.

You can find Funcom ads at a number of gaming related websites. Not just the ones that deal specifically with AO, either. Compare that with their competitors - when is the last time you saw an EQ or DAoC banner ad? Have to give them credit, at least - the ads attract attention even if they are a tad over the top (most feature female characters, some with the tag "we've got implants"...).

The booster pack is another clear example. I'm honestly not sure if you could find AO at the regular venues - the small shops focused on games (e.g. Electronics Boutique in the USA) might have it on the shelf, but at the retailers I frequent (Best Buy, CompUSA) I'd be hard pressed to find the box (if they even have it). The booster pack is aimed for release right before the holiday season, 2002. One effect this will have is placing a new, shiny, and comparatively cheap ($20) product on the shelves. Having something on the shelves is of immense value, especially near the holidays (used to be the industry lost money the first three quarters, more than made it up in the fourth quarter; not sure if this is still true). The new competitors, if they are released by then (SWG, AC2, Earth and Beyond, Neocron) will almost certainly cost twice as much, and in all likelihood cost $50 or more if they are selling well. While $20 might be unacceptable to some current players (and this is not an issue I'm trying to raise here), $20 is very acceptable to someone wanting to try out a new game, especially an MMORPG.

In short, Funcom is recruiting new players more aggressively than any other company I can think of. The closest competitor in this regard is Microsoft/Turbine, in that they also give away the client for free (if the customer is mindful enough to send in the rebate form). The troubles Funcom faces are two fold, in my opinion: not having a product on the shelves and living with the bad press they received a year ago. The former is being remedied by the booster pack. As for the latter, while the game has improved leaps and bounds over where it was a year ago (and this has been noted by players as well as the gaming publications), the bad press lingers in the background. Many old customers are unwilling to return (even if they would enjoy the game currently) out of principle. And then one has to consider the impending release of the four games mentioned above. Is the market big enough to support so many products?

Statistical tests
Hi!

For fun (yeah, I know, I know), I applied a statistical test used to see if arithmetical averages are sufficiently different as to be significant (on a 5% level).

I could only do these on the test by Tetra since he posted the numbers. The results are that neither the 200ish nor the 400ish series of tests show a large enough difference in average to varant the conclusion that the dual wielding damage is higher (on average) than single wielding.

In the second series (the 400 one), it isn't actually that far off. The means are 335,9 and 350, 3 - i.e. the difference is 14,4 points. To be significant, the difference in the averages had to be 21 points or larger. As the numbers stand now, there is about a 20% chance that the averages are different because of random noise. I won't bother to calculate the exact chance.

Executive version: There is a good chance the difference in averages in Tetra's numbers are random. This does not mean that you can conclude that the averages are equal either, though. So in short, this proves nothing either way. Thank you all for letting me once again prove the usefulness of scientific method .

(This test was done using the assumption that averages in damage distributions follow the normal Gaussian curve, although this is not entierly true since the chance of crits throws this off.)

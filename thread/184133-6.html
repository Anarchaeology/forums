Here's what I think is the best approach to fixing the problem:

1. Make versions of the maps in the old player-friendly uploadable format. Make the requirements high if you wish.

2. Lower the requirements on the adventurer-only maps and make them easily available (perhaps from a special adventurer vendor somewhere) so that pretty much every adventurer will have them.

Now, in almost every team there's going to be a person or two who hasn't raised map nav or gotten all the maps installed, so the adventurers would be able to cast them for the team and still have their role to play as keepers of the maps. With the low requirements teams would find that if they invite any adventurer to their team they will almost certainly all have maps ("Hey dudes, I don't have maps, find an adventurer"). At the same time for those to whom the maps are important there is a way to have them all the time if they want to make the effort. So no one has their fun ruined.

Nothing is taken away from anyone and everybody is happy, and the new nanos still have a purpose. It also wouldn't require any new features to be coded as some of the other suggestions would. Make sense?

Maps are included in games like this to make up for the incredibly lacking qualities of a "world" that is viewed through a flat piece of glass. They reduce eyestrain and allow players to become more convincingly immersed in the game world. As a player looks at BOTH the "field of view" AND the overhead map, the player's mind can more readily imagine that the textures on the screen are a world. Restricted access to such mapping features in a game like AO is BAD DESIGN. This is so obvious that it hurts, yet some posters here want to make this some other type of discussion.

The issue is whether or not maps are such a fundamental part of experiencing the pleasant, but still highly artificial AO world, that they should be unduly restricted only for very good reason. IMO, they are.

YES, if AO was a "real" world, or even a convincing VR world, the argument could be made that players should learn to play without an overhead map. Such is not the case.

Therefore, overhead maps in games like this are necessary in a way that goes beyond most other elements of the game. FC made a mistake in trying to blend this necessary element with the profession system. It is as if you had to have a nano cast on you by some profession to use a reclaim or a bank. Or if you had to "rest" or "eat" or "defecate" at certain intervals in the game. It was a design mistake.

Until you can give us VR, FC, you MUST give all players the possibility of permanent maps of all playfields unless there is a very strong reason for crippling the quality of your game. Give all players overhead maps of SL or we will go play some other game that more convincingly replicates a world. You have made enough mistakes in SL that you wont be able to fix. This one is easy.

FC please correct your design error.

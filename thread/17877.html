Attack speed - What i found out with a stop watch and a pencil
I always thought there was some truth to the findings that were made by slicer many months ago, and i thought that initiatives reduced the recharge speed greater than the attack speed, so i thought that the mausser and the suppressor(1/3) were faster weapons than the MCS(3/1)... this is what i found out with a digital stop watch and a pencil.

My mesurements were made observing the attack/recharge bar and not the interval between shots.

I used several weapons of low QL that i could self equip, in the 10-30ish range. i used fast weapons, slow weapons, weapons with same attack/recharge times and weapons with different attack/recharge values. And i came to these conclusions, these conclusions are based sololy on timing the attack and recharge speed on the bar (top left corner of screen) while attacking.
for clarification, 0%bar= full deff; 50%=midle; and 100%=full aggro; My ranged initiative is 1014

What i found out:
Setting my aggro bar to 0%(full deff) i noticed that the attack and recharge speed is the attck and recharge speed declared on the weapon. The Suppressor shot at 1/3; the MCS at 3/1; and the Galahad Inc. 055 (2.50/2.50) shot at 2.50/2.50 etc... what this means is that unlike what i previous tought, ranged initiative afects attack and recharge speeds in the same way.

Moving my aggro bar to 50%(midle) i noticed a decrease of 1(one) second both to the attack and to the recharge and setting it to full aggro(100%) there was a 2 second reduction to both attack and recharge times.

I also noticed that with ver fast weapons (less than one second attack/recharge), i didnt shoot any faster than 1/1 so the cap is at 1/1... what this means is that when you can fire them at full speed on 0% aggro bar, then you will not get any more benefit from increased initiative

so:

1000 initiative = Declared attack speed with bar at 0%
bar at 50% = -1 second to both attack and recharge speeds
bar at 100% = -2 seconds to both attack and recharge speeds
attack speed is capped at 1/1

What i dont know:

What i dont know is how initiatives work if you have less than 1000... meaning, if you only have 500 will you shoot at double speed for that weapons declared speed (with bar at 0%) or is it an increase in seconds....

Understanding Attack & Recharge-Experiment 2

Well, Appleseed is now level 6, a bit richer, and with a small arsenal in which to experiment with. The big question that came up during the last experiment boils down to, "At what setting is a weapon's attack and recharge performing as advertised?" Let me explain this a bit.

Weapons have an attack and recharge speed that can be improved upon by the appropriate initiative skill, and altered somewhat by use of the aggression slider. Stated mathematically, the time between shots is:

t = a + r - i + g

Where 't' is the time between attacks, 'a' is attack speed, 'r' is recharge speed, 'i' stands for initiative, and 'g' is a modifier based on where the aggression slider is set. By using a low level character, I removed combat initiative from the equation, to get something that looked like:

t = a + r + g

Assuming that 'g' was a value of -2 to +2, and "a + r" was listed as is in the weapon's description proved to be in some way, drastically wrong. Either the formula looks like "t = N(a + r) + g", where N is a constant that might be set around 2, or the value of 'g' ranges from 0 to +4. In today's experiment, I attempted to find out just where 'g' equals zero. The theories that are conflicting here include: g=0 at the middle setting (halfway between aggressive and defensive), and that g=0 only at the full aggressive setting.

The procedure for this experiment is an expanded version of what I did last time. I acquired from the stores around Omni-Trade and Omni-Entertainment a number of different weapons to use as test. For each weapon, I made several test firings against targets in one of Omni's "Virtual Training Grounds." I set the aggression slider at one of 5 points before a battle, shot down one of the many young lizards in the 'Virtual Swampland', and record the time it takes between shots. More specifically, after the attack meter charged up to full, I would time the period where it empties (recharge), and fills up again (attack). Theoretically, at the point where g=0 (or more accurately, at the point where g - i = 0) comparing the value of t with the value of a + r would reveal a constant N.

The weapons I acquired for testing included four models. The first of which is the Solar Powered Assault Rifle, the same one tested in the last experiment. The second weapon is an Ill Treated E-Beamer. This is only slightly slower than the previous weapon, but still useful. The third weapon is the Ill Treaded Seburo C-99 A assault rifle. With 2.5 listed as both attack and recharge, I expect this weapon to be literally half as fast as the Solar Powered. The last little toy I played with is the Ill Treated ICE Flashpoint Laser Rifle. This has an attack listed at 8 and a recharge of 5, so we should see a big difference in firing rates between this and the previous three.

So, what do I see in this data? To start with, for all weapons there appears to be somewhere between 4 and 5 seconds difference between full aggressive and full defensive. Therefore the affect of the defensive-aggressive slider on weapon attack and recharge seems to be constant. However, I was looking for that point where that slider does not modify attack and recharge. Let's take a look at the N values I measured.

Full Defensive: 3.316, 2.81, 2.413, 1.519
Half Defensive: 2.64, 2.52, 2.002, 1.612
Neutral: 2.268, 1.885, 1.72, 1.352
Half Aggressive: 1.752, 1.52, 1.371, 1.501
Full Aggressive: 1.171, 1.28, 1.298, 1.256

The results here seem clear, the value of N changes significantly as the weapon gets faster for every setting except full aggressive. This means, weapon attack and recharge times in the weapon's description work as is only when you have your slider set to the far right. Moving your slider away from aggressive will add time between your attacks. It also appears that a Rubi-Ka second is approximately 1.25 real-world seconds long.

Some weird things I have noticed: There appears to be a random component to attack and recharge. The variance in timing the fast Solar Powered I would have easily chalked up to human error, but this became more pronounced as the weapon got slower. By the time I finished checking the IEC Flashpoint, these little variances actually started to outweigh the adjustments to the aggression slider. Several times I had to double-check my settings just to make sure I was actually testing the setting I thought I was testing.

Also, another bug reared its ugly head when I was testing the IEC Flashpoint. The Flashpoint advertises a load of 5 rounds of energy ammo. Yet, after firing the Flashpoint 4 times it starts the reload cycle. Even so, it still uses 5 rounds of ammo at a time. Also interesting, is that when it happened, the test target would keel over for no apparent reason halfway through loading. I suspect that all 5 rounds are being fired from the server's perspective, but the client thinks the clip only has 4 rounds. Definitely weird!

I suppose it has been long enough now where I should re-test and get a solid grasp of attack and recharge again. Since I have both the Mausser chemical streamer (3.0/1.0) and Mausser Particle Streamer (1.0/3.0) I now have the tools to confirm certain parts of the Slicer formula. Measuring each gun at various aggresive settings would determine whether the 1/600 attack and 1/300 recharge holds, or whether the numbers are actually closet to 1/400 for attack and recharge each.

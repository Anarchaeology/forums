Meatshield
This is my opinion, please understand that my characters have never been the "meatshield".

I believe that it is best for the entire group to target one mob. The meatshield, or someone else designated, should select that target and the rest of the group use a macro to assist (/macro assist /assist <name of meatshield>). This way one mob is getting the full fury of the group. When he drops switch targets and repeat.

If the group attacks multiple mobs it just gives them that much extra time to hit on people.

It is my opinion that it is best to take out the hardest mob first. There is an exception in the boss room because the boss takes so much time to kill that it is better to take out minions first. So target the toughest or most dangerous mob (note that monsters seem to do more damage and attack with worse types of attack, chemical or radiation etc, than NPC's). However, if you have auto attack on and have aggroed yourself on a mob it is probably not worth the time, effort and confusion it would cause to switch targets. You can use this time to select who your next target will be.

Always use judgement. If the toughest mob is banging away at a bot or pet and another weaker mob is sending your doc to perdition then go after the weaker mob. Docs get aggroed a lot and tend to die a lot. Protect your doc he is trying to protect you (if he isn't then get a better doc). Actually the above case is true for any class that is not normally a meat shield or melee type but can draw aggro. NT's often get aggro after a particularly good nuke and they do not normally have the hits to handle it. Many other support classes will also draw it on occasion. Check the situation as carefully as possible but don't waste combat time doing it, use your tab, function, and team bars to see who is getting hurt, who is getting low, or even dead. Good situational awareness is not as important to a meat shield as it is to, say, a doctor but it is still important.

Finally, don't rush off to the next battle and leave your nano casters behind. Most nano casters (NT's, nuking MP's, Doc's and more) have to recharge after almost every battle. When I am the doc and the team runs off it is very frustrating. It takes time to catch up, size up the situation and, if they have run too far, refresh the dumb health bars. Then to hear them whine because I didn't start healing soon enough is almost unbearable. An extra 10 sec. at the battle site is not going to impact the time significantly but will impact the group dynamics radically.

Hope this helps.

Some Agreement, Some Disagreement
I 100% agree with you that everybody should be fighting the same mob, a point I made in the guide. And yes, I agree it is the meatshield who should choose, if for no other reason than that ideally everybody should let the meatshield get in at least the first hit, and maybe the first two. And I guess it doesn't go without saying but yes, mobs that attack with damage types you are less protected against (like radiation, or poison, or for most characters cold or heat).

I'm exactly 100% in disagreement with you though, about the order.

The thing is, mobs in missions have very, very high minimum damage. The amount of damage you take from a yellow mob verses a yellowish green mob isn't that different. Nor are they going to hit you much less often. But the easier mob will pop first. Fighting your way up the chain from the easiest mob to the hardest will cut down on incoming damage the fastest.

Or more accurately, you should fight the mob with the fewer health points first. Facing a dark red NT mob and a yellow Enforcer, the NT will keel over faster.

All of which assumes, by the way, that your puller has failed to bring you only one mob at a time in the first place, which he or she should have done. But it doesn't always work, I admit.

And the one time I think you should attack the hardest mob first is in the Boss room ...

... but if, and only if, you have one or more Calmers in the group. Why? Because you can't Calm boss mobs. If you fight the minions first, you'll take damage from one minion and the boss. If you calm all the minions and fight the boss first, you take only damage from the boss ... probably about a third less, on average.

Now if you don't have a calmer with you, yes, by all means pop the minions first - for the same reason I mentioned above, to reduce incoming damage faster.

However, since I believe it is definately the case when a rooter is on the team it also has the added benefit of allowing me to minimize the complexity of strategies that I have to try to memorize.

In the boss room a good point is made. With a good calm/rooter or two you can calm all of the minions and then take on the boss first, later killing the minions one at a time. I must admit I have not played on many teams when we had a good calm/rooter.

This illustrates one of the fascinations with teaming. The team mix often changes the tactics. Below are some situations that change the team tactics a lot.

Teams with many pets: Pet teams work best with a good puller. While many people think that a good bot will be a good tank it just isn't in missions (I have seen this work very well in hunting). The Bot usually has a hard time hitting enough before aggro is passed to one of the other team members and then it takes awhile to pull aggro back to the bot. If there is only one pet class on the team then they can often go room to room. You get two or three and pulling is really much easier to keep team cohesion.

Docs: Docs do add ability to a team. They are hit points. Heal pets are ok but they cannot respond like a doc. The secondary healing classes are good too but get more than one. MA's should help when needed but they are also an attack class. To make them the primary healer is usually a mistake.

Calm/Rooters: It is always a great relief when you have a calm/rooter and they can freeze those adds. I love looking around and seeing two or three mobs just standing there. One note here to tanks, give us docs and nanos a chance to recharge before breaking the next root. Also, MP's, don't have a mezz pet around when there is a good root class operating (actually my MP never casts his mezz pet in teams only in solo). I believe that the power of the rooting classes is often overlooked in favor of the tanks.

These aren't all the possibilities they are almost limitless. That is the joy of team play. Even if two teams have exactly the same class types individual players play differently.

Now for a whine to people out there. A little patience goes a long way to making a team operate better, safer, and more skillfully. If you are too anxious to wait for your team mates to buff and prep then go get a team full of tanks and rush around to your hearts desire. Teamed with an Engie the other night who just could not contain his desire to explore where the rest were not. One team mate caused more problems than the other five put together.

Here's my 'primer' for team missions.

For sub 100s:

Everyone follow one direction. Lefts or rights.

Everyone attack the same mob.

Everyone keep autoattack off.

If you can heal, heal.

If you can calm, calm adds.

That's all that's needed for an effective team. Nothing fancy.

As you go up in levels, you can simplify this even more.

For 100+ teams:

Everyone deal dmg.

That's it. Avg team I'm in with a level 143 didn't matter how many adds or if people run off on their own. Those ql 180+ mobs are laughable and only get more laughable since you fight them from level 90+.

On the boss, if you have a calmer, calm adds. If you have a crat, aoe calms CAN calm the boss. Whether this is a bug or not, who knows.

If you don't have a calmer, then kill adds, kill boss.

Nothing fancy.

WARNING: DESPERATION TACTIC FOLLOWS

Okay, I've got one role and two things to add here.

Defensive Meatshield -- This guy's job is to stay healthy. That's it. You'll see why when I get to the second point. Ideally, should be able to turn from low to high damage on a dime (MA's are best at this, in my experience)

Point one: Do NOT train things outside the mission entryway. If you can, kill them off. Again, you'll see why in just a minute.

Point two: Don't be afraid to retreat. Ideally, only the healer or Defensive Meatshield should be giving this order. ONE of them, not both. If the entire group is in a potential disaster, then it's time for the Def Meatshield to go to work. The original meatshield simply won't be up to the task at this point. The Defensive does everything he can to get aggroed, then STICKS AROUND WHEN THE RETREAT ORDER IS GIVEN. Everybody else, regardless of condition, RUNS for the nearest exit. Then, when everyone is WELL clear, the Defensive switches off agg/def and makes for the door behind everyone else.

This relates to point one, for obvious reasons. The last thing you need is to be out of nano, low on health, and face-to-face with the thirty greens you thought you'd left behind when the mission entrance proves to be a bit too much.

I've only tried this strategy once, but it worked like a charm. It turned a "certain death" situation into one where everybody got out alive. Team Healed, Damage buff, full complement of attacks, then a taunt and another team heal thrown in for good measure. The only problem is the Defensive Meatshield is a role that requires a certain amount of altruism. When the retreat order goes out, you are VERY likely to die, like I almost did. But it's better than EVERYONE being sent to reclaims across your side's territory, isn't it?

Classes
What I am about to write may offend some people if they don't read it fully. Please take things in context. This is a reply to other classes and experience vacuums.

If you give me a team that contains 2 Enf., 2 MA's, and a doc plus a third damage dealer we will rock the hardest missions with little difficulty. Make the third a calmer and you minimize any risk of trouble and make the boss room easier. This assumes the classes know their job. The doc heals, the MA's help healing when required, you have at least 4 good damage dealers, possibly 5 plus if the doc sneezes on a mob and does damage it is value added. This thing about docs not fighting is crap, IMO, if they can fight and not affect their heals fine. If they can't then healing is their primary function. This team will always be the strongest combination in PvM, but there is a difference between strongest and best.

This does not mean that this is the team I prefer to team with. For hunting many classes add value to teams that are not recognized in missions. The run buffs etc. are really useful in hunting but are of dubious value in missions unless you get into trouble. In missions having a prof that can warp you out at the end is a god send. Of course fairly early on Docs can perform this function, not as fast as fixers but still very effectively.

My goal, though, is not to team for optimum efficiency but to team for optimum fun. I would prefer to team with a fun fixer than an Uber Enforcer who is a jerk. I would prefer to team with a Trader who plays as part of a team than a ninja looting MA. I would rather take on a lower qual mission with a nerfed fun team than an ultra high with a group that bickers and argues.

In fact, I tend to like blitzing through a bit lower qual mission and gaining more xp/time with less danger/encounter than to push the limit and gain ultra xp/mob but lose team mates occasionally. To me it is far more entertaining to rock through the mission. Unfortunately I find that most Uber characters want to only do Uber missions and I end up dieing more often with them than if I team with average to below average characters who excercise caution and know how to think through a challenge.

Also, many classes that are nerfed at one level come into their own at another. My xp is slightly less but those teams seem to offer more pure enjoyment than any other except for a full guild team.

So any class that is having problems leveling during a portion of their career, contact me. You need to absorb some xp while giving less than your "fair share" to a team for awhile then I will be happy to carry you if I can. Soldier Role
You are a great fighter and a good meatshield, your enforcer should be the group meatshield. Watch and pay attention, announce adds you see coming in and provide a direction vector that makes sense, "add" is nice "add left" is even better.

Watch the other hits happening, you want aggro on the meatshield, but if it switchs to a soft target, your doc, mp, nt etc you want to pour on the aggro then to get it off and if on you your meatshield can then have reasonable time to recover aggro.

really, really tough targets. Work to share aggro to some extent, works very well when there are a few people that can add some heals and make the most of HoT's

The best plan to go through a tough team mission is not team move room to to room, but to pull room to room. At most after the first room and pulling close rooms to entrance, work bringing the target to your group rather than moving your group to the target. If you clear a room that has adjacent mobs to it, pull those MOBs to your adjacent and original room.

!st room, you should with patience be able to pull singles or pairs from here. Its true that some classes are best for it, but a good puller bringing just one of four or more is better than any Fixer bringing 5. A good puller, regardless of class is like gold for your group.

Always pause between multiple MOBs when possible. They are just sitting there anyways and trying to kill them 5 seconds faster may mean you can't get out without a casuality. Go through a process, "ready". "target is %t". "attacking %t". "all can attack %t". The purpose of the ready and target is, is to allow time for people to ready themselves, recharge and reload and most importantly to voice not ready. It would be good if the person that calmed targets make known what they think should be first and your target selection be that, you depend on the calmer for being the traffic cop, makes sense to go after the next to awake rather than the one that was just calmed.

When I pull I let my group know that I am going out by sayin "lookin". Its a heads up to everyone that something will be enroute back to us real soon. When I can I target and tell my group "pullin %t" and then pause before getting aggro. When I am comin back with my pull I say "inc %t" . Everyone then knows what is coming and what my target will be. If its a bad pull some may arrive before me and/or my target. The calmer knows that the target there is not my target and can calm, everyone is waiting for the right target because they assist. When the target arrives the meatshield will see it come in and attack without reservation, quickly gaining aggro. Start attacking when you see the meatshield go through 2 or more rounds of attacks as target.

When you pull you can not have any sort of reflect up at all, a hit from your reflect shield/bracer will draw any MOBs in the current room and may aggro adjacent rooms as well. When pulling in a hard mission you want to have a buffer room between you and the kill room to limit or remove the chance of adds. In the example given, room A, B, and C, killing in room C may draw aggro from rooms D, E and F. I think best to pull from D and kill in B when room C is clear.

Here are some basic practices that have served me well in teams.

1. If rights are called, GO RIGHT! Do not go off your own way! You will get yourself or the team killed.

2. After you establish the designated tank, LET THEM LEAD. Do not run into the next room until the tank is ready. You will get the team killed.

3. Everyone hit the same mob! It does no good for the person calming the adds if everybody is hitting different mobs. You will get the team killed.

4. Do NOT expect the Doc to keep your a$$ out of the reclaim 100% of the time. No Doc can keep up with the entire team all of the time. They have to focus on the tank and throw the occasional team heal out as well. Use your own first aid kits and treatment labs.

5. Docs need to start healing as soon as the fight begins. Do NOT wait till somebody is at half health before healing them. The truly good docs will keep you so full of health that you almost never need kits. (use them anyway) Please also heal the tank up inbetween fights. Fast heal time for the tank = fast finish to the mission. Also, be prepared to use a ton of nanokits, it is part of the cost of being a good doc.

6. NEVER, EVER FIGHT IN THE FIRST BIG ROOM! You will pull 5 or 6 more adds and you will get the team killed! Always let a puller pull the mobs back to the first starting room to fight. You will avoid adds and the fight will be much more manageable. As you are making your way to the rooms around the main room, fight in the smaller rooms only. You will avoid adds and keep the fights easier. (this does not apply to teams 160+ where you can easily fight 8-10 mobs at once without anyone dying)

7. DO NOT wait to enter the fight with a mob until after you have looted the chest in the room. This is just plain bad form and should never be done. If you wanna loot the chests at the end of the mission, or get some time while people are healing or rebuffing, that is find. Obitus covered a lot. Just want to re-iterate one thing to the docs out there.

Heal Early, Heal Often.

Don't be a nano hog, if you can't afford rechargers ask for extra loot at the end of a mission. However, if you keep those health bars full you keep your team happy and when things do go a bit south you aren't in a premade bad situation already. You can't save all of them all the time but you can keep your percentage high.

One thing that helps keep me interested is to anticipate the mobs hit. I try to time my heal for right after the mob hits the target (very easy if someone is holding aggro most of the time). Since the mob will hit once or twice while I recharge this allows me to utilize the max heal for the nano with the greatest chance to bring the target to full health with the least number of hits until I can heal them again. With practice you can become an artist of a doc.

Another word for docs. We are casualties. Less often the higher you get but you have accepted a noble and dangerous profession. Don't get pissed if you die. It is frustrating. If it was the teams fault or a member of the team then counsel them and ask they change their tactics. However, learning to die with grace (and this is true for everyone) and not letting it ruin your fun or theirs will pay great dividends in the long run.

Trade and economy
The player-driven economy on Rubi-Ka seems to be non-existent right now, at least on the RK2 cluster. There's some trade in unique items at the very high levels, but nothing at all at the newbie levels, and that's a pity. I've recieved items from mission rewards that I know full well are sought-after items (Rider Executioners etc.) but have been unable to even give the damn things away. At the end of the day, I'm much more interested in seeing someone get the benefit of the item than I am in receiving the pittance the vending machines spit out for second-hand goods.

The problem seems to be that there's no good way to reach your target market ATM. Yes, you can try the shopping channels, but they only reach that part of the player base that's online right now and don't buffer for offline players (for good reason - imagine the spam if you logged in after a couple of weeks absence...). I've not had an enquiry for anything I've advertised, and the shopping channels generally seem to be dead. Some nights I don't see a single message on them.

The upshot of all this is that it's often harder to find a buyer than it is to find items to sell. This is probably why someone chose to simply dump a quantity of low-level gear in the middle of Borealis last night - too little re-sale value to bother with in the shops, and no valid means to find a private buyer. A pity, because I'd have been interested in buying one of the pieces of armour - but I couldn't pick it up, because it didn't belong to me, and the owner wasn't there to trade with.

There are several possible ways to remedy the situation. If players were able to rent space in vending machines, naming their price and paying a percentage up front, then a second-hand economy could actually get going on Rubi-Ka overnight. I know that Funcom is probably worried that this will develop into a method for easy twinking, so perhaps a system of random NPC buying could be implemented to keep players on their toes. Besides, there's always the risk that the item will sell to a genuine buyer before the seller's alt gets there with the Yalm's cr1 asking price. Finally, if the item didn't sell after a week, it should be returned to the seller's bank account, to prevent the machines becoming too cluttered.

Other alternatives are possible though. Two other threads on this forum have suggested a newspaper and a mail system for Rubi-Ka. Paid ads in the Rubi-Ka times are an obvious possibility for reaching the mass market, but perhaps a better solution would be an in-game bulletin board system, with shopping boards divided by level and possibly faction as well. A postal system would make either of these methods viable, although again Funcom may raise the twinking issue - perhaps requiring both parties to be online for a mail-order trade to go through would resolve the issue.

I'm in a tradeskill profession, so I have a particular stake in this (there's little point in my making items if I have no means to sell them). What do the rest of you think, especially non-trade types? Any other suggestions?

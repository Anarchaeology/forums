Funny thing is, it works as a fantastic money sink. That Via Rosso 2 apartment costs buku bucks in Neocron. Overall, they certainly got the apartment thing right. So far I counted the following types of apartments:

Via Rosso - level 1 (small but posh)
Via Rosso - level 2 (large and posh)
Plaza - level 1 (small and ok)
Plaza - level 2 (moderate sized ok quality)
Plaza - level 3 (gigantic and ok quality, the clothing terminal is great)
Pepper Park - Sleazy (tiny apartment, boarded windows)
Pepper Park - Small (moderate size, has a second floor loft, some holes in the floor though)
Pepper Park - Large (large apartment, nice view of the redlight district)
Outzone - Sleazy (extremely cramped, rundown dump, windows broken, rotten floorboards)
Twilight Guardian - Standard (it's a cave, what more can I say?)
Military Base - Standard (moderate sized, feels like living in a storage room piled with junk)
Tech Haven - level 1 (cramped, very blue )
Tech Haven - level 2 (also small, two stories though with a grav lift, second story has a glass floor, very nice looking)

Each apartment is equipped with a GoGuardian (the equivilent of a bank), a Gene Replicator (the equivilent of an insurance terminal), a Citicom Terminal (used to check the forums, get missions, read email, trade stocks, etc.), and a "recreation station" (a bed which heals you fully when you click on it). The Plaza level 3 apartment has a terminal where you can change your clothes. Each apartment also comes with cabinets where you can store items, as well as chairs to sit in, and other various furniture. Some apartments have nice views, e.g. the Via Rosso 2 apartment has a balcony with a view of the ocean.

There are also apartments that are designated as clan headquarters. The clan apartments are the same style as the other apartments but as a rule MUCH larger. The style of the clan HQ is based on what faction the clan belongs to.


None of these apartments actually take up any extra space in the world because the entrances work similar to AO. The difference is that you enter an elevator and type in your "apartment code" to go to an apartment, instead of simply defaulting to your own apartment. This means anyone who knows your code go go to your apartment. When you arrive at an apartment though, you are in front of the door, only the person with the key can open the door, so you never have to worry about folks with your apartment code actually sneaking into your apartment; the most they can do is wait outside if you aren't there.


So, what would it take to get Funcom to implement a similar apartment system into AO? Petitions don't seem to work. Competition might though

I discovered the cause of bugged doors is in fact a different bug entirely: sometimes your character becomes unable to "use" items in the world in Neocron. Logging off and waiting for a few minutes corrected this. It was of course a beta, you couldn't expect it to be perfect.

As for the NavRay, I think it's funny that people can complain about a feature that doesn't even exist in any other game to date. For those that don't know it, the NavRay in Neocron is an interface that gives you directions to certain things from where you are. Initially when you click on where you want to go, an arrow will appear on screen pointing you in a direction. After a little bit of time, an actual flourescent dotted path appears in front of you to follow to your destination. It doesn't always work perfectly, but then again, pathfinding has a "big O" that is exponential as opposed to logorithmic. That means in English: it takes big computers to do it well, and imperfect shortcuts to do it at all on a small computer. Nonetheless, it was great for finding where your home apartment is in case you couldn't find it.

Enough of talking about Neocron, the point here is to show folks the way apartments in AO really should look, and the kinds of options that should be available to players.

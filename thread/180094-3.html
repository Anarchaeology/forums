Kudos to Rapella for recognizing the inherent relativity of the whole good and evil deal going on here.

All extremes in sides could honestly seen as wrong. Extremists and fanatics often shoot the philosophy they so rabidly champion in the hypothetical foot. It is the rational, the ethical and the moderate who carry a cause forward to make progress or prove a point.

In the Omni vs Clan conflict, NPCs seem to show more of a rude and callous face versus the polite and compassionate one. In the Unredeemed versus Redeemed conflict, it comes down to ugly and superior versus pretty and personable. I don't see good versus evil in these icons. What I see is aesthetic and socially programmed choices. For those of you who maintain that the Omni NPCs are all Evil and Clan NPCs are all Good, I have three names for you: Portman, the Sentinels and the Dust Brigade. No one can tell me that Simon Silverstone doesn't have gallons of innocent blood on his hands. The man may well be responsible for as high a bodycount as you attribute to the urbane and honeytongued Boss Ross.

To make an example from one of the Redeemed quests I have completed, part of what you are asked to do is to "save the souls" of three creatures using a "blessed" artifact created by someone the Redeemed say made some grave errors. When you complete this task and return to your questgiver, he outright admits that he doesn't know exactly what it is that you've done and continues on to admit that it has most likely "relieved them of their lives" in the process. If you make an arguement of Good = The Sanctity of Life (as we are conditioned to socially accept), you have been made party to an evil act. The fact that you relieved them of their lives in a pretty, soft way does not change the fact that you ended their lives.

It is pointed out that the Unredeemed wish to salvage and restore the Shadowlands in one resource-consuming stroke and the Redeemed wish to do the same thing, but in a long, drawn-out and conservative way. The end goal is basicly the same. The only real difference seems to be in their methodology. Oh. And in how pretty or ugly the faction happens to be. The fact that these factions cannot put aside their differences to achieve the common shared end goal only demonstrates that neither is superior from an ethical standpoint.

I feel that the Redeemed and Unredeemed echo in macrocosm the Clans and Omni struggle to which we are more accustomed. There is (to use those often abused and misused terms) good and evil contained in both factions. The choices we make are all, yet again, based on a very fallible socially programmed aesthetic. Among the player base you will find little difference between omni and clans. Both factions have their fanatics, their moderates, their saints and their griefers. The only difference seems to be what armors they wear and whether they do what they do to preserve their right to earn a respectable living with a multiplanetary megacorp or to establish their rights to live on RubiKa and earn their livings free of the aforementioned company.

I don't really mention the neutrals in this, as you can't really clump all the different people that make up the neutral population together under a common goal or banner. In the end, the neutrals pretty much find themselves in the role of the quest silvertails -- victims of the factional struggle doomed to be used or victimized by BOTH sides, with no recourse or benefit except that freedom to move on all territorries with the same ease or lack thereof that they enjoy anyway. That is until they are forced to choose between being abused by both sides and denied anything that makes playing come off of nightmare mode OR joining one of the two warring factions and escaping ONE side's constant abuse. Those who stay committed to their neutrality past level 100 deserve EVERYONE'S respect. Far from fence-sitting, the committed 100+ neutral has demonstrated a REMARKABLE strength of character and uncommon valour.

When they make a Helper vs Greifer factional conflict in the game, THEN I will show you a good versus evil scenario.

Just my 2000 credits.

You might know Rubi-Ka, the bright, red and dusty planet where Anarchy Online takes place 30,000 years in the future. What if Rubi-Ka was more than the political and economical center of the Galaxy, like today? What if it actually is the Cradle of Life in the galaxy? What if this Cradle, this land of ancient technology, hidden for eons in a metaphysical dimension, suddenly becomes accessible again? What if entities found in there are fighting an age-old conflict of light vs. dark - of preservation vs. exploitation?

Backstory. Mankind originated on Rubi-Ka many, billions of years ago. They were immensely powerful and being the children of the Source of Life and its highest creation, they did not feel the sting of death and through their extraordinary technology they ruled the entire galaxy from its center - their home world.

But they got greedy. They wanted to control the source of life itself! The original beings of the ancient Rubi-Ka split into two factions. One wanting to control, harness and extract - the others to let live, gather the surplus of the land, sow and reap- naturally, at the expense of their own power.

The extractor power faction won, and the weaker faction left in gigantic arks - fleeing what they knew was an impending disaster. The disaster didn't come at once though. The rulers built a gigantic machine, Ergo, to control the land more efficiently and drew more and more power from the Source - for thousands of years, until something cracked.

What happened is unsure (it is up to the players to discover) but almost all the powerful people of the land were killed and the rest expelled in a cataclysmic event. Those that were spared were only semi-intelligent breeds, engineered by the rulers to serve and obey.

Ever since then, the once-ruling Faction that became the Unredeemed has secretly plotted to return once more, to somehow lift the ban and armed with new knowledge finally Redeem themselves - by force if necessary. The other Faction, called the Redeemed, were the source of mankind, both on Earth and on many other planets. Like the Unredeemed, they all disappeared in the mist of time but descendants might still hold the true blood to rise to the challenge of the Unredeemed...

The Unredeemed traveled through the galaxy, killing one off-shoot of the Redeemed after the other, until there was only one place left - Earth. They kept it alive, because there they found a potential way to rule the universe once more. Through the citizens of Earth, they tried to return to Rubi-Ka once more by pumping power and meaning into Farmatek, a mega corporation on ancient Earth. They corrupted the power-hungry kindred souls of the so-called Omegas, which are humans made immortal through the rejuvenation technology of the Unredeemed. Through Farmatek, they laid the Earth to waste, spreading disaster to all life and cloaking it in darkness, while going into bunkers themselves. Thousands of years later when the Great Winter had passed, they emerged, and wielding superior weapons technology, they dominated the degenerated human civilization. With the guidance of the Unredeemed, the Omegas then founded Omni-Tek- the hyper-corporation that leases Rubi-Ka today! With the Omegas left on Earth as its ultimate rulers the Unreedemed thought the return home would be straightforward and easy. Not so.

Much of this is found in the backstory book written by Ragnar Tørnquist, called "Prophet Without Honor". You can read this on our home page, or purchase it!

This is the setting - the Unredeemed control the Omni-Tek Corporation through the Omega (not necessarily the Omni-Tek players, we shall see), while the Clan get sympathy from the Redeemed - or at least their petty servants in the Shadowlands.

This "mundane" outlook now explodes into a struggle for the soul of mankind and its very existence. Dark vs Light in its truest form."

Some words on multiwield
Multiwield works in a rather mysterious way, but there are a few aspects that are known

Attack Cycle

The attack cycle when single wielding is as follows:

1 - Begin attack (press Q)
2 - Attack winds up (wind up time is the attack time of the weapon)
3 - Attack bar reaches the top - weapon discharges
4 - Attack winds down (wind down time is the recharge time of the weapon)
5 - Attack bar reaches the bottom - return to step 2

When multi wielding, this is how it's processed:

1 - Begin attack (press Q)
2 - Right hand's weapon attack bar winds up (wind up time is the attack time of the right hand weapon)
3 - Attack bar reaches the top - right hand weapon discharges
4 - Left hand's weapon attack bar winds up (wind up time is the attack time of the left hand weapon)
5 - Attack bar reaches the top - left hand weapon discharges
6 - Both attack bars wind down (wind down period for BOTH weapons is the biggest recharge time of the two weapons, so if you have a weapon with 1s recharge and one with 2s recharge, the wind down period is 2 seconds)
7 - Both bars reach the bottom - return to step 2


Making use of this information, some clever adventurers computed the following example:

Let's imagine you're using pistols that do always 200 damage and are dual wieldable, their attack speed is 1s and their recharge speed is 1s

If you use just one weapon, you'll do 200 damage every two seconds, or 100 damage per second.

This is how it works:

0 seconds: attack winds up
1 second: damage
2 seconds: attack winds down


If you dual wield the two weapons, you'll do 200 damage in the first second, 200 damage in the second second, and both weapons wind down in the third second, as such:

0 seconds: Weapon 1 winds up
1 second: damage, weapon 2 winds up
2 seconds: damage
3 seconds: both weapons wind down

As such, you'll be doing 400 damage every 3 seconds, which translates to 133 damage per second.

From this we can infer that the gain in damage from using two weapons with the same damage and attack cycles (in other words, two equal weapons of the same QL) is 33% over single wielding.

Taking this reasoning further, we could say that, since each weapon does half that damage, or aproximately 67%, inessence each weapon is losing one third of it's damage potential when dual wielded.

Adapting this to the formula in the above guide, we could have that:

damage for dual wielding = max[(min damage weapon 1 * (1 + (attack rating / 400) + damage buffs)),
((random (min damage weapon 1 , max damage weapon 1 ) * (1 + (attack rating / 400))) - (target AC / 10) + damage buffs)] * 0,67 + max[(min damage weapon 2 * (1 + (attack rating / 400) + damage buffs)),
((random (min damage weapon 2 , max damage weapon 2 ) * (1 + (attack rating / 400))) - (target AC / 10) + damage buffs)] *0,67

Phew, hope that made any sense

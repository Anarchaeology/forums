Not Uber, But Equal
I've now played an Engie, an MP, and a Crat up to the low 40s. I gather that above that, it doesn't change qualitatively, just quantitatively - or am I wrong?

(In fact, that Engie was my first character. Played it up to 13, got burned out, switched to MA. Went back to pet users when I got to the level 50 doldrums. Once I'd been playing an MP and a Crat long enough to actually understand the game, came back, and found out that Engies rock; I was just stupid before.)

Blimp herding sucks. You think you have trouble getting over-sized bots through doors? MP pets get stuck in walls. (And because they hover so high, have the same problem Slayerdroids do with being too tall for doors. But MPs have this problem starting at level 1.)

Every half dozen rooms, you end up finding out that your healer pet got stuck three rooms back and you've got to go back to it, /pet behind, /pet heal all over again. (And somehow, it's almost always the healer pet, probably because they're slightly bigger).

Yes, MPs can self-buff into higher level pets. But among the effects of this is that MPs are constantly broke. A pet at half again to twice your level costs more money than you can possibly raise - that's why MPs charge through the roof for buffs. It starts to feel like having to buy a new Yalmaha every three levels.

Oh, and because you end up wanting to have macro bar buttons to separately control all three pets, setting up my MP's toolbars was a massive pain. I finally found a trick to save myself from having to redo one of them from scratch every few levels, when one of the default pet names changes. But it's still the only character I have where the first seven toolbars are almost entirely full.

But yeah, the truth is, if you can handle the annoyance factor from blimp herding, and you have the patience to refill your wallet at least once a week by spending an entire evening running MC/TS buffs and assembling implants, then yeah, MPs rule. They're actually over-powered. That's why Funcom isn't ever going to fix the mez pet.

As for the Crat - the first time you get "long charm," the long-duration mind control nano, you feel so uber it's sickening. But the long charm updates come few and far between, so they only work for about half of your career. Short charm has its virtues, but it's only barely a substitute for having a massively better pet. When I'm playing my Crat, I wish to Ghu that I had AC buffs. When I'm playing my Engie, I wish to Ghu I had calm nanos. It all comes out in the wash.

I will say this - the one time I prefer playing a Crat to both the other pet profs is when the first room has three Probe droids at yellow or above. I've yet to see what'll happen now that I finally got around to building an Aggro Trimmer, but with something with that high a Perception, I never managed to pull just one at a time. And with three of 'em at once, at least one is blowing my poor Engie out of the water. And first-aid kits only go so far.

A Crat, on the other hand, just casually walks in and tells his bot to attack one. Second one attacks the Crat, he short charms it, and sics it on the first. Third one he calms. He then yawns and enjoys the show. (And that's assuming he doesn't long charm one and short charm the next. Watching a mob go down in a two-way crossfire between Probe droids, while your barely-red guardian idiot takes occasional swings - now that's comedy.)

Generic piece of advice to both Engies and MPs: when you enter the first room (and finish rebuffing your pet(s)), /pet wait. Then open the door. Wait until you can catch the attention of only one mob. Make it chase you back into the entryway. Make absolutely sure that it can not shoot you, and do not shoot it yourself, until you are both back in the entry way. THEN tell your attack pet to sic him.

If you accidentally pull all three, leave the bot/blimps, and run right out of the mission. When you come back, only one of them will probably still be there. Maybe none of them, because in "wait" mode, they seem to mistake my bot for a piece of avant garde sculpture or something, and ignore it. (If only this worked on Probe droids. With them, it works just fine - until the first one gets hit, at which point the other two rush right to me even if they're thirty meters, a door, and two walls away.)

Everyone seems to want to know what the role of the engineer is. It's simple. Who takes the first hit on Tarasque? Not the MP. His mez pet won't keep him as safe as the engineer's aggro trimmer.

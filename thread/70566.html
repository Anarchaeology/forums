Laws and Coffee
(OOC: This post is intended for those very few who actually read the thesis and are questioning its integrity, its meaning, ect. If you havent read it yet, I urge you to. It did take some thinking and I dont think enough people have read it to justify the time it took to write heh. Ill post more tomorrow, and I hope to see some replies to this as I'm quite tired and want to get some sleep. If your interesting in reading the thesis: http://forums.anarchy-online.com/sho...threadid=69325))

It was a quiet evening. Neon lighted streets blew down their blinding lights as he walked down the huge walkways. Broken streets and winds added to his disappointment, his feet set on reaching a place of relaxation. It was the end of the day for him, work had just concluded. Something buzzed passed him. He didn't care to look. He just kept walking, walking, and walking, until he reached it. He struggled up the ramp and entered the musty bar.

Hello, he thought. The warmness of the place was inviting. He removed his coat, revealing yet another layer of heavy clothing. Was it really that cold? he thought once again. He shrugged, throwing his coat onto a chair and stepping into yet another room. Noise filled his ears, the people gathered, and smoked blocked out the already little lighting their was in the room. This was it.

He took his usual seat. The place was windowless, he liked it. The seat was in the back corner, away from the troubles and away from civilization. Just what he needed after a long day of slaving over a desk. Somebody approached him, giving a slight nod. He knew what it meant.

Coffee, please. , he said.

No more words were spoken. Within a minute of him speaking a coffee was at the small table in front of him, steaming into the endless abyss above it. He looked at it. Pure blackness, filled to the top of a spotless white glass. Black and white. He picked it up, taking slow steady sips as he reviewed the day in his mind. He was startled by what came next.

Mr. Griffel?, a voice called to him.

He turned his head slowly towards the direction of the voice, glancing at the tall figure cloaked in a white coat. He arced a brow.

Mr. Griffel, hello. Mind if I take a seat? , the voice said.

Before he could speak the person sat down without permission, removing his coat and letting out a slight sigh. What? he thought to himself, taking another sip of the coffee.

I hear your a man of the law, so to speak. Of course, by reading your thesis, I'm in no way calling you an Omni-Pol officer. You seem to have a strong desire for it, no? From what I've see at least, I do believe you are the one I need to speak to.

So much for relaxation, he thought. A slight sigh as he took one last sip of the coffee, setting it back on the table, he started to reach into his pocket when the person, apparently a man, offered him a cigarette. How did he know that? He shrugged, accepting and taking the cigarette in his hands.

Like I said before, your the one I wish to speak to. I read your thesis Mr. Griffel, and I agree. But some do not, and I want to hear your response to their questions the man said.

Now he was getting a bit irritated. Was this some kind of interview? He'd play along.

Questions, of course. Ask away. he said.
Very well, Mr. Griffel. In your thesis, you said the following:
And that is what exists in Rubi-Ka. Pure anarchy, where laws are shadowed by the actions of the people, where corruption runs through the streets in the form of blood, where people die and people live with the constant fear of death and prosecution without representation. I still cannot look past the fact that people are constantly being accused of crimes that they might not of committed. I laugh at the integrity and sureness that Omni-Pol, Omni-Admin, and all the branches of Omni-Tek law have in their accusations.

Now, many would dis-agree. First of all, the statements you make about some of the highest authorities of Omni-Tek is disturbing. You point your thesis to all factions yet you chose to pick on the ones that you affiliate yourself with. I'm just curious: why?

He glanced in the mans eyes, curious at where this was going. The man seemed a little bit too interested, but still he'd play along with this game.

Well, sir, allow me to say I don't intend on changing anything in the thesis to suit you or anybody else who disagrees. As far as only picking on the organizations in Omni-Tek is because they are the only ones familiar to me. I'm not a prejudice person, sir. I do believe Clans and Neutrals and Omni-Tek all have equally convincing arguments.

He smiled, that seemed nice enough. The man was fiddling with his wristcomm, glancing at some message on it.

I must run Mr. Griffel. If you are here tomorrow, we'll speak again.

With that, the man ran off. He laughed a bit, once again picking up the coffee and taking a sip.

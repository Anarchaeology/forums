quote:
Originally posted by Revolutionary


I never fought these mobs, so I'm not sure...Can someone tell me why this would be an exploit? It appears to me that its simply taking advantage of a weapon's range, just like using a LR weapon and a root/calm/snare.
Usually, any tactic that gives you reward without risk is considered exploiting. You'll find that this sort of philosophy is present in every mmorpg. What’s happening in this situation is these water-based creatures are unable to come up on land to where the player is shooting from. This is really no different than when players used to be able to shoot at a range further than a monster could detect them. The end result was reward without risk. There must be enough risk involved to balance out the reward a player is getting.

Honestly, in real life it would be smart for someone to kill a shark by shooting at it from land. However, you don’t gain XP, go up levels, and gain new abilities by doing this sort of thing in real life, so you shouldn't compare the two situations. Also, in reality these water based creatures should be intelligent enough to counter this sort of attack in some way (fleeing, diving deeper, hiding, etc). Unfortunately AI in games just isn’t up to the level of sophistication to handle every player created plan. Thus, rules are made.

In Everquest they went the simple route of making it so you couldn’t interact with something in the water if you were on land, and visa versa. This applied to attacks, heals, etc. If you wanted to fight something that was in the water, you had to get into the water where it could attack you back. If you stood on top of a house and rained spells down a monster that couldn’t reach you, it would teleport up to your position on the house. Seems lame, but not as lame as the idea of a monster just standing there letting you kill it because it doesn’t have the ability to figure out an alternative to just standing there and soaking up damage.

Actually, medium sized stars do not go supernova. In fact, our sun will not even go Nova. To make a star go Nova, it must be approximentally 4 times the mass of our sun. To make a star go Supernova, the star must be at least 10 times the mass of our sun.

A supernova is caused by the star dying -- it burns up most of its fuel (hydrogen which fuses into Helium in Nuclear Fusion). When that happens, things in the core start to fuse together -- first the helum fuses into Carbon, and then the Carbon fuses into Iron -- a very dense and energy absorbing substence. This new fusion is caused by the sun's gravity collapsing in on itself -- without the force of full nuclear fusion of the Hydrogen into Helium, the star starts to collapse. This emmense pressure creates heat - the tempeture inside the star when it fuses into Iron is appromentally 700-800 million degrees Celcius. (Rather hot.) The materials continue to fuse into super-dense materials. These, through an unknown process, absorb much of the energy from the gravitational compression, and then the star goes critical when the gravity can no longer support the every growing energy in the center. When it pushes away from the core a bit, it sets off a chain reaction because the pressure from gravity decreases the farther away the materials are from the core, and therefore, it continues to excellerate until the entire cloud engulfs approximentally 20 lightyears worth of space, generally forming into nebulas which are the birthplace of new stars.

Our sun, however, when it runs out of fuel, will turn into a Red Giant -- this is because the mass is not enough to stop the compression until it Supernovas. So, it turns into a Red Giant. This Red Giant will engulf the Earth -- the sun will be that large. (The radious of which would be around 100 million miles wide). However, it isn't very dense -- though eventually it burns away almost all of its energy, making the gravity once again take control. This then forces the sun to compress into a White Dwarf - which is incrediably dense. It continues to fuse the few remaining bits of hygroden until it finally burns out -- turning it into a Black Dwarf. A dead star.

Tradeskill Soapbox (Weaponcrafting)
A long, critical essay on trade skills - weaponsmithing in particular:

a) Trade skills can add value to the game. Conceptually, they provide dimensions for character development, challenge & achievment, rewards (financial, experience, and item capability), and social interaction.

b) In order for them to be viable, there needs to be some economic balance between challenge, cost, time invested, and reward(s). Currently, this seems to be out of balance making them unattractive.

Here is my reasoning, based on my experience so far (focused on weaponsmithing):

For many items, aside from a base kit, as many as a dozen component items need to be acquired. Because of the need to keep quality levels close (either to be high enough to work with a given kit or low enough so as not to waste credits), the wide range of QLs, the wide variety of component items, and the relatively long time between "store restocks" (a change in the items and QLs available in a given store machine), simply acquiring the correct set of components for one complete "kit set" can be extremely challenging, time consuming, and credit consuming.

Then, presuming there is not some unexplainable bug preventing the kit set from being correctly assembled, the result is an item that could be acquired directly from a store machine. But for FAR less credit outlay than building it. Let alone the time commitment - simply saving up credits and checking stores over the same amount of time is much more likely to get you the same item at the desired QL, along with a lot more credits still in your pocket. (Trade skill-only items are an obvious exception, but must therefore result in a superior net in item capaiblity.)

In other words, the financial/item capability reward levels are negative. (Although the achievment may be cause for a minor celebration, since it is so difficult to even compile a full kit set!) Had the time and effort been spent in simply hunting or mission runs, the experience and credit gains would be far, far higher. Essentially, this is a negative economic return.

What could possibly motivate someone to thus invest the time and go through the frustration? In EQ, certain tradeskills were worth a negative economic return in the beginning - because of a primary difference - the only effective way to increase a trade skill level in EQ was actual "practice" of the skill. Negative short term economic return was actually an "investement" in the long term return - the ability to create powerful or profitably marketable items. This is not the case in AO, where the only thing needed to increase a trade skill is gaining levels and distributing IPs. It begs the question why there would even be low QL (1-20) kits, since the cost (in credit, time, and frustration) of creating even a simple item will be high, the result will be an item that can at best be given away to a newbie or sold at a great loss to a not-quite newbie. And there will be no improvement in the crafter's skills to create higher QL, more desireable items in the future.

From personal experience, I began gathering items, both from keeping loot items and more often searching out and buying "needed" items, for 3 different kit sets, over my 4th - 17th level gains. What I wound up with is a bank full of misc. items, a perpetually tight budget, lots of travel and shopping that was time I could have spend acquiring experience and credits, but finally two complete kit sets. (I made the mistake, on the third set, of trying to build a SMG, and have found that SMG kits can almost never be found and have never seen one below QL 30.) For one of those two complete sets, I've found I made an error on the QL of one of the components compared to the kit, and have now "suspended" construction on that partially complete kit while I find a replacement part. On the other, I have run into an unexplained inability to combine the 3rd component into the kit. It is the correct component for the recipe and is of appropriate QL for the kit. There is no explaination for the failure other than "These items cannot be combined in this way. Maybe the order was wrong?"

This time, effort, expenditure, and frustration have forced me to "give up" on trade skills, at least for now. I think I've given them more than a fair shake, since they've been the focus of probably 35% of my game time and much more % of my credit expenditure. But I do think several things could be done to bring trade skills back to "positive value":

1) Change one or both of the following: increase how frequently stores restock, or rework the selection/mix of items and QLs involved in a restock. (It's just silly to always have 15-20 different QLs of "xyz construction kit" but only 1-2 QLs each of only a small fraction of the dozens of different component parts.) The goal of this change is to reduce, but not entirely eliminate, the challenge of gathering a set of component parts for a given recipe.

My personal opinion is you should always have at least a 25% chance of finding ANY part within +/- 10% QL of your target in any "shopping center," every time the shops restock. That means shopping centers need to hold about 1/4 of the total possible combinations of all trade skill items at all QLs for the QL "range" of the shopping center (basic, advanced, superior, etc.).

2) Reverse the inequity between the cost of components for creating a given item and the price of the same item whole at equal QL. Ideally, if you were able to find all of the components at exactly the QL of the construction kit (or within +/-10%), the total component cost of the item would be less than the cost of just purchasing it whole at the same QL. Enough less to justify the time/effort of crafting, and leave some room for player-sale market profit! (Since there is no "practice value" to using a skill, you need be able to craft profitably even at low QLs.)

3) Increase the number of "trade skill only" recipes, and make sure the resulting items are more desireable than substitute items at comparable QLs. (For example, the trade skill only SMGs should be more desireable than the Mausser or Suppressor when QLs are equal.)

4) Personal fixer gripe: Where are the damn SMG kits? I can find a bazillion Shotgun kits every time, but no SMG kits! (Fixer skill costs encourage investing in trade skills, and naturally we're going to want to craft the one type of weapon that we use best...)

5) If there are bugged kits/recipes, fix them! For assembly failures, give a CLEAR reason why the components can't be assembled! (Only reasons I can think of are QL mismatch, incorrect combination of parts, incorrect sequence of parts, or skill deficiency - so you should ALWAYS say which problem IS occuring, not that one MIGHT be occurring.)

I think if these issues were addressed in future patches, the desireability of trade skill use will increase. If the economics just naturally get better at higher levels (admittedly 17 is still relatively low in AO), then there's really no need for all the low-QL components and kits clogging up stores.

Make tradeskills the game enriching field they could be!

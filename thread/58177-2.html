Greetings All,

I just have a few short comments to make in regards to this issue.

Most everyone before me has said all that really can be said about the initial topic. When you have a situation that is born, exists, and is expounded upon in the public eye, then there will always be issues that arrise with those on the outside 'wanting a piece of the pie', so to speak.

Some people bake their own pies, others try to steal a piece. Unfortunately, the more negative aspects of real life are reflected much more brightly in-game, because of the relative anonymity afforded in the digital medium. Funcom does what it can to enforce these rules, but often, it's not enough.

What Nepentheia pointed out is Funcom's latest attempt to rehash the rules and bring them into public light. Of course, those rules are much easier to see on the forums, as everything said here typically remains, to be seen by others (including the moderators).

In game, issues of harassment are much more difficult to prove. No number of eyewitnesses seem sufficient, and short of an ARK or other Customer Service personnel witnessing an event first-hand, little can be done about it.

That; I feel, is a shortcoming of the game design, and one that could easily be rectified. EverQuest had a /report command that captured the 10 visible lines of text on your screen; along with other information about the sender, and forwarded it a queue that the game's GMs could access. That information could not be altered, and was often used to settle disputes and punish those guilty of harassment. Funcom could easily implement a similar system in AO.

Speaking to Bonefish a moment, I don't think that Nepentheia's post was directed to you; but rather indicated that there is a framework in place regarding these situations. I don't know about anyone else, but I liked the "non-politically correct" post, much better.

Now, to Aktrez, regarding your comment...

In reading the guideliness that were posted, I don't see issues of exclusiveness stated anywhere. It's written in such a way as to encompass all players of the game, and posters in the forum here.

That said, you must consider that you have placed yourself in the public eye. As such, you've opened yourself to alot of issues that others might not have to deal with. Well known members of society must often deal with far greater issues of criticism, scrutiny, and invasion of privacy. Often times, yes, those things do come close to, or pass into, the realm of harassment.

I'm not saying that's something that should happen, or even be tolerated; simply that it does, and that's the way life is. Alot of it too, depends on how you respond to a situation like that. Do you try to ignore it? Do you bring it into the public forum? Do you step back and let friends try to defend you? It's much harder to victimize someone who doesn't present themselves as a victim.

None of those ways really address the problem, sadly. Until Funcom gives us better tools to deal with, report, and end harassment, we're all stuck doing what we can to deal with the ugliness, in the meantime.

In closing, I'd just like to wish everyone luck in their endeavors; especially those which work with the story that Funcom has presented to us. Hopefully Funcom will aid us all further with better tools, in the near future.

- Velek

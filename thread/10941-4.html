Good job making the best out of a bad situation
I work for a computer networking company & I understand how complicated these things can get.

I'm proud of FC for investigating, once it was obvious something had gone wrong. How were they to know in the beginning of this that someone with access to their servers had used bad judgement & move a character?

I totally understand the reason FC has for refusing to move characters, even on a case by case basis. It would be a real challenge to make a tool to do it automaticly, and any manual move would definately take time & careful thought.

I have a suggestion for how this could be done fairly. I heard from a friend who plays EQ what they have started doing. This policy makes tons of sense.

EQ will do case by case transfers under a few strict rules

1. There is a $50 fee for the transfer
2. The character can not take ANY items with them, including money, and their bank does not move.
3. They reserve the right to not make transfers if they don't want to. This gives them the freedom to check things out & say no if there is any technical, or political reason.
4. The $50 is per move, per character, if someone wants to move back, OK, that will be $50.

I think this setup is really fair, I believe they also do not gurantee how long transfers may take, as it has to be done by humans who have other things to do, and things like server patches, emergencies will de bone before character transfers.

Just an idea, but I would rather see my Fixer fixed, than get a character moved.

$50 might be a bit harsh and then you'd still get the people who think it's their god-given right to get it for free.

Thinking about it a bit more, I think one of the primary reasons might be duplicate names. We know FC can already dupe players (for testing purposes). Now whether that's on a single-server or only to the test-server is unknown. But if they can do it across servers, there's still that pesky problem of name collisions that turn up if they try to automate it.

Maybe a compromise is in order. Let those that don't have name collisions do a one-time transfer from the login-shell. Check names, dupe the character, verify, and then destroy the old one. All steps must be verified in order for the old character to be destroyed and the new char to be available to ensure integrity. However, most of the basic functionality seems to be in-game already.

Now for those that have name collisions, maybe have the option to append something to their name or pick an entirely new name later on. That might be a bit more to program in though. But at least they might be able to cover the rest with an automated program now.

I'm not saying it's easy to do. Even small but critical things like this take a lot of testing, testing and some more testing, but if they restrict it to their login-shell, they can test it to their hearts content in-house since it doesn't really modify the client handler in anyway.

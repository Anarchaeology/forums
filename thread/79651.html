Canto
Supa Leet
"Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic." Arthur Clarke

Imagine a world 30,000 years in the future where death is not permanent and matter is almost infinitely manipulable via microscopic machines.

It is difficult to predict what characteristics a particular culture will find aesthetically pleasing at a given point in time.

The "high fantasy" aspects of AO could be reflections of the aesthetic sensibilities of the culture. For instance, in a world where matter is easily manipulated, killing is a way of life, and death not permanent, why would all weapons look like "guns?" Guns look like guns today because they have to. If they could look like cool swords or a chocolate cake, some would.

In such a world, why wouldn't the aesthetic sensibilities of the nanotech scientists be reflected in their creations (monsters)? See Eldon Tyrell's quarters in "Blade Runner" for a reference.

This is why the argument that "such and such a profession shouldn't be good at such and such" is bogus. Applying a modern day definition of "doctor" or "trader" to a culture 30,000 years in the future is counterproductive. The game uses those names to avoid other types of confusion. A trader, for instance, is really closest to a "vampire" in a 21st century understanding, a creature that sucks the life out of others to become stronger,but to name the profession as such would have created several types of other problems.

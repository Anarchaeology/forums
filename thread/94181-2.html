Clan Militancy?
Foremost, and to clarify the legal principles underlying this debate:

(i) pursuant to the terms of the Rubi-Ka Lease, Omni-Tek Corporation of Rubi-Ka, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Omni-Tek Corporation, was conferred, inter alia, the duty to ensure the safety, stability and security of all corporate assets (including employees and the land of Rubi-Ka). It is for this reason that Omni-Pol was formed, and it has full right and authority to conduct investigations, interrogations, arrests, etc., in any district or area of Rubi-Ka as it sees fit.

(ii) despite the assertions of various clan members, the city of Athen (also referred to as Omni-2) is situate on lands that are legally-entitled to Omni-Tek Corporation of Rubi-Ka, as a result of the annulment of the Tir Accord. The only area of Rubi-Ka whose status is somewhat in de jure dispute is the city of Tir, due to the existence of the Clans Rights Treaty and the current occupation of said city by the Sentinels.

(iii) any citizen, regardless of political affiliation, who threatens or actually commits acts of violence against a member or members of Omni-Pol are thereby committing a crime by threatening the safety, stability and security of a corporate asset or assets (as the case may be), and may be subject to questioning, arrest and/or incarceration, should the investigating officer(s) see fit.

Secondly, I find it quite striking that two members of the Rubi-Ka Mining Cooperative would undertake such bold measures as to (i) declare all members of Omni-Pol persona non-grata within the city limits of Athen; (ii) overtly state that, should an officer of Omni-Pol violate this illegal declaration, such officer should be ceremonially and without question assaulted for so doing; and (iii) utter a direct threat against all members of Omni-Pol to the effect that, no matter wheresoever situate or located, the threatener will commit an act of violence there against.

One of the guiding principles of the Rubi-Ka Mining Cooperative is that there are no factional lands; it is a deregulated market, after all. It is thus quite puzzling that Ms. Wright, for example, would be so bold as to declare Omni-Pol is not welcome any more in Athensor any other clan territory.

Note that I make no judgment as to General Pierces (or any other officers) actions. As I stated previously, if you do have a founded complaint,, you must address this through the proper channels, lest this erupt into consequences beyond all of our nightmares.

I have had a number of dealings with General Pierce on a professional level (and my sister, Emiliy, seems to look up to her), and I can honestly and truly say that General Pierce has always conducted herself in accordance with her legally-entrusted duty.

OOC in reply to Templus:

I never mentioned you by name, though I have seen you around West Athen a lot. My problem is with everyone who ignores the gas status to RP getting into fights they shouldn't be able to get into, or to strut around in the opposing faction's areas as they please while griefing and being annoying.

I used your organization and an incident that I witnessed as examples, though yes, I do single your organization out as one of the worst abusers of suppression gas that I have witnessed. No, I don't see everything, nor do I try to. But the thread's topic is Omni-Pol in West Athen. And, to be blunt: I don't see any other Omni RP-oriented orgs coming to WA on missions involving violence against other players in non-PvP gas, and I'm around WA quite a lot.

For some reason, given the heavy NPC guard presence in most Omni grid and whompa areas, I doubt that there is quite the same level of Clan activity in comparable Omni areas as there is Omni activity (not limited to Omni-Pol) in West Athens.

OOC in general:

What bothers me is this: The suppression gas is a wildly unrealistic workaround for the simple fact that PvP involves too much griefing and "foul play" to be permitted without strict limits, especially in player gathering places and areas that are important to newbies, like cities. Furthermore, most people dislike PvP and, given the choice, will congregate in areas where it isn't permitted. So, it's not feasible from a game design perspective to allow PvP in cities.

But, that also allows people who "shouldn't" be able to hang around in cities like West Athen, or Rome, or Omni-Ent, to do so. Even though it makes no sense at all - a Clanner in Omni-Ent would be arrested and sent to Omni-Reform, exiled back to Clan territory, or shot. An Omni in West Athen would face a similar fate. But except for NPC guards, which are fairly avoidable and at high levels are reasonably easily defeated, there's no game mechanic for this.

In a way, 75% gas in particular is especially nefarious from an In-Character RP perspective: In-Character, there's no difference between an Mob NPC human, and a player character. The player Omni-Pol should be able to help their NPC comrades, just like the player Clans should be able to help the NPC Blades and Sentinels, when the guards get attacked.

Despite these (and other) shortcomings, however, gas still is part of the story and background, and no player or organization of players should attempt to claim "one-way" immunity from the gas's limitations as part of its roleplay. This especially applies when operating in the opposing faction's (or Neutral) territory, and in my mind just represents acknowledgement that gas is a hack, and that we can't have it both ways. Either we all can attack, or we all can't. And, the game says, can't.

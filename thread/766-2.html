Supply and Demand
Great ideas! I really would like to see more playable weaponsmiths and other traders in Rubi-Ka. It would greatly improve game's depth.

I just want to remind that there must be also demand for your trade items and services. Currently there is great demand for implant builders, because only practical way to build high level implants is to asks someone who is specialized to building build them. Jewelrycraft works because you can increase your money income that way (or give your selfmade ring for nearest girl). And trimmer creation, because you can save money by building your own trimmers (well... I haven't never tested this, but I assume that this is true).

But for weaponsmithing and nanocrystal creation there is only little or no demand. There is many reasons for this:
1) Good loot and mission rewards. For normal player's this is most efficient way to get equipment.
2) Too much money/Too hard to find trader. It is much more faster to go shop and buy what you need, than find a trader who has right equipment and skills needed.

And especially for weapons (and maybe someday for armors...):
3) Overequiping. There is no demand for items lower than quality level 70. How many weaponsmiths there is who can build quality level 100 items?
4) Guilds. "Only bad thing in my guild is: I will get everything from there"

Allowing traders/smiths to make more powerful items and limiting some items for player build only, would increase demand. It seems like this is going to be happen for crystals. One problem in this is: if supply is too small, this might cause hyper inflation for player build only items. And thus, these items would be unavailable for average player. Maybe there could be more tradeitems and partially build items in missions and loot?

Making service finding easier would also probably increase demand. Maybe there could be ingame items, like banners with the text "I make implants". And also experience reward should be increased (but not too much), so that there is more traders and smiths.

Overequiping is too hot topic too this message so I leave it away. ... but if there is going to be dramatical changes in this topic, there should be also long transition time.

One solution for guilds and their "heirloom" items could be item's "decay rate". Maybe long used items could lose the quality slowly or increase their chance to break. However this shouldn't happen too fast, because this would make high level/cost items almost useless. There could be also item repairing tradeskills.

As a RL software designer, I don't believe that there is going to be "custom build" weapons or item's decay rate soon. Implementing and designing that kind features would be enourmous task. And also it would most likely cause bugs, unstability and ingame inbalance...

I think that Funcom is trying to make a supply-demand chain here. They want that we (adventurers/soldiers/enforcers and so on...) would bring all trade goodies what we can found (or get by other evil ways), for non-combat traders and engineers who could "process" these items further and sell them back to us, with a small profit of course...

Currently this supply-demand chain doesn't work, because:
1) There are too many different kind trade items out there. How could stupid Atrox know that <insert trade item here> is needed, when building <insert most wanted Atrox weapon here>? Also trade item's quality level limits seriously its usefulness.
2) There is no efficient way to tell for that Atrox, that some trader could use that item. And there is no efficient way to tell, which trader might need that item. (Maybe this is impossible... after all he/she/it is Atrox...)

Making item building simpler and lowering quality level limit could solve problem 1. It could also make easier for poor Atrox to find that trader who wants his/her/its items, because their would be more demand for the items...

Solving problem 2 would need some kind advertisement method for the traders and smiths, BUT not too powerful. If there were too powerful methods like "global bidding system", it would cause that there is space for only couple serious traders/smiths in game's ecosystem. Maybe there could be city specific "local bidding system" and fixers could have access to the global system. That way there would space for also average trader or smith....

If all trade items would be too easily accessible in the shops, it would also mean that instead "loot-adventurer-trader-adventurer" supply-demand chain (which doesn't currently work), there would be only "shop-trader-adventurer" chain. Damn... there could also be "loot-adventurer-fixer-trader-fixer-adventurer" chain.

I think that only some of the trade items should be easily accessible in the shops. Maybe those items, which are most important when produced item's quality is decided (this is not true currently, because all items are needed to be almost same quality). This way there also would be space for more complex supply-demand chains.

Why? Complex (and working) supply-demand chain would increase player interaction, which normally means more fun. It would also create more variety in the equipment, because there would be some rare trade items, and this way we could avoid "clone armies" (Do you remember freedom arms?)...

These proposals don't solve all problems what I listed in my first post. There is still no demand for low level trade items, almost no demand for player build items (because all items are available at shops and there is too much money) and there is no natural mechanism that will remove some of the items (like weapons and armours) from the ecosystem.

quote:
Originally posted by Resetgun
Oh... and I forgotted that Funcom has a way to remove items from the ecosystem without "decay rate". They just introduce new and better armors and weapons in each patch, which makes current armors and weapons obsolete ... (I think that they forgotted those uber pistols from their last patch...)
To this day, I've never seen an MMORPG get this right, and find a solution to in-game inflation. It's not necessarily connected to trade skills: money enters the system on monsters, as loot, as mission rewards, and so forth. It only leaves the system when people buy things from the shop terminals, and when items get destroyed. So, arguably, the only way to save the economy is to introduce some form of upkeep cost that actively affects the players. Ammo is too cheap for this, especially at high levels: in fact, since player profit increases with level, the upkeep also has to increase with level.

The best solution that I can see is to introduce item decay. Make some 'automated' system for repairing things, like a shop terminal, that charges cash for the repair. Then, allow player engineers / traders / whatever to do fixes too: but make it so that doing the repair costs them money, much less than the shop terminals, but a little nonetheless. Either make the process consume some cheap commodity that can only be bought from the shops, or make the relevant nano formula(s) cost credits to activate. They'll be charging other players for their services, and they should be able to charge less than the store price and still turn a profit: but there's another useful application for trade skills, another way for players to gain money and experience without entering combat, and a built-in money sink to save the economy as well!

Time to Die

Now...

Shifting the position of his head caused a few moments of amusement through what kept him to that small, compartment like room. Flicking saliva from his tongue, watching the evaporation wisps fade amongst the atmosphere that seemed as heavy as the binding that encased his wrists. They did not trust him. They feared what he could do perhaps. That he could escape from where they placed restraint on him once the effect of the drugs boosted into his veins began to thin? Not a single being, in period of two days, had been to keep watch on him, to feed him, to offer subtle means to get answers they obviously wanted from him.

All in all, a quiet secluded and almost peaceful little hole. His cage. Clicking into life, a small red light was born and registered in the mind of Gabriel almost instantly when his eyes recorded it from a corner from the ceiling. Monitor Officers were awake. Nostrils opened partly, tasted the air and detected heated coffee. It was morning and those watching him would soon slip away, under knowledge that their superiors would never know, to find other more interesting things to follow than continually stare at transmitted images of a man who never moved from his seated position, who now and then moved his head to what could be in responce to perhaps expecting company that would never come.

Not yet.

Eyes became focused. Half closed eyelids filtered the outside world partially to keep the surface moist. Thoughts began spilling across murky ground, common ground, certainly to those reclawed marks trailing savagely toward and away worlds created and destroyed with each pulse that echoed across the illustrative offering. Sliding flesh and hollow bones cried out to hold a non physical being, redeemer to them all. Each stored in a twisted form of safety net to be collected and used as and when a situation called for them. To release that power.

Failing signs of hot neon buzzed fiercly outward, reaching to pull him in. Cries of fools hopes, laughing in a lie to themselves that all is good, all is well... when the chill settled softly upon the spine. Raked tendons reacted to the serrated and hooked blades of fear that shredded away the skin of protective faith, spasming across muscle of non-existant truths and snapped and twisted out the bones of what kept them alive... they're alive?... That familiar projected voice reversed its way up that spiral pathway at the speed of an opened and revisited memory.

The dream of energies that fueled and burnt deep within his own designed furnace, violently wrenching back the initial thought it hungered for. It gathered for a second as it spun to a searing heat before releasing itself back up the tunnel, faster and faster toward true recollection and then exploded in his mind in full content with all it's dark, now contained, glory.

The dream had begun.

She strolled through the narrow, shady streets of Omni-1 Trade District, unable to focus on anything more serious than the blue, nearly cloudless sky, the chill air smelling of salt from the sea close by, making the city an artificial peninsula.

Dressed lightly, a white, knee-length, silky dress that she felt rustling on her pale skin with the occasional breeze, she would have danced if there had been music playing.
For a moment there Michelle felt right. One of those days you wanted to hop into your Yalm and fly, enjoy life as it was, forget war and loss or really fly with the wind stroking the feathers of your wings. The trace of a smile played on her lips.
Malachii was buried under concrete and steel, the Omni-Pol detention site.

And she had found him or the other way round

Her feet stopped walking suddenly. A veil put before the suns, covering the sky above with an unnatural gloom. She gasped for air, like a punch into her stomach blurring shapes or skyscrapers and a deadly coldness.
The people around her didnt seem to notice the change, no one stopped to look at her and she even heard a girl laugh but dampened like her head was wrapped up in cotton.
Space seemed to bend around one single spot a few meters away a black hole, contorting hope into despair so easily, swallowing voraciously light, feel and warmth, sucking up every notion of comfort that was in her just a few seconds before.

A little leet sat there, unmoving. She felt drawn to it. The air started to vibrate. Hostile, threatening and at the same time familiar voices were piercing her head as she stepped closer when all she wanted to do is turn around and run away screaming. But her lips just moved in silent agony, her eyes were widened in terror.

It had no eyes.
A void, darkness no, not completely. An evil will crawled inside, a will to destroy where things were built, to devour where seeds hope were about to grow.
And so cold

She fell on her knees before the mockery of an animal.

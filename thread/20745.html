Roleplay Credibility
Roleplaying is not made easy for lower-level characters. While a level 150 character can basically roleplay anything (s)he wants and be taken seriously, a level 30 character can't. Lower level characters are forced to play as subordinates to the higher level ones due to the nature of the game. People won't take things seriously if a level 15 guild president declares war against some other guild.

I have some great RP ideas. But I can't get past this credibility barrier. How am I supposed to play someone with more influence than bumbling Cadet Jones if my character is at a level that only fits a cadet?

I'm sure you all know what I mean. Someone's of course going to tell me "you're wrong, there's plenty of people who will roleplay and disregard your level", but in this case "plenty" will never mean more than a handful.

I really don't want to spend the time and effort to get Jones up to level 150 so that I can roleplay and be taken seriously. But since that's my only option, he'll have to still be bumbling Cadet Jones for the next couple months...

Advice?

True roleplaying requires that you accept the physical limitations that you are bound by in the world you play in. RP doesn't mean you can do anything you want. Even in RP worlds, there are restrictions. RP means that you take the scenario presented to you and construct a personality to function within.

As such, of course no one is going to pay attention to you if you're not powerful enough to intimidate. Doesn't mean you can't still roleplay to the fullest extent. You just need to RP that your skills aren't developed yet.

Everyone wants to be a chief.... the real good roleplayers understand that you can be an indian and roleplay as well.

Just RP or RP/Level
The problem with what you're talking about is you then negate the other person's commitment to the game and the hard work they've put in completing missions and making a name for themselves as someone on Rubi-Ka.

Now, I'm not saying your wrong, but in an environment where those campaign ribbons (levels, seasons, what have you) mean something it's hard for someone to just up and accept that you. Cadet Jones are in fact Mr. Jones, President of Omni-Tek Lawn and Manicuring.

This problem can be overcome with a lot of time spent on your hand roleplaying out what it is you think this Mr. Jones should be. You have to convince enough other people that in fact, just because Mr. Jones is not combat hardened does not mean Mr. Jones is not someone to contend with. In fact he has many contacts and resources at hand.

Unfortunately it's just the nature of the beast at this point. The other thing you might consider is finding people who ARE higher level and get them in on your ideas. You then would have a way to facilitate the storylines you have in mind.

A problem that I have with that Bionitrous is that in a real world situation you can usually tell if someone's bluffing. Experienced people walk differently, hold themselves differently, have their weapons in easier to reach places, never sit with their backs to the doors, etc.

A character that had experienced and survived such things would have minor but noticable differences from someone who was just bluffing.

I agree, players can't make a good roleplaying MMORPG without the help of the company and vice versa. It's a nasty, never-ending cycle.

I agree that it is the impossible dream, I've seen enough of the 1st generation MMORPGs to have a pretty good idea. I remember how psyched I was when I got into the Ultima Online beta:

"I love Ultima! Finally, a chance to RP there! With other people who enjoy it as much as I do!"

A month later the beta ends (a month long beta? No wonder there were/are so many bugs and cheats!) and the next week I'm assaulted by SuPaK1lla and his spiritual brothers enmass. The roleplaying community on UO thrashed around for a bit, became more active for time, and by now, I'm sure it has pretty much died out.

There can be more to playing this game (or any other MMORPG) than level, level, level ... the problem is, the average person does not know how to roleplay. It's not an easy thing for the average person to do. It means you have to be able to step in someone else's shoes, something that is very difficult for anyone to do. I'm just thankful I got started with dice and AD&D manuals at a young age. Roleplaying isn't used by psychologists (and by trusting lovers in the bedroom) for nothing ... it is a powerful tool that can teach you more about yourself and other people than most people have the guts or inclination to learn. It's a sobering event, learning that the world does not revolve around YOU. (It's also the beginning of true adulthood.)

A company can't make l33t d00ds into roleplayers. A company can only try to cater to the roleplayers, maybe try to find a way to lift l33ts to that next level by sucking them in, interesting them. Rest assured, if you don't want to roleplay, you won't. Or at least won't do it well. It is a learned skill, polished by practice. If saying that makes me elitist, so be it. True roleplayers are a whole other breed entirely from your average gamer. Imagination, empathy, acting ability, the ability to separate fantasy from reality, character from OOC.

MMORPGs have many interfaces to help RPers roleplay. Emotes, OOC channels, etc. But if you seriously want to roleplay, I suggest you get pen-and-paper, dice, and a gamemaster who will cater the experience to you and your fellow players, tell the story, interpret the rules, all real time. It's also a great way to get friends together and have a ball. No computer interface will ever replace true pen-and-paper roleplaying.

I enjoy this game thoroughly, I play it for many reasons, including the people I have met both in game and on these boards. When I tire of it, I will move on. I will try my best to make a difference, but in the end I'll probably be back, rolling up characters, drawing out maps, and preparing my friends for a new adventure in some dungeon somewhere.

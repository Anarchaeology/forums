So one day I'm wandering through the pages of Beta Bytes (a website dedicated to beta of all sorts, and the people who participate) and I see Anarchy Online. I'd heard about it off and on while playing EQ and figured I'd give it a shot.
Signed up ... got accepted.
"hmm...y'know, for a fourth beta, a FINAL beta, this thing is buggy. Reminds me a lot of my EQ beta days," I tell a friend on ICQ.
"Just wait til release," he advises me. "And be sure to send in bug reports for as many bugs as you can find."
"Yup, I know. Will do."
And so I do. Bunches of them. My fingers are now as accustomed to firing up that ol' bug report tool as they are using ctrl-alt-del.

Game is released, and the bulk of the bugs I (and many, many others) reported during beta are still rampant. Still, I give Funcom slack. At the time, they had decent communication on the board and their promise of board/email/ingame support was intriguing. I really enjoyed seeing Fadinaway's replies. He came off as honest and concerned, even if his boss is of the 'Canned Response Will Suffice' persuasion.

Time goes on, bugs get fixed, issues get addressed, tech support turns out to be middling to worthless, leaning heavily on the support issued by other players through the boards, the game rocks. I trumpet AO's wonders to old EQ friends, old UO friends, old MUD friends, and friends everywhere else. I've been upset, I've been amazed - in general I've been happy with it all. See, while they screwed up big time on the release-sans-playability thing, they were at least working their rears off to fix it and make up for it.

Then I notice something that caused me concern. They profess that they're working overtime to fix bugs, they're hiring more people to help, they're expanding their tech support, they're working diligently.

There are still bugs. Bunches and bunches of them. Bugs ranging all across the board, many of which severely inhibit enjoyment (such as city lag or inventory loss), but they find that they can take time out of their developers' busy schedules to assign them to a class balance issue?

Well, agents do have a LOT of balance issues. Take the rifle power, for instance. Rifle range? How about the utter worthlessness of concealment/hide in pvp? Roots! Maybe they could fix the roots/snares..? Ahh, betcha it's the way one of the most attractive abilities of our class, the profession dupe nanos, have the potential to screw everything up and make an agent nothing more than a weak version of another class PERMANENTLY. Oooh! oooh! I know! They're gonna remove that stupid green fast attack skill and give us something else, something useful!

Nuh-uh. Nope. /moon
They're gonna screw with concealment. After all, agents were DOING something! Bleh!
"Can't have none 'o that, now can we? Silly Agents were spending hours and hours, gaining only the most minimal xp, using a class defining skill to perform an action -an action agents were MADE for, considering the manual/in-game description- to attain something that is, by our own admittance, just about worthless: Credits."
But hey, it's still conceivably an exploit. Soo... let's consider the exploit itself. Here's an excellent example by Caelfoth: http://aoforums.funcom.com/showthre...?threadid=37030
Well, there is the chance that he's underexaggerating, isn't there? Maybe he just had a particularly bad go at things. So let's overexaggerate for a moment.

First some baselines.
1. Mission must be red, since they have the best rewards (and we're gonna exaggerate said reward)
2. Distance must be extensive, since red missions and short distances don't go together in the same sentence unless bound with a NOT
3. We're doing this solo

Okay, so we choose a red mission at level 27. Said mission has a net reward of 20,000 cr and a piece of QL 30 nano armor of whatever sort (the best as far as ac is concerned).
Supposing we have absolutely no need to stock up on anything pre-mission (yeah...right), we have no initial cost.
We're off! Now we don't want to lose what xp we have since xp will be slim on this trip, but don't bother with the cost of saving, being as it's free (we just leveled and are saved at a place optimal for this trip). Long trip, big world, mean critters ... takes us an hour and a half to get there all told.
We go inside (provided we don't crash zoning and are able to zone in the first place...can't fix those bugs, gotta nerf agents first!). We have an excellent trip, no deaths. The idea is to maximize our money flow, so we visit every room, clean every chest and heal between traps, finish our (non-violent) mission objective. Takes us an hour.
We got some small amount of xp from the doors, chests, mission completion and acceptable random encounter on the trip over, so we don't wanna die to warp back. Means we walk. Reverse of the trip there: 1.5 hours.
Armor is useless to us considering it's nano and we don't want to screw up our other skills just to have that great armor (after all, we're maximizing our conceal so we can exploit, si?).
Time spent: 4 hours
CR Recieved: 20,000 (including sale of armor)
XP gained: Minimal, but we need it.

Now we choose a local mission with a measly 3,000 cr reward(completely unexaggerated; we want this as unfair as possible), half hour to prepare/find/enter, hour to kill EVERYTHING for money and xp, and complete the objective and return. Money from each kill, xp from each kill, money/xp/item from objective.
Time Spent: 2 hours
CR Recieved: 3,000 for the objective plus another 7,000 for loots and item sales. So total 10,000

Compare:
Mission One Time to Cr Ratio: 1/5,000 (one hour per 5,000c)
Mission Two Time to Cr Ratio: 1/5,000 (one hour per 5,000c)

BUT! Consider this... Mission two also netted xp an an arguably higher rate of items (chest drops AND mob drops). Then there's the whole idea that the first was exaggerated.
So now we have this incredible exploit that allows those with a lot of time, effort and IP invested in making a very specific type of character to get credits without having to do the killing in between (infiltration, you dig?).

Is this so important when people are losing the contents of their banks or backpacks? How about duping+selling exploits? Or line of sight exploits?
/angry

But that's not what really takes the cake. Nope. It's the loss of IPs involved, it's the loss of time, it's the fact that our aimed shot SUCKS now. Without aimed shot, without a suitable (what use is los sneak when the mob's los extends the whole zone?) concealment, we're just sh*tty <insert other class here> without the benefit of the better weaponry.
Sniper rifles suck in AO, but they have a redemption in the idea that the first shot might hurt and give you that extra chance you need. Agents as damagers aren't all that interesting but for the idea that the boosts we see with aimed shot now will become truly formidable at higher levels.

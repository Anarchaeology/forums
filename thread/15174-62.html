Features » Editorials
A Rant On Exploits
by Tregon
We are all playing in a 'game universe'. Some follow both written & unwritten rules of conduct in our interactions with others. Some do not - I address these others in my rant.

We hear players say: 'the publishers are not preventing us from doing these exploits', 'who is it hurting?' & the ultimate argument - 'STFU!'.

Let's try some counter-arguments to the exploiters in order to illuminate the subject.


----- Number 1: Mr. Exploits-R-Us sits down with a deck of cards (an archaic gaming tool) and lays out the cards, after shuffling, for a nice game of Klondike Solitaire. Instead of turning the cards over 1 or 3 at a time (depending on the version you play), he fans the remaining cards out like a large Poker hand and proceeds to cherry-pick the best cards to fit the layout. Within a few minutes, he has all the cards in their winning slots and he is a Winner! Emboldened by this magnificent strategy of cunning he rapidly plays some more hands of the game (is he having fun yet?) and runs out to tell his neighbor that he has won 9 out of 10 games of Solitaire. Boasting to the neighbor how he did it, he awaits the congratulations he feels he so richly deserves.

Is the neighbor's response:
a) to congratulate Mr. Exploits-R-Us on his fantastic ability to win the games against the odds
b) to remember that it was time to change his car oil and makes an excuse to leave the vicinity
c) wonders how much he can get for his house if he can sell it to a prospective buyer without letting the new guy know he's going to be living next to such a total loser as Mr. Exploits-R-Us.


----- Number 2: Mr. Exploits-R-Us invites the new neighbor and wife over for a quiet night of Monopoly with his family. After setting up the board and issuing the game money, he excuses himself and goes to the next room. Whirring and cutting sounds are heard and our hero returns to play. Buying up everything he lands on he soon has a huge pile of property. Every few minutes he goes to the next room, sounds are heard and more property is bought (and his money pile keeps growing - amazing!). The new neighbor follows Mr. Exploits-R-Us into the next room at one point and watches as he puts the game money on a nice color copier and runs off several sheets and cuts them on his paper-cutter. Asking Mr. Exploits-R-Us just what the heck is he doing, the response is: 'well, there's nothing in the rules that says I can't copy the money and if the game publisher didn't want it to happen, why didn't they make the money so I couldn't copy it'.

Is the neighbor's response:
a) to congratulate Mr. Exploits-R-Us on his fantastic ability to win the games against the odds
b) to leave the house and vow to never let his daughter date this loser's son
c) start cursing out the guy who sold him the house when he realizes he's stuck next to this guy because he has a 30-yr mortgage


OK - these are examples in a situation where we are social gaming, there are no long range effects, no real property loss, etc. However, would any of you want to play with Mr. Exploits-R-Us in a game of Poker or any other game where there is money in the pot?
The mindset that thinks like Mr. Exploits-R-Us is the same that tells a guy who has been convicted of multiple DUIs (and is driving a car with a breathalyzer attached to prevent him from driving drunk) to call his buddy over to activate the car so he can drive it while tipsy or worse.
It is this mindset that lets a student rationalize away cheating on a final exam ('if they didn't want me to cheat - they would strip-search me before the test so I couldn't bring in my crib-sheets - so it's the tester's fault I cheat'). Do you want to drive over the bridge (that this guy may have designed) in a car (the brakes of which he may have designed)?

To exploit/cheat or not to? ... that is the question. It is a matter of maturity & responsibility, words that are difficult to live up to and with, that define the adult vs the child. It is not a matter of age, we all know children who are more mature than some adults. It is not a matter of acting childish - as an adult I can enjoy a water balloon fight or laugh at great cartoons (but I don't do these at work). Maturity & responsibility are a mental state, it feels good to play a game within the rules and still win against everything the game can throw at me - try it sometimes.

Mindfood: Damage output ranking.
This is how I personally think the damage output for different professions SHOULD be ranked. From top damage dealers to the lowest. (NOTE: I will discuss the pet classes later on. The are hard to fit in correctly here, especially since I have limited experience in playing them. These are also my personal opinions.)

1: NT. Ranged class, very low hitpoints. The NT should reign supreme in the damage department. Defences; Calms and the rare nullity. If a melee class comes in melee range, NT is (or should be) toast.

2: MA. Melee class, medium hitpoints. Defences: Heals and good evades.

3: Agents. Ranged class, low hitpoints. Defences: Roots, meta heal pet and the 14.2 aggro-dehancer.

Why this order? Well, NT's are the weakest defensive class in the game. MA's are the melee class with meager HP and bad heals, Agents are also lacking in the defensive department. So, whats the best defence? Offence. These 3 classes need to take down an opponent fast. Melee classes should do more damage then ranged classes, since melee users are far more vunerable to roots, snares and range weapons. Which 2 classes are getting self-only crit buffs? Right on. Something needs to be done about the NT's though, the are rarely pulling aggro from teams, no matter how unwisely (aggressive) they play their profession


This bring us to the tank classes:

4: Enforcer. Melee class, superior HP. Defences; HP, healing (mongo), root-breaking, good AC.

5: Soldier. Ranged class, good HP. Defences; HP, good ac and MK reflect shields.

Now remember, just because these classes are the best at taking damage does not mean that they should be the best at dealing it. I'm sure many enforcers agree that it's a chore to keep aggro at certain times, this is why we have aggro enhancers, taunt nanos, da taunter etc.


Anyways, the adventurer is hard to put in correctly in this scheme, an adventurer could go weak defense/strong offence and vice versa, thereby moving around in this rating.

6: Adventurer. Melee/Ranged as chosen, medium HP. Defences; Healing, decent AC, decent reflect shield, decent evades, decent runspeed. Medium damage dealers.


Here things get a little difficult. This is my opinion on how professions should be in the damage department, please don't take offense.

7. Fixers. Ranged class, medium HP. Defences; Superiour evades, roots, snares, rather bad HoT, and amazing runspeed.

8. Trader. Ranged class, medium HP. Defences; Healing, mezzes, roots, deprives.

9. Doc. Melee/ranged as chosen, good HP. Defences; Superiour healing, init debuffs.

Should fixers really outdamage Traders? Yup. Look at their defenses. The Trader is the strongest profession in the game, from a defensive point of view. Fixers are excellent at running away from danger and I've enjoyed many an astounding Fixer tanking with their superiour evades. But they have far weaker healing/rooting capability then the Trader and lack the mezzing capability. But Fixers, Traders and Docs are/should be of utmost importance to have in a team due to their team benifits (healing/mezzing/wrangling etc).

Now to the pet professions, they are a bummer to fit in here. Let me rank their order between themselves.

1. Engineer: Ranged/Melee as chosen, decent HP.
Offence: Nothing apart from pet and weapon.
Defence: superiour AC, damage decrease.

2. Crat: Ranged/Melee as chosen, low HP.
Offence: DD nanos and pet.
Defence: Roots, mezzes.

3. Meta: Ranged/melee as chosen, low HP.
Offence: Nano buffs, DD nanos, attack pet, mezz pet.
Defence: Nano and Damage debuffs. Heal pet.

Now, since the engineer doesnt have any other offensive/defensive capability then pet/weapon and AC they/their bot should do the most damage hands down. Crat and MP's should be a close second since their offensive/defensive capabilites are so much stronger then the Engineers.

How do we fit the pet professions in the general ranking then? Engie at 4th place perhaps, just after Agents and before Enforcers. Crats and MP's somewhere in the 7-9 range, they should be warmly welcomed into a team for their team benifits, not per damage dealing capability. I'm not sure, feel free to argue with me on this.


Anyways, these are my opinions based on a risk/reward analysis and I hope you find it interesting. Also, think about the changes made in 14.2, how they fit in this ranking adventure of mine.

Generally a class's relative strength at offense and defense should be inversely related. You give up defense to get offense and vice versa.

But you also have to take into account a class's support and miscellaneous abilities (enhanced travel, tradeskill abilities, etc). So a class might give up some defense but not necessarily gain an equal amount of offense, but get tradeskill bonuses, teleports or some such instead.

Given the large number of classes, many with roles that are vague or aren't really supported by the game mechanics, and the flexible skill system, balancing becomes a difficult task. An engineer looks bad compared to mp. Mp have pets to rival that of an engi, yet also get nano buffs and other useful abilities. It looks unbalanced because the tradeskill advantages the engineer was designed to have can't really be factored into the balancing equations because tradeskills are so poorly done.

I don't feel like I know enough about all the classes to comment on whether they're listed in the right order or not, but the general philosphy of trading offense for defense is one I agree with.

Davedread I think you have missed a few things.

Firstly you have to separate PvP and PvM, the relative values of different 'attacks' and 'defenses' are entirely different and you can't use them to balance PvP without screwing PvM or to balance solo PvM without further screwing PvP.

For example, in PvM the ultimate defence is a calm nano. Nothing beats it, nullities, TMS, healing are all way way second best in comparison (thats assuming you have a very good chance of landing the calm obviously). So for PvM you'd have to say anyone with a calm is the ultimate solo PvM and should have almost no attack (after all, they can stop the fight any time they like, heal up and go for it again). However in PvP calming isn't exactly much use.

You are also comparing static abilities and dynamic ones e.g. hitpoints versus healing - an enforcers hitpoints advantage of an Atrox MA is not that great at all, when you throw healing in. the balance changes again. Enforcers get healing but its very very slow HoT, again almost impossible to compare against an MAs quicker healing.

In PvP a root against a melee class is a huge defence - whilst it remains in force you take no damage. In PvM its less viable unless you are hunting outdoors against melee mobs.

Evades also are difficult to use the way you have, since its still easy for some classes to get smeared by a mob that never crits on them.

The ability to run away from danger lets you put fixers almost at the bottom of the pile yet the ability to calm an attacking mob doesnt stop you putting NTs at the top of the pile? come on, where is the logic in that?

You cant just say an agent with a heal pet should do more damage than a soldier with TMS and 1000 more HP. A decent heal pet does that much healing over the course of a long fight (numbers obviously are level dependant but you get the point). How much HP does the TMS save the soldier and at what levels and how does it compare with heal pets, intrinsic healing of docs/mas etc and how does it compare with different ability to use first aid kits between professions?

Run speed PvM accounts for nothing other than the ability to blitz missions for rewards. Any class can have enough runspeed to run away from danger in PvM - either raise the runspeed or buy a burst of speed stim.

I could go on. For instance how do you balance a calmers ability to deal with multiple mobs in a solo mission against someone like an Engie who's only tactic is to run around and hope their pet grabs the aggro?

Sorry, there are just too many big flaws in your argument.

(The only way to do what you want would be to have a completely different set of PvM and PvP abilities. Its far better to do what Funcom look like they *tried* to do which is to ensure that at each level every class has enough damage output and/or secondary abilities to solo 50-60% missions and hopefully something useful to bring to a group. Yeah OK they haven't succeeded yet but I think its more likely to succeed than to try ranking damage output).

Over-Equipping:

For instance...say there is a piece of armor that requires 100 str to use, and gives you 300ac. In order to wear it, you need 100 str...but if your strength should drop, you will still be able to wear it, but it will be less effective. Basically, every point of Str = 3ac. So if you were buffed from 80str to 100str...the armor will provide 240ac when the buff wears off. This should be checked everytime you are buffed or a buff wears off (get more computer power if this is a problem, but its a real time game, we should have real time effects)

So, leave it as it is right now with requirements to use, but make the armor not function as well when the buff wears off. That's the best solution, and it will make traders sought after in teams, since they can use their 50QL higher weapons and peak performance =)

Go with this idea =)

Sorry this is pretty long. I was involved in some of the original discussion about this months back, that and some of the things people have been saying trying differentiate a "good" exploit from a "bad" exploit were irritating, led to this being longer than I had planned.

First, overequipping is not exploiting unless you exploited to overequip. There are no grey areas to exploiting. You are either an exploiter or you or not.

Weak arguements that it is not a serious exploit, everyone else is doing it, an aspect of the game is broken and this "work around" (exploit) makes up for it, are all justifications for doing something wrong.

Whether I steal $5 or $50,000.00, the fact remains that I am a thief. The latter will carry stiffer punishment, but the spirit behind both is the same; I want something I am not entitled to, and I want the benefits bad enough that I will forsake the rules to get it. And the "it wasn't written anywhere" arguement doesn't fly either.

Tell that to your Professor after cheating on a test "Well you never said before this specific test that I couldn't cheat" or to your mate "We never discussed my NOT having sex with someone else, so what's the big deal if I had an affair" may be extreme examples, but show that we do follow unwritten and unspoken rules everyday (and there are consequences for breaking them).

The bottom line again is, there is no grey area of exploiting. Exploiting is exploiting, and you should be banned. If you don't like being called an exploiter, change your behavior so you are no longer exploiting, and that problem will go away.

I am all for 1 warning to see if the person will change their behavior.

So I agree, one warning then a ban. And that is for all exploits, whether you are duping tokens, abusing treatment clinics as an exploit to overequip implants, or doing full hard missions with a layout where monsters are easily trapped behind objects due to a pathing problem that prevent them from fighting.


The overequip issue has been discussed at length in times past. The mechanisms in the game exist to overequip. The problem with NCU has been addressed with the self-only complit buff. Others have suggested limiting who you can buff/be buffed by to anyone not grey/red to you. I like this and have suggested it before as well.

Is it possible that buffs for some weapons need to be toned down (or others raised)? How many points can rifle be buffed compared to other weapons? Equipping of all weapons, with hard work and diligence should be about equally buffable.

Another change would be to have your skills in using the weapon play a larger role than it does currently. A lvl 60 whatever, using a QL 130 weapon should not even approach the damage output of a lvl 100 whatever using the same weapon.

If I no longer meet the reqs for the weapon, make the chance for crits, AFTER all modifiers like scopes and buffs, zero. If I am learning guitar (and sound half-decent), and you give me a top of the line Kramer, I will not all of the sudden be able to sell out shows nationwide. The much better instrument will make my playing sound a little better, but only in the hands of an expert will the true potential of the instrument be realized.

If I don't meet the reqs for a special on the weapon, then I can't use the special. That might bring things into more balance between using nanos and using weapons.

If people are still upset about previous exploits hurting the new players (or those who refused to exploit), increase the size of the inventory screen by 1 row, add a bag to everyone's inventory that has enough slots to hold everything a person could possibly have in all 3 wear screens combined. The next time we login, all wear screen items are in the bag, and have to be equipped. Funcom could even give us all the extra 300 creds in case you were broke when you last logged out so you can equip your implants.

Having implants 25QL higher than others your level because you are willing to exploit when others aren't, is an unfair advantage that has nothing to do with my lack of ambition or my not working hard on my character. Stealing $5 still makes the stealer a thief, exploiting just a little, still makes the offender a cheat, and I am not a cheat so I didn't use the well know treatment stack exploit.

The other thing that has to be addressed at the same time (and hopefully they would be tested together for over 1 month) is that monsters would have to be brought in line with the new changes. Their AC, HP, and NP would have to be toned down.

The final touch would be to really listen to the feedback from the testers, including copying some higher level characters to test to see how things work. I play on test, but it will be a while before I can test out anything mid-game, much less anything in the high lvl game.

Thanks for the letter, it shows that you are really concerned. I just hope the implementation of the solution you decide on is well though out then carried out accordingly, and if things don't go well if it makes it to live, please make advance preparations so you can quickly fallback so people don't struggle for a week or 2 while waiting for a fix.

Regarding the overequipping issue, I tend to favor the 'scaled'
capabilities solution. However, the game has clearly been designed to
allow buffing oneself up to use better gear so we have to decide where
to limit this feature.

Gaute suggested an allowance factor (10% for example), and I think
that's a good idea. It allows for some overequipping while still
setting a reasonable ceiling. Should it apply to all equipment in the
same way? Probably not. The biggest problems seem to be in the area of
implants and NCU capacity. I would like to see implants closely limited
to a QL near the character's level. In this case the 10% would work
well, and scaling should NOT be applied. Make it a hard limit. I think
the NCU system can be left as it is, since the change to Trader's
CompLit buff has effectively limited overequipping sufficiently.

If implants are limited in the way mentioned above, then perhaps weapons
and armor will be constrained enough. I'd say that we should try the
implant fix first and if we are not satisfied with the results then try
scaling weapons and armor according to Gaute's suggested plan. A 10%
QL grace margin with proportional scaling of benefits (weapon damage and
armor AC) beyond that seems fine to me, though it may need some tuning.

There are two issues that concern me. First, the PVM play experience
needs to be carefully evaluated while testing equipment changes. If we
are reducing the effectiveness of some current players, and that appears
inevitable, then the NPCs must be tweaked to make PVM viable at all
levels. There are many stories about how difficult PVM becomes after
level 50, and reducing the equipment potential for everyone only makes
that worse. Additionally, allowing a wide range of potential equipment
for characters still raises balance issues. Is it necessary for all
characters to maximize their equipment to play PVM? Or can someone opt
to spend time and money on other areas (nanos, cars, trade skills, etc.)
and still be able to fight solo or contribute to a team? It is
essential that adjustments to NPCs of all levels be made as a part of
rebalancing player equipment.

Second, what shall we do with current player's equipment? I have been
discussing things from an ideal point-of-view...how I think the game
system should operate. But we must account for the fact that Rubi-Ka
now has history, and sweeping changes may alienate a great many players
who have grown up with the rules as they are. Clearly, the proposed
changes to equipment ARE a 'nerf', but one that is justified in my
opinion. They cannot be implemented without causing some grief.

There are many issues with simply causing items to unequip, especially
when we are talkiing about dozens of items (armor, implants and more).
I'm not entirely opposed to forcing a re-equip of items under the new
rules, but it MUST be handled smoothly. One idea that comes to mind is
a new temporary bag that appears in a players inventory and contains all
forcibly unequipped items. The first time you log in after the big
patch, you have this bag containing all items that were removed due to
skill checks. Once you have dealt with the equipment as you see fit,

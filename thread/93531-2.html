If they all were fighting each other for sales, the price would drop out of the market.
There is one key difference between this and the OPEC cartel - punishment.

OPEC is a cartel - an oligopoly. The success of such organizations in manipulating the equilibrium price at which the product sells hinges on each member-producer maintaining its allotted production quota.

However, one member can easily inch up its production by fractional amounts and derive HUGE increases in profit, since the actions of one producer does not affect the equilibrium price, and are indeed difficult to monitor. Thus, cartelization creates a moral hazard, because provides incentives to "cheaters". In absence of punitive sanctions for cheaters who get caught, everyone cheats, not only because they can, but because it is very likely that other members already are.

Eventually, the mistrust and cheating decimate the cartel, and free market conditions are restored. Now, free market is perfect competition, in which producers can only compete on the basis of price. However, producers still have to cover their costs - labour, land & capital - and if the price drops below the shutdown point (which, simplistically, is the cost of labour and unfinished goods required as production inputs), the producer shuts down; it can't afford to pay its workers, nor can it purchase the raw materials required to keep production going.

Furthermore, producers expect not only to cover their costs, but also to receive a reasonable rate of return on investment - profit. Simply, the minimum acceptable profit margin equals the profit such producer can get from the next-best alternative (making Bronto Burgers, for example). If the rate of return falls below this level, producers voluntarily shut down and buy burger stands, because more money can be made for the same amount of work.

Thus "pricing out of the market" never happens (except in cases of public goods, but that's WAY beyond this). The only case of undercut pricing occurs in instances of predatory behaviour, which becomes increasingly unsustainable the greater the number of market participants.

'Snow' is unlikely to occur in any semi-realistic interpretation of Rubi-Ka because :

1. Rubi-Ka is a mostly desert planet apart from some areas around Omni-1 that have been terraformed, please not in world terms the current zones cover a very small section of the planet.

2. The system that Rubi-Ka is located in is a Binary star system, gauging by the approximate size and colour of the Suns we're lucky to even be alive down here.

3. Axial Rotation is 90 degrees to the plane of the twin suns, basically without a serious gravitational anomaly or the complete suspension of basic science your not going to be experiencing any short recognisable season, though the planet probably does have season of a sort..... Its just that they would most likely be 10,000's years long.

4. Given the facts the most likely location of any polar activity would be at the east and west poles (which are the equivalent of Earths North & South as far as planetary rotational dynamics is concerned.) At best guess such polar ice area if it existed would be reasonable small unless the area was of sufficient altitude.

Of far more interest would be Rubi-Ka's North and South magnetic poles that would receive massive amount of radiation each day when they are pointing directly into the twin Suns. Some of the result of this we see in the Ion storms that would grow increasingly worse closer to the magnetic poles.

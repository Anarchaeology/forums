Well Suiste, that (a personal view of good and evil) is a subjective way at looking at things. Not a objective. I'm not disagreeing with you, each person's set of moral's determines what they feel is "good" and what is "evil." But, not matter someone's personal views, it does not change that universal constant of what is "good" and what is "evil."

Anytime you apply good and evil to a storyline, where those principles can be realisticly realized and represented by some outside force, such as when someone enters the Shadowlands and sees thier Soul's representation when viewed by a universal constant, judged by a scale outside thier own personal views, that universal constant must be set and cannot be controlled by each individual. I've always called it "Story Goodness."

An example is Dungeons and Dragons, which have spells and effects that only effect "Good" or "Evil" creatures/people. What determines who is good or evil? The universe, not the person. It's not a very intillectual way of handling the matter, but it is effective and it lays down universal laws/structure that everyone can follow and have a good time using them, rather than debating if that "Holy Nuke of Righteousness" can affect my Rogue for hours upon hours. The Rogue can debate for hours if her actions are good or evil, and makes for great RP, but guess what, the universe says you just got nuked, game on.

I used to feel the same way when playing White Wolf games, debating for hours after a session if my character's action warrented him/her loosing a point of "Humanity" when I felt that my character felt perfectly warrented commiting a certain act and that it was justified to themself and would cause no change in thier outlook on life. Then I realised something. My character is not this Universal Constant, my character is not the God/Devil of the universe, my character doesn't control what is good and what is evil, my character only looks at the universe with his/her own perception, and acts on it in that way. This perception does not affect how the universe perceves my character, though.

No matter how justified my character feels in an action, there is a force in the universe that slaps his/her soul on the wrist and says, "BAD!" *wags a finger*

So, no matter how justified Jypsie felt that night, as an officer of the law, she went into the Roompa Bar with Omni-Pol(greenies, not the guild, although I think they were there that night) and massacred Pajama clad Partygoers when they refused to follow orders to go home, that was a slap on the wrist.

I always play my characters with thier own personal moral view of what is good and evil, but I expect there to completly be some universal constant of what is good and evil that is not under my control. Just my characters are not aware of this and act as they feel they should.

Gimmie some horns, bat wings and a cute tail, please.

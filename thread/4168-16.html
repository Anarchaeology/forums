I didn't understand this either, until I played a Trader myself. We can do it better than anyone else. Docs heal; Enforcers tank; Traders bend the equipping rules. That's our thing, contrary to the belief that Traders, well... trade. quote:
Originally posted by D.Shaffer
'Casual' gamers vs 'l33+' users. Even if there is a real difference on the sides of this argument BASED on the time devoted to the game (Which I'm not to sure about), something you need to think about. Funcom is a buisness when you get right down to it. A buisiness needs to make money. What does this mean? Funcom needs to make the MOST amount of people happy as it can. If this means the casual gamers like the idea, they're more likely to get their way. Rather then PURPOSELY ticking them off (R U newbie?! Your idea SUCKS cause you havent spent hours everyday playing so I'm not gonna listen to you!), you SHOULD be figuring out a compromise so EVERYONE is happy, because I can gaurantee the casual gamers MASSIVELY outnumber you and Funcom is always going to be looking at the bottom line here.
'Massively outnumber'? That's debatable, depending on the extremity we're speaking of, here, and the level of play. At low levels, you are exactly right... in fact, one can breeze through levels 1-10 with no armor at all, with little difficulty. By 20th level or so, nearly every player has figured out that they can use those generic +12 ability buffs to help equip better armor. In the higher-level game, people simply do not use gear within their unbuffed limits. They are not 'l33+'... they've simply applied their knowledge of the game mechanics. Plus, it's neccessary to do this. The mobs grow tougher, faster than your characters will. I like this aspect of the game. It's challenging, & it forces one to 'play smarter'.
If you look at it from the business perspective, then your point is correct, but only in the short-term. Very few people stay unskilled at something if they keep it up. 'Casual gamers' become 'l33+' as they learn more. Either that, or they quit & move on to something else (younger people can be more prone to this). A simpler game may draw more people in to try it & catch on quickly, but a more complex game system that implements strategy is more likely to maintain a long-term core following.
If they look at the 'bottom line', they'd see that they shouldn't neglect the needs of the long-term player. Very simply: don't let us grow bored. Simple game with simple rules & no fine-tuning = boredom. A good number of new player are going to try the game, see what it's about, then quit. This happens no matter what. But we, who have been here since July or earlier... we're already hooked (except for those who've left because something had been changed). And many of us will stay, if we can only keep playing the game we've grown to love, & we don't get bored.

hmm.. well two things:

1) the entire 'combat system' of this game, most notably in the pvp area, has almost nothing to do with skill and a whole lot to do with equipment; it makes little difference how much u know about the mechanics and controls of the game, and even how long uve played, when u face someone with a weapon of twice their level or higher -- it really ceases to be about skill at all. these proposed changes might return a sense of fairness - not equality mind u - the professions are simply not designed to go toe to toe in every possible combination - but fairness. so in that area, i think these changes make sense.

2) although at points in the past the game was grossly abused through constellations of bugs and exploits, creating unbeatable pvp characters, much of the 'balancing' and 'tweaking' seems to have been a reaction to this minority. while thats all fine and good, the net result was a game that quite simply cannot be played with equipment of one's own level. while surely it makes our lives easier to obtain gear of our level, and many design conceits within the game clearly indicate that this was the original intention, i hope the development staff realizes that the vast majority of ppl who 'overequip,' as it were, do so not to gain undue advantage, but rather because a) no one will team with them otherwise, and b) the mobs are not even remotely survivable in the 100+ game.

it is of course a bold move, and there will be huge amounts of flak over this, should it proceed. but in the long run, and for the continued existence of the game, it is probably the correct move - that comes, however, with a very large caveat: if we are forced to use equipment ( or have our equipment reduced in effectiveness to that ) of our own level, it is absolutely imperative that we be given a game that is playable with such equipment. for many of us over level 100, 'clearing' a dungeon of mobs is a distant memory. while some of us have the means to sneak or run or mesmerize our way through a mission, the idea of 'soloing' a SOLO mission is most often a complete and cruel joke -- and this with our so-called super gear.

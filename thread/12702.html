Gameplay balance from the underside
I've been looking around these threads heavily, trying to find other general complaints about gameplay balance in general, but most or all of them seem to be from the POV of people who are relatively or VERY advanced characters. That may be valid (how the heck would I know?) but right now I'm more interested in the beginner perspective.

Try this on for size.

1. A MMORPG has both quest/combat aspects to it and social aspects to it. I, personally, am here for both. But if I'm socializing, I don't want to be questing ... and if I'm questing, I quest alone. I do not adventure well in teams So I will be entirely picking solo missions.

2. I don't particularly want one faction in the game automatically coming after me because of some uninformed choice I made during player creation. So I'm neutral.

3. I'm looking for some character type who mostly operates by stealth and guile, since I'm not a head-on fighter. So I become a fixer.

A lot of experienced players are shaking their heads right now, knowing I have chosen a recipe for disaster ... but how is a new player to know these things at the time?

So here I am, a level two character with about sixty credits to my name. I can barely kill rats, I can't successfully finish even the simplest mission I try to find (well, I finished the starter mission, but that doesn't count), and therefore I have no real way to get either the money or XP I badly need. I can't even replace my useless starter gun.

And I wander around seeing all these characters levels 25 and up - really, these seem to be the majority in any "city" environment I go to - and I think "How did they get there? Did they earn it themselves? Where did they get the stamina to slog through what they needed to get that high?"

In other words: It's a good game, and it sure does look like people are having fun, but somehow it's fun my character is unable to have. Not a lot of options. Sure, I'll try a different character class and start over, knowing what I do now ... but that is not the point. The point is that you are supposed to be providing an interesting experience for ANY combination of character and play style, at ANY level. Stuck killing rats for slow, slow, slow XP gain is not the way to do that.

Nor is the repeated experience of finding out that everything interesting you can do is seemingly miles of stat points away .... So much is made of the Fixer's Grid abilities in the docs, for example ... you know how disheartening it is the first time you actually find a grid terminal and learn you have to have a Computer skill in, what, the eighties to use it at all? Lots of rude surprises like that. The world opens up in big ways for players in levels above the teens, I gather, but I wonder that anyone has the patience to get there.

Nice post. You describe what probably most new players go through when they start out. I know I did.

Personally, I think this is a good thing. The thing I enjoy most about this game is the sense of accomplishment I get. Whether it's because I discovered a new cool place in some area I hadn't been before, or because I can use a new nano, or get a higher level token board... it all adds to my immersion in the game in that it makes me feel part of the world my character lives in.

I remember how I felt when I first entered the grid, how I felt when I first got a full suit of Chilled Plasteel, when I discovered Bliss in The Longest Road. That's the sort of experience only RPGs can give you.

I'm an adventurer who's just turned level 50. I got my wolf morph a level ago and I'm having *the* best time with it. I love watching my two-headed lupine alter ego running around in the dense forests of Varmint Woods from 50 feet up in the sky. I love going into the backyard, helping out newbies and watching their amazement at that big wolf that's just healed them. I love going into a frenzy and killing every leet outside Tir West gate, only to sit down afterwards and say "woof" at bystanders.

This game may be slow going at times, but to me, that only makes the reward at reaching something new so much greater. I don't know if I'll ever make it to 200, but I'll sure have a great time trying.

That was encouraging, Mharc. (Actually, your signature was the most encouraging thing - for a while I wasn't sure there WERE any other soloing, non-twinking, non-pvp'ing, etc etc ...)

I still believe, though, that there could be genuine improvements that would make the game more fun for beginners without making it TOO easy.

For example, partial XP awards. The "kill or nothing" system is harsh, especially for cases where you get attacked by something much tougher than you are and yet ALMOST manage to hold your own without dying. That's experience, for sure, but you have zilch to show for it. (I can understand if this is meant as a handicap for later levels, but isn't that why XP awards get more stingy as you go?)

I also think it would be a nice thing to be able to find a couple of pure errand-boy missions - i.e. no combat threat whatsoever, just a straightforward delivery job. Yeah, I know, these errand missions are exactly what some people dislike about the early stages of some RPG's, but they'd be a great excuse to explore the world and gain some XP in the process. (After all, isn't knowledge of the universe "experience"?)

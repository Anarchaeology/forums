Yup, it's another roleplay thread...
OOC) this thread is totally ooc

Well, I've been seeing these roleplaying/ooc griefing threads now for weeks, and everybody has a reason why their storyline or method of roleplaying is better than somebody else's, or more plans for a roleplaying convention.

After reading all those threads, I'm getting the distinct impression that it won't work.

Why?

Because everytime somebody has a problem with the direction the story is moving in-game, or what somebody is saying IN CHARACTER, suddenly they take it out-of-character, complain that somebody crossed a line, or is infringing on their fun.

And no matter how many roleplaying organizations get together, map out a well-planned storyline, and carry it through, it seems there will always be one person who arrives late, and hollers loudly, carrying a bunch of people along who hadn't bothered to get involved in the first place.

I can think of about five cases I've read in threads or seen in-game, where this has happened. I'm not writing this to point fingers, simply to make a point.

Think for a moment, people, about acting . You're a character in a play, or shooting a film. You have a storyline, and you have an idea of who your character is, and what your he or she is doing.

Now the other actor forgets a line, and improvises. Do you drop what you're doing, and complain loudly that they've spoiled your play? No! You go along with it, and if you're really good, you'll run a really great scene with that other person. Whether your scene is about love, war, exchanging friendly insults, or having a good knock-down, drag-out fistfight, it's going to be good. Because you stayed in character , and didn't suddenly break into oocs, or grief the other person, or worst of all, camped and started another 5-page thread about it.

Perhaps someone has pointed all of this out. I don't know. I don't have the leisure time to read every long-ass thread all the way through anymore. Or the patience.

I think the roleplay convention is still an excellent idea, and I wish wish wish I could be there, but I have real-life commitments that will interfere. But I do have a suggestion and a little reminder about that...

Too many cooks spoil the broth. If you're going to do another multi-organization story, don't send more than two people per organization. And lay down the law to the rest of your members, this is the story, we're participating. If you do this, your story will be new and fresh to many people, and hopefully one of our many roleplayers who haven't quite managed to separate ic from ooc won't break character and start complaining.

Just some thoughts, folks. I won't be online to check responses for the rest of this week, and probably most of next week.

So good luck with that convention, and to the Americans, Happy Thanksgiving!

Some good points. No matter how hard you try to script an event, role playing is as mush about reacting as acting, and one should therefor be prepared to ad lib a little and bend with the flow of those performing around you. Like in any good tabletop role play session, it should only be the basic plot that's scripted. Let the outcome be effected by the characters who participate.

Obviously to some extent one has to be willing to allow some give and take, after all, we'd soon grow tired if our rp enemies insisted on winning all the time. Just use some common sense, and we can all have some fun with a great feeling of participating in something big where everyone has a personality.

Some of my most fondly remembered rp scenes have been the ones that caught me by surprise.

OOC:

The question of IC and OOC does need to be kept in perspective.

Most of the time the majority of RPers achieve this very well.

Every now and again a situation arises where by an IC action that seem perfectly reasonable to the majority reaches someone personally.

Often they are reminded of a similar but different bad experience in their past. Old emotions get projected forward. On a really bad day that person is in an emotional frame of mind and shares their feelings of anger with those they consider responsible.

It comes partly from the reacting part of RP, where many RPers enjoy their drama because it does stimulate real feelings for artificial RP reasons. Many of us have had a great RP session and been on a roll because the right emotions were stimulated.

If you don't react when you RP, perhaps you will never empathise with what happens to someone when an RP incident reaches past the normal IC/OOC barriers and triggers strong OOC emotions.

Most of us learn to hold it better as we get more experienced, or we learn to rampage in a closed or private communication area until the emotions have passed and reason returns.

I've not lost it in AO, although I have in the past in a political PBM where I have thought someone's action was injust and unfair, personal and not IC.

So far I have seen the AO RP community recover quite well from these kinds of misunderstandings, but I am aware that every piece of OOC stress erodes the creative energies one has to spare for RPing and creating stories for others to partake in.

I know most of us create our stories for the benefit of our close guildmates and allies and to enrich the RP community. This is done with a sense of bringing something positive and creative to the RP Community. So it is painful when it is thrown back in your face as if you had purposely gone out to cause someone else some pain or trouble.

I'm sorry for anyone who gets personally upset by RP events, those emotions are always real and valid for the one who experiences them.

I'm also sorry for those whose creativity has been injured by having what they see as their good intentions attacked by someone else.

I hope everyone can recover from this latest incident.

/me applauds the sanity, and Enerbias for pointing out table-top

For those of you who have played good old AD&D, or even those who may have watched people or who know about it, when have you ever seen one of the participants suddenly errupt in rage and start whipping 20-sided dice at his buddy across the table for doing something he didn't like? (Nevermind. I'm sure that's happened plenty of times, and with far worse objects than dice...)

The thing is, in table-top, you have one person who knows everything that's going on at all times - who the characters are, what the background is, what is happening in the world, what the plot is, what events will happen to accelerate and enhance the plot, etc. - and the players sitting their with their character sheets and notes are fully reactionary to the DM/GM. The DM's only interest is making the game as exciting, interesting and intriguing for the players as is possible, and has no pride or image to hold up, aside from what the people around the table might think of him/her when, at 4 am, they finally decide to camp for the night...starting to sound familiar?

I don't mean to chalk up all of the IC to OOC wars to Funcom - most of it amounts to simple misunderstandings between players where no one is ultimately at fault - but given that the context from which these storylines are developed is so up in the air right now, it is understandable why these misunderstandings develop.

Case in point: the raging debate about the "role of the neutrals". I can't remember who, but someone posted a thread about a "greenie" surveying neutrals last night for their opinions on their role. But, there have been calls for months for Funcom to definitively set out what role, if any, the neutrals will play. As such, you have Elite Operations' recent attacks on OTRK facilities eliciting accusations that they are "Clan"; they claim that they are simply mercenaries, loyal only to the almighty credit. If you look at Elane Oak (from the animated series), she traded in illegal weapons, doing so against OT's wishes to benefit the Clans; not because she cared about the Clans, but because she wanted money. If Funcom would issue a definitive, unambiguous statement about who the "neutrals" are in the grand scheme of things, that neutrals could use to guide their actions, arguments such as this wouldn't occur.

Second case in point: the OTSEC release/CoA response. The problem with this lies in the fact that there is no voice to speak for the Sentinels in this matter, nor is there a voice from an official OTRK intelligence agency, to either substantiate or deny what what people have said. OTSEC took the initiative of creating a storyline that conformed with the current "official" events. The CoA took offense to the fact they weren't included in planning. Fine. The harm (if any) is done, and statements can't be rescinded after they've been printed across the AO community. But, what if an Funcom, realizing that the situation was spiralling completely out of control and seeing the harm that all of these IC to OOC wars are doing to their game and to the community, actually had Silverstone issue a release saying that it was all filthy lies by OTSEC and that the CoA wasn't involved? Then have a counter-response by, perhaps, Sergeyevich?

Funcom is the only effective entity that can mediate these disputes. Why? Not because the RP community is too immature or uncreative to conduct themselves, but because s-h---t happens. Disagreements occur; interests conflict; and when other RPers get involved and try to mediate these conflicts, all that does is add more flames to the already raging fire.

As RPers, the only authority we recognize and respect, in an OOC sense, governing our lives on Rubi-Ka are the people that provide the game and create the world - the effective DM, that tells you what is and what is not. The DM is always the head of the table, and right now the table we all sit at is quite headless.

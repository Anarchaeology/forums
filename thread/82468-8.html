Attacking for the express reason that your opponent is offline not playing the game is dishonorable in the same way as:

Looking at your opponent's hand when they are away from the table.

Raiding the bank in monopoly when your opponent is away from the table.

The common denominator is taking advantage of your opponent's absence from the game to your benefit and their detriment.

The gray area of AO is that the game is persistent, while the players are not. People play NW with the understanding that they are subject to attack at any time. Can they legally and ethically be attacked at any time? Yes. Is it fun for all when the possibility exists of loss while not playing? No. It's not even fun for the victor (unless that person is demented/immature). But that's the way the game is set up. How then does one play a fun and fair game and still be a hard-charging competitive player?

Where is the middle ground? I believe that the line between fair play and dishonorable play in this instance is how knowledge that an opponent is not playing is used. It is a matter of intent. I could care less about proving intent, though, more interested in persuading people who haven't given the issue much thought, and getting FC's ear if they are listening.

If the majority of the people playing FC who believe in good sportsmanship in games generally take the extra step and apply the same code of fair play to a game where there is much less possibility of accountability for unfair play then the game is a better game for all. The game theory/free riding issue is obvious here, but AO is not a zero sum game so I fail to understand the "win at all costs" mentality some people want to apply to this game. What are the stakes?

For the fourth time, I am not advocating setting up a tribunal to determine guilt.

Armor advice for new players
So you�re new to AO and you�re wondering�what kind of armor should I wear? Well, you�re not the first player to ask. You won�t be the last player to ask. This is one of the weekly questions that appear on the Adventurer and Enforcer forums, and I�m sure the other Profession Academies see it just as often. So read on, and be enlightened.

The first thing you should do is get an Omni-Med Suit. It doesn�t give you any armor at all, but a full Omni-Med Suit raises your Treatment and First Aid 78 points. With the help of your Med Suit, you should be able to use Quality 15 to 30ish First-Aid stims. For levels 1 to 20, using these high quality First Aid kits will heal more damage than armor would be protecting your from. Omni-Med Suits can be purchased in OT Clothing Stores. If you�re Omni, now is a good time to learn where the stores in Trade are. It�s shouldn�t take more than one lap around Omni Trade to find an entire Med Suit. For Clanners and Neutrals, the Second Hand Peddler in the backyards sells Omni-Med Suit parts as well. You�ll have to try multiple backyards, but eventually you�ll find all the parts. Omni-Med Suits only come in Quality 1, so don�t try looking for anything higher.

After you�ve worn your med suit for a few levels, you�ll want to cover it up. If you visit the Abandoned Subway, it shouldn�t take you too long to get a Vagabond Cloak off a Violent Vagabond. It doesn�t give you any Armor, but it covers your med suit and gives a +35 to all your evades.

If you're a crafty low level person, you can run out to the Temple of Three Winds. Omni and Neutral people can use the portal in Rome to get to the Temple area quickly if they have 1,000 credits to spend. (Neutrals beware: OT Guards aren�t aggressive, but Rome Blue and Rome Green have wandering OT mobs that are. If an OT guard sees you in a fight with an OT mob, the OT guard will open fire on you! Rome Red, the park area with the grid and Whompa, is free of hostiles. You�re best bet is usually to use the South Gate in Rome Red to enter Rome Green.) Clan members will have to survive the run across Tir to get to the Temple. Whatever your alignment, don�t expect to be able to do much in Temple of Three Winds until you�re level 25. We�re not going to the Temple for XP this trip, we�re going there to be sneaky.

The trick we�re going to use is to watch while a higher level (L30-60ish players) gather. If you follow this type of team into Temple of the Three Winds, the odds are good that they�ll kill everything in site but leave most of the loot behind as their goal is to get to the Exarchs or the other bosses deep inside the temple. If you follow them, it�s likely that you�ll be able to loot a
Robe of the Faithful off one of the Faithful followers of the Three Winds Cult. There are no requirements to wear a Faithful robe, so it's a good start to covering up your med suit. If the team is really in a hurry, you might even be able to snag an Acolyte Robe. Every robe besides the Robe of the Faithful that drops in the Temple of Three Winds has a level requirement of 20. Once you have your first robe, have some fun and try to survive the run back home.


In the deeper parts of the Abandoned Subway, mobs drop armor. I�m pretty sure they even drop Living Cyber Armor, which you can also obtain by doing a quest. Parts of the Quest have been closed off to some players due to high level guards introduced after the Living Cyber Armor quest.

By now, you should be starting to get an idea of what playing AO involves. Once you get past that level 20 mark, it�s time to think about armor. By level 25, you should at least be wearing a Temple Robe.

As far as armor goes, there is no armor that beats the availability and usefulness of Carbonum Plate Armor. Carbonum is a trade skill only armor, so you�ll have to buy the plates of Carbonum and have another player make your armor, or buy your Carbonum. (If you�re lucky, some players like myself will run a mini event for armor give aways. The extra NCU memory you get from wearing Carbonum armor is a big plus. You need seven Carbonum plates to make a full set of armor. The plates for a low quality set of armor shouldn�t cost your more than 100,000 credits, and can be purchased in the Armor and Clothing Components or Mechanical and Electrical Engineering Components terminals in your favorite supermarket. Carbonum Armor is my personal favorite for low levels. The extra NCU is my favorite part, but the extra nano pool and nano init is nice too.

Another armor to consider is Junkmetal armor. This armor is more expensive to make than Carbonum as some of the tools to make it are one use only. To make a full set of Junkmetal armor, you need to find eight pieces of Robot Junk. A trade skiller will run your Robot Junk through a Precious Metal Reclaimer to get Junkmetal plates, but the Precious Metal Reclaimer is destroyed in the process. As Precious Metal Reclaimer cost 10,000 credits each, you�ll want to give your trade skiller at least that much to cover the costs. The rest of the tools used to make Junkmetal are the same as Carbonum armor.

So now you�re wearing Carbonum or Junkmetal, or you�ve found your own path. It�s back to the Temple of Three Winds for you!

As you progress in levels, and get deeper into the temple, you can grab better robes. Since all the robes have a quality of [SPECIAL], you have to look at their stats to figure out which is better. I'll do that for you, from "lowest" quality to "highest" quality:

Robe of the Faithful
Acolyte Robe
Reverend Robe
Windcaller Robe
Exarch Robe

All robes but the Robe of the Faithful require level 20 to wear. Beyond your level, there is no requirement to wear the robes, so the robes are great for all professions and all breeds. An Exarch robe is nearly as good as a Ql100 tank armor, and can be used to get you a pass for the Inner Sanctum. The robe is nodrop, but the passes can be traded. High level people tend to give you anywhere from 250,000 to 2,000,000 credits for an Inner Sanctum pass, depending on how badly they need one, all the more reason to work and get an Exarch Robe (or two)

If you have the patience and friends needed to camp the Guardian of Tomorrow, you might get lucky enough to loot a Guardian Tank Armor, but it's not as easy to wear as an Exarch Robe and the ACs and Buffs it does give aren't a huge improvement over an Exarch robe. If you're going to spend the time and effort it takes to get a Guardian Tank Armor, get it as soon as possible to get the maximum usefulness out of it. Personally, I don't think the Guardian Tank Armor is worth the extra effort, but everyone has different opinions.

That�s enough from me. Hopefully this will get you started. If you�re wondering how in the world you�re going to raise money to get your first set of armor, make sure you read Thedeacon�s Making LOTS of money as a new player guide and check the list of List Of Low QL Valuable Items by Doctah13.

Credits for this document go to anyone who has taken the time to answer the "What armor" question for any new player. There are too many of you out there to list.

I agree with a lot of this (indeed, I've given the med-suit/vagabond cloak advice to anyone who will listen).

The only thing I disagree with is the Carbonum suggestion. Credits for the young'uns can sometimes be an issue and unless you can find a tradeskiller you don't intend to tip (or who doesn't want one), this can be a very expensive route.

Carbonum has a very large hole in its Energy defence values so it's not a good idea use it against Energy wielding mobs, of which there are plenty in the static dungeons.

Finally, Carbonum requires AGL/STAM, not stats that everyone always raises, at least not in the low levels.

So, what to do?

Depending on your breed and which primary statistics you max out every level, you might consider some of the 'normal' armors. These don't give the nice bonuses that the newer tradeskilling armors bestow, but with buffs it's often possible to get on a suit much higher than your current statistics. (This isn't an OE thread, so search elsewhere for what stat buffs exist in the game and how to go about OE'ing your armor appropriately.)

The important point about all of these is that they are all accessible as mission rewards. That means that you can spend minimal credits at a mission terminal and collect yourself a full set, or hire a blitzer to do the legwork for you. Again, no bonuses (except the biomech), but accessible to the masses.

And now the list:

AGL/SENSE: The opifex's best friend is the 'steel ribbed' line. There are two improvements to this, Nadir and Obtru, which are available as you go up in QL.

STR/STAM: Atroxs love this stuff, as do enforcers. Omni-Pol and Omni-Pol elite are the white and black (respectively) armors you see many people in. It has the look of 'storm troopers' and since str and stam are the easiest abilities to buff in the game can often be worn well above your level. Note to clanners/neutrals that these are omni-only.

INT/PSY: Cyber armor are the blue armors you see on a lot of nanomages. INT/PSY aren't easily raised, but if you're conserving IPs and concentrating on your strengths this isn't a bad armor to be using. The improved lines are Waitt and Bau. Nano armors are another good alternative with holes in different places. Comes in boy and girl flavors.

AGL/STR: Flowers Tech armor is a good alternative for those without access to the Omni-Pol armors above.

PSY/STAM: Biomech armor is the standout in this category as it adds some to first aid and treatment, even if it does look like you're wearing a lampshade for a helmet.

Finally, consider ways to plug your holes. If something is weak in an area you can wear another piece from a different set to bolster that weakness. Some even do this as a fashion statement.

Very good info here! I do want to add to it a bit, however, from my experience as a doc.

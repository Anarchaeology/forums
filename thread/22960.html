what I'd like to see
1) way, I mean way, more static dungeons. And for the love of nutbunnies, start using level restrictions on the things. That way, you can have the mobs inside have a sane hp level. DAoC got away with very very few dungeons because each sides land mass was so tiny. But in AO, we've got this enormous landscape, that just has NOTHING in it. There needs to be more. Hella more.

2) Put in quest timers. For example, if you pick up the sealed orders from the mine director in the TLR dungeon, you shouldn't be able to pick it up again for AT least 2 weeks. Same goes for all the dungeons and unique NPC's (like nugget, daria, etc). This by itself would go a long way to reducing camping.

3) More NPC's that give out quests (like Alvin/Dogma). And please, get rid of that npc despawn thing. It just makes it impossible for anyone who doesn't already know about the quest to ever have a chance at it! You'll never just happen to wander by and see alvin there. There's no point in just exploring to find npc quests, since the npc will only be there for 10 seconds every 4 hours!

4) Someone mentioned in-game news terminals. I think this is a great idea! Little terminals like the mission ones that allow each side to read up on the news of the day, or even buy a newspaper. This would be great for a sense of immersion.

5) Apartments that work! I know it sounds silly to newcomers, but I'd love to be able to take some time and decorate my apartment with really expensive stuff (at level 125, I can afford to). Also, I'd like it if when you went up a title level, you could do a special quest to get a larger and more fancy apartment. Perhaps the highest apartments could even have things like bank terminals in them. It's not a big deal, but it would give you something to do when you need a break from fighting.

6) Less lag. Well, we all want that.

7) More events (how about a sudden raid on a town by a horde of deranged leets? That would just be funny).

8) more trade skills. Like customizing existing items.

9) a wider variety of armor found in missions ( I mean found as loot in chests or off mobs). All I ever find is steel-ribbed, nano, and sometimes elite. How about some flowers/primus types? A lot of us use those, it would be nice to be able to occasionally find them instead of doing 8 missions to get a full set.

10) A Virind appreciation day. Where everyone has to sing my praises for the whole day, and ummm, give me stuff. mmmyup.

Just some idea's.

... I'd like to see the realistic amounts of NPC in cities. It doesnt have to cause lag (no too players need to see the same non targettable non interactable NPCs wandering around - could be generated locally on your PC for you alone. ...
You mean, like the existing "client wildlife" setting, the one that generates the swarms of birds, butterflies, bunnies, and tiny robots. Of course. I should have thought of that! And if they stuck to only a few clothing textures (say, men's & women's Omni uniforms in the south and black & grey robes in the north), it wouldn't be a major matter of textures loading, either.

In the animated cartoon, when Boss Ross looked out his window, you used to see flying vessels moving around the city. My first thought was, "Wow, Boss Ross gets a window! Now that's luxury!" And yeah, in Lush Fields you do see a few stationary aircraft hovering in place. But there's no reason why they couldn't use the "client wildlife" setting to have one or two Yals flying around in each of the major cities. That model and texture get loaded the second you enter a city, anyway, because surely some player is flying one around nearby.

Without more traffic, the larger cities feel inappropriately creepy to me. Rome, in particular, is huge, with giant skyscraper apartment buildings everywhere. And except for maybe two dozen guards city wide, and the occasional player passing through, you never see anyone on the streets. It feels like it just got built for no reason, just the other day - or just got neutron-bombed, or emptied by a plague. But no, we're supposed to assume that there are thousands of people thronging the streets at rush hour - we just can't see them.

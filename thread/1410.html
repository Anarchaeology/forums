Envisioned - In game "Sell Terminal"
Great idea FC!

I think a world-wide consignment system would work best. A good reasons for this is to make trade skill items that can only be found as loot more available to characters via trade. (That assumes there are items like that, I wouldn't know. ) The best reason is for player made items. This one addition would be a huge encouragement for players to develop trade skills. It would facilitate the role-playing of a pure artisan as a role-played character. AO could be an RGP that one really doesn't have to just kill stuff to develop a character.

Here's how I'd like to see it work:
- The player walks up to the terminal and "uses" it. (right-click) They see a standard shop interface (understanding that the 'standard' shop interface will change, the rest of this is based on the current form.) with an additional "Withdraw" button (see below). The "Sell" portion has only one inventory space and a "Sale price" field.
- Items are purchased like they are from any other shop interface (seller is anonymous).
- When sold the seller receives a system message showing an item reference, sale price, consignment charge (see below), and net amount deposited to the character's account. This message would be stored as an off-line message if the character is off-line.

An item is put on consignment in the system by:
- Placing it in the "sell" inventory space of the interface (like the current sell method).
- The "Sale price" field is set to the minimal sell value in the item database. A separate 'minimum consignment sell value' might need to be established for balance issues. (See "deposit fee" below)
- The standard shop "cost" field updates with the amount of the deposit fee.
- The "Sale price" field is editable. This is done either by direct text edit (ideal), or via a plus/minus button. If buttons are used, the amount they change the value increases the longer the button is pressed. The longer one presses, the faster the price changes. The minus button is for over-shoots. The minimum price allowed is the minimal sell value.
- The "Accept" button places the item in the consignment system and charges the deposit fee as well as any item purchase costs, the amount shown in the "cost" field.

"Withdraw" button:
- If a character currently has items for sale this button is active.
- Pressing this button changes the items shown in the "for sale" area to the items the character has put up for sale.
- The character can remove items by "purchasing" them back at a cost equal to the consignment charge. The world-wide consignment system does require funding after all. Maintenance of the machines, Grid fees to ship inventory, bank fee's, etc.


Use considerations:
Deposit fee: Suggested to be 10cr per character level, adjustable for game economy balance. This can also be per item by being based on the 'minimum consignment sell value' in the item database. The fee is for role-play believability and a mis-use deterrent.

Consignment charge: Suggested 10% of the "Sale price" set by the selling character. Although there is a good role-play justification, this is mainly another mis-use deterrent. This should deter the 2nd bank and 'twink' mis-use (see below). It will also encourage the player to give more thought to setting the sale price if they know they will have to pay to get the item out of consignment.

Political affiliation: It would be acceptable if the selling character could also choose who they are willing to sell to when the item is put up for sale. Ideally the consignment terminals should be Clan/Omni segregated. A great benefit to neutrals would be to allow them to use the consignment terminals and disregard seller choice or political segregation. "Organization (guild) Only" buy/sell terminals would be good also, basically a guild member only shop.

Item quantity limit: A game mechanic issue only. I don't see any game balance or role-play reasons to limit the number of items a character can have in the consignment system. It's the storing, retrieval, and display of a potentially huge database that imposes any limits.

Item display sorting: The issues being looked at for the normal shop interface would carry over to the consignment terminals. In the interface's current state, it would be very cumbersome to browse through a vast number of player sold items without a filter and/or sorting choice. It's this concern that would warrant a per character quantity limit.


Mis-uses considerations:
'Twinks': High level characters putting high level loot up for sale at a very low price so that a low level character can buy it. An issue with the "self twink" (same account, different characters) and remote twinking (charters separated geographically/politicaly). Deterrents are: The risk of someone else buying it first, deposit fees, minimum consignment sell values, and consignment charges.

Junk loaders: Players putting up items that they don't really expect anyone to buy. The deterrent is the deposit fee. Perhaps the minimum consignment sell value could be set to "-1" on some truly "junk" items. It would mean that the item in not sellable via the consignment system.

2nd bank: Players 'storing' items in the consignment system. Either because they ran out of legitimate bank space (Not possible, is it? ) or a bank terminal is not as conveniently located as a consignment terminal. Deterrents are: deposit fees, consignment charges, and only placing consignment terminals near bank terminals.


What do y'all think?

The original post is a very good start at sculpting a system like this, imho. I would add some comments:

-Selling between OT, Clan, Neutral should be decided by the game story. If the story says we are all at war, then the governments would not realistically allow the exchange of weapons and armour

-I think, the simpler, the better. Less bugs, less confusion, more user-friendly.

-Make them pay, pay, pay. Charge stiff but reasonably to use this service in ANY way, to avoid the many misuses described in this thread.

-I think it would be very difficult to come up with a system that decides what is an item that will sell, and what is junk. It is so very relative to anyone's opinion. Everything should be allowed to be sold, but to make the system practical to use, allow many filtering and sorting options e.g. 'show me ranged weapons' + 'MIN QL 57' + 'MAX QL142' + 'DMG TYPE ENERGY AC' + 'MIN PRICE 50000'....well, you get the idea. This could be a series of choices in the sell terminal window.

-Fear not the screen! It always makes me laugh at how little space a lot of these windows take on the screen. I'm not in a battle now, so mr. shop terminal, feel free to use more than 5% of the screen I know this is something already 'envisioned'.

-Want to avoid misuse even further? Make time limits on consignments. Say, one week real-time. If the item doesn't sell in one week real-time, it goes back to your bank (insert charges, notifications or whatever here). There will be a lot less people out there who are willing to go through the bother of setting up a sale of useless junk or use it as some weird storage if it's going to reset in a week (and it will, because it's junk, remember? )

-Twinking. Selling items for extremely low prices. Well, this is kind of wishy-washy imho. Where I come from in past gaming, twinking was people making new characters with maximum level gear. Twinking in AO is extremely mild in comparison. With skill restrictions, everyone is capped the same in the overequipping area. The only twinking comes in when someone can afford something another player cannot. Now, is this twinking, or simply part of the player free economy? Many guilds give each other equipment daily for free, recycling gear over and over to the new characters that come up. I don't really consider that twinking, moreso resourcefulness. I'm not saying it's NOT twinking, but if you played some of the games that I've played where the REAL twinks were (to speak in AO terms) wearing ql200 gear at lvl5 and hitting for 500 at lvl 5, that was when twinking was a real problem. I think FC did a good job in curbing this practice as much as possible for their game. I also would go off topic here a second and say thanks FC for virtually eliminating kill-stealing (taking another player's target and finishing it off to get quick and easy xp). Now that you get xp for how much damage you do, the only real problem is when you are in a team and are overwhelmed at your camp by another team or teams and cant kill anything fast enough.

-This may not really be appropriate here, but kind of deals with the worry of *too much stuff*. I remember reading somewhere that one of the FC people said there are over 30,000 items in the game. I also read somewhere that a player went to daoc and laughed at how few choices of gear there were. This is all fine, but let me ask this: How many items of the 30,000 are actually useful? 20%? 40%? I can tell you that from my exp playing AO, the trade channels, guild channels and shout channels are filled with a comparitively small number of items. In the shops, how many weapons out of the 50 or so are actually worth buying, even as a fairly crappy, but useable item, for any of the classes? 3? 5? I guess the point I am trying to make here is that maybe life would get a whole lot easier if one of two things was worked on: 1) Analyze and remove the mass amounts of *useless* items in the game. Nobody buys them, nobody has bought them and nobody will ever buy them. Even people who try to be different won't stray too far, otherwise they just won't be able to *play*. 2) Analyze and update the mass amounts of *useless* items in the game to make them *not so useless*. I realize this may be one of those things that comes up in a development meeting and statements are made like "18000 items are basically crap from what we've seen of game feedback and stats; this would take years to fix; ack! *hair pulling*" So, I dont expect either of those 2 things to ever happen. However, I thought it was worth mentioning since this whole selling-consignment-economy situation might be streamlined immensely by changes in this area.

-ads. One of the things mentioned was letting players put up want ads and sell directly when possible. This sounds fine, but no service should be anywhere close to free, to minimize misuse, abuse and general spam to the serious user e.g. I dont want to sift through 2000 ads by noobs wanting to buy a 100 nova for 1k while listening to SPARE STUFF PLZ and DO YOU HAVE MONEY FOR ARMOR around me (incidentally, can we institute a function where if people do that incessantly, that no matter what the level difference we can kill them? hehe)

Let me finally say that this is one of the best ideas I have heard for this game. PLEASE move ahead with at least saying "we will implement some sort of selling system", because I think it will make soooo many people very happy. I hate selling. I hate sitting around in trade in the shopping channel forever going shift+uparrow, enter, shift+uparrow, enter, shift... only to have someone make a ridiculous offer. I also hate buying. Waiting around, sometimes for hours, for an item I need. Only to have a card-carrying member of the scrooge greedorium tell me to make an offer, knowing full well that if I offer too much, he'll more than happily rip me off and if I offer too little, he'll react like I cussed out his mother; if it's neither of those, it's someone who has decided to start an auction for his once-in-a-lifetime item, there is only one in the whole game, bidding starts at 940trillion creds, where I just have to sit there and laugh. I do very little selling and buying because of all of this. Fortunately, I am in a guild that believes in need before greed, share and share alike and the ol' one man's junk is another man's gold, so we take care of our own as much as possible. I love machines. I love how they just stay in one spot, show me the item, let me take as long as I want to analyze and give me a straight price. It's like the ATM revolution; I can't remember when the last time was that I went to a bank teller and I can't think of any reason why I would want to 'discuss' my withdrawal. Here is my card BOOM here is my pin BOOM gimme this much money BOOM bye BOOM meanwhile, 15 years ago, at the bye BOOM part I would have been still waiting at the teller counter while bubble gum hairspray girl is finishing her gossip chat with whoever and deciding if someday she might like to actually do what she's being paid for.

holy cow what a ramble

I think that's it for points I can think of in this area.

This is a good idea. It's simple, and would work.

There would need to be some limitations on it, like a limit to the number of items a player would be able to sell at one time. Otherwise, people would take every single piece of junk they found in a mission and try to sell them through the terminal.

It wouldn't be a good idea if the removed items automatically were moved to the player's inventory or bank, as obviously then you'd have problems with items disappearing when the inventory and bank were full. So instead, have the items stay in the selling terminal waiting to be removed by the player, but have them not viewable or buyable by buyers.

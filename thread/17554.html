Welp I can give you my views on this. I played AO from beta then went to DAoC moreso because I like midevil settings rather then sci-fi. I had several characters in DAoC and played on all the rp servers. But after a while I soon realized that the depth of the game did nothing to make the leveling treadmill easier to deal with. The game is simply to shallow and for me regardless of what I did (crafting, exploring, rvr) it still became very boring quickly. I think I left AO just before Christmas and came back this past month so that's not long at all IMO.

In DAoC there was no depth to anything that I could see. And I guess it's hard for me to explain what I mean by that, but from my point of view, there was no story, no true meaning to taking a fort in the frontier only to have another realm take it back and repeat to infinity. Classes, combat styles, spells, and mobs were basically the same from realm to realm just given a different name.

DAoC is just very shallow IMO. The developers took bits and pieces from other games they had and put them together, threw some gloss on top and released it. And after you play the game for a while it starts to really show.

AO, though it is sfi-fi still has a trememdous amount of depth in tradeskills, nanos, classes, weaponry, armor (can use some work here though), and landscape. There is a story though I've lost touch with it since my absence. Mobs are similar though there's still more to them then in DAoC. The music really draws you into the game even, or for me it does at least. I can still remember the feeling I get of being totally alone and in the wild, desert, or widerness when I hear this one specific tune. It's just very cool IMO.

There's no doubt in my mind each time I log in to AO that the developers put a huge amount of work and thought into their designs for this game. Compare that to DAoC and it's obvious to me that many things like rvr for instance just wasn't well thought out or well designed at all. Then you get to the weak combat system and such and it becomes a no brainer.

Granted there's still room for improvement in a lot of areas for AO but for me even with it's faults the game is still head and shoulders above DAoC. And I'll still look for a nice fantasy game on the horizon but for now AO is doing just fine.

But I always say each persons likes or dislikes for a game will differ so to each his or her own.

I consider myself to be a casual gamer and an avid rper. I really dislike treadmills and enjoy tradeskills, socializing and whatnot mixed in with hunting and exploring. Of all the games I've played DAoC taught me that there comes a time when varying your activities and not concentrating on one area too much just isn't enough. There has to be depth to a game otherwise trying different activities becomes meaningless and as boring as trying to constantly level does for some.

And notice I said that's just for some. There are gamers who live to level, or who live to do tradeskills and that is where they find their fun and is what makes a game fun for them. But if there's no depth to the game in the first place then any activity will become boring and tedious very quickly no matter how much moderation one exhibits.

Now that's what DAoC taught me.

AO, on the otherhand, has that depth for the most part and has room to add a lot more if the developers so choose. In fact it has so much depth in tradeskills and implants and such that at times I feel sorry for new players. But then I remember that learning this stuff is a part of what makes AO fun.

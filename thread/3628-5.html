I, too, am really disappointed with apartments ; I remember having this initial spurt of joy at having my very own apartment as soon as I entered the game, and one for every character, too. (I came from UO, where owning the smallest, most pitiful house available cost a relatively large amount of money.) What is even better about apartments is that they don't actually exist in the game world, so to speak, they are sort of out in the middle of limbo, in their own little zone. (This is much better than having houses scattered out in the middle of every occupiable "wilderness"; not only does that ruin the effect of the adventure setting, but it also makes it really difficult to survive sometimes - it isn't really that amusing to be dodging 100's of houses while your ranger character is getting his arse kicked by a gargoyle, who, after killing you, loots all of your reagents {assuming that you even found your corpse, of course}...Aarrgh!)

I think that the companies that create these games really underestimate what these sorts of things (like apartments...useful apartments) can really do for the fun-factor of their game. UO is a prime example of this difference. I've yet to come upon a game that had the amount of detail as UO (and I don't mean graphic detail). Any of you who ever played UO probably understand. The game had immersion, even if this immersion also came with an ungodly number of bugs. Not only could you really be unique if you wanted to, (I am Sir Drakesthar, the mace-wielding fisherman chef - baker of fish pies, king of the camp fire, and destroyer of worlds!) but you could do so many things...and just because something was somewhat useless, doesn't mean it wasn't fun. For example, just about anyone who ever had a character in UO ended up playing "dress up" for some amount of time: with all of the different types of clothing combined with layering and being able to dye your clothes a lot of different hues, it was really cool to just get dressed. (This also allowed for guild colors and guild garb, which was pretty cool, too.) And once you actually owned a house...that was bliss. You could put so many different things in that house, including your own vendor on the steps. And later, you could lock things down and secure certain items - but, even before then, you could store stuff inside of chests, etc.


I think that fixing apartments is as easy as looking to UO.

1) For instance, houses in UO got progressively more costly as they got larger; well, if everyone got their starter apartment, but were able to upgrade, so to speak, to larger apartments if they payed a certain amount of rent, it would be really neat. (I doubt it would be a huge problem when the apartments aren't actually on the map...) That could take care of guild houses, too. (And if somebody wanted to live in a guild-house-sized apartment all by himself, well, then he could if he pleased, if he could afford the rent...plus, the larger and more prestigious guilds will likely be able to afford larger guild houses {it makes sense to me that larger guilds have larger guild houses}.)

2) Furniture. Well, UO had plenty of furniture. And different syles of it, too, that you could arrange however you pleased. You could put real personality into your dwelling. (And this may be dreaming, but I thought it rather fun to have bookcases. And books. And blank books that you could write in. And if you put a book that you wrote in a public bookcase {I'm not sure about the ones inside of personal dwellings}, it would sometimes spawn into bookcases all around the world.)

3) People are talking about letting others into their apartment more easily. Why not just have a list of players that are allowed in your apartment regardless if you're teamed with them? (This is good for guild houses, too, or if two people are sharing rent, married, etc.) And there could be two lists: those allowed whenever they want (almost like co-ownership), and those allowed only when you're in your apartment (Who'd want to walk into their apartment only to find their drunk friend lying in their bed with a girl he picked up at the club? ). (Maybe you could enable a "doorbell" so your friends don't walk in while you're on the potty ...That is, of course, if they implement bathroom fixtures...pretty low priority, I'd imagine. ) The people on the first list can move stuff around, open drawers, etc., in your apartment, but other people, including those on list 2, can not (like a lockdown so people don't steal your things).

4) Different apartment styles. Maybe I don't like the cold metal look, or maybe I'm not too fond of the current layout.


Anyways, those are just some things thrown out. On a side note, I think it would be neat to introduce, say, catalogs. Like get a Miiir catalog delivered to my apartment. I could order something, send in the money (plus shipping and handling, of course), and it would be there in 3 to 5 business days. Extra for rush delivery. Or get a guild newsletter delivered to my door. Or either of these delivered to a console in my apartment (like what people on this board were mentioning) (although that's a bit like e-mail, so maybe just the catalogs).

I think that Funcom is on a good start, they just need to get nudged along.

(PS Did anybody else besides me notice that the sound that a rollerrat makes sounds an awful lot like the sound a female deer makes in UO? I find the resemblance uncanny. And annoying...I hate that sound, with a passion. I've always turned my speakers down when fighting one of those two creatures.)

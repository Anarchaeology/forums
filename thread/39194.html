Anywhere out of the world : Keredwyn in Wonderland
The nightmares are going on.

I really try to keep them at bay... to drown my life in parties, hunts that can last for weeks, and travelling as much as I can, but sometimes... sometimes, I get the feeling that my life is a dream, while it is my dreams that are rthe real life.

But dreams are dreams, not reality... at least, that's what I used to think... until I discovered that you could really pass through the looking glass.

It was a sunny day at the beginning of April, one of those days that can't possibly go wrong, that shouldn't go wrong. I had decided to taunt the devil's luck one more time by infiltrating the Mutants' Domain, past the outbounds of the Greater Omni Forest. I had promised one of my contacts to retrieve a weapon he had crafted, some months ago, and that had been stolen by outcasts and brought there, but the other reason was that I really like this area and the peaceful atmosphere usually reigning there. I admit that I also wanted to climb the high cliffs once more, peek at the hidden village they're surrounding, and look at the sky and at the whole forest again. I remember how dangerous it was, when I was a little younger in this profession, and had just began exploring the areas near Rome and Omni-1: assassins hidden behind the trees, snipers aiming from the rocks where they used to settle down, awaiting passers-by for the sheer pleasure of plundering, failed experiments gathering around the most clever of the Mutants... Now it seems that I just run too fast for them, and they don't even try to catch me again... and if perchance it should happen... I can deal pretty well with them. Those creatures and plunderers don't resist my twin swords very well.

But here I was. I had sneaked to the village by the end of the day, hiding between rocks, simply watching them mind their own businesses. This was something I had begun to understand a long time ago already; you could say whatever you wanted about them being mutants and highwaymen, but the fact was that if you left them alone, they usually left you alone too. Without being a specially comfortable place, the biggest of their villages still appeared to me like a place where they were satisfied with their living; they had friends, they had families, and thus, I simply kept on watching, enjoying the view, before going minding my business, not very far from there.

This had been tougher than expected. Instead of a handful of thieves, I had found myself nose-to-nose with a well-prepared group of spies and sharpshooters that were seemingly awaiting some visit in the days to come. I must confess that it was very hard; I usually fight lightly and quickly, but their slowness, this time, was easily compensated by the fact they hit at least three times harder than me, and without my fire swords, I would have died again and again, the resurrection shock stronger and stronger at each new insurance reclaim. It's not as if I had all to lose - careful as usual, I had insured myself again not far from the forest's entrance - but this process is really painful, and if I can avoid it, well... I do it. It took me more than four hours to get rid of all of them and find the sword that had been stolen to my 'client', and when I finally went out, my clothes and armor soaked with dust and dried blood, the night had already fallen, and given the position of the moons in the sky above teh forest, it was well close to what my father used to call 'the hour of the Wolf'.

If I don't make too many wrong encounters, this will be a good trip back to Omni-1, had I thought at that moment, breathing the fresh air for long minutes before actually turning back and heading to the city, the retrieved sword carefully hidden in my bag. I was longing for a good shower and nw clothes to put on, but one can never know what will happen, so I still took a few minutes to recompute the formulas I had lost during the fights - a light shield in case of one of those 'bad encounters', a speed program if I happened to have to run really fast, and last but not least, one of the programs I preferred, the one that allowed me to morph my whole being into a Reet shape. Reets don't fly very high, but with wings like theirs, I could go faster than with any other human form - and I usually take pleasure in flying, even at only one or two meters from the ground.

All was so calm, so silent, so peaceful, that at first I didn't realize that something was going wrong. Oh, of course, I had passed near a few of those highwaymen preparing another ambush, near the lake, but as I said, the night was already very deep, and when I stopped seeing people, even far from me, I didn't really wondered. It was only when I actually left Greater Omni Forest to come nearer the Screening Area that I understood what was wrong.

There was no sound around me. No birds in the trees, no rustling of feet in the thickets, no groans from some outcast wandering along Dragonsback Ridge - only the sound of my own wings, as I was flying now faster and faster to the city gates. This feeling of loneliness that had begun to creep in me was more and more present. I am used to hunt and travel alone, as this is something I've done a lot, not being able to stay in a given town for more than a few days, but this time, this time it was really frightening. I was completely alone, and nature itself seemed to have frozen around me!

The feeling I experienced when the first houses came to sight was deep relief, and I hurried to the first shop I could see, turning back into my huma form. The door was locked... or should I say that it was like there had never been any door? As if it had simply been put here, against the wall, or painted directly on it. There was no way of entering - neither in the weapons shop, nor in the pharma shop, nor in any of the nearby houses. I looked around me for the bank terminal - nothing. No insurance terminal either. No reclaim booth, and no new citizen sitting in front of it. All people, all machines had simply... disappeared. Only I was left.

I ran and ran, again and again, between the sets of houses along the external wall. In none of them could I find any terminal or mission booth. The door leading to the Dancing Atrox club - the one where I had attended parties and met friends, a small place, but so comfortable - seemed like a fake too; I had half hoped to be able to enter there, talk to the bartender, find a few people whose presence would convince me that this was only an illusion, maybe even found a few friends dancing with Russel Aaronstein, the Atrox that usually worked there on Thursday evenings... but nothing. Strictly nothing!

Afraid, I looked at my map. Not so long ago, I had bought a few add-on modules that allowed me to spot people and aggressive beings, which was a very nifty tool when I had to go from one place to the other and had no time to waste with being attacked on the road. This time... my map was empty. Totally empty. The only living spot I could find on its screen was myself, standing in the middle of the deserted village like some lost child waiting for her parents to come and take her back home. I turned my communicator on, trying to log back into OT main channel for the last news, but the only thing I received was a faint buzz, as if the line was dead. I couldn't contact anyone else either - nor a friend, nor my contacts, nor even the one to whom I was to bring the sword back.

Fear slowly turned into alarm, then panick. This was a nightmare. This was just another nightmare. Certainly would I suddenly wake up, and realize that I had brought the sword back a few days ago already, or even that I had never come back in Omni Forest on this day.

But what if I never was to wake up?...

I just couldn't stay here, without attempting anything. Gathering my wits and courage, I simply resumed my run until I reached the southern gate leading to Omni-Entertainment East. There were no Guards at the entrance, which only added to the fear growing in me, threatening to flood me if I was ever to give up for a single second. The sound of my steps echoing on the long tunnel's ground was so scary; not even the usual buzz of the confinement field, not even the usual noises coming from the town. When I reached the end of the tunnel, I closed my eyes for a few seconds, muttering to myself, desperately hoping that when I'd open them up, I'd find myself back in the Entertainment District, as if nothing had ever happened. Hope is life, according to the saying. Hoping, and helping oneself.

I opened my eyes, and gasped for air, because the shock was so strong that even if I had wanted to scream, I couldn't have managed to push a single sound out of my mouth. The desert. The desert lying in front of me, around me, everywhere, an infinite landscape of heavy sand muffling every sound, every echo. For the first time in my life, I was tasting what the words 'end of the world' were truly meaning. I really was at the end of the world with no way of coming back to the life I had known until then. I thought of my father, always so proud of his children, telling us that one day, we'd be able to travel the whole planet and live extraordinary lives. I thought of Corwin, who had studied so hard to become an Engineer, and then had suddenly disappeared after this incident at the Mines. I thought of Brendan, who had taught me how to fight, how to use twin swords to their full potential, how to make my way through the adults' life. I thought of all I had known, and lost in a handful of seconds.

And then, fighting to back up the tears, I suddenly understood.

I was in Wonderland.

Anywhere out of the world : Keredwyn in Wonderland (Part II)
'Wonderland' is a legend told by a few Adventurers, but according to what I had heard about it, some other people had already experienced it too. It was said that in very scarce situations - so scarce, actually, that there was only one chance over a million that it could happen - the nanobots generating the confinement fields between each suppression zone could see their programmation fumbling for a micro-second, causing the Notum field to disrupt on a tiny part of its net. That wouldn't make the whole field collapse, but it was enough to cause one or a few people to... slip in the interstice. Adventurers called this 'falling through the looking glass', referring to an old book that had been written on Terra, many centuries ago. And truly, this was a reflection of the world I had known. A perfect reflection, crafted from the memory files of the machines generating the confinement, but without people, without animals, without any terminal - just the illusion of my world.

Overwhelmed by the immensity of the desert in front of my eyes, I slowly backed up into the tunnel, turning my back on the infinite plain to head back to Omni Forest. I felt like crying, crying again and again, but I knew that standing here wouldn't be a solution. I tried to recall what exactly I knew about Wonderland. I had no means of contacting anyone on Rubi-Ka, not even one of the Advisors working for Omni-tek, not even one of the Chief Engineers in charge of the confinement fields; this time, I was all by myself, lost in a part of Rubi-Ka that even to Alice would have seemed a nightmare rather than an extraordinary journey on the other side.

Maybe I could try to find back the exact point where I had crossed the confinement field... maybe if passing through the field again at this exact location, I would come back to the true Omni Forest... the one I had always known... This was hazardous and highly likely to fail, but this was the only solution I had - the only hope I could cling to at the moment. I had very little food and water on me - when I had headed to the Mutants village, I knew I'd only need a few hours to go back to Omni-1, and thus, I hadn't bothered with putting too many things in my backpack; if this situation was to last to long, I'd end up dying of hunger, and I didn't dare count on the last insurance scan I had down, at the entrance of the Forest. And if dying in Wonderland didn't allow you to come back?... If dying here meant that I'd never, never wake up again?...

I was afraid.

"If you're lost, always try to find a road or a river, and follow it. It will eventually lead you back to a town, or at least to some people", my father used to tell me. And so, I followed his advice, and let my feet carry me along the road cruising through Omni Forest, solitary being trying to find her way home. I knew that I had very little chance to find the exact point at where I had crossed the confinement field, but I remembered having been not too far from the road when this had happened. The Mutants Village was at the south-east - this at least I remembered well, for having been there more than one - and if following this way, once I'd reach the proper coordinates on my map, then my chances surely would increase.

I hoped they would, and that my map system itself hadn't been flawed by the disruption.

I ran, I ran, for hours that seemed to be days, going mad with the overwhelming silence, going mad with fear and loneliness. This feeling... this feeling I used to experience in my dreams... I didn't want to have to deal with it in my real life! I wanted to go back home. I wanted to meet people, and to drown myself in the rush of the town, though usually I can't stand doing so for more than a few hours, or a few days if things go well. I wanted to hug my friends, I wanted to find my family again, I think I'd even have jumped at the neck of a Clanner, if I had met one, just to hug him and feel how it was good to be surrounded by sentient and living beings again. I was thinking of the past, of those old songs again. "Tousan ga nokoshta atsui omoi, Kaasan ga kureta ano manazashi...". How odd, that in such a situation, one can still have this kind of thoughts...

I ran, I ran, more and more desperate as the hours went by, as the dawn came and the first rays of the sun illuminated the empty woods and the ghostly landscape, looking for the limit that would mark the confinement field. This couldn't be very far, could it? Any supression area was limited by a certain size, or the gas simply wouldn't spread enough to be really efficient. That was what confinement fields were for, after all. I had to find one. It couldn't be otherwise.

And then, all of a sudden again, I felt the slight static shock on my skin again, and the buz in my comm-link becoming sound and voices again. I saw silhouettes running on the road, running toward me - two Soldiers back from hunting or from a mission, certainly. I heard birds singing and groans again, I heard the wind, and the silence disappearing to leave room again to what were the natural noises of the forest.

"Human beings...? At last! Humans!" did I shout, half-laughing, half crying, running in the Soldiers' direction. They looked at me askance and passed their way without a word, though thay surely must have talked about this between themselves later, but I didn't care. I was in my world again. I was dancing like a madwoman on the road, but I was in my world again. And I was feeling so alive, as I had never felt before. Never, never would I want to fall back through the looking-glass again. My world wasn't the best of all, but it was my world.

Dazzled, confused, walking as if I still was in a dream, I headed back to Omni-Entertainment; this time, when I passed through the field, everything was normal, as it had always been, the rush of people, the Guards with their heavy guns checking the ID of every people wanting to pass the gates, the noisy crowd, the violent buzz of the whompas echoing on the square each time they were used... Without thinking, I took the one leading to Rome, where I had to met my contact to give him the sword back. If he found me a little odd, a little absent-minded, at least he said nothing about it, and let me go after having paid me. He wouldn't have understood if I had mentioned Wonderland, anyhow.

I stayed at home for the rest of the day, sitting in the main room of my appartment in Blue Backyard 7, looking at the pictures hanging on the walls. One of Zack, in his uniform of the Army, the day before he officially joined the rank of the Soldiers. One of Corwin and Brendan, surrounded by a cloud of nanobots. One of the twins, standing with my parents near the door of our house, before it was destroyed in the last and fatal attack. I had this song in my head again, humming it for myself - an old song from Terra, which lyrics I hadn't understood until Mom explained them to me.

Tousan ga nokoshta atsui omoi,
Kaasan ga kureta ano manazashi.
Chikyuu wa mawaru, kimi wo kakushte
Kagayaku hitomi, kirameku tomoshibi
Chikyuu wa mawaru, kimi wo nosete
Istuka kitto dearu, bokura wo nosete...

Father left me his burning desire
Mother gave me her eyes
The Earth is turning, hiding you,
Shining eyes, twinkling lights,
The Earth is turning, carrying you,
Carrying us both who'll surely meet...

I had been happy, in that time. I had been happy, before finding this cursed Soul Stone, before the dreams had begun.

Then, when I realized that night was falling again, I came back to my feet with a sigh, and opened my wardrobe to find something that'd fit my mood for what I was planning to do.

Tonight, I'd spend a few hours at Rompa's.

I needed to be with people again.

I needed to feel alive. Simply alive.

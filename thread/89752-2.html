Re: Wanna laff ?
quote:
Originally posted by Infamine
" The Soldier is the master of firearms, and the pillar of any combat squad. He has a huge potential for dealing damage at long range, and the health to withstand being on the receiving end. The Soldier is lacking in most other fields though, but with the use of the proper nano programs he is so effective at dishing out damage, that anything else becomes insignificant. "

Quoted from http://www.anarchy-online.com/conten...6/soldier.html
Actually I think that description is very acurate. For the life of me I can't understand why soldiers in such numbers constantly complain, they genuinely seem to be under the impression they are gimped somehow. Soldiers make excellent team mates with their combat prowess and attractive buffs. Soldiers are top notch in towers pvp and the only profession who can actually tank numberous opponents and expect to get away with it. (well, apart from docs that is )

I think that some of the disgruntlement is caused by the opinion they share with many playing the so called "combat classes" (IMO all classes are combat classes); that they should be superior to anyone in combat. To a large extent they are superior, but in an almost exclusively combat based game, there must be cross profession combat balance. Some support classes; doctors and traders foremost, have disrupted that balance over a period of time. But traders have had their pvp ability curtailed, first with the heal nerf/fix and later the crit nerf affected that profession harder than most. Consequently traders are a lot closer to acheiving balance than ever before in my experience. Certainly traders make deadly duelists, but in towers pvp they can be swiftly dispatched. Only doctors (and probably NTs) really outshine soldiers in towers pvp, though an assault with EQB enforcers is NASTY.

The point of my reasoning being, you can't give much love to a profession already that good in pvp (ofcourse non-pvp related aspects could be adressed), without foregoing of what I consider vital to the enjoyment of all professions in the long run, balance.

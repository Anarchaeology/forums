Percentages
Any designer of a program that has to take into account thousands of events per second that may involve multiple calculations per event would be best served to avoid percentages like the plague. The overhead of a floating point calculation versus a simple addition is appreciable when taking into consideration the amount of players originally envisioned for Rubi'Ka.

Think about these two scenarios:

1. Hit a mob for x and add y to it for total damage.
2. Hit a mob for x, multiply x by z to get y, and add y to x for total damage.

Kind of the round about route but it shows why you avoid any kind of percentage like the plague if you are concerned about server lag. A comparison of attack rating versus defense to determine if you hit is much preferred to dividing attack by defense to get a percentage chance to hit and then 'rolling the dice' to determine if you hit.

When playing pen and paper or single player games, the designer has the opportunity to make things as realistic and interesting as possible. With up to 10,000 people playing at once, you have to cut corners at every chance you can.

Imagine if the designers were so short sighted as to not have total damage adders included in the character object. I doubt this would be the case. But in that instance, they could possibly end up with having to multiply your initial damage by an adder for your left ring, then multiply for your right ring, then multiply for any nano effects, etc., etc. All this for thousands of potential players at the same time. Adding is a lot easier.

Assuming they gave in to the dark side and decided to stick with straight +x damage then could simply modify the minimum damage number on your weapon (hidden behind the scenes to you) whenever you equip a ring or change weapons. In that case there is ZERO extra math work required to be done during combat. Pretty sneaky.

When it comes to the experience adders they can afford to relax a bit. Things actually die a LOT less often than things are being shot at. Also the priority of handing out experience is a LOT less critical than knowing how much damage you did on your last shot. As long as the monster is dead, most of us can wait 10 seconds to see how much experience we got. We can't afford to wait that long to see if our last shot finished off the monster when we only have a sliver of life left. Thus, combat rules need to be as simple as possible.

What this all boils down to is that over the next six months as things settle down, FunCom absolutely must work to provide information to the players (or Prima for an updated guide) as to how things work. They also have to fix descriptions for things that don't work as advertised. I pity those people who are paying millions of credits for rings that add as little as 5 damage to their attacks of over 500.

Blackpetal awoke with a start, gasping for breath. She wiped away the cold sweat clinging to her face and looked at the clock: 0323. Throughout the room, blue-green shadows danced and swayed as reflected light from Rome snuck in around the edges of the window blinds, flickering across the walls and swimming across the ceiling like algae. Petal shuddered and turned her head slightly. Next to her, Betji remained peacefully asleep. Blackpetal gently moved her lover's arm to free herself and sat up in the bed.

This wasn't the first time Petal had awakened in this manner. Still, the nightmares had subsided for a while, drawn into the background noise of her subconscious. It seemed clear now, however, that the demons that were haunting her had not been dispelled, but had merely been biding their time...waiting. She shivered again. The room wasn't cold, but Petal felt an almost supernatural chill creep along her spine. She quickly pushed the feeling aside and stood, letting her bare feet touch the floor. She stretched, her long thin arms extending high over her head.

As she stood, Petal became captivated by her shadow on the wall. The projection mirrored her precisely, of course, but Petal scrutinized every detail of the pattern, as if waiting for a tell-tale sign that the shadow was merely mimicking her, and could be revealed if caught off-guard. After a moment, impatience gripped her exhausted mind; the shadow stubbornly held to its secrets, and Petal slowly made her way across the darkened floor to the bathroom. She quietly closed the door and turned on the light, wincing as the pure white light reflected through the bathroom. Petal hurredly shut off the low-light filter in her optical implant and sighed in relief as the room returned to only modestly painful brightness.

Petal started a stream of cold water into the sink and splashed a few icy handfuls in her face. As drops of water dripped off the end of her nose and off the tip of her chin, Petal sighed and examined her face in the bathroom mirror. I look tired, she thought. Not without good reason, though. The past two weeks had afforded her little sleep, the nightmares encroaching on her every somnolent moment, while her waking hours (those not occupied with the business of Omni-Tek, Devil's Advocate, or her own legal practice) were devoted to unravelling the deeper mystery that seemed to be at work both in her dreams and on the streets of Omni-1.

Arakiba's death, the latest in a string of mysterious killings, was very inconvenient. Whatever secrets he might have known seemed certain to have perished with him, and Omni-Tek's records of him had faded into nothingness, leaving Petal to doubt even her own memories of her encounters with the enigmantic agent. She sighed and examined her face again. Arakiba claimed that each of Demoneyes' "chosen" bore a mark, but there was none to see. Petal smiled ruefully. Her "protection" might have shielded her somewhat, but it hadn't saved her from nightmares, or from mental "visits" from the monster in her waking hours. Petal spat distastefully into the sink and pushed several more handfuls of water into her face, as if to purify herself.

Petal clenched her fist resolutely. Whatever was coming (and the apocalyptic visions in her mind left Petal with little doubt but that SOMETHING was coming), she would come out to meet it. A day of reckoning was fast approaching.

March 29477 RST

However, as we all know, this technology does not make us immortal. One can die of nartural causes, and the life-force remains dead.

"He claimed he needed "followers" to help him... and he begun to... bite 'em. He was carrying a virus, and he used to transmit it this way. There's one thing I can tell ya: I know of *no* Nanomage who survived the disease, though, apart from Damon himself."

There is no murderer on the loose. It is clear to the evidence presented that instead of a murderer, it is instead a viral infection that is being spread. "Why yes it does indeed seem to be a story from fantasy, the fact remains it is just that: Fantasy. There has been fewer cases of Solitus proving to be very resistant to the disease. "This is easy to believe. After all, these races are natural to Rubi-Ka and their bodies themselves are not as well-built as the others. The Atrox were built as 'super-Solitus' and therefore are able to deflect the negative effects of the disease hardly even noticing it."

But it ain't weirder than corpses dissolving in a puddle of goo in a matter of minutes."

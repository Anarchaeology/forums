Thoughts on a Fortnight (In Three Parts)
+ Part One of Three +

Greetings,

I've watched the progress of things over these last two weeks and, like everyone else, have decided to weigh in with my input. My intentions are not to present myself as either a fanboy or a Funcom hater, but as an average (informed) consumer and an insider in the software business (though not the game business). I doubt that what I offer in commentary below is a revelation, but after two weeks of frustration, perhaps getting things off my chest will give me the breath of air that I need to wait out the fixes pending in this game so that, eventually, I might even get to play.

First, I am not an avid gamer. My career doesn't offer the free time to spend playing games and especially not addictive, time-consuming MMORPGs. Anarchy Online, however, caught my attention and everyone recommended that I give it a shot. After all, with people like the id Software folks looking forward to the game, there had to be something redeeming about it, right? So, with a company-forced vacation coming up, I ordered it from Amazon. I even paid to have it shipped next-day air delivery. I never take vacations and other than playing this game, I didn't have a clue how I would pass the time, short of sneaking onto the network from home (I telecommute from another state) and working anyway.

The day after I ordered the game, I began to hear rumors that the game was going to flop big time. As my anticipation for the game grew, I became more and more obsessed with reading everything I could about the game, the characters, the story and the features. It wasn't long before I started seeing warnings of server and scalability problems for the AO world. Messages from beta-testers who doubted the viability of the finished product and, by that evening, users who had picked up their copies of the game and began running into the most ridiculous of problems. Some of which I list below:


Registration Process
Despite the documentation stating that the registration process (requiring a credit card) was done over a secure server, it was not. Tens of thousands of people transferring their credit card numbers over insecure http. Funcom stated that it was a problem with their certificate supplier. In truth, that's a very likely scenario, but when 35,000 credit card numbers and the success of your product and company depend on a single certificate, I would think this would be one of the very first details you would consider before even releasing your product to manufacture. Things can and do go wrong. In this line of business, you need to prepare for them. Yes, credit card fraud on the internet is rare, but it does happen. There are many reports of millions of card numbers being stolen at a time from crackers around the world. If your front-end for acquiring the credit card number isn't secured, how can one reasonably expect the backend (database) to be secured as well?


Patching
Fortunately, I avoided having any of the patching problems. I logged in and the patches were automatically applied. Unfortunately, thousands of people had an extremely difficult time acquiring the patches, applying them and then using AO afterward. Interesting was the fact that the server upgraded me to one version, restarted and then upgraded me to the next version after that, instead of just upgrading me to the most recent version which would usually (with most games) contain all of the previous fixes as well (usually there are two versions - one for people patching from earlier versions and people patching from the most recent version). I'm on a fast connection, but for modem users, this would be horrendous, even though the patches themselves are not huge. All in all, the patching process (for me) has not sucked, but could be better.


Logging In
Initially, logging in was a pain in the ass. In fact, it was nearly impossible. Even after the first seven days, it was difficult to log in. Not a unique experience, I spent an entire day trying to log in(and I logged it in a text file just for kicks - remember, I was on vacation here and it was either this or sneak in getting work done for my employer when they had forbidden me to!). It took 14 hours of logging in and getting various error messages (dimension not available, server not found, character already logged in, server timed out . . .) before I could stay logged in more than 90 seconds.


Creating the Character
Creating my first character, despite the lag and choppiness, was fun. I liked the interface, presentation and walkthrough. I can see where they were going with this. Light on the physical documentation and heavy on the in-game walkthrough. I believe this is the way future games will continue to progress. Where a person can install a game and not have to read a single bit of documentation, yet comprehend the game and its usage fully by context-sensitive help and guidance. Creating my character took forever, mostly because I was not alone in the room (there were two other people) and it took two or more minutes between each click of the mouse and the response of the server. For instance, I'd click on the "next face" button and it would be up to several minutes before the face actually appeared. All in all, I probably spent over an hour creating my first character.

Also, the naming process sucked. I tried some bizarre names and none of them were accepted. I finally had to look on the back of an aspirin bottle and combine a couple syllables of a couple strange ingredients to come up with something that wasn't taken. Allowing spaces and multiple word names would have helped. Or, since the system automatically generates a non-unique first and last name for you, using all three as a unique-identifier should have worked. For example, the server would generate 'randomfirstname "yournick" randomlastname'. As long as nobody else with your nickname has the same system generated first and last name, it should be considered unique and, thus, allow an almost unlimited supply of names. Alas, they didn't and it doesn't. So you have to choose from the inane and unintelligible and pull something out of the air.

Of course, the fact that there were two minute lags between each name choice made it more frustrating.


Lag
Usually, lag is a function of your distance (hops) from the server and it's not the host servers fault if you experience horrendous lag. With a game like Quake, there are enough servers to choose from that you can almost always find one close enough to you that you can get a decent ping. Since I moved to DSL, I usually find a large selection of servers hitting at 12ms to 60ms ping. Lag is relatively unheard of there.

With AO, lag is mostly (at this time at least) a result of the server and how the world is populated and hosted. Except for a few reported rarities (such as the people in these forums who claim that there are no such problems and that any reported problems are the fault of the user and their systems), there is nothing you can do to avoid the horrible pings, frame rates and lag. In fact, if the lag would drop, the frame rates would increase massively. The world and graphics are quite complex, but a competent video card can handle it without problems (even at a high resolution such as 1600x1200x32). When you introduce a large number of players and the use of TCP as the networking protocol though, the frame rate is limited by the need for the server to keep tabs on where each person is and what they are doing and all this information eventually has to funnel down to you. This is why you'll often have the super-duper-card-of-the-month but maintain a 4 fps rate.

Now that AO has largely reduced the login and disconnect problems, this seems to be the place their attention needs to be focused the most. Who cares if you can log in if all you're able to do is stare at choppy figures hobble past your screen and complain and whine through a laggy chat with other players?

And despite the suggestions, changing your resolution and graphical options will change very little since the textures and everything are already on your machine. The bottleneck is the network(ing) and not the graphical capabilities of your machine. I may be wrong in this statement, but my testing has shown that with all of the options enabled to their highest ability, my fps and ping (as well as general playing experience) is exactly the same as when I drop all the options down and set my resolution to 640x480x16.

A lot of threads have been dedicated to the discussion of TCP versus UDP as a method for networking in the AO landscape. It seems that Funcom has utilized TCP exclusively and this too may be a downfall. Others with far more game developing experience than myself have a better understanding of this, but it appears that TCP is necessary for some things in an MMORPG, but UDP is needed for the majority. Due to limitations (features, actually) of TCP, it can limit current real-time data and information in favor of ensuring historical information and data are transmitted. For example, with properly deployed UDP, you would know that there is a monster in front of you and it is attacking you at this moment. With TCP, you're more likely to wait for the server to send you the packets telling the client that the monster is walking up toward you and, due to dropped packets or slow packets, your client is just putting together the "here comes the monster" packets while the server has already finished sending the "the monster just hit you three times and killed you" packets.

Another aspect may be too little bandwidth at Funcom and too few machines in the server farm. Granted, maintaining a complex world with tens of thousands of player characters and thousands of NPC's along with weather and animals is a monumental task when doing so for 35,000 people and (presumably, eventually many more). A combination of greater bandwidth, additional machines in the server farm and tweaking of the server code and networking (I sure wish they'd take the id guys up on their apparent offer to work on their networking code), they could potentially resolve things. In the current reality, it's fine to expect users to tolerate a little sporadic lag, but you can't expect players to continue tolerating it over the four year arc of the game. Either they have something special up their sleeves or they are going to have to start over again from scratch (more or less).

Again, I can't stress this enough - no other aspect of the game matters if the lag is not addressed. Specific video card troubles, game play quirks and system bugs are irrelevant if the game can't actually be played in 'real-time'.


The AO World
Funcom's ambition here is perhaps one of the most alluring prospects and why so many of us found it exciting and anticipated the game's release. With other MMORPG's, you were served by one world or domain when you logged in and another user may be served by another world and domain. They were separate but unconnected copies of the same world. Quake is an even more limited example of this. There are thousands of servers available that you can play Quake on. Each of the servers is running quake. Each server, however, has only a limited number of slots it can fill (I believe this is 32 or 64). Once the slots are full, players have to hook up with another server.

Funcom, however, had the idea to put everyone in the game into a single world. A world where every person could exist and interact without dimensional limitations. This is what a true MMORPG should (eventually) be. One true world. One true course of events. One environment in which the actions of one player or faction of players can ripple through to the rest of the players and the world. Unfortunately, this seems to be one of Funcom's greatest downfalls at the moment. As many others have suggested, Funcom may need to split AO up into multiple worlds. I honestly hate this idea and hope Funcom has something up their sleeves to get around it, but if the only way to play is to have multiple worlds, then so be it (although many of us purchased the game because of the expectation that it would be a singular universe).

The world itself looks beautiful. The sunsets and sunrises, the other planets in the sky, the shadowing, the movement of the monsters and other players - it's all fantastic. If this were a beta, it would have everyone drooling for the final release. Since this is the release, however, it just has everyone anxious and distraught.

Options and Configuration
The configuration (OPT) settings should be a little more informative, since little about them is documented in the manual. In general, they're not too bad and they're easily accessed with the F10 key. I have not tested this yet, but if it can not already be done, you should be able to customize the options for each account/character (including resolution and audio settings).

+ Part Two of Three +

Interface
The interface is nice, but needs a lot of work. You need to be able to re-size all of the windows from the information windows when you SHIFT+LEFT-CLICK on an item to the inventory, map, wear and program windows (and definitely the chat window). You need to be able to change their position on the screen. You should possibly be able to change the colors (trivial, but it would be nice).

Moving your character around is far too difficult. In some situations, first-person is a preferable view, but third-person is necessitated under some circumstances. Unfortunately, third-person too often pits you behind a large object as you watch your character run down a long hall or across half the city without you. Or they'll walk through a doorway and you'll be stock on the other side, unable to see anything useful. The graphics for casting nano-programs and firing weapons are choppy/transparent in first-person view, so fighting in third-person is usually easier. Unfortunately, you often find yourself walking into a room in first person so you aren't left behind in the previous room and have to quickly switch to third person to fight characters, then switch back into first person to see the next bad guy, go through the next door or find out who the hell is firing on you already. You may also find that you are attacked by a monster in the room in front of you, but because there is no texture on the space the door would be in, you can't see the monster or the inside of the room.

Along these same lines, it is completely frustrating to see NPC's getting stuck in the corner of a room, trying to exit it as if there were a door there or to be attacked by an enemy that is on the other side of a wall and can not be seen. Even more aggravating is when you are attacked by a monster and you have to proceed through extra button-presses and clicks to begin using your nano-programs on the monster. When your character is a nano-proficient person and not proficient in physical weapons, this is very detrimental to your progression.

Additionally, there's nothing like being attacked by two or three monsters at a time (RollerRats for example) but having the interface limit you to only efficiently fighting one at a time.

Another interface related gripe is all of the small things that have been overlooked. As an example, the option to "toggle planetmap" is mispelled as "plaentmap". Yes, an inconsequential mistake, but when it is compounded with all the other spelling and grammatical flaws throughout the game (understanding the descriptions of some of the items is nearly impossible), it becomes a larger issue possibly indicative of the lack of beta-bashing in the earlier stages before the game RTM'd.

Chatting is not fully usable yet, either. Some features (like ignoring people) seem to work only partially or occasionally. The 'shout' method is used far too often as a general discussion mode. I like the idea of being able to talk and whisper within a vicinity and to shout within a slightly larger area (with restrictions) - anything further should be via a /tell only message directly to the person, group, team or organization you intend to receive your message. The chat area is just too full of information about battles, conversations and actions as well as status updates. You can modify these reports in the settings configuration, but you may not want to remove them completely - just separate them or squelch them to a degree somehow.


Gameplay
The potential for fun, balanced gameplay is looming. I don't think its there yet. A lot of this has to do with various glitches, such as credits, experience and possessions randomly disappearing, users being unable to zone into areas of the game, people getting stuck in shops or cities or even having your character stuck in between zones - unable to play at all. Rumor has it that you can petition a GM to remove you if you give them the coords, but in a RTM'd product, it hardly seems that this should be necessary so frequently. Also, the only time I have seen a GM in the game is when they are broadcasting an impending "server-going-down" message.

A sufficient commentary on gameplay is impossible at this time since lag prevents one from experiencing it. From my limited exposure, however, I can return to the initial log I kept in a text file over the first day or two of owning the game (about a week after it was released).

To start with, it was a battle just to log in and stay logged in. After dozens (well over 100) attempts to log in, a 15 minute session was finally accomplished. This took approximately 14 hours to achieve. After this, it was a matter of sitting in a lonely backyard area wondering where all the monsters were. Due to lag, they simply weren't there. I left the machine on nearby while I worked on some code for one of any number of projects and logged in when the connection dropped. It was another 12 hours before any monsters began to appear. After 26 hours since my initial log in attempt, I had my first kill. It was 4am and I had the server to myself for almost an hour before things picked up, people started to return and the game became unbearable again.

I attempted to play off and on that day and it took almost an entire day of playing just to achieve level 4. By midnight, I had made it to level 5 and couldn't figure out why I couldn't get any more missions. I finally realized that perhaps you can't get any missions in the backyard after level 4 was accomplished. A few other players suggested that it was either level 4 or level 5. I may have missed it, but I don't recall seeing this in the documentation and the mission dispenser certainly didn't suggest that I was not qualified for any of the missions. Instead, I spent an hour trying to get missions and wondering if perhaps lag was keeping the mission dispenser from fulfilling requests.

Once I figured out that I had to get into town and start doing missions there, the GM logged in and told everyone the server was going down in 15 minutes. The server was down for 90 minutes and, according to one of the sites that has tracking the server up time, the US server averaged 13 to 15 percent down time per day for the entire first week. Not terribly great considering little or nothing was accomplished to make the game playable yet.

For the last two days, I haven't even bothered playing. I'll log in a couple times each day to see if it is worth spending time on, but the lag is always so unbearable that I just press the X key, wait for five to thirty seconds for the server respond and see my guy sit down, then exit the game. During this time, I usually see countless other people spending their time online complaining via chat about how they cannot play. How Funcom manages to anger tens of thousands of customers with a not-ready-for-prime-time game and keep them coming back like the masochistic lemmings they appear to be astonishes me. Hundreds of people just sitting there in an unplayable game complaining about the lag and how unplayable it is. If that isn't irony, I'm using the wrong dictionary.


Funcom
Funcom has need to be wearing asbestos suits at the moment. The community in general is very angry and understandably so. While many are forgiving and patient, many are angry and irritated. While some sit quietly by, waiting for each pending update for that magical day when everything may come out all right and they can finally log in and play, others threaten lawsuits and attack those members of Funcom who bother to spend their time responding to posts in this forum.

Funcom has made a lot of mistakes in how they have handled this. In the beginning, they did not even seem to comprehend that the problems were as huge as they are. Then they promised to give us daily updates and get to work on the problem immediately. A week later, they've only managed to eek out one response in two or three days after incessant whining by customers. It may have been the weekend, but with the money of 35,000 people on the line and the future of an entire company, I would hope that the full crew would be in-house battling these demons all weekend and giving updates readily. The thought has crossed my mind that perhaps in such a socialist country as Norway, they are not legally allowed to work over-time or on the weekends (I have had many troubles with other European countries who frown on workers putting in so much time - often I've had to make up for them by working a great amount of extra time myself. Of course, being an American and being a salaried one at that, it's just considered 'part of the job' for me.).

As Funcom has mentioned, they don't feel that it is worth giving updates often when the community backlash is so strong and so many people don't believe the updates in the first place. I don't see this as a legitimate reason for avoiding the updates, though. Ignoring the phone, FAX and e-mail is a great way to avoid confrontation and ill-will, too. In fact, ignoring customers completely is a wonderful way to avoid these things. Unfortunately, dealing with them is a part of business and they should keep their word as initially stated; update us daily. In fact, it wouldn't hurt to update us several times per day. Even if nothing is accomplished, tell us what you're working on. Are you working on the lag? Are you working on splitting the worlds up? Are you working on keeping the world as one and fixing the current constraints? Are you neglecting the lag issues and just focusing on individual video driver problems? Do you see things being fixed this week? This month? Should I just return the game and look forward to the next hot MMORPG? Give us something and give it to us often. The worst news is no news at all. It's the lack of news that keeps people foaming at the mouth and propagating conspiracy theories and suggesting lack of interest on the part of your company.

Refunds
The way the software industry is positioned, there's no chance of getting a refund from Funcom. The way the retail industry is set up, there's little chance of getting a refund from most distributors. The few and far-between cases of success will be by those who make strong initial statements to their retailer or distributor and those who are consistent, insistent and unwavering.

Some people have suggested that they will simply resell the game. Unfortunately, you're stuck there also. The package clearly states "not for resale if opened" and "not for sale by original owner". In other words, you are stuck with this game like a club-foot. No getting out of it.

At this point, I have contacted Amazon and inquired as to the possibility of returning the game. I provided a detailed example of the problems everyone has been encountering, the state of the product and links to this forum, several other sites with common forums filled with complains, that comic strip poking fun at AO and it's deployment debacle and to the reviews of Amazon's own customers on their own website (including a very lengthy review by me which should appear in the next few days).

I described two possible scenarios to Amazon and requested their comments on each. Scenario one involves me sending the product back for a full refund, including the cost of next-day air delivery (I wouldn't have had it delivered by air if I'd known to expect a product in a currently worthless state) and the cost of return shipment. Scenario two (and this was my preferable one) involves me keeping the game for an agreed upon amount of time, hoping that Funcom solves these problems and keeping the game if they live up to the hopes. If they fail by the agreed upon time period, then I return the game under the same refund compensation as mentioned in the first scenario.

Amazon has been responsive to me in the past. In fact, rumors of their customer service by colleagues is what prompted me to give Amazon a chance in the first place (I was always one who despised their company). I've had $200 products that failed, filled out a return slip online and had the product there at my door the next day, before I had even been able to package up the original, let alone ship it back to them. And, as I pointed out to them in my e-mail, I have spent a substantial amount of money with their company over the last few months and have every intention of continuing to do so indefinitely if they can use this opportunity to prove that they are a reliable, flexible and responsible company. If they don't take this chance to show a little consideration for their customers, then I'll gladly return all of the products that I have purchased which are still fully under the return policy and cease all future ties with them.

I feel confident that I will receive a favorable response from them, but will not be surprised if they still reply "sorry, we can't do that". As I explained to them, returning AO is not at all like returning a CD because you didn't like the music or a typical game or other software product because the disc was bad. No number of replacements is going to fix this product and I do not care to risk $65+ on a product that may or may not ever live up to the hype.

I sincerely hope that Funcom blows everyone away with coming fixes and makes us all happy. Funcom is at a point where they can make a lot of life-time fans or a lot of life-time enemies. There are thousands of other developers out there who would be happy to receive our money in the future, leaving Funcom as nothing more than a footnote in software history and a measuring stick for future MMORPG failures. I want, as much as anyone else, to see Funcom pull this off and I will continue to bear with them for awhile. I'd like to hang around and let them work things out for a couple months if I could, but if I and others don't start moving on potential refunds now, the slim chance of a refund will surely turn into absolutely zero chance and the game may never be playable. Some people appear to be willing to gamble with their money, but not everyone feels so freely about it. Most of my purchases in life are based on the fact that there is a claim made by the manufacturer of the product and a service intended to be rendered or a need fulfilled by the product. If any or all of those are not met or achieved, then the product is defective or misrepresented and returned for a refund. It's that simple. There's no need to measure the software industry by any other standard, though holding them to it is exceedingly rough.

I can sympathize with Funcom and the engineers who produced it (and certainly the poor souls in technical support who likely receive our harsh words and criticisms hourly). I know what the software business can be like. Those at the core of the process and development don't want things to fail. After all, this game is their baby. They've poured four or more years of their heart and soul into it and they, like all of us, wish to see it succeed and thrive. They've nearly created a living breathing world. What could be more exciting on their part? And watching it croak and spasm miserably has got to be completely frustrating. Even more frustrating is that it is the fault of (if anyone at all) marketing and management - as is so often the case in this industry. I'm sure the designers and programmers couldn't have cared less if the deployment had to be delayed another six months to produce a beautiful and smooth experience for the end-users. But the typical fashion of a product manager is to say "well, it's 90% complete - let's release it and catch the last 10% in patches after release". When it comes to the bottom line, this is understandable. It's just a shame that the remaining 10% was the entire crux of the product - namely, networking and scaling.

Though I have my suspicions and am somewhat paranoid about the prospect that perhaps Funcom intentionally released this buggy and horrid product on the world and misrepresented it, I am by nature lenient and more likely to feel (unless given great cause to believe otherwise) that they are simply underdogs who had a bad release and saw a lot of things blow up in their face. I really want to see them pull this one off. I want to see their product thrive and become a war-story they can talk about in the pub after long nights at work. I don't want to see this fail at all. But, like everyone else, my patience too will eventually grow thin and evaporate.

If I can successfully return my product for a full refund (including all shipping and return costs), I could walk away from this experience as a frustrated but sympathetic customer. If I find that I am stuck with a pretty-box and a shiny disc for the price of my monthly phone bill, I'll come away from this as a frustrated and angry customer who will not do business with Funcom again and will steer friends and colleagues away as well.

If it comes down to it, I'll simply return the product (assuming Amazon will accept it) and start looking forward to another MMORPG (I won't let this experience keep me away from exploring MMORPGs).

Wishes of success to Funcom and patience to fellow gamers,

A good post.

However, one thing that I would like to point out is that FunCom have made a lot of advancements over the last 2 weeks.

The game is now playable - has been since Sunday for me. Sure, you really need to stay out of towns and try not to zone too much, and at times lag is really bad - but it is playable.

Which professions need a LOVE PATCH-- desperately?
Yes, 1 in 6 players are now MAs. Doesn't that suggest something is wrong??

Some professions are so underplayed, the need for Love Patches speak for themself.

NEED Love Patch:

Traders (2nd least played)
Bureaucrats (least played)
Engineers


Could USE a Love Patch:

Nano-Techs
Doctors


None of these professions are fundamentally broken. But they do need to do more/better and to be more/better than they are right now.

Traders-- Face it, their charm sucks. Give them the ability to charm like bureaucrats, but limited to one pet.

Bureaucrats-- charm is useless for huge level ranges due to incorrect psychic checks, which really gimps them hard. This profession should have the ability to charm 2 pets for longer periods of time. Their damage output stinks with the weak droid + mostly broken charm (level 100+). Give crats more buffs like a weaker NT humidity extractor line and/or a weak pet buffing line.

Engineers- Pet pathing is the bane of engineers. Since they can't calm/mezz/stun they can quickly be overwhelmed when a mistake is made. Engineers would be fine if it weren't for pathing.. they need something to compensate. Ideas?

---
Could USE a Love Patch-

NTs-- the real problem with NTs get worse with the next patch. MPs and Bureaucrats too. Weapons are getting better and better but direct-damage nukes stay the same. Something needs to be done about this, otherwise playing nukers will become pointless when weapons profs can do way more damage per unit of time.

Docs- Nothing wrong with them but at high levels MOBs die in 10 seconds or people just start dying regardless a doc a not. They just don't seem too useful in many circumstances at high level. Docs should maybe get a new line of super- HP buffs.

My take on these things...

Traders: They have too many broken, useless, or bugged nano lines, and these ought to be fixed or made viable. The nano-drain-over-time lines and the charm lines are a great example. The armorclass drain lines need to take up less NCU. Health funnels really should return more hitpoints to the user at mid and high level. Possibly, possibly, now that 20% OE rules are in, the amount of NCU a wrangle eats can be reduced. But wrangles (and mochams for that matter) have been infamous NCU hogs as a way to limit overequipping. I don't know if that's really a bad thing.

Traders are relatively unpopular now not because they are underpowered, but rather, because they require careful planning and development, are expensive to equip, and are most effective with a strategic play-style centered on plunder-laddering (which is hard to maintain). They remain extremely powerful in both PVM and PVP despite the heal 'fix' (sarcastic quotes), but they are no longer undefeatably uber. I think that's the biggest barrier to people playing traders - they start traders with the expectation of charging 300k for a wrangle, soloing 100% team missions, and PVPing five people at a time by themselves, all without breaking a sweat... in the first week. When they find out that it's a lot harder than it looks, they roll an easier character to play. Add in that traders aren't 'glamorous' in concept and you end up with a profession that I bet almost everyone has tried and almost all have shelved. My trader got to level 7 before I got bored

Crats: Their charm lines need to be tweaked slightly, so that it remains useful while they are title capped and at higher levels. Raising the psychic checks at least on the unstable charm, or giving the profession a good psychic debuff would go a long way towards making this possible. But really, they are a very powerful profession for someone who knows how to play them, one of the best professions at soloing in the game while still bringing a great deal of value to a team.

The primary barriers to playing a crat in my mind aren't pet pathing, but rather that like traders, crats require some strategy to get right. They have many crowd control options and up to three pets with their charms, which must sometimes be refreshed. As a result, they need to think on their feet and juggle a lot of things - calms, charms, roots, robot, and nukes - to fully realize their potential. Finally, being an 'office worker' in concept isn't very glamorous or heroic in most peoples' minds.

Engineers: This profession needs some serious help. Their AC buffs are NCU hogs well out of proportion with the value of armor class, their robots lack the capability of holding aggro in any situation, and the robots all do far too little damage-per-second. The main thing they bring a team, an ultra-deep-red robot tank for high end missions, was hurt badly by the OEing changes at mid levels, and at high levels the robot is no longer very helpful because teams outrun it. For at least 3/4 of their levels, they are dependant on outside buffs to be at their peak effectiveness - expensive buffs like mochams and wrangles, and more so than any other profession bar none. Their other major strength, trade skills, is much less useful than it could be due to broken recipes and the fact that anything worth constructing through a tradeskill process first requires finding a rare sealed receptacle for a weapon or an even rarer PPPE for armor.

Many people will play engineers without really encountering these issues to about level 80 or 100 because at those levels their robots are the ultimate tanks, outdoor hunting is still viable and pets shine at it, mobs have far fewer hitpoints so the weak damage isn't as noticeable, and low level pet pathing is not a large problem due to slower team speed in missions. Engineers have more glamor, too, which will attract people to play them.

My suggestions for this profession include dramatically reducing the NCU cost of all their buffs - AC, damage shield, reflect shield - and introducing engineer-only tool items that boost their various trade skills by +150, more than a maestro but that also stack with maestros. They should be bar none the masters of building stuff. Then, there should be more stuff to build. A great addition would be a trade skill process for NCU belts and NCU memory units. Trimmers, if they even work, should have their effectiveness tripled at the very least.

Nano Tech: I feel NTs are fairly well balanced in most PVM situations. They need to equip a weapon around level 100 to really keep up, but hey, so does everybody else. They've got great value-adding buffs in crunchcom and HE, and their roots and calms are excellent for crowd control. They could use some reliable higher-end nukes that they don't need a mochams to cast, and higher min damage on most nukes, but otherwise I think they are fine.

Soldier: Like the engineer, soldiers need some help. Mainly, they have excellent defensive capabilities and no way of holding aggro. Most of soldier help should come in the form of better weaponry and better weapons buffs. Their reflect shields and mirror shields are great, but they need powerful guns that are 100% assault rifle, or 100% ranged energy, they need working taunts, and they need heavy duty buffs for their attack rating and all their specials. This way they'll be able to equip guns that will do enough damage-per-second to keep them in the thick of things.

Enforcer: They don't need much help, but they do need a full range of melee weapon and melee special buffs, and they need desperately an effective method of taunting and keeping aggro.

Doctor: Improve the damage-per-second of mid and high end DOTs (speed them up, basically) and raise the minimum on the high end direct heals and team heals to improve their average heal per second, and the biggest doctor-specific problems will be met.

So that's what I think

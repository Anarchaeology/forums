I agree with what you say about MMORPGs being subscription driven. That was what my whole competition argument (first paragraph) is based on.

However, mothly fees include FC's profit, maintenance, plus whatever loan payments they need to make for equipment. Once you start dedicating man-hours for other purposes, like an expansion, you're eating into your monthly profit. You'd like to advance yourself some cash as soon as possible (initial box sales).

Now, expansions have been great ways of grabbing new and keeping old subscriptions. The main way this works is usually by adding new content, with graphics that compete with other games on the shelf. So little Joey flips AO box around and says "wow", instead of "hey, the back of the box looks just like last year."

However, the booster pack doesn't do this. It provides zero for a new subscriber. Same graphics, same areas, same (no) content. With all the other games on the shelves, what would make you buy AO from the booster, that didn't make you buy it a year ago?

If FC's motivation was to keep/increase their subscriber base, they would have no reason not to make the booster an inexpensive download for current subscribers, or give the free month to new and old subscribers. In fact, not having these options is horrible marketing for current subscribers, makes you feel like you're being cheated. The ONLY reason a company would risk that is if they were low on dough.

Don't forget that this idea came from nowhere from a new CEO, that had to be coping with financial issues.

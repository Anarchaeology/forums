How AC's REALLY work
Most players seem to have a misconception of what the effect of AC's is. Generally people believe AC's will reduce 1 point of damage for each 10 points of AC your target has, on each hit. This is not correct, AC lowers you maximum hit by AC / 10 points, then a random number is generated between your minimum damage and maximum damage - reduction from AC's.

Example: Let's say you hit between 0 and 1000 damage on a mob with 0 AC's, now let's raise his AC's to 2000, you will now hit between 0 and 800 damage. Your average hit went from 500 to 400. This means you will averagely do AC / 20 less damage, unlike AC / 10, which everyone seems to think.

For nano based damage it works different though, first a random number is rolled between your minimum and maximum damage, then AC's reduce damage (and damage ofcourse can't go below your minimum damage).

Same example for nano based attacks: Assume your nuke hits a 0 AC mob between 0 and 1000, average hit is 500. If we raise AC's to 2000 on that mob, you will always hit for 0 damage IF the random number that was generated was between 0 and 200, if the number was between 200 and 1000, you will hit for a random number between 0 and 800. So for the interval [0,200] you will always hit for 0, in the interval ]200,1000] you will hit for averagely 400. Our real average becomes: (0 * 200 + 400 * 800) / 1000 = 320.

Basically for nano damage there's an increased chance to do minimum damage, unlike for weapons, because the AC reduction and random roll are done in a different order. Nano damage is more affected by AC's than weapon damage because of this.

Now which weapons are better, weapons with high min damage/low max damage, or weapons with low min damage/high max damage ? (Assuming the sum of min and max damage is the same for both types from here on.) Both have an advantage over the other in different situations: IF your targets AC's are high enough to ALWAYS push your damage to minimum damage with the high minimum damage weapon, it will do better than the other. IF the AC's aren't high enough to make you do 100% minimum damage hits with the high minimum damage weapon, the high maximum damage weapon will do better because it will do higher crits. (Crits are based on your max damage with AC's reduced + crit modifier.)

This might sound a bit confusing so I will give an example: Assume weapon A that does between 400 and 600 damage against 0 AC's. Weapon B does between 100 and 900 damage against 0 AC's. For both weapons average damage (500) is the same if we neglect critical hits. If we raise AC's to 2000, weapon A will hit between 400 and 400 damage, averagely 400, weapon B will hit between 100 and 700 damage, same average, 400. I conclude from 0-2000 AC's weapon B will do better damage because, and only because of the fact it will do higher critical hits. If we raise AC's above 2000, for example, 4000, weapon A will still hit between 400 and 400 damage, weapon B will hit between 100 and 500 damage, averagely 300. Weapon A now does better damage because it "ignored" part of the target's AC's (2000 out of 4000 AC's had no effect). (Technically weapon B could still do better than weapon A between 2000 and 5000 AC's with high crit chances, but I consider crits so rare we can neglect them once we go above 2000 AC's and weapon B starts ignoring part of the target's AC's.)

Bottomline: High minimum damage weapons are only better if they ALWAYS hit for their minimum damage, meaning some of the target's AC's had no effect.

Hellspinner for example has 201-443 as base damage, against AC's that are low enough to not always make you hit for minimum damage with this weapon, it will do the exact same average non-crit damage as a 1-645 weapon. Making the sum of min and max damage is a fast way to compare different weapons.

I hope someone understood some of this gibberish

=(Min+Max)/2 - (AC/10)/2

Well the main things that make overequiping possible are ability buffs, implants, deprive skills, ransack skills, and skill wranglers. This is coming from an agent so I should know my buffs. I feel that the deprive skills line should be for attack skills only and Ransack skills should be for nano skills only. As it is now you can decrease a target's nano and attacking skills all at once with either line. Having to choose either one or the other would allow for more strategy. Skill wranglers shouldn't increase main attack skills of the team...instead it should increase the special attacks such as aimed shot, brawl, full auto. The requirements for special attacks on guns and other weapons are generally fairly low. However large increases in the special attack skills should effect the damage you put out using those special attacks. This would give a similar transient effect on weapon users as skill wrangers do for casters.

Ability buffs for all abilities should be available equally...ie buffs for intelligence and psychic available more readily. If you buff to put in an implant then some of your base abilities should be decreased when you no longer meet the ability requirement for the implant...stamina is the obvious choice. Reductions in the speed skill class such as ranged initiative, nanoresist, evade, dodge, run speed, etc could also occur if you overequip implants or armor. The severity of these reductions would depend on how big of a gap you overequip. Small gaps would only be a reduction of a few points in each...large gaps would be excessive 10-20 point reductions. The key is making it less effective to "overequip" excessively...cause if you overequip excessively then the skill reductions you get should make it not worth it when you aren't buffed.

The way weapon damage and criticals for attacks are currently calculated for guns and weapons encourages overequiping and constant upgrading of weapons. It would be better IMHO if the effectiveness of a weapon was determined by how far over or under the minimum requirements of the weapon you were at. If you have to buff in order to equip a weapon then when the buff wears off the weapon shouldn't be effective. If you are under the requirements for a weapon then your chance to crit and/or attack near the maximum damage of the gun should be greatly decreased...ie next to impossible. In effect you would hit for the minimum damage of the weapon every time you attacked with little if any chance to crit at all as long as you were still under the requirements for the gun. If you just barely meet the requirements for the gun then you would then start attacking with the normal damage for the gun at the average crit rate. The higher your skill surpasses the required skill for the weapon, the larger your chance to crit with that weapon. If weapon damage was set up in this fashion then you would only find a benefit in upgrading weapons if you wanted to have a larger maximum damage or a larger critical hit. It doesn't make sense that you could weild a weapon you are underskilled for and attack with it as optimally as you could with something you had the skill to use. The above method would give weapon users the sense of being able to do more damage (more chance to crit) with weapons they have been familiar with for a while (player skill far exceeding minimum requirements to equip the gun), doing less damage (normal crit rate) with weapons you barely meet the requirements for, and finally doing little to any damage (crit = 0)for weapons you buffed to equip and after equiping fall below the min requirements. If you do more damage with a lower ql weapon than a higher ql weapon you can't use without buffing then no one will want to overequip weapons even if they have that ability.

A similar effect could occur with nanoprograms. The farther you excede the requirements to use the nanoprogram the lower your chance of fumbling the program. So in essence you should only need to worry about fumbling programs that you barely know(barely meet the skills to cast).

A similar approach could be used with chance to counter a nanoprogram as well. The farther you exceed the requirements on a spell the harder it is for a player or a npc/monster to counter that program with nanoresist.

My final thoughts on this
As Shen says, we just need a system where current skill level strongly determines how effectively equipment can be used.

I've been doing a lot of analysis in my previous posts, but all that aside, the thing that really bugs me about overequipping is this: it destroys the atmosphere of the game. The skill requirements on equipment are a way to stop low-level characters from using high-level equipment, nothing more: in most cases they make no sense at all in terms of the game world, so that must be the reason for them to be there. So, we're left with this bizarre situation where people have to get their guns welded into their hands and their armour grafted onto them, because if they let go of that gun, they won't be able to aim and fire it again...

That's not a great, immersive roleplaying experience. From my point of view, it's completely stupid. It's not that I object to the idea of spending time and effort improving my characters, it's just that I want to be able to put my gun away and take my helmet off when I go into the cities, without having to fight the game rules to use them again.

So, what do I think should be done? Well, balance issues aside, I want to see a system where anybody can equip anything at will. Something, therefore, has to be substituted for the existing system, as a means of ensuring that low-level characters with high-level equipment will not gain too much of an advantage: so, obviously, re-balance the system so that current skill level controls how effective that character's equipment is.

There are several possible ways of doing this. Some are more interesting than others, but downgrading the effective QL of anything that's over-equipped is probably the simplest one. Anything that's either working or not working, like floating torches, can be disabled and coloured red on the equipment window: looking at the upcoming patches, this must be possible.

Given further time, more interesting changes might be possible. Str / Sta armour might retain the same armour factors, but impose heavy initiative, evade and runspeed penalties when overequipped, perhaps eventually reaching a point where an insanely overequipped character can literally do nothing except sit down or remove the armour. Cyber armour, controlled by the skill of the wearer, might lose armour factor. Don't meet the burst requirements for a gun? The burst recharge time gets increased. Don't meet it by a very long way? You can't use burst... Don't meet the basic weapon class skills? You keep missing, your attack delay increases, maybe eventually you can't reload... Don't meet the requirements on a melee weapon? Attack time increases, damage decreases, you can no longer parry... And so forth.

I think it could work. I think this was what was originally intended. It would take a lot of tweaking, yes... But I'd really like to see it done!

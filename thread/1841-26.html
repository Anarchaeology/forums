quote:
Originally posted by GryphN
Why does everyone have to be roughly as powerful as everyone else their level?
Because that's the way the game system tries to balance things. What weapon you can wield or what nanos you can use are based on effective skill, sure, but what monsters you get xp from, what people you can group with and what enemies the mission terminals set against you are based on level.

Over-equipping, by which I mean allowing people to continue to use weapons for which they no longer meet the requirements with little actual penalty, completely wrecks the correlation between the effective skill scale and the level scale, either for the over-equipper or the non-overequipper. Since over-equipping is so effective, this mismatch can be huge, and this means that if the game is 'balanced' (interesting, challenging, possible to play effectively) for either group (pro- or anti- over-equipping), it will be severely broken for the other group. (See one of my much earlier posts, possibly in another thread, about why over-equipping is too powerful...)

I solo mostly too, but that should be feasable as well, shouldn't it? As for 'twinking', well, I can only conclude that this is just another problem with having a level-based system. UO is entirely without levels, and creating secondary characters in that game solely to fund your main is not only practically required, it's generally accepted and approved of by the community. But in UO, there is no artificial yardstick of 'level' to segregate people and monsters: so a heavily 'twinked' new character isn't a fantastically overpowered low-level character, but a fantastically unskilled high-level character...

Still, it's probably a bit too late in the development cycle to remove the concept of levels and xp. :-)

quote:
Originally posted by GryphN
I think the people who are really against it, are simply jealous. I don't mean to insult anyone by saying that, but, it happens every day. There are rich people in the "real world", I admit, I get jealous of them, but, I get over it and move on with my life.. Once again... many over-equippers say this. Perhaps it's what you'd expect us to feel given what you want out of the game, but what we want isn't necessarily what you want.

I'm against over-equipping partly because it's visibly throwing the game out of balance, and partly because it's crippling any possible sense of immersion. I don't PvP, so I don't really care how powerful your equipment is, provided it doesn't affect me: But look, it does affect me. The monsters have been re-balanced to be challenging for you, meaning that eventually I might well have to start over-equipping just to keep playing.

Then what? I'm not really here for the combat, after all. It's a role-playing game, at least to some of us, and that's about pretending to be someone else. The 'system' should be as transparent as possible, since those numbers aren't 'really' there in the context of Rubi-Ka: they're just an approximate description of what we're pretending is there, and sometimes they come with artificial restrictions to keep everything working.

So, sure, I can't execute that nano program yet because I'm not skilled enough, or I can't wear that particular bit of armor because I'm not strong enough. That makes sense. But I can't wield that perfectly balanced katana because I'm not skilled enough, wheras I can wield this broken, chipped, unbalanced, rusted-over one? Huh? How does that work? You hold the handle in both hands, and apply the blade to the enemy, how hard is that? I'm easily strong enough to pick the thing up, so why can't I swing it?

Now, under normal circumatances, a little bit of suspension of disbelief will see us through. Sure, I should be able to use that weapon, but I won't be able to afford the thing until I'm good enough to use it anyway. If I happen to get hold of it, maybe I can put up the required skills somehow, and just not think too hard about why, just like you sometimes have to turn off your brain when watching Star Trek.

But when I have to start spending large amounts of time on the great 'wield the good weapon' project, resorting to complex negotiations, conducting hideous, recursive cybernetic implant surgery on myself, and using fantastically advanced swarms of nano-bots just to get both hands around the hilt of a perfectly normal weapon, that's about the time that it stops becoming fun.

I don't think I should be forced to do that. Some gamers play for the end result, for being powerful, for having got to the next level: But some of us don't. Some of us play not to get to the destination, but to enjoy the journey: and when I have to over-equip, because that's the only way to keep playing, that's when it's no longer worth my time to do so. Call me lazy if you like, many people have described us anti-overequippers that way, and arguably it's true... But what, really, is wrong with that? AO is supposed to be fun. I don't find over-equipping fun, I find it to be far too much like work.

So, really, I'm not jealous. I don't want to have an over-equipped character. I am, however, rather disgruntled that what could be a great game for people like me is turning into something I no longer want to play, because, as far as I can see, some other people can't stand the idea that some of the little numbers on the screen are going to become smaller numbers.

Sorry about the rant, I just regularly feel that this side of things is highly under-represented, and I really hope that I can make some of the pro-overequippers out there understand why we dislike it so much...

What is a Wrangle good for?
I'm surprised more people can't see this half-assed implementation of 'skill-checks' as nothing more than an easy way to slow people down.

You know what a Wrangle was supposed to be for? It was supposed to be like the MetaPhycisists Anima. Only, it was for the people on his team. The other five players were supposed to get better at the sacrifice of the Trader. It was supposed to be a Surge of Power prompted by the Trader's transference of skill to the damage dealers (through weapon/nano skills) and healers (through nano skills) on his team.

Only one problem. The game was never tested or balanced. Not from Day 1 has 132 points in a skill had any appreciable difference in damage output. The way the game works is that you need 400 point increase in order to get a full integer increase in the damage shown on your weapon's info display.

The effect that a 132 point skill increase had on your overall damage was Completely dwarfed by the difference that a better weapon could make. If you swapped in a better weapon in the heat of battle, you sure would notice the difference, however. The supposedly 'anti-twink' Qlvl system turned into the twink's wet dream as a result.

So we end up with two major results. First a mostly unintended system rich with avenues for advancing your character beyond the same old level, level, level, level. This has kept a lot of people in the game and more than anything else, probably kept the game alive through the first 12.6 fiasco. AO was 'different'.

The second result is that people got 'too good' at over-equipping. Anything as addicting as over-equipping is sure to be pushed to its limits at every turn. We apparently ended up with too much variance between the weakest players and the strongest players. I'll concede on this point for the sake of the game as a whole.

Now, FunCom, listen here. It's your intended solution that has every single player who knows how the game works pissed off. The fact that a 21% over equipped weapon can be worse than a 0% over-equipped weapon is the most outrageous an ill-concieved game convention you could have come up with. Regardless of any rationalizations that posters may put forward, it just makes the pursuit of excellence into a worthless effort for a large percentage of your audience.

I know what you wanted to do with Wrangles. I KNOW it wasn't necessarily over-equipping. Actually, if we the players would be willing to subject ourselves to the Tedium of swapping guns everytime a Wrangle started or ran out, we'd be fulfilling your vision now. "OK, Wrangle coming up for the Big Fight. Everybody get your best weapons ready to swap in!" Only thing is you aren't going to have 4 weapon users swapping in a bigger weapon after a Wrangle just before they try to take on that Ace Enforcer.

So how about you take this opportunity to do this NERF right? (Lest we forget this has ANYTHING to do with anything other than stopping the populace from reaching 200 in droves let me remind you most of us are getting NERFED): Plainly and simply mutiply damage by (Actual Skill)/(Required Skill). Add hard caps to ensure that damage never drops below 75% penalty or 50% bonus.

Have 50 skill in a gun that requires 100 skill means you do half damage.

Have 150 skill in a gun that requires 100 skill means you do 50% more damage.

The Wrangle cast right before a tough fight now means something in terms of group effectiveness. It might even bump you into Bonus damage. The debuff means something (your damage goes down). Going from a 20% overequipped weapon to a 21% overequipped weapon probably still gets you a point or two more damage. Same for a 38% to 39% jump. Only the returns are diminishing. Meaning that the effort to do so is only for those who are over-equipping for the sake of over-equipping.

You don't take the game away from the over-equippers, you just dampen their rewards to an APPROPRIATE degree.

You now ask the players to accept punishment for thriving in a game you didn't test. You picked the wrong variable when you decided on 400 as your magic number. A simple change to 250 months ago could have alleviated this mess. Sure, you would have had to scale back damage en-mass. But, buffs would have meant something. Debuffs would have meant something. Having skill would have been as important, if not more important, than having a better weapon.

You guys did this to yourself and now take the easy and poorly thought out method of the 'safety net'. I've programmed enough of these into my projects when I was a neophyte and made enough of my staff take them out and fix things properly as I've matured. Confront your programmers/item designers/game designers and see if they can look you in the eye and tell you this is anything other than a cop out.

Then go home tonight and look at yourself in the mirror, Gaute, and look at yourself and try to convince yourself that these proposed changes are nothing more than a copout. I would be ashamed to address over-equipping in such a simplistic manner if I were you. As a programmer and game designer, I would be outright ashamed.

Her bones were beginning to ache, and muscles were seizing. Before too much longer they would freeze up on her and she would collapse. With the exception of her first stop, she had been in perpetual motion since leaving Baboon's, days earlier, and was rapidly succumbing to physical exhaustion. She weighed her options carefully as she chugged closer to her destination. She could keep going, hoping her body could hold out until the next stop. Based on her knowledge of the world she had made her home, she estimated speed and matched it with the coordinates given to her and calculated that she would arrive in about 5 hours. The second option she considered was activating a self-termination option built into the reclamation technology. She would reclaim at a remote terminal she had passed four hours ago, but while her body would be fresh and whole, her psychological exhaustion would remain unchanged. Also, that would move her estimated time of arrived from five to nine hours. That was nine hours she and Elizabeth didn't have. The third option was to use the muscle stims that Michael had left for her at the last stop.

She looked up at the sullen sky and wished desperately that she was above the clouds, wings outspread and speeding toward her destination faster than any Yalmaha had ever flown. She loved the freedom the sky offered her, and would spend hours just soaring over the hostile landscape of Rubi-Ka. Thoughts of the sky brought back a memory. A memory of a mother, with a daughter's head in her lap as she sang softly. Hattie recalled the ancient earth song and sang as she wheezed her way onward.

Early in the morning sunlight
Soaring on the wings of dawn

The words "Searching for the rainbow's end" played over and over in her head. In ancient times, her ancestors spoke of a pot of gold at the end of a rainbow. What prize, or pot of gold waited for her? Her pot of gold remained behind, a temporary loan, but now repossessed. She knew where her thoughts were going, and she drove them from her mind. If she so much as thought of her name, she would be tempted to turn back and abandon the child she thought had died nine years ago. Her pot of gold would go on, forgetting her over time. Nevver was .....

She beat her fist against her head as she thought the name. Driving it out of mind as she had done since receiving that first message from Michael.

Her mind flashed to anything, to get thoughts of Nevver out of her mind. She replayed the security tapes she had watched at her stop. A video from Elizabeth's fifth Birthday Party. The child had asked as she opened each present "Were is Momma". Each question torn tiny pieces from Hattie's heart as she watched, spellbound. But she felt a fist clench her chambered pump when Michael would answer sympathetically, "Momma doesn't love us anymore, and wanted to go away."

Whether it was a reward or punishment, Hattie couldn't decide. Her girl was there on a small mobile monitor, breathing, opening presents, and whole. How could she have watched what she watched. Even though at the time she was deeply under the influence of a stim fog, she could recall the feel of the tiny hand slip though her fingers and see the child flail her arms as she fell. Her death was an incontrovertible event. One witnessed by both herself and the Walker family security tech who planned their rooftop escape. The young woman who was always smiling at her. The one who came to her in the deepest pits of stim madness, and told her how she and her daughter could be free.

How could she have been so stupid! If she had listened to the security tech and waited until the drugs were out of her system, the plan would have worked and mother and daughter would have been together for each following birthday.

Her mind shifted focus to the tech who told her that she was her sister. Hattie knew that Tynchre blamed herself for an imperfect plan. Hattie blamed herself for rushing the plan. But none of that mattered anymore. She had watched her daughter celebrating a birthday three months after she had plummeted to her death. She was alive, and Hattie could do something to keep her that way.

At the first stop, nausea had clenched her stomach when she opened the pack, seeing food rations, water and a supply of various stims. But now she realized what had kept her from throwing the drugs from the pack.

She stumbled in her run, as leg muscles cramped painfully. She had a way. A way to keep her muscles going indefinitely. She pulled the pack off of her back as she limped slowly forward. Reaching in, she pulled out a stim, and shoved it furiously at her thigh. As the muscles relaxed and refreshed almost instantaneously, a fog of detachment washed over her mind. It was a glorious and divine feeling. Why had she held out so long, she thought. Picking up speed, she raced a little closer to her end.

Something big had happened, and Tynchre had no idea what that was. One thing was certain. Whoever the man was who came to Rubi-Ka with a 13 year old girl, he had to be found. And time was running out.

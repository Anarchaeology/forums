You're Not Entirely Wrong, But Still ...
There is fun that everybody can have. There is fun everybody can make. There is fun that happens to everybody. And there is much fun that only applies to mid-to-high level characters, so if your primary character is the only high level character you have, then no matter how little "chrome" that profession has, it's your most fun character. But here's how my thinking works:

Fun for All:

Exploring, hunting, fighting, PvP, trading with/for obscure items, making friends, chatting, getting to know your guildmates, starting guilds, throwing parties, going to parties, going out dancing, hanging out at the bar or the coffeehouse, hiring strippers, being a stripper, dressing up and being stylish, stirring up mobs for political causes, joining political causes, arguing tactics on the OOC channels, learning new stuff on these fora, writing for the various web sites.

In other words, no matter what your profession, there's plenty of fun stuff to do. My ratings are just on the "chrome," the "icing on the cake."

Fun for ...

Adventurers: Shape-shifting lets you role-play the various animal forms, each of which has its own personality fun (and eventually, even flight without vehicles). If Funcom implements the "cute animal sidekick" feature they're promising, adventurers will become even more disneyesque, and this time I mean it in a good way. Talking and dancing robot sidekicks are fun, but a talking and dancing pet leet, that's going to be hilarious. Score: 5.

Agents: Face in the Crowd (et al) gives even more flexible shape shifting than Adventurers get, admittedly without the buffs or eventual flight ability. Perhaps you've noticed all of the "probe droids" at the mission terminals? I don't know what the limitations of agent shapeshifting are, because I don't have the patience to play one that high, but judging by some of the tricks I've seen .... Oh, and the ability to run False Profession means that if you're clever enough, you can come up with a combination of abilities that totally surprises people. And wait until you see the new hats they're getting in 14.2! Score: 5.

Bureaucrats: Limited "pet" tricks, doing fun stuff with pet rename and pet chat, lets you do almost Laurel & Hardy/Rebo & Zootie/Ren & Stimpy humor. Some fairly determined, fairly clever 'crats have also used/abused the "long charm" nanos to drag improbable mobs across ridiculous distances (and then try to keep them tame) for humor's sake. Score: 4.

Doctors: Enlarge, which at very, very high levels has some potential for amusement. A tiny selection of trade skills; better than nothing, but only if all tradeskills are fun for you, no matter how repetitive. Constant shortage of cash, on the other hand, means even less money for entertainment and clothing. Score: 1.

Enforcers: Nope, the general stuff's about it. But at least they can make some good money, some of them. A few of their weapons, like the chairs and the banjos, are funny by themselves. Score: 2.

Engineers: The same "pet name" and "pet chat" tricks I mentioned above. Only these get better about halfway through the gladiatorbot line, when "pet social" means the pet can be made to gesture and dance; the scripting potential has hardly been tapped. Oh, and as a trivial form of occasional entertainment, they do have good trade skills. Score: 4.

Fixers: Trade skills. Poorly implemented hacking. That's about it. OK money-making potential. Score: 2.

Martial Artists: A few cool fighting moves. But low expenses and good solo mission prospects mean lots of money. Barehanded fighting style cuts down on the "don't you ever put that gun down?" fashion faux pas so many heavily wrangled characters go through. Score: 3.

Meta-Physicists: Pet chat x 3. Pet rename x 3. That's about it. Good money-making abilities, unfortunately totally offset by ridiculous software and NCU upgrade expense. Oh, but at fairly high levels, they can fly without vehicles, which can be fun, not least of which because a lot of the best photography on the web sites was obviously shot either by MPs or by 200th level adventurers. Score: 3.

Nano Technician: High software costs also mean that they're dead broke all the time. Nothing particularly funny or social in their nanoformulae. Score: 1.

Soldier: Incredible variety of weapons turns on the gun collectors. Otherwise, yawn. Score: 2.

Trader: Trade skills. Plenty of money for fashion and playing around, but need to over-equip shotguns makes for more "don't you ever put that ugly orange gun down?" again. Score: 2.

What, if anything, did I miss? Oh yeah, the Stockholm Syndrome: the super-hard, super-gimped (or formerly super-gimped) professions form really, really tight, and really, really supportive guilds, which is a minor boon to adventurers, agents, and fixers.

Anything else?

Fixers
No profession gets enough IP to do everything they can do. Everybody, by the time they get to level 30, is having to decide just what they aren't going to do.

I've got a nearly 40th level Fixer. Yes, I know, the real crying is coming from people far, far above me. But I'm using a dirt-cheap self-buffed, self-equipped Mausser Particle Streamer, a QL54. I could go higher, I just haven't bothered to go shopping yet - because at something like level 36, I'm doing more damage than my carefully tuned 58th level Martial Artist, and at the same attack speed. The MA crits more - but all that means is that roughly once per fight, the MA gets the kind of damage that the fixer (at 3/4 the level) gets twice per fight, from Area Burst and Burst.

Yes, the 58th or so level MA has more AC & HP, and enough nano to self-heal about 25% health two to maybe three times per fight. But the fixer (again, at 3/4 the level) has root and shoot, and HoT, and will pretty soon have Reckless Digitization.

To get this "uber" I had to give up on two of the nano skills altogether, and am about to give up altogether on all but two of the trade skills. I will probably never have Grid Armor on this character; regular armor will have to do. I give up on gun construction. I want back the few IP I put into Concealment, plus the substantial IP I put into WeaponSmithing and MechE ... and I'll get them back any day now, when 14.2 rolls out. This will give me the attributes to further ridiculously update my armor and (self-built, natch) implants. In exchange for what I gave up, I'm totalling out Str, Agil, Stam, MG/SMG, Burst, CompLit, NP, First Aid, Treatment, and B&E. I'm spreading the remaining points across Nano Pool, all DMS skills, R/S, the 4 nanoskills I need to grid hack and HoT. (Those same 4 also give me the roots and damage buffs.)

So far, I'm doing fine. And when the NCU Hacker Interface comes out in 14.4, I expect to rock even further.

Think of most characters as having a core set of skills, and then a set of options. The fixer main package is max Str, Agi, Stam, BodyDev, R/S, MG/SMG, Burst, and FirstAid, plus lots of CompLit, B&E, and MapNav. The option packages for fixer are max combat, weapon construction, sneaking, item summoning, grid hacking, and HoT. If you picked more than three of those, that's why you're complaining that you're out of IP. (I obviously picked max combat, grid hacking, and HoT.) You should have specialized more. Come 14.2, use your IPR Points to cut some stuff loose.

Others have complained that the MPS compares unfavorably, in damage per IP, to the Vektor. I'll admit, it may be true, but barely - the default calculations don't include Area Burst, which is a nice chunk o' chemical damage every 20 seconds.

Arinia and Letah, you missed my point. It's probably because "Fun" is an oversimplification of what I meant by this category. What I was going to call it was "chrome", a developer's term, but I didn't think anyone would get what I meant.

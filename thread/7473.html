quote:
MMOGs aren't really geared towards the low attention span gamer. Maybe twitchy, first person shooters or Diablo-esque click-fests are more your style.
I don't think he ever said anything about the combat system, Miiir.

But on the subject of FPS's....
If you're going to talk about first-person shooters, I believe the word you're looking for is "immersive" and not "twitchy".

MMORPG's only use the combat system they do, because turn-based combat is so hard to do in online games, but they still want to make it accessible to purist RPG fans. It's a last resort, not a choice.

The reason why FPS's are so popular is because they don't stop you in the middle of something interesting, or cut you off from interacting with the game. They further the concept of "abdication of authorship", by becoming more immersive. Immersiveness is something that MMORPG's have always lacked. They instead thrive on the concept of gaming addiction. FPS combat also gives the user more control over the character, and requires more concentration.

And, gaming is evolving to where you can combine both the immersiveness of FPS combat, with the critical thought and progress of RPG's.
Deus Ex showed that first person combat can be done successfully in an RPG game.
Neocron is showing that it can be done in MMORPG's.

Cutting the user off from the game in any way, is always looked at as a bad thing.

quote:
Q3 and UT were immersive?
Those were classic-style multiplayer games. I certainly wasn't referring to them. Games like that, Serious Sam, Gore, etc.... are made for the purpose of fun.

quote:
Sure, games like RTCW and Half Life were immersive. But they were more like a sugar-rush.
I beg to differ. Half Life set a new standard in length of single player shooters games, and how much depth a single player shooter has. RtCW was surprisingly interesting. Wolf 3D was just "go kill nazis", but RtCW actually added some elements of suspense and a good story to it. (I'm not saying that this is inherent at all of all new first person action games; there are crappy ones out there that should have been done better. But the same goes for any genre. For every Quake 3, there's a Diablo. For every Daikatana, there's a Pool of Radiance 2. Every family has its black sheep.)

I guess I should give more specific examples. In immersiveness, I'm referring to games like Clive Barker's Undying, Aliens vs. Predator 2, and System Shock 2. These sorts of games affect your emotions (mainly fear) and pull you in. The affect simply could not be accomplished with an overhead game. The essential factor is that YOU are the one pointing the gun, YOU are the one at risk, rather than a "god-view" of controlling your character (or avatar) from above our outside. It adds a higher element of connection with what is happening in the game.
Isometric and third-person veiws inadvertantly disconnect you in a way from what is happening in the game.

quote:
The immersive, single player aspects of those games rarely last for more than 20-25 hours.
And that's for many reasons. One, is because they are much much harder to design graphically than, say, Baulder's Gate. This means that much more of the development process must be spent on graphics than in such games. It's not the nature of FPS's, it's the nature of 3D gaming.

quote:
They are then relgated to online play which is ruled by who can twitch the best. Who has the best connection. Who has the fastest machine. MMOGs use a turn based system because they don't want the system to be unfair for those who don't have top-end systems and t3 connections. It is turn based so they can take latency out of the equation when they are tuning the game.
When CS came out? Yes. Now? No. It all depends on how much time is spent optimizing the game. How good the netcode is, and how customizable the graphics options are. The current reports of Neocron for example, claim that there is very rare, if any, network lag, even on a 56k modem. Half Life, on the other hand, had shoddy netcode. Neocron is taking steps to ensure that lag is not a determining factor. All early reports, even in Beta 3, are confirming this.
And a good example of how framerates will matter in coming games, you can take a look at the Neocron movie, which was made at bare minimum specifications. (i believe on a 16mb Voodoo and a 300mhz Pentium. i sh*t you not.)

quote:
First Person Shooters are like the junkfood of computer games.
I take it you haven't played Deus Ex.
Amongst Deus Ex fans, the question to each other is not "did you finish", but "how many times". The main game takes over 20 hours to just run through ("shortest way" meaning not performing side quests, exploring, finding secrets, using stealth, etc.). The game had more ways to finish any given task than anyone can even think of. People are playing the game in ways that even Warren Spector didn't consider when making it, and fans are still finding different ways to play the game over a year and a half after its release. And then there's also the mods.
If you ever find a gigantic, re-useable, bottomless bag of Lay's with recipe's on the back, please let me know

On an extra note, Spector STILL considers the game as "too linear", and is taking steps to make sure DX2 has MORE choices (e.g. getting through the entire game without any weapons).

To stick to silly tradition in the gaming industry is just depriving it of progress and innovation.
To be elitist in the current state of gaming is nothing but depriving yourself.

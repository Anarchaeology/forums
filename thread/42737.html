Analysis of multi-wield
(If you don't want to read the math and tests, just skip to conclusions.)


Premises:
Initiatives reduce your attack speed by ini/600 seconds. Initiatives reduce your recharge speed by ini/300 seconds. Neither can go below 1s. This is quite universally accepted as the way weapon initiatives work.

Single weapon cycle goes atk+rch. Well, sure seems to be the case.

Dual weapon cycle goes atk/atk/rch(both). I've observed this personally back when there was only one attack bar. This pertains to non-MA dualing, I have not studied how a MA+weapon cycle goes. Using melee weapon tests, it is easy to study that the in-game dualing works accordingly to the atk/atk/rch(both) cycle.

Melee weapon dualing and single-wielding test:
Weapons: two merchant executioners, QL7 (IIRC, did not write this down and did this test a bit earlier)
Atk: 1.33s
Rch: 2.19s

Melee ini 203

Resultant weapon speed:
Atk: 1s
Rch: 1.51s

Estimated ROF (swings/minute):
Single: 60/2.51s = 23.9 ROF
Dual: 60/3.51s*2 = 34.2 ROF

Estimated ROF increase while dualing:
34.2 / 23.9 = +43.1%

Test method: one minute period cut from ongoing combat with /say Hep. This minimizes the effect latency has due to making the start and stop markers go through the same latency. The combat has started prior to the start marker.

Test results, ROF (swings/minute):
Single: 23,23,24,22,19(oddball),24,23,21,23,22
Average: 22.4

Dual: 32,30,32,33,33,31,32,32,33,31
Average: 31.9

Perceived IN-GAME ROF increase:
31.9 / 22.4 = +42.4%

Difference between estimated ROF increase and actual in-game tested ROF increase: 0.7 percentage units, resulting in a 1.6% variation from estimated ROF increase. Attributed to short period of test time and too few repetitions, and well within acceptable range to maintain the validity of the atk/atk/rch cycle being valid.

Also of importance is the fact that the Anarchy Online combat engine seems to run roughly 6% slower than real-time. If I were to guess, I believe there are small pauses in it (affecting all combat) where "time stops". All sorts of validating processes are probably ran in this time period.

Ranged weapon tests:

The reps were smaller, due to me getting bored a bit. They were still consistent though.

QL9 Mini shotgun:

Ranged ini 28

Estimated ROF increase while dualing (unlimited clip):
35.2 / 24.9 = +41.4%

Test results, ROF (shots/minute):
Single: 18+18, 18+19
Average: 18.25

Dual: 27+28, 28+27

Perceived IN-GAME ROF increase:

How clipsize effects dual wielding: While one weapon is reloading, the other one can continue as if single-wielded. This results naturally in a higher ROF increase due to the period where no shots are taken is lessened considerably.

Estimated ROF increase was +41.4% and perceived in-game ROF increase was +50.7%. The clipsize being only 5 results in a large amount of reloading periods, and thus dual wielding shines by allowing you to continue to fire even while the other weapon reloads. How this changes on higher clipsizes is one feature of the next test.

Even with a clipsize of 15, the difference in estimated and perceived in-game ROF was only 0.9 percentage units, or 2.8%. And as most of this can be accredited to the reloading period firing, this again confirms the atk/atk/rch(both) cycle being valid.

Conclusion on ROF in single and dual wielding:

Dual wielding melee gives you a ROF increase which can be very accurately calculated from the atk/atk/rch(both) cycle. Weapons maxed to 1s/1s with initiatives give a 33% ROF increase. As atk>rch, the increase becomes smaller. As atk<rch, the increase becomes greater.

Dual wielding ranged gives you a ROF increase which can be accurately calculated from the atk/atk/rch(both) cycle if the weapons have a medium+ clipsize. On small clipsize weapons, the actual ROF increase is higher. Weapons maxed to 1s/1s with initiatives give a 33% ROF increase. As atk>rch, the increase becomes smaller. As atk<rch, the increase becomes greater.

Damage over time capacity of dual wield on the premises of ROF increase just shown above:

(a little math, skip to conclusion if not interested)

Wielding a x/x weapon gives you a ROF increase of 33%. All "damage average" in the following formulaes mean the average damage IN-GAME(!) of either off or main hand. This results in the following damage over time capacity:

Mainhand average damage 100%
Offhand average damage x%

Two dual cycles, each taking 3 time units:
Time units used: 6
Hits: 2 main, 2 off
Damage: 100%, x%, 100%, x% = 200% + 2 * x%

Three single cycles, each taking 2 time units:
Time units used:6
Hits: 3 main
Damage: 100%, 100%, 100% = 300%

Break-even point:
300% = 200% + 2 * x%
100% = 2 * x%
50% = x%

Thus a weapon which gives you 50% of mainhand damage on average (IN-GAME) results in a break-even of damage over time. For dualing to be of any bonus, you need to wield a weapon on your offhand doing over 50% if both weapons are at x/x speed after initiatives.

-------

Wielding a 2x/x weapon gives you a ROF increase of 20%. All "damage average" in the following formulaes mean the average damage IN-GAME(!) of either off or main hand. This results in the following damage over time capacity:

Mainhand average damage 100%
Offhand average damage x%

Three dual cycles, each taking 5 time units:
Time units used: 15
Hits: 3 main, 3 off
Damage: 100%, x%, 100%, x%, 100%, x% = 300% + 3 * x%

Five single cycles, each taking 3 time units:
Time units used:15
Hits: 5 main
Damage: 100%, 100%, 100%, 100%, 100% = 500%

Break-even point:
500% = 300% + 3 * x%
200% = 3 * x%
66% = x%

Thus a weapon which gives you 66% of mainhand damage on average (IN-GAME) results in a break-even of damage over time. For dualing to be of any bonus, you need to wield a weapon on your offhand doing over 66% if both weapons are at 2x/x speed after initiatives.

Conclusions about damage over time:
If using fast weapons that are maxed at 1s/1s, you need to do atleast 50% of damage (IN-GAME AVERAGE) with the offhand to break even. If you are doing less, unwield your off-hand and give yourself a boost.

If using 2s/2s weapons with a high ini, but scoped to near 0, it will give you an estimated speed of 2s/1s. This is due to reduced initiative not affecting recharge speed, according reports by for example fixers where their maussers do not go slower than 1s/2s even if their ini is dropped. I first thought this was a silly bug or feature, but now I understand it is a design decision. The reasoning behind this design is probably in the fact that scopes for example slow your _aiming_, but you are still naturally quick so it will not slow your recovery from a taken shot. Anyways, when wielding a 2s/1s weapon, you need to do atleast 66% of damage (IN-GAME AVERAGE) with the offhand to break even. If you are doing less, unwield your off-hand and give yourself a boost.

Analysis of multi-ranged and multi-melee:
Multi-melee is exceptionally good extra damage skill. It is possible to wield two QL200 weapons, for example SOL E and SOL F. If used with 600ini, dualing them will give you on average a 33% higher damage output.

Multi-ranged is more difficult to analyze. The new good pistols usually also have a good clipsize, so that combats the reloading time bonus of dualing ranged weapons, so low that it has virtually no bonus. The question then becomes, how high can you wield in your offhand? I'm still awaiting for some lvl200 adv to report the max cap on multi ranged, that will enable to do atleast some comparison on actual values.

In the mean-time, if you have two identical guns (for example River Seasons Xp's), here is a way to roughly compare whether it is worth it for you to dual them:

Figure out your atk/rch speeds. For recharge, deduct ini/300 from it. Very often you end up at 1s, higher up nearly always. Use your unmodified ini. For attack, deduct ini/600 from it. Use your modified ini. For every +aggdef bonus you have, both are reduced. I'm not sure whether this is bonus/100 or bonus/50 seconds, use bonus/100 to be on the safe side. If something else is affecting your atk/rch speeds, calculate them in too. No idea what that something else could be.

Take your atk/rch speeds and do this:
A. Count Atk+Atk+Rch
B. Count Atk+Rch
Then divide A by B, and deduct 1. This number is how much you must do IN-GAME AVERAGE damage with your offhand to _break even_.

edit: The number is a percentage. 0.66 means 66%, etc.

Then there are a couple of options. You can estimate what your mainhand damage on average is, and what your offhand damage on average is. Divide your offhand average with your mainhand average. If this resulting number is less than the one you got above, stop dual wielding! You are making yourself do less damage. Also of note is critical hits: does your offhand crit for atleast that above calculated number? If it does less, then depending on how much you crit you hurt your damage over time a bit, and a borderline better value on normal average hits may not be enough to better your overall damage over time. If it is over, then you are doing somewhat more damage over time.

Another option for estimating average damages is this:
Write down your weapons base min, base max, and the atk/rch speeds you got. Also of importance is your usual fighting attack rating, write this up too. Add up all your +damage rings, and normal damage buffs you usually have running (morph, normal +dam buff), and note this number down. Then the hardest part is estimating the AC your usual opponents have. I'd guess one option is to write down your normal critical hits on both weapons vs. the stuff you fight, and the crit hits you do against leets in the backyard. Multiply the difference by 10. This ought to be the AC of your enemies.

Then armed with these numbers, go to http://www.student.oulu.fi/~isalmenk/AO/ and fire up your Excel or OpenOffice Calc. The _ms. version is MS Excel format, the other one is OpenOffice format.

Write into the fields the min dam, max dam, attack rating, AC, buff total. Skip the timer input fields, just write up the "Average damage vs AC" field. Do the same for your offhand gun. Compare the estimated average damages, and proceed to throw your offhand to the garbage bin (joking... well sort of ).

As for dualing weapons of differing attack/recharge speeds, YUCH. That is way more complex, and I'd rather not explain how to compute the ROF increase. It is doable though, but if either gun has a low clipsize the accuracy goes whacko.

Analysis:
Depending on your weapons, ini, scope, enemy AC, the bonus damage over time with dualing compared to single varies.

Best dual bonus is gained when fighting monsters with EXTREME AC, and enough initiative even if scoped to remain at 1s/1s speeds for your weapons. But even when scoped, you can still garner at best almost 10% more extra damage by dual wielding. Also with these weapons, scoping yourself to 0ini actually can hurt your overall damage. Where the optimum ini/scope+crit% lies between 0ini and 600ini is another thing to calculate. I'll just say, with these weapons that ini is very good to have.

When fighting high (5000 in this example) AC enemies, the ability of the better mainhand gun to punch a bit better through that armor lessens the bonus of dualing. But with 10% crit chance and 600ini after modifiers, you still gain a good 20+bit % when dualing, over single. Again going the heavily scoped and crit buffed route lessens average damage gain from dualing, and as noted above, going un-scoped to a degree is actually better for your overall damage. Dualing still nets you a bonus though, in the case concerning above weapons, again almost 10%, bring dualed scoped to 0 ini weapons above an unscoped 600ini weapon.

Conclusion:
If heavily scoped, damage bonus from dualing is less but still there. The bonus of scopes on damage over time is however not enough to make you want to scope no matter what. The closer you are to 1s atk time, the more important it is for you to sacrifice that last 1% of crit chance to make it a 1s atk time. At best you can look at a bit over 20% damage increase dualing. At "worst" (meaning using a good dual combo of the currently available weapons, scoped to high heaven), you get around 10% damage increase dualing.

Most important note:
At worst, you are gimping yourself. These numbers were with an identical pair of weapons. Using two different weapon types can drop your dualing damage way under your single weapon style, if you pick the wrong choices. The popular v2/custom reet combo may be good when you can get it, but quite probably after a while when you can equip a better mainhand will get, NATURALLY since you haven't also upgraded your offhand, worse.

Personal view on dualing pistols:
If you are not good at maths, I'd suggest you dual identical weapons with good min damage and not so high multiranged reqs. The River Seasons seem a good choice for example. If you are good at maths, go ahead and check whether your choice you are thinking of could bring in some extra damage. Aim for 1s/1s speed with initiatives (600 for Seasons).

Also of note is how implant clusters conflict. Personally my ranged weapon choice of today is the rifle for adventurers who are still short on IP. One less skill to implant, rifle is easier to implant without conflicts, only need to be able to find one good weapon, more easily buffed into, etc.

Personal view on dualing melee:
Dual and be happy! *slice'n'dice*

Well, this may be true QL to QL...but it's not really true for "any given situation"... Why? Requirements!

Electronicum is only 895 pistol at QL 200. This can be wrangled on at around shortly have the level 100 cap on an Opi without being OE. (With Ballad and Expertise running, of course.) Only need 712 running Pistol skill to do this. QL 170's are equippable without OE before the level 100 cap. (So, as soon as level 78-82, which an Opi's green skills max--may be different for other races.)

River Seasons XP at QL 200 requires 1036 pistol, requiring a bit more work to get on without being OE. This requires 829 pistol skill running to achive, which isn't possible until you hit around level 145 with maxed Sense/Agility/Pistol and have QL 150 implants in. (Of course, implant twinkers can do it sooner...but many people don't go that route.)

Other advantage to the Electronicum is Energy AC over Projectile AC on MOBs. Which seems to make a difference.

50-350 (320) - projectile ac
1-391 (345) - energy ac

I dunno...River XP probably doesn't have much of an edge unless you're min-hatting every time. But, there are probably better options for that, if that's the situation. Of course, I can't try a QL 200 River XP since I can't equip it yet. =)

Oh well, Electronicum's have their time and place. I think an Electronicum/River XP combo wouldn't be so bad. But, River XP's can be notoriously hard to find.

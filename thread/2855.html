Ironically, the "whining" didn't actually start until some of the fixes were implemented for other classes, like the nano-technician. I have played a Fixer to level 50, and am still playing that character.

My meta-physicist is level 20, and on hold. I specialized him in 2 hand edged (cheapest training outside "pure" martial arts), and the new weapons using exclusively blunt was a serious blow to my fun. I also did a lot of testing with a Soldier, but missed having the ability to heal too much.

What is really wrong with the fixer:

1: Fixers are a "rogue" class, alonside the agent. We are supposed to be masters of the rogue skills, and the ultimate treasure hunters, using breaking & entering and trap disarming to reach things out of reach of everyone else, yet traps and locks are currently so trivial that even a nano-technician, enforcer, or anyone else is really challenged.

2: On a similar line, fixers are supposed to be second only to the agent in concealment, but the fact is we are a distant fourth, because we don't have the concealment buffs of the agent or adventurer. That's right, the adventurer is better at concealment than the fixer even though their concealment skill is more expensive than ours.

3: Our nano skills have the most severe cost of any of the professions, both in terms of how much IP is required in raising a skill, and how many skills our nano formulas require to use.

4: Fixers don't really have anything of value to contribute to groups at high level. We can't tank as well as a soldier, or enforcer, or even a martial artist or adventurer. We can't match the damage output of these professions either. Crowd control becomes very difficult at high levels compared to the trader or 'crat since we simply can't keep our nano-skills up to date. While we can heal, Fixer HoT nanos are expensive both in terms of IP cost, and NCU cost to the target. The heals of the adventurer, martial artist and doctor become much more valuable to the group because they heal instantly, with no NCU cost. We don't even get any combat-skill buffing beyond the general formulas.

5: High level fixers (80+) apparently get really screwed because the only way for us to fight is to play soldier and shoot targets to death, at the same time, the other combat classes are doing double the damage the Fixer is doing because they aren't limited to doing their damage in the "projectile" category. Most "monsters" are practially undefended against energy, while there is nothing out there that doesn't have a significant defense against projectile types.

6: The fact that we are limited to buying 4 skill per level in all trade skills puts us at a significant disadvantage to the trader and engineer, simply because they get 5 per level, and they both have powerful trade-skill buffing nano formulas.

What other professions offer:
Adventurer: In spite all their problems, they are equally adept acting as either "tank" or "healer." They are second only to the enforcer in melee combat (usually using twin Rider Executioners), and capable of fast healing using their nano-pool.

Agent: Alpha Strike. Agent Aimed Shots are very powerful when they have a good ammount of skill, and when the going gets tough, they are capable of using (almost) all of the support nano programs other classes use.

Doctor: Team Grid access, healing. Doctors are the ultimate healers, having more healing options and more powerful healing than any other class period.

Enforcer: Tank. Enforcers do powerful damage with melee weapons, and are very capable of taking a beating and pulling opponents off of teammates who can't take a beating.

Engineer: Tank, beacon access. Engineers are capable of some of the same things enforcers are, by proxy. By maintaining a powerful robot pet, engineers have the equivelant of an enforcer teammate at all times. They are also second best in the trade skills.

Bureaucrat: Crowd control. Currently the 'crat is probably the only profession sorrier than the Fixer, but what they are supposed to be very good at is controlling the fight. A crat has the same ability of the enforcer to pull an opponent off of a teammate in trouble. The difference is, the opponent isn't pulled onto the crat, but paralyzed, charmed, or otherwise rendered unwilling to fight.

Martial Artist: Tank, healer. Martial artists aren't as powerful in the damage dealing department as the adventurer or enforcer, but are second only to the doctor for healing, are capable of soaking up more damage than the enforcer through evasion techniques, and can pull opponents off of teammates as effectively as a soldier.

Meta Physicist: One-man-army. The combat pets of the metaphysicist are the weakest of the bunch, but when combined with a healer pet with the power of a martial artist or adventurer's healing formulas, and their own ability to both increase anyone's nano skills, or shut those skills down completely, the Meta doesn't really need teammates.

Nano-technician: Root & Nuke. The Nano-tech acts as the artillery of the group, doing significant damage from a safe distance.

Soldier: Tank. Soldiers are much like the enforcer in what they can do. However they can do it just as well over long range. Being able to taunt opponents off of teammates, and with a powerful mirror shield to defect even the most extreme firepower, they are just as good a tank as the enforcer.

Trader: Skill support & crowd control. The trader's skill transfers are too powerful in a group. Anything a trader's group can do, they can do a lot better with the trader supporting them. That means more powerful guns can be equipped, and more powerful nanos cast. Traders also share the Meta's ability to shut down the nano-abilities of an opponent, and significantly reduce the target's attack skills in the process. Traders and martial artists work VERY well together. They are also the masters of trade skills.

Fixer: We can run fast.

Get the picture?

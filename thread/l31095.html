Funcom: Playability / Puff Out Your Chest (not a flame)
If any Funcom eyes read through this, please do so from beginning to end. Though parts of this may appear harsh, I make these comments without the intent of accusation and most certainly without malice toward the individuals responsible for the game itself. I offer this disclaimer as a professional courtesy and not a fanboy suck-up. Take these comments for what they may or may not be worth.

From: Public Relations News In Brief
quote:

As we all know, the launch of Anarchy Online didnt go as smoothly as we would have hoped. In spite of that, the last week has marked considerable improvement (see todays update by Martin Amor), and although we acknowledge there are still some issues to resolve, we now consider the game playable. As a result of that, the month included with the purchase of the game will officially start as of today.

From: Martin Amor
quote:

First of all - from our side it looks like it's getting easier for you to play the game. We are getting more and more people online every day. We have improved stability a LOT the past week and you should not get as many "server lost" messages as you did a week ago.

Of course when we have more and more players online other issues arise - i.e. performance problems, both server-side (what you experience as "lag") and client-side when you are in a city with 700 other players in your "close" vicinity. We have pin-pointed some server performance bottle-necks (the symptoms are 2-3 seconds lag at times, rising sometimes to bigger lag bursts of up to 30+ seconds.) We don't have the fixes ready yet - but they are in the works. I just wanted to let you know that we are very aware of the problem and are working on it.


Mr. Amor, please do not confuse stability with playability. While many people continue to have stability problems, even those of us who can log in and stay in for awhile have playability problems. Yes, I can somewhat reliably stay in the game, but the lag is so intense that I can't do anything but watch people complain in the chat portion of the screen.

Please, provide more detail on what improvement you have seen over this last week? Logging in has improved (although it seems to have become unstable again tonight) and disconnecting has been reduced (although it also seems to have returned to an unstable point tonight). Don't assume that because more people are able to login and stay on the server that these people are actually able to do anything. Other than this, there are countless real problems beyond just little in-game quirks that make this anything but playable.

Consider the horrendous lag. Occasional lag is acceptable. Consistant three to ten second lag with thirty second bursts of lag is out of the question. Attempting to leave the city or enter a building dozens of times before it the transition is actually initiated on the server is out of the question. Losing your characters posessions, dying and losing your possessions and then logging in to find yourself in the same spot you died with one hit point left is out of the question. Dying because two monsters attack you and you can't keep up due to the lag is out of the question. Dying before you even realize you were involved in a battle, due to lag, is out of the question.

I was pleased to see that Funcom finally acknowledged the lag as a legitimate issue that must be dealt with, but the other statement suggesting that the game is now playable (and payable) causes me to pause and consider if Funcom really understans the weight of the lag situation? Right now, for many of us, lag is the only issue. Granted, this may be our only major concern at the moment because the lag is preventing us from playing enough of the game to encounter other major issues.

You also state that a second dimension will allow the support of twice as many players as there are now. I do hope this was a slip of the tongue. You are incapable of supporting the number of players you have now. If you have a second dimension, I suspect that you will be able to support the number of players you currently have, but if you double the number you'll simply have the same problem you already do.

Your comment that "of course" there will be problems arising when there are a lot of players online and especially when there are more than 700 in a city is obvious. Anyone could have told Funcom this months ago when 90,000 people expressed interested in participating in the beta.

I know that, if your situation is anything like most in the computer industry, you are constrained by various limitations in resources and that marketing has pushed you into directions you and your team did not want to go. I'm also certain that your marketing persons have made statements that you may not agree with (whether or not you care to vocalize your agreement or disagreement). Among these may be the idea that the game is playable and that we should begin to pay for the monthly use of the product. My experience in the industry and with others like myself in the industry is that we prefer to produce quality rather than quantity and that when we rush things, it is only because we are being forced by the powers that be in marketing and management to do so. With this in mind, I assure you that many of us have no complaints against you as a professional. You have been kind enough to keep up to date on a lot of issues and offer some relatively consistant responses in the daily news.

We're not asking for your head on a plate. We're also not asking that you make the game perfect tomorrow - or else. We're simply asking that you (and Funcom) do what you can to ensure that we are not charged for a product that we cannot use or that can only slightly fulfill its advertised capabilities until such capabilities are resolved and the product is finally playable. These feeble moves by Funcom, in the face of all these obstacles (lag, etc) make it very difficult to have patience, maintain an optimistic outlook on the situation and not return the product.

I know your policy may be to avoid providing dates, but it would be very helpful in deciding whether to keep the game or return it if Funcom would publish a statement regarding various aspects of the game and what stage the fixes are in with a generate targeted fix date. After all, we paid for the RTM'd beta. We are already irate and disgusted with the quality of the product. You seriously can't expect us to become any more upset if you don't accomplish something by a date you've suggested.

Make all the disclaimers and qualifying statements you care to. Just give us something a few levels beyond the warm-fuzzy "we're working on stuff" abstraction. Tell us that you believe you found a solution for increasing the frame rates and that it will involve a client side fix which you hope (but can absolutely not gaurantee) will be fixed in the next week. Tell us that you are not sure how to fix the lag but that you do have personel on it 24x7 and hope to have a plan of attack by the end of the week and maybe a solution to all the lag by the end of the month - but that you're just giving us the timeline as a courtesy and can't be held to it. That's fine with a lot of us. Believe it or not, there are projects out there that actually make the defects public in an accessible database with status reports and target fix version expectations, too! This is not unheard of in the industry.

We - I want to expect the best out of this experience. I want to feel confident that I can keep the game knowing full-well that in the near future, I'll actually be able to use it and enjoy it. I want to know that, until that day comes, you won't take my money. I want to know that I can trust Funcom in the future. I want to know that Funcom is as serious about this product and its customers as those at the heart of the game (designers, developers -- even support) are. Many of us have been or are in various parts of the same industry. We know that your tech support persons have no control over the product and are doing the best they can under their management. We know that the coders are not sitting in a room eating pizza and drinking soda while wasting away the hours playing Q3 Arena over the company LAN. We just don't feel confident that Funcom has the same dedication and interest.

Please, as a customer and a fan, I ask you to do what you can to convince your company to stand up, grab its balls, pound ferociously on its chest and say "This is still going to be the greatest game you have ever seen. We have so much faith in it, we will do what it takes to please our customers in the mean time. Stick with us through this and you are going to participate in the most exhilerating multi-player experience gaming has ever
seen".

I know the feeling most of you who are shouldering these daily complaints have. It's the feeling that you cannot please everyone and that the harder you try, the more people ask of you. This is true. There's no denying it. But there are those of us out here who really do not want to dump AO or Funcom. We just want to be sure we're not idiots for sticking by you by keeping this product. Every inch you give us makes it that much easier for us to ride this out.

Just don't hide anymore and play these silly games (Funcom, not you personally Mr. Amor).

With respect and expectations,

I admit that I'm beginning to feel foolish for having any expectations for this game. It seems to be my nature to give people the benifit of the doubt and to assume things will work out in the end. I'm not going to starve or suffer sleepless nights over the loss of $64 (with shipping) on this game, but I am beginning to feel slighted, frustrated and betrayed. I have purchased other Funcom products and was pleased with their quality, but we're talking things like NoEscape which can't even compare to the magnitude of AnarchyOnline.

Still, as someone who has been on the other side (closer to Martin Amor's side) in these situations, I know that we have to read between the lines of likely Funcom propaganda and limited (by Funcom) honesty (or even denial) on the part of the developers. I have conflicting instincts on this one.

I do believe they would be shooting themselves in the foot with this decision to begin charging though. The closer users get to the point that additional funds (over and above the cost of the CD) will be withdrawn from their accounts by Funcom, the more they will rush to return the game at any cost. There are a lot of us out there who can be very convincing when dealing with online and brick-and-morter software retailers and we will get our refund eventually. Even if it is only for the principal of it. But I digress. My point is that the closer Funcom pushes people toward the date that these additional funds are going to be charged, the more they're going to alienate people who are not confident that the situation will ever be resolved and that ideal play will occur. This seems like a disasterous step to take.

This is what I was nudging toward in my comment about Funcom needing to grab their balls, suck it up, puff out their chest and convince us that we're not lemmings waiting to plummet to the rocks beneath and be swept out to sea, sans our ~$50. I don't want excuses and I don't want ambivolence. I want to know the real status of things. The expected timespan of solutions (even if they don't hit the mark when the date arrives) and I want a take-no-prisoners attitude that built successful game companies like id Software.

It's unfortunate that the only real bravado has come from Funcom sympathizers instead of Funcom itself.


As a side note. I have considered whether or not I would pay for a beta product. Silly, I know - I already have paid for it. What I mean is that, if Funcom had announced that they were looking for beta testers but that they would have to charge $50 for every accepted applicant over the first 2500, I would probably volunteer as long as that $50 also came with a full boxed-set of the final game whenever it was released. It sounds absurd to pay for a beta product, but as long as I would be given the finished product at the end of the testing and I would be refunded the money if the project is terminated, I'd have no problem. In this business, it may even become a standard some day. (Who knows?). Some of us delight in participating in the creation of something like this and would gladly make a small investment of money and a large investment in time to be part of these things, up front.

Just a thought.

Playable?
I logged in today after trying to play all weekend. Now that the game has been deemed playable I thought, "Great. They finally got the game to a playable state and all without a patch. Amazing!"

Alas, it seems someone at Funcom is playing a cruel joke on me. I logged in and as usual it took about 3 tries before I could finally zone.

Not dwelling on that problem I decided to group with a fellow player and go hunting. That was fun until the creatures in the area stopped respawning! Everything had been killed by us and the other players in the area and there was no sign of any new creatures to join the fray.

After waiting about 15 minutes for some creatures to respawn I decided to go on a mission. It was the first time I had a chance to go on a mission with two other people. How fun I thought. I created the mission, duplicated the keys and off we went. Except something terrible went wrong. My map directed me to one area but for the other people in my group their maps took them to entirely different area. Very strange I thought and in the end it didn't really matter that we ended up in different areas of the world based on the same mission because neither mission entrance could be zoned into. I kept getting an error message stating the mission couldn't be generated at this time.

After that little fiasco I decided to wonder aimlessly around the area in the hopes of finding a lone creature that had not been picked off by the masses, but alas no such luck.

Out of the two or so hours I played I think I managed to get in about 15 minutes of gameplay.

I think maybe the term 'Playable' should be better defined. I don't consider this game to be even close to playable and it really saddens me knowing that my 30 day trial has started because I fear that I will never get to see the full potential of this game before it is in working order as I will cancel my account.

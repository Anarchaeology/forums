Thanks for reiterating one of the worst misconceptions about the game. Many people think PvP is about one on one.

I'd like to see fighting classes that kill and can take damage. Damage classes that add damage but are frail and susceptible to quick death. Utility classes that make life easier and are susceptible to death.

What many players seem to want is all classes able to kill with a similar amount of safety. Seems that there is a desire for the damage vs. safety ratio for all classes to be equal with the only variance being the 'how' that the damage is done (pets/weapons/NF).

Hence, we see the unbridled cries for the ability to be able to 'root-and-shoot' rather than 'root-and-run'.

I always thought that roots were supposed to be used in emergencies. For example, an NT in a skirmish that gained agro would be able to root the opponent and run for the hills or wait for his teammates to stabilize the situation. In one-on-one this meant rooting the opponent and running for the hills (or zone line). If you decided to nuke the rooted enemy then that should be a tactical decision.

This is where Izgimmer's came in to play as far as I thought. IF the opponent was within one-shot kill then the opponent was rooted long enough to hopefully get that last 'finishing' shot off.

Snares likewise would be a way to escape. The Fixer should have the highest degree of safety while offering little in the way of damage. He's the guy you 'just can't catch up to to kill'. Players don't seem to get this concept. You hear Fixers complaining that they die going toe-to-toe with combat classes. My response: 'Well, duh. You should have been going in the other direction.'

I heard that in EQ you could root an opponent and kill from range with impunity. Sounds silly to me.

AO is unfortunately populated with characters who all desire the capability to solo the monster population and be able to PvP any other character with a fair degree of success.

I'll chime in...

I am an army of one. One that knows how to have a good time. I protect and patrol and get liquored up (not all at the same time however) When I'm on duty, which seems like nearly all the time, I wear my uniform and carry a big gun. When I'm in Rompa and armed/armored you can bet I'm responding to a call for backup. I like Rompa bar, and was a patron long before I joined Omni-Pol. Guns and drinks don't mix. That's why when I'm off duty getting liquored up at Rompas I neither wear my armor nor wield a weapon. ((True I'm glued into my weapons for 1 more level, but this is a situation I don't like to be in. I am a strong proponnent of self-equipping... but sometimes ya gotta do what ya gotta do. That said, I'll still take off my glued equipment if I'm gonna rp being off duty. Wrangles arent *THAT* hard to find)) While I'm sitting there having a drink in my leather shirt and pants I don't want to be bothered with dispute resolution, or talk of politics... I WANT MY BOOZE.

As somewhat of a history buff I can recall from ancient text files that police officers had their own "hang out" spots. Often bars where off-duty personnel would congregate in order to relieve stress. Unsavory individuals were usually not a part of the atmosphere in these places unless trouble was brewing. Things here seem to be somewhat different. The clientele at Rompas ranges, as you've said, from Police to Clanners(!) and everything in between. This is the way it *should* be (perhaps without the clanners) as people need to understand that OP officers are people too. On the flip side, I have noticed that some of my fellow officers are reluctant (whether from past experience or who knows what) to let their guard down and have a drink. Armor is not required to be worn at all times (though sometimes I will wear my body armor if I'm feeling particularly paranoid) as the level of supression gas present in the bar limits violent acts to the occasional ashtray tossing contest.

All I'm saying is that when I'm in Rompas in full uniform and brandishing a weapon, I mean business. When I'm wearing my civvies I mean to get pissed and pass out.

As for people being intimidated by OP I'm not sure there's much that can be done about it. People always seem to hate cops unless they're in need of some protection. It's amazing that the same people who call us for help one day sneer at us the next when they see us on patrol. I've done the best I can to be personable and approachable while in uniform. I am often reticent while on duty as getting involved in conversation seems to evoke a "hey, what's that stinking cop want now?" response from others.

I know that I and many others in the ranks of OP are out to help. We really do protect, and we wish to continue. Take a look inside some arrival grounds and you may just find a friendly OP officer handing out starter packs to new arrivals. There is an entire division of OP dedicated to just this (and other positive PR) and they take their jobs seriously.

I know we look intimidating in our uniforms, in fact, that is intentional. Walk up to us and ask us for help though and I'm certain you'll receive it. (Disclaimer: Not all help may seem like it at the time, but trust me, we're helping you).

Next time you see me knocking a few back in Rompas, come say Hi... I might just buy you a drink.

Re: Omni-Pol: law enforcement or intimidators?
quote:
Originally posted by Tyndall
I know Omni-Pol is supposed to be here for our protection but more and more often my encounters with them leave the impression that they are an occupying force. I grew up in Rome Blue so maybe my opinion is colored but when Omni-Pol are nearby shouldn't I feel safe? Instead I feel like they don't want respect, they want fear.
It's an interesting question, isn't it, Tyndall? As someone who has certainly had her share of issues with Omni-Pol over time, let me try to offer an assessment...

First of all, Omni-Pol DO tend to mass. They favor "overwhelming force" tactics designed to squash even the THOUGHT of resistance. Although this can can come across as being brutal if it is misapplied, the tactic itself is actually very effective in PREVENTING casualties... No resistance equals no officers injured, and, hopefully, no injury to the suspect as well.

Also, I would tend to discount Tarryk's objection to Omni-Pol as seeming overly militarized. Honestly, given the weapons a typical Omni-Pol officer might encounter in the course of carrying out his duties day-to-day, the armor and weaponry seem a reasonable response. Omni-Pol have a responsibility to protect themselves AS WELL AS the employees of Omni-Tek, and the equipment they carry is in line with this mandate.

The problems with Omni-Pol are not institutional or systemic, unfortunately. They are the result of a leadership that has encouraged the "overwhelming force" tactic to be applied to even the most trivial of offenses. Omni-Pol's current leadership has also adopted an extremely defensive stance toward any criticism against itself or the larger organization. Critics tend to be labelled "rebel sympathizers," "instigators," or "criminals." They have made little to no effort at genuine outreach to the community they serve, choosing to rely instead on intimidation and fear to keep THEIR FELLOW EMPLOYEES "in line."

Given these policies, it isn't much of a surprise that otherwise loyal Omni-Tek patriots experience feelings ranging from discomfort to outrage to hostility when they encounter Omni-Pol officers. The organization as a whole has a tarnished reputation as a result of these short-sighted decisions on the part of the leadership, and the unfortunate fact is that the rank-and-file officer walking a beat is probably in more danger as a result.

As someone who has argued consistently for an internal reform of Omni-Pol, some type of judicial review, or, preferably, BOTH, I can't say I'm optimistic. Still, try to treat each officer you meet as an individual. I know that I don't appreciate the stigma sometimes attached to me because of my affiliation with Devil's Advocate, and I'm sure at least a few of the Omni-Pol officers you interact with will appreciate if you take the time to deal with them as human beings, rather than lumping their organization's baggage on their shoulders. ^_^

Assumptive Roleplaying... a discussion
<OOC>
Ok, I admit I didn't use the search feature before starting this thread... hope I'm not covering whipped dead horse territory, I was away for a while smoking evercrack and darkagecrack so I haven't followed the forums since just after launch.

I wanted to ask about assumptive/presumptive roleplaying... it was explained to me as doing something that and assuming success without allowing the person I'm rp'ing a scene with a chance to stop me. I can see where this leads to problems. But my question is this. Shouldn't some roleplay be assumptive? Lets say I'm level 100 and I encounter a level 50 char. Ok, so ooc I know they're half my level, and I may know this IC though it doesn't make much sense... but what I do know IC (via whatever excuse you wanna make up for pressing "T" on someone) is that this level 50 is likely to lose to me in a fight.

For instance, let's assume that I'm level 100 (and show up as adventurer to another player) and I meet a level 20 (who shows up as thug) and I can see that they don't have a chance against me in a fight. If/when a fight breaks out between the characters wouldn't it be safe to assume the level 50 would lose horribly? If I emoted that I threw them to the ground and stabbed them to death or, not to be so radical, snatched the weapon from their hand shouldn't it be assumed that my greater skills allow me to succeed in this endeavour? Another example... when a level 200 soldier walks in the door and stands next to me looking mean and brandishing the biggest assault rifle I've ever seen wouldn't it be prudent to not provoke him for fear of a swift death?

Now, you might say that things related to game mechanics, like levels shouldn't matter in a role playing environment. But I think that they do. If I get info IC on your char... lets say I'm using my gridfeed to look official company records on someone and I find out their title is CEO, wouldn't I want to think twice about attacking them for fear of being squashed by ninja bot master?

So this post mainly addresses scenes containing violence, what do you want, I'm OP for crissakes. I don't think assumptive roleplaying fits in under a lot of other situations as there is no mechanics in the game to back up the assumption. If I assume someone is going to say yes in marrying me, I can't go outside to a marriage booth and get a cert with both our names on it if the other person says no. (<IC>hmm, perhaps thats what I oughtta do when they say no</IC>) But in a combat situation if I emote that I've subdued you I can pretty well assume that I've succeeded (if you're lower level) or probably failed (if you're much higher level) or, if you're of similar level we could roleplay to find out who wins and I wouldn't assume anything. (/me tries to subdue %t). Why not? I mean I can take you outside to the arena and kick you around there... shouldn't that game mechanic back up my RP?

What do others think about this? Anyone up for a discussion?

Consent
Theres a rule already extant in most text-based, non-MUD RPGs out there that covers exactly the situations you describe. Its called the Rule of Consent. What it boils down to is that the folks involved in a particular RP scene agree that various and sundry Bad Things might happen to them.

Take your lvl 100 character coming up to a lvl 20, for example. Assuming that both of you are roleplaying, the usual thing to do is to 'ask consent' OOC of that lvl 20 before you actually do anything IC. Knocking him to the ground and choking him to death, for example. He would have to consent for this to happen, at which time you would play out the scene as you both saw fit, and he would presumably /terminate as his death came upon him.

Conversly, that same lvl 20 might decide to blindside your lvl 100, perhaps with the aim of coldcocking him and robbing his unconsious arse. Once again, OOC consent must be gained before he can blackjack you. You might decide that theres no way his hulking Trox enforcer butt is gonna sneak up on your highly-trained 100 Opi MA, so you could concevably deny consent, proposing that you caught him sneaking up on you from the corner of your eye, and then the scene would play out from that point. Offering an alternitive outcome when asked for consent is called (surprise!) Negotiating, and being known as a good negotiator is concidered a mark of a good player in pure-RP games. So is taking your lumps when you're obviously outmatched.

As you can likely see, it is entirly possible to abuse the consent rule, such as backing out of a death at the last moment, or the like. This is Bad Form, and you'd be advised not to RP with that particular dastardly individual in the future. In a pure RP enviroment (such as that found on a MUSH), this fellow would find himself with no RP at all in short order if he made a habit of abusing consent.

Interestinly enough, the term Twink is applied to such an individual. In fact, its used as a general label for anyone who cheats OOC to gain IC advantage, be it lying about his capeablities, using OOC information IC, or the like. I was quite surprised when I came upon the term in an MMORPG enviroment, because personaly I dont concider MMORPG twinking as cheating. Then again, Im sure Stalin thought deep down he really was a nice guy.

Hope this rambling helped. On a personal note, I am still an avid MUSHer, playing on AmberMUSH every night while I'm soloing missions on AO. Killing mobs keeps me occupied when Im waiting for other folks to pose on the MUSH.

The short version:
Keep "assumptive" roleplaying for those player you know and form whom you can make assumptions.

The long version:
When roleplaying with a stranger, you have tread very carefully
because you just don't where they are coming from characterwise.

The assumptions that (1.) being Omni Pol your are in a position of authority (2) being higher level means you can probably beat them into submission are not unreasonable, and I think a lot of roleplayer would choose to go along with it. But then you will find roleplayers who do not look at level as having any in-character value, and is a perfectly legitimater choice. And there will also be people who do not recognise Omni-Pol as having any more authority then any other player guild.

Even if someone does accept your level and role in Omni-Pol as a fact, things in the character's background you don't know about might come into play. Maybe the low level character's background includes the fact he is the son of a big shot and as such somewhat untouchable? Or maybe he plays an android and even at low level has superhuman strength. Or maybe his org have just played a scenario in which Omni-1 government has been taken over by Sol-Banking and Omni institutions like no longer exist Omni-Pol.

Even if he where character-wise willing to play along with your scene, he may simply not be in the mood for or does not have the time for it.

The point is nobody has to go along with anybody elses roleplaying. In my opion you need to show the proper respect to the player you wish to roleplay with and seek some sort of consensus beforehand.

In the example you make, I'd perhaps send an ooc tell the person, for instance /tell Xzy (( Hi, I play an OP officer. Are you free at the moment to be brutalised by the police? And then maybe one day you and your buddies will get back at me in some dark alley. ))

It may seem inelegant, but remember you are dealing with a perfect stranger and all in all I think it is more important to deal with them with respect that it is to suprise and dazzle them with your roleplaying talents. With people you know and already have an understanding of course you can wild, you already know what is appropriate and what isn't.

I agree with Savoy. On every point, actually. I'd also like to add that I feel it's important to be humble and not climb any kind of horse, not even a pony. Even a three times world boxing champion can be taken down if he's unprepared or attacked by in a dark alley. Let's say...if that lvl 20 drew a weapon and had you staring down the barrel of the gun, while you were having a night off-duty, drinking and watching a holoflick....would it be too strange if he manages to fire his gun before you can get your gun or dissarm him? However well trained you are or what level. Or...could it be possible that the lvl 50 agent manages to shoot the gun out of your hands, sniping off the roof of some building, while you're out doing patrol? Or the lvl 37 doc slipping some strong laxative in your cup of coffee while you were tapping frenetically on your comlink to get the file of her brought up? And so on and so forth...

Another thing...I have to say I really dislike the "assuming" emotes that close up and leave no room for counter-reaction. Example: /me throws a chair at XYZ, hitting her straight in the head. This kind of emotes doesn't leave an opening for the other player. He or she has no choice but to get smacked by the chair (of course there's always a choice, but you get my point). If the emote was like this: /me throws a chair at XYZ then XYZ would have a choice; get hit, dodge or catch the chair and throw it back etc...

Using the "bad" emotes, especially towards strangers, is a bad, bad assumption. It makes you invulnerable, and all others are but puppets in your strings. And I personally hate that. Hence we move to my next point. Whatever the situation, whatever the event, I think every player and either side should be willing to "lose" if the situation suits it. If you have set your mind on "winning" and will tolerate nothing but a glorious success, then you have closed up for anything that might happen that could be cool and add a good thing to the rp. I'm assuming the outcome of the event hasn't been agreed on beforehand, of course.

I try (and I admit I'm not always successful) to play like this...Be humble, expect other players to do unexpected things that is perfectly suiting the situation, but maybe not your character, be wiling to be the weaker one and not always the strongest...and generally...have fun. RP isn't about being the strongest, most ingenious or clever. At least not to me.

That said...even if my character levelwise could kick your OP arse in the arena , she would hardly ever attack you, should they meet. Just because you're OP. I'd let her be taken in for questioning or whatever, even if I know I could escape if I, the player, wanted to and saw it fitting. But that means I would be taking a boring flight back to Athen, instead of a exciting evening at the Pols .

Wilin,

RPing effectively is a lot like Improv. There's a natural form: a give-and-take. When someone violates that in Improv and constantly forces a situation on you, we call it pimping. The best players (improv-wise anyway) are those that give but don't push.

Conversely, accepted decorum is for the person on the receiving end to accept whatever was given. But since not everyone you meet knows they're Improving or roleplaying, this is never guaranteed if you pick strangers randomly.

What you can do is take a hint from audience participatory Improv. Basically, never put the audience member in a bad or precarious situation and only put them in situations that involve one or two lines. Trying anything more against someone who isn't prepared never works in Improv, nor does it in RPing.

Wash, rinse, repeat...
A couple of people have said things that I really agree with, so I thought I would just condense and restate... ^_^;

1) Whenever a particular NPC has a particular item and appears in a particular place on a particular schedule, it creates a camping situation. Automatically. As long as that situation exists, there will be camping. There are a few things that can be done to address this problem: a) Make these items worthless to mediocre, but unique. In other words, these items are souvenirs, or b) Randomize who these items spawn on and where. Since the conceit that these items are "unique" is already spoiled (after all, multiple people HAVE the item, right?), create a more generic system for dropping the item in a particular dungeon, area, etc.

2) It is far more compelling and organic to have resources that allow talented PCs create items, rather than relying on unique drops. This compliments the idea of more generic drops. If certain key (but non-unique) ingredients for particular items can only be found in dungeon x, then people will go to those areas to find the items they need, without actually camping a particular critter at a particular spot.


Now, the problem with all this, of course, is that if you (Funcom) have gone to the trouble to create a unique boss, you want people to fight him/her/it. So how do you encourage people to actually fight these bosses? I think the answer is to tie other events to defeating these bosses, rather than items on those people. For example, a castle dungeon with a dragon boss. Defeating the dragon unlocks a section of the castle that is otherwise locked off, allowing people to fight the monsters there (which may have better odds of dropping crucial ingredient x). Now, obviously, ANYONE can get into the locked area once the dragon is defeated, but that's OK. The point is that people will have to fight the cool boss, and they'll each get to loot a t-shirt that says "I beat the dragon." Enough people will want the t-shirt, or the prestige of beating the critter, that the monster will remain an important part of the dungeon, but defeating the creature will no longer be the ENTIRE reason to visit the dungeon (resulting in disappointment if he is beaten by someone else.

My two cents...

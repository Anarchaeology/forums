Value of any buff is dependent upon the buffee and their need
I never ask for any specific amount for a buff. Generally if someone is asking for a buff around me and I notice it, they'll get it if i can give it. If they tip me afterwards, then that's fine.

I have stood occasionally and /shouted that I'm buffing for tips, but I never say how much and often give them away free even so. I mostly offer buffs for tips when I'm really stretched for cash and need to buy something specific (e.g. aug nano armour and imps recently).

If someone gives me 500 creds I'll say thank you and how generous and if someone gives me 50,000 I'll say thank you and how generous. If someone doesn't tip me at all, then that's cool too (though I won't thank them specifically ).

Why? Because the engineer who can put up his warbot or slayerdroid thanks to my buff can make a LOT of money out of that buff. As a result, they tend to tip quite highly - because it is worth so much to them. The Doc, who gets a little better heal out of a buff, probably has less to gain and tends to tip less.

The e-bay bought money player (perfectly valid) will tend to tip highly because each credit is worth less to them. The MP who killed a thousand reets/leets/blubbags to get the dosh together values each credit and tends to tip less (but I appreciate it more).

Someone tipped me 100K for the +92 NP buff the other day. he was building a set of QL 125s and had spent 2 million on the parts. He was gaining in total probably +500 skill points in total he said. It was worth it to him and he had the money. I was tipped 2000 credits for the same buff about 20 mins later (I was putting together my own imps at the time). It was a lvl 30ish Atrox who was building two low clusters on a low level implant who would probably get +30 skill points total....

I know that tippers have trouble working out what to give.... but what makes you think that the buffer has any better idea? The tipper at least knows what the buff is worth to him. He knows what it's for and what benefit he will derive from it. The buffer knows none of this - so how can he tell what the buff is worth? As others have pointed out, the buff has no intrinsic value. it's a button click and done. But it does have more or less value to the receiver and that is what should define the price.

As it turned out I desperately needed the +80 treatment buff to get my imps in and tipped 100K. I was getting my first frenzy out of it (despite skill cap), the ability to ride a low level yalm, better nano and health rechargers, closer to infuses, way better hitpoints, way better run speed etc etc out of my imps. 100K was certainly well priced for all the benefit I got from the buff. The doctor wanted to give some of it back... but after spending close to 2.5 million on the imps themselves... the 100K was a drop in the ocean...

It's not just buffs either. A Supreme Mindreaver crystal can sell quite easily for 250K these days. Someone who has no mez pet might well jump at it. Since I already have a Greater Mindreaver. the benefit would have been negligible so I turned one down. the crystal wasn't worth 250K to me, but it might have been worth it to someone else. The price paid for anything in the game is always relative to how much the buyer wants/needs the item and how much benefit they will recieve from the item.

The best thing to do is to work out what the buff is really worth to you and then tip accordingly.

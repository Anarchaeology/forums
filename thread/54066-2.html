I'll chime in here, although the topic does seem to be pretty much exhausted.

RP, unfortunately, is what you make it. Those of us from 'old school' D&D days have a different perspective on it than the products of the 'first person shooter' generation (I know thats a generalisation, hear me out). The first person generation who RP seem to believe that RP=getting other people to obey you, or being better than everyone else (as so much in life is all about competition, who is the best seems all important)

In a traditional RPG the player _could_ end up running the show (but in my experience this always killed a campain as no problem became too big for the players), but because all the GM had to do was keep _his_ players happy thats not a problem. Scale this up to a MMORPG and this attitude is big trouble for all the reasons Bio has brilliantly said.

So we come back to 'RP is what you make it' for some that can only mean 'RP makes me the focus of the attention' the reasons are many (unpopular IRL etc) but unimportant. Each player must realise this isnt a game full of NPC's put there for their amusment, but a world full of PC's (with NPC's there for target practice :-) ) who have their own opinions on what is and isnt acceptable.

This situation, unfortunately, suddenly mirrors real life too much. Some people think you're an idiot, others think you're great, some are leaders, some are led, some people 'play' their characters this way, others just play their characters as themselves. Its impossible to tell whats what with any but the most 'notorious' RP'ers (Aktrez and co) but since these are now _real_ people all of a sudden it _matters_ what you do, as your actions effect other people (where it wouldnt matter with a paper RPG). The worst part is that in a paper RPG the players are 'the best', in a MMORPG theres probably one or more other players who are not 'in your party' who are better than you, people find this hard to accept and try and compensate (read: be better) in other ways.

Level _is_ a problem. Many player view level=power. Let me ask you this...what level is Ross? Having never seen him I dont know but back in paper RPG-land the leaders were high level, but not 'the highest'.

Bear in mind, in a fantasy setting, the powerfull _do_ lead - this is not neccessarily true in a sci-fi setting. Jean-Luc Picard isnt as physically powerful as Worf for example but hes still _more powerful_ than Worf, so you need to define what _is_ power.

Which brings us back to level. Because combat is the only viable way to gain levels in AO, level=combat=power. This immediately puts RP'ers at a huge disadvantage to the powergamer in terms of 'who is powerful' because that = 'who is the highest level' which basically = 'who has the most time to throw at doing missions/hunting'. However because very little RP takes place in PvP zones this 'measure' is completely irrelevant but people cant disconnect from the level=power scale because that is the only 'measure' the game gives the players.

Personally a level 1 crat _would_ be 'powerful' if he ran an organised crime syndicate (bearing in mind 'crat' is a generalisation of that characters talents) and so would be a very difficult person to cross since he has lots of 'heavies' to do the killing for him. He wont be a high level due to the AO XP system and the fact hes not actually the one doing 'the dirty work'. The problem however comes when he sudenly forces himself on another group or RP'ers and this is usually where it all falls down. If he were to walk into the Rompa and suggest that the managment take out some 'insurance' against property damage what hes basically doing is begging for cash in a game mechanic sense and this may, quite rightly, cheese a few people off ('who the hell does this guy think his is' etc)

Heres where the problem can get worse. Aktrez and co (sorry to keep using you as an example) play along (for the sake of RP) and want to hire some people to 'dispose of a small problem' they have. Mr crimelord needs to be ready to either back off (still in character) or play along (i.e. RP the confrontation, or take it to a PvP zone and pray no one barges in - a PVP zone suddenly brings level into the equasion so its no longer RP, its a game mechanic and in general the highest level character will win - notwithstanding certain factors).

Usually this starts the 'my dads bigger than your dad' game where both parties ficticiously 'tool up' in an attempt to beat the other into submission which tends to ruin the whole thing (hoping all the time that no one is stupid enough to take any of this personally).

Now what Mr Crimelord _should_ have done is aproach Aktrez OOC before starting anything and the pair of them planned out how it was going to go down. Problem is if Aktrez tells Mr. Crimlord to sling his hook this guy now feels his 'really good' rp idea has been shot down by 'that self important nobody' (you get the idea).

Bottom line, as boring as it is, RP in AO should be limited to your own group of friends/guild to ensure that no one steps on anyone elses toes. This depresses everyone as RP stories have no 'importance' in the grand scheme of things (I know they dont anyway but people feel more invloved if 'other' groups are invovled as it feels like a group of NPC's).

Even if FC _do_ start story events for players, all the players who dont get involved (or arent able to), or are on the loosing side, will feel cheated because they didnt get the opportunity to effect anything (although you can argue that loosing _is_ effecting the outcome). Any event FC does run in this vain will more than likely be level/combat based and that brings us back to all the problems mentioned above PLUS the involvment by the powergamer/non-RP'er who is bored of the levelling treadmill/lack of high level content and just wanted something else to do. RP'ers all get shafted at this point.

OT: I think SWG will make life alot easier on the RP'er, not from a story point of view, just from a game mechanic point of view

Garzu, it's certainly difficult to impact the storyline. But I do believe it's possible. The main barrier for us as players is that it takes a sustained effort by a significant number of people, working towards limited and realistic goals over time, to do it. This is hard to achieve because there are very few leaders among the AO population that are capable of organizing, and especially of motivating, enough people over enough time.

The Council of Ares would be an example of this when it works, as much as many people disagreed with their ingame politics and goals, they 'made it into the storyline' with their actions. Why were they successful? Because they got several guilds with a good membership base to put out the effort towards a common goal over several months. I personally believed their goals played into FC's hands and that the CoT was slated to fall regardless, but they nonetheless were incorporated by the writers as one of the reasons for the Fall of the CoT. They succeeded in making the front pages of the FC storyline.

Too-often we as individuals hang our hats and our hopes on 'silver bullets:' fast, easy, and simple (often one-step) solutions to major and complicated situations and problems. Especially on AO. People in general tend to underestimate the time, effort, and other resources required to build an engine for impacting the storyline, and at the same time overestimate the impact they will have and the time-table according to which it will happen. People also tend to be ego-centric and presume that others will see that they're right, fall in line, and do the real work for them, if only they talk long and loudly enough. It's too common that people on AO want to lead from behind, to avoid personal sacrifices while asking, even demanding, sacrifices from others.

Throw into that mix that the 'followers' on AO are often unconcerned, uninformed, or misinformed about the game's story arc, if they're aware of it at all, and generally care mostly about their own power and advancement in-game, and even the best of leaders will soon find herself beating her head and her heart into a brick wall of indifference, ignorance, and selfishness on many fronts.

But... Rome wasn't built in a day, nor was it built by only one person, nor did the builders spend half their time on coffee break yelling at someone else to move that rock. If you really want to affect the storyline, you have to sacrifice.

You have to lead.

This has been explained and debated time and time again in the last year, and a lot of people still don't seem to understand it. This is a skill-based game, not profession-based. Just because you play a certain profession doesn't mean you have any "special" things you can do (with a few exceptions). It just means you get to buy certain skills cheaper, so you have the POTENTIAL to be better at those things than someone of a different profession. If my engineer puts all his IP into theiving skills and your agent puts all of his IP into engineering skills, you will be a better "engineer" than I am, and I will be a better "agent" than you are. Too many people just can't seem to get past the EQ mindset of, "what can my class do?".

As someone who's been attending them for 30 years, I can tell you that most trade shows are really easy to crash. In a lot of industries, you can just ask your local retailer who's in that business and he'll get you a pass (If he likes you and you aren't a pest) . If you go the day before, you can usually just walk in the rear where booths are being loaded in and out. Then you just need to find a friendly looking face (who isn't real busy) and ask them if they can get you a pass. Most of the guys doing set-up are low level and generally happy to help...even if it's a " meet me at X at X AM and I'll slide you a pass". If you hang out in a bar in one of the hotels you can usually find someone who'll get you in. If you want to be more direct, you can just print up a business card that says your employed in the appropriate industry and pay for an on-site badge. Of course you need to dress the part so don't go try any of these in a ripped T-Shirt drunk on your ass. At the LA convention center, where E-3 is, there are back doors where people go out to have a smoke. There are rarely guards there to check who comes back in. The younger amongst you may have trouble pulling any of these off of course, though carrying a big, empty box that is labeled " Microsoft-Trade Show Materials-URGENT" thru a back door and saying your Mom, who is waiting in the car, sent you might work *This comment is in no way to be construed as an endorsement of tradeshow crashing!

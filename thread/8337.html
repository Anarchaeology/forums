Dungeons and Special NPCs
Hmm this can be long winded and everyone has something they think should be rare and the like, hard to acquire. But what is happening in the game doesnt make it rare because you have to go thru a quest to get it with a bunch of teammates. No.....you just need to know the time the mob spawns and camp him. Voila. Instant Ebay success.

What are we talking about. Hmm lets see the Crat Suit? Who hasnt been there and see level 100 Plus players camping. Fix to this.... Cap the dungeon. The campers who get to the boss and get the kill? and never let anyone get a turn. or just the plain griefers who kill and even (it has happened) deleted the items others were waiting for. Increase the spawn rate by a lot. Dont let players stand sitting around for hours waiting for a mob....its not fun thats just stupid and not very satisfying. And make the item a timer you cant get one for another 1 month or the like.

All those steps are in dungeons on Asherons Call. Capped dungeons. Items you cant get for 1/2/3 months timers. So items can still be traded if you dont need it....making low level players for some dungeons in AC a useful commodity with high level players.

As for the special NPC mobs in the field. Your heart was in the right place but your implentation has just sucked. ONE LONE MOB on a 19 HOUR timer? You do have rocks in your head. CAMPED TO HELL! Increase the spawn rate to something far more sensible. ANd make the item once only. For very good items you could only do this once on Ahersons CAll for more lower magic items you had a fixed timer. So you can still trade the item if you wish but your character can never get another. Whats that mean well the players have a more increased chance getting past the camped ebayers to aquire the item. I dont want it to be easy stoill to aquire but nor do I want my efforts in the game screwed up by some one who wants to make money off a bit of computer data.

For all its faults Asherons Call team actually worked out some good quests that still made items highly desirable, still tradeable and even when probably if one third the players base had the item was still a sort out item.

These are suggestions but at the moment most of your new content is not real fun once some high levels latch onto it.

Please, pleast Listen to the players.
Cosmik,

Thanks for opening the discussion on this with the player base.

I think your designers have opened up the doors to some of the absolute worst gameplay brought about by the game mechanics. The intentions were fabulous. The results are almost worse than before we had these great dungeons and special MOB's roaming the lands.

PROBLEMS:

1. Players twice the intended level for a dungeon are claiming it for their own with no 'risk' and spoiling it for those who could actually have some fun in their. High level groups are 'claiming' the boss monsters for their own.

2. For Boss monsters in dungeons who are dropping NODROP gear, super high level groups are teaming with super low-level twinks who get to loot the item they did no part in earning.

3. The unique MOB's roaming the playfields have super high level guilds who 'camp' out the monster and kill it as soon as it spawns. This is a result of the MOB always appearing at the same spot at a predictable interval and dropping predictable gear. You were warned of this and disregarded the pleas for innovation.

4. Your test 'Static Missions' such as Dodga are a knife in the side of anybody who simply decides they want to go and try them out. If you go to The Rising Sun at any given time you will see two or three players sitting in a line waiting their turn to talk to Dodga. The second player in line could very well wait five real life hours to simply talk to Dodga and then have to do it again on the return leg. This is not fun and serves no in-game purpose. The difficulty should be in the quest, not the trigger for the quest.

These player behaviors are ruining the experience of unique monsters and your nice new dungeons for 90% of your paying playerbase. I can't repeat enough how awful this is for the huge majority of your playerbase. It's these kinds of things (dungeons, quests, items) that keep people in the game once the leveling gets old. What you've done with them is enough to make them not worth experiencing.

SOLUTIONS:

1. A Dungeon should spawn for each team that enters. If not feasible for huge dungeons such as the upcoming Camelot, then the spawned dungeons should be level-based. For Steps of Madness players should go to an instance that is for lvls1-50, 50-100, 100-150, 150-200. Teams should go in based on average level. Players who are 'grey' to the highest member in a team should go into their appropriate instance of the dungeon regardless of the team.

Result: No more groups of lvl150's camping Neleb's cloak to have a lvl8 loot it while a group of 6 lvl 50's fight their way in only to be laughed at by the uber doods. Those who missed out on items from newly added dungeons can go get them without ruining the game for those who need to 'earn' them.

2. The unique mobs such as the Ancient Gargantula should spawn within a playfield with a consortium of assistants at any number of random locations. Think of UO which had orc camps that spawned with chiefs, shamans, archers and the like all around a pot of stew. These groups would pop up anywhere within a certain defined area of Britannia. You could not say where they would appear or when. The groups were self-sufficient in that they had the 'chief' (though no special UNIQUE loot) and all his attendants. If a 'special' MOB has been killed, he should respawn at a random one of at least 10 spread out locations within one (or even better two or three) playfield.

EXAMPLE: The Trash King and his lackey appear at the same location with the same loot on a schedule like clockwork. There are multiple Junkyards on Rubi-Ka. If he and his buddies decide to revisit the world, it should be in Athen Shire at a junkyard OR at one of several locations in Agean OR at any of the dozen other suitable locations where it would be fitting for him to be collectin his 'junk'. If he is a non-agro special MOB, it would be appropriate to provide him with a 'patrol route' to further enhance this behavior.

RESULT: If you want to _hunt _ down the Trash King you actually have to _hunt_ him down. You don't _wait_ for the Trash King, you hunt him. This eliminates the problem pointed out in the other post of guilds such as DC having 10 guys sitting at his exact X,Y coords and capping him once he materialilzes.

4. Once a character has received a particular MOB boss drop (such as the Crat Suit Order Form), that particular item should, at a minimum, NEVER drop for that player again or for any kill he has looting rights for. It is a travesty that high level Enforcers are killing the boss mob in the mine dungeon over and over and over and over for this item that they can't even use. Let them get it once and then it's time for the next player to have his turn.

RESULT: Every player has a chance to get every item in the game. Items can still be handed off to other players if the original looter has no use for them. The practice of a single group or player killing a boss MOB over an over is eliminated.

5. Increase the spawn rate of the MOB's that drop the special items. It serves no purpose to make people wait for the excitement of killing a super monster. The excitement and feeling of accomplishment should come from actually killing the monster and getting the loot to prove it. If you combine this with the randomness suggestions from above, you avoid the 'death-loop' syndrom for your poor unique MOB's. Nowaday's having something unique shows that you are probably a jerk in a jerk guild or are so socially deprived that you have the patience to sit for hours and hours staring at a computer screen waiting for your special MOB friend to 'pop'. To be frank, this sucks. DAoC got this right. None of the unique loot is earth shattering or playbalancingly overpowered. This is something you got right and should be commended for. If every damn 'crat on the planet had his damn suit, it would not be the end of the world.

RESULT: The game becomes more fun. Opportunity is there for everyone. Not just those in uber guilds or with no real lives have an opportunity to experience the nice things done by the world designers.

6. 'Static' Mission providers such as Dodga should NOT disappear once they provide a mission to a player. This provides NOTHING in the way of enhanced gaming enjoyment. If a group team leader talks to Dodga, the whole team should get a mission icon.

RESULT: No more tedious standing in line for hours and hours simply for the chance to talk to an NPC.

7. Save your timed spawn, same location, super-MOB's for things like the end of the Camelot dungeon. Notice, I did NOT say Uber-loot. These monsters should take upwards of 50 players to kill. If they are going to be in the same spot every time, then they damn well better take an epic freak of player organization to kill. Once dead, if they drop a single unique item for only one of those fifty players to loot, I'll QUIT the damn game. If I participate in killing this monster, I damn well better get something dropped in my inventory just like every other player who had a hand in killing the beast. EVERY player who did damage or was of an appropriate level in a team that did damage should get the reward. Even if it's just a card that says I participated in the raid, I WILL NOT go to help kill that thing if it becomes a lottery as to who gets looting rights.

RESULT: Players will organize to go kill these 'epic' MOB's. There is no squabbling over what the thing drops once it is finally felled. Player effort goes into actually being effective and killing the boss while reducing casualties and less into trying to make sure you get looting rights.

Cosmik, I hope you are serious in stating that you are interested in player feedback and pass my comments along with any others in this thread. If it sounds like I'm a little terse in my wording, it's because I'm so upset with how these fine additions by your world designers got implemented. The developers simply have to take into account the poor sportsmanship of the majority of online gamers. They also have to take into account human nature. Any creature will take the path of least resistance to it's reward on a regular basis. The suggestions I outline above address some of the loopholes that allow poor sportsmanship and level the amount of resistance for your players (from uber-twinked uber-guildie to Joe Casual) to get the reward.

problem, making it 30mins of course would help but is a
bandaid solution, see #3 for more of a solution than just
making much quicker respawns [both should be done].

2) Qualified Yes on the Level Capping - Bio Lab is a good example,
lots of uber greys, a team of level 55 players generally
get decimated without having a few strong "over cap" players
helping to clear [the bodyguards are brutal]. So I would say
if you claim a dungeon is for Level A to B then make sure
the mobs can be handled reasonably well by those a team
of those players [steps of madness anyone?]

3) Capping total number of people in the dungeon, thought
it was going to be only a couple teams at a time? If not
it really should be. Seems like it gets quite insane there
at times with the number of people - this needs to be
tweaked back.

4) Loot restriction - because of profession imbalanced I would
use caution about lower levels not getting the loot because
a higher level player was on the team and did more damage.
i.e. say a Level 70 Enforcer vs. Level 50 Fixer?? Enforcing
the dungeon level ranges [and making the mobs reasonable
for a decent team in that range] fixes this problem right
on the spot.

5) Saw this in another forum not sure what game it was,
the team partipating in the kill of the NPC ALL members
automatically get the loot sent right to there inventory.
While not "realisitic" [what in AO is??] is sure as heck
increases cooperation and reduces camping, please
consider this strategy!

6) Patrol routes and some randomness to the spawn location
for outdoor NPC's like the Trash King? I like that idea. Still
though would like to see a quicker timer and the idea of
#5 implemented with this as well.

7) Would like to see NPC's in trouble call in for reinforements
from time to time, make things a bit exciting [I think Neleb
does this to some extent], might be some real cool stuff
you can do with outdoor NPC's though [dropship comes in
and drops off 6 Duster Commandos to fight]..

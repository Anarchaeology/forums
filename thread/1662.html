The streets were just as dirty as Jennal remembered from years past. The cobble stone was broken in several places.. making walking a hazardous proposition if you weren't careful. Why don't these people just pick a side and be over with it, she thought. They'll at least get some sort of infrastructure support. But, no, for whatever reasons, the city council of Newland preferred to keep itself distant from the rebellion, even though it was situated smack dabbed in the middle of the Territories.

Part of her wanted to spit in disgust, but another, deeper, more primal part embraced the freedom of doing whatever she wanted. Living in Omni-1, while it had its ammenities, also meant that she had an obligation to conform. She couldn't, at least not at the measily pay rate she was given. No, there were prizes to be won. Not the power that most other Omni-Bureau employees were involved in struggles and plots over. Something simpler.. more pure. This Opi wanted money. The truest material form of power. With it, she could finally get off this dusty rock. With it, she could finally leave indentured servitude... servitude to Omni-Tek and to society.

Jennal tripped as she completed this thought. She looked down at the patch of weed that caused her to stumble. The patchwork of cobblestones had given way to the bare dirt of the gentle slope heading towards the cafe. To her left, she could hear the whirrling noise of the ventilation fan for the incomplete subway system. Its noise, partially masked the voices of the daily crowd that had gathered at the cafe.

She looked down at her wrist, the time was near. In a few minutes, she would be rich, rich beyond the dreams of Avaris. Jennal peered into the side alleyway leading to the cafe. Good, clear, she thought. The sound of her footsteps along the small patch of stone beneath her feet beat in unison with her quickening heartbeat.

Exiting from the alleyway, she began to see the collection of personas at the cafe. Some were dancing on the tables. Others, tossing apples in the air, as if they were waiting for gravity to change its rules. Even the local drunk made his appearance.

Bump. "Damn, who keeps moving those chairs?" said the drunk as he staggered his way across the cafe, knocking over everything that wasn't nailed down. "You're my best goood friend.... well, aside from that lady who gave me this burger." he said to another customer. Swaggering back and forth a little, he continues, "so we all were gathered at Meetmerde, right? Me and all of my buddies, right? To just relax and like have a beer, that we stole from OmniTek transportation, right? And then all these guys showed up, right? And like started shooting..so I picked up my heavy automated gun and...." then snoring. Seeing a chance, the sequestered patron cautiously inches away from the drunk. Jennal lets out a sigh and shakes her head.

"Heh, crazy ol' Strachal" a mumbled voice is heard from her right. Out of the corner of her eye, she could see a young nano in a cowboy hat leaning against the wall of the Neuters club entrance. She caught a glimpse of him eyeing her through the smoke of a nano-cig. Is he the pickup? Possibly. They didn't tell me who was going to pick it up. It doesn't really matter though. I'm not suppose to have contact with him, she recalled. As if on cue, the nano throws his cigarette towards the ground and walks back into the club. The nano-cig, before it reaches the ground, vaporizes into thin air, leaving no trace of its existance.

Jennal looks at the scene again, and walks over to the far table, where she was told to drop the case containing the information. She sat down and ordered a chocolate chip muffin and can of Lime-Slime to pass the time until the drop-off was to occur. Four more minutes.

"Hello Miss." said tall Solitus man with blond hair as he approaches her table. "What is it that you do?"

"Huh" a slightly confused Jennal responds. "Um... I work at Omni-Bureau. But that's not what you came over here to ask me about is it?"

"No, I'm looking for information" he continues.

"Well, I'm sure if you filled out the correct forms, they'll be happy to help you at HQ. I'm off-duty now so if you will." Jennal finishes with a wave of her hand toward the rest of the cafe.

How do they expect me to make a drop in such a crowded place she says to herself. Her eyes slowly scan the scene from her vantage point from the corner. A woman dressed in a Sabertooth cloak sits alone nearby at the bar. Another dressed in white at the table next to hers. A minute passes and they continue to sit in silence... waiting, waiting for something. Am I being paranoid Jennal thinks to herself.

She looks at her time piece. It's almost time. Just one more minute and I drop the case and leave. That's the plan. The money will be waiting for me when I get back to Omni-1.

Heads suddenly turn towards the center of the cafe. People sitting one table away stand up in amazement at the commotion. "Someone just killed Pilker!" cries a voice from one of the tables closest to the center. At that instant, every able soul in the cafe rushes toward the dead body. All except one. Jennal, sensing the that no better opportunity exists, drops the case next to her seat. It couldn't be better, even if I planned it myself, she says silently to herself. She gets up, pushing her chair slightly back, almost knocking over the case. To be this close and almost screw up would just be ironic.

But no one is looking. Even the two su****ious women were completely fixated on the commotion. Jennal walks slowly toward the side alley. Just before the alley walls eclipses her view of the table she had been sitting at moments before, she sees a scarred man with white-blue hair cautiously but swiftly bypass the crowd and sit down at her table.

Her job is done she reminds herself. Jennal takes a deep breath and then disappears into the shadow of the alley.

At the touch of a key, the screen goes blank. Albinos continues to stare at the now black slate, that moments ago had held the security information for the meeting place. A one in three chance of success he told himself. It was not enough to just kill Radiman, that itself would be simple enough. No, it is crucial that it be one of Ross personal helper-bots that dealt the final blow. It must be tied directly to Ross, otherwise the general public would think it to be yet another assassination attempt from a renegade Omni-Tek faction. This was the tricky part.

Turning over the newly acquired information crystal in his hand, Albinos played out the scenario in his mind over and over again. A new dampening field was in-place at the building where Ross and Radiman would meet. This made it impossible to simply aim a high-gain signal directed at the structure as was the original plan. No, his man will have to get in close to the helpers inside the building. Close enough to use a hidden mini-broadcaster to alter their program.

That slight alteration made the mission gave him an uneasy feeling in the pit of his stomach. Omni-Security would be scanning everyone that got that close for any concealed weapons. Granted, the implant was not normally scanned for, but given the recent assassination attempts, they would leave nothing to chance. Could his man enter the building where the meeting is to be held?

Unfortunately, Albinos couldnt ask in person. That man was already on his way, taking a circuitous route through the badlands to avoid the usual Omni-Pol checkpoints. A tall dark fellow, but otherwise undistinguished. Awaiting him at an Omni-1 drop box would be a message describing the change in plans. After which, according to the new plan, he would attempt to bypass the expected crowd gathered outside the meeting place, and make his way into the building. Another in his employment would at that instant, send a grid worm into the heart of the I.C.C. scanner database.

Peace must not exist on Rubi-Ka for the sake of the business Albinos muttered under his breath as he once more reviewed the scenario in his mind.

The streets of Omni-HQ are empty except for a slightly increased number of security guards. The spectators who had previously come to witness the anticipated arrival of Henry Radiman have all left. This much was planned, for the security detail knew that the citizenry would not have the patience to wait through yet another arrival of a supposed doppelgänger. But no, this time, it was real.

Walter watches the procession of guards from a twelfth floor window of the Omni-Admin tower. At the center, he sees Radiman. A tall distinguished gentleman. He carries himself very much like a Botos, Walter thinks. Perhaps we are related by some long distanced bloodline. No matter, blood or not, this man will have to die.

Getting into the building was no trouble. At the predetermined drop-box in the Omni-1 slums was waiting for him an access badge, a micro-broadcaster, and a note. It was the note that troubled him the most. A last minute change to the plan and a promise for extra compensatory funds. Such things never bode well. The badge itself showed as being from Rubi-Ka News Network. A cover story already had been planted, giving him access to this office.

He looks to the body slumped over the desk. That bureaucrat was so initially ecstatic that he had showed up to interview him. She had even offered Walter some of priceless old cigars imported from Earth before lighting one up herself. Little did the poor soul know that was the last time she would enjoy such pleasures. The smell of cigar smoke still lingered in the air, despite the air regulators.

Ah, theyve made it inside. Walter removes a portable treatment kit from his bag and begins the diagnostic routine. All systems functional and ready for transplant scrolls across the small display of the kit. Walter then punches a few keys and the process begins. At first, he feels a prick, as the nanobots work their way into his skull. Then pain, as they slowly disconnect one neuron at a time from the head implant. The pain subsides quickly as the final pain receptors are left dangling micrometers away from the implant. Now, it is time for the nanobots to disassemble the rematerialize the implant into the kit. From this, he feels nothing, except a slight tingling sensation as the bots move to and from his head. Finally, there it is, the micro-broadcaster that he affixed to the implant. The tool, that was undetectable to security sensors due to its small size and position next to his brain stem.

His next task was to make his way through the winding belly of the administration building, up to the top floor.

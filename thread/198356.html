DNA, shifts and physical strain
For months now, she had worked on it. Was is possible? Every scientific article she came across said it wasn't. One couldn't jump the dimensions and move ones physical self between Rimor and Atlantean. Not without a complete rebuild and reset.

The last couple of months she had been torn. On one side, she had tons of work to do, both for the organization and for her private clients. She had to build a completely new database, now that the shadowlands started showing the effects it had on peoples physical and mental health. She had loads of tests to complete, and her lab in Jobe was equipped with the top notch bio scanners and ray-belts. Some of it wasn't even fully legal on Rubi-Ka, but she didn't give a damn. She wanted to be ready when Jobe opened the gates to shadowlands, and she was.

On the other side she was tired. There was no conflict anymore, no fights between Omni-Tek and the clans. Most discussions on the gridfeed debated either councils, peacetalks or discussions on technical marvels. That was all good, as a doctor, her job was to save life, not see them harmed, even if she was well equipped and capable to end life as well. No conflict meant that the lab work and the medical work was calming down. However..she had always worked best under pressure. And these days, she was getting a bit fed up with doing the odd laserplastics or the brain augmentations, the bone tissue analysis and accellerated growth jobs. It was getting...too square. She wasn't used to this. She felt somewhat useless.

Her organization loved her, as she loved them, but she felt she wasn't using her full potential, and that she should really be where she would be needed. Lately, she had read about the bio-viral attacks where both dustbrigades and some crazy woman called Midia was involved. This had sparked her interest to the extent where she had started investigation on the Atlantean/Rimor shift. Was it possible? Would the body be able to take the strain? Would the DNA completely dissolve? She had a good deal of studies to do. She wanted answers..no..needed answers. If it was possible, if there was any way..maybe she should travel to Rimor.


[Basically, I'm utterly frustrated with Atlantean. There's no conflict, there's no excitement, there's hardly any badguys and there's so many peacetalks it's nauseating. No one wants to be unfriendly, except the NLF (bless you). Maybe I should turn Lille into an evil mad doctor? Also, it seems Rimor is getting all the love from the event crew, which is a big nail in the side for me. I don't know why nothing is happening on Atlantean.. if it is, it's escaping me. I know I'm ranting. Sorry.]

No one will begrudge you a rant. Nor can we begrudge Rimor for getting their fair share of events, its long overdue.

Many characters support peace talks because that is what, in-character, their hearts and mind tell them to do. AO isn't just some Tolkien knock-off, where dwarves hate goblins because they are goblins. There has to be a compelling reason to fight, something worth killing or dying for. My characters have not found this in the rather stale Clan-Omni conflict, and I guess I am not the only one.

As for baddies, for most people playing the villain goes against the grain. We like to bond with our characters and see them bond with characters around them, all of which is even more important in a game where you can literally spend years playing the same character. We like to be the protagonists of our own stories and by the conventions of pulp fiction (which AO is) the protagonist is the good guy, and the good guy is the one who wins and gets the girl in the end.

Those few who choose to play evil, selfish characters end up having a really hard time. Try teaming, begging for buffs or even attending an ooc friend's in game wedding when your character is meant to be driven by greed, hatred, desire for revenge, power or other assorted unpleasantness. The truth is the evil character's life is a lonely one. By definition he has few friends and even his interaction with is enemies is usually limited to one gloat scene and one fight scene in the fianl reel.

The Nanomage Liberation Front is something of an exception, because, ultimately, they see themselves as the good guys and even those opposed to the NLF have to consider, if only for a second, "what if they were right, what if notum is running out?". Likewise criminal gangs can trade off the romantic image of the outlaw figure to remain likeable even what they do in practice can be quite horrible. Even then I don't think its all that easy to pull off and of all the various criminal orgs that have come and gone, DA seem to be the only one that has stood the test of time.

For all these reasons and more, in RUR created our own conflicts and baddies - something we could tailor to our needs and which would make sense for us. We created the Neo-Luddite Movement, a fanatical anti-robot group (which is actually made up of RUR alts). And of course, though we did not create the cyborgs, we created our own cyborg plots. Basically we have invested our time and energy to made something happen for us rather than sit back and wait for someone to come and entertain, or in LRP terms "monster" for, us. The $10 a month does not buy you a personal GM, ou have to work to make things happen.

There was a while back an initiative (I think spearheaded by Ammicus/Emiliy) which involved a bunch of roleplayers getting together and each creating a master villain character. It seemed like a good idea as playing a villain as your main isn't really all the practical or rewarding. As often happens though, I think the initiative ran out of steam.

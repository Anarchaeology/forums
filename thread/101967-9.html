Price formula
Good idea with a price suggestion list, Elbo, so far, my pricing has been double the price of the components (but then i've bought them also), 25-50% price of component if just combining them.

I don't know is this is the right thread, but i feel it's relevant here anyhow.

No pricing in the game is linear. There is no 1k/qual, that works all the way. Your list also suggests that in dividing prices in qual ranges.

I've studied the prices on a few things and worked out how the pricing formula works, it can be used on your prices to make a continueus (sp?) function.

The pricing in shop works like this:
Quality price (QP) * Quality pricing factor (QPF) + Base price (BP)

Saying 1k/qual would make the QPF 1, 3k/qual thus 3, and so on.

My studies show that the quality factor = Q^2 - 2*Q in percent overprice.

To get a simple multiplying factor, we adjust the result to the formula, Quality pricing factor = ( Q^2 - 2*Q )/100 + 1

This is a simple and good formula that is perfect for a script:
Script "qual" (or QPF or whatever)
/tell helpbot calc (%1*%1 - 2*%1)/100 + 1

Just do /qual 54 to get the QPF of a Q54 item.
The cost of an item/service, whatever, would then be as above.

The formula shows that (shop pricing) makes Q1 = 1, Q32 = 10, Q50 = 25, Q100 = 100, Q150 = 222, Q200 = 396 times the base price.

Linear pricing from 1 - 100 makes the Q50 (relatively speaking) very expensive.

What's the use of this?

Using good values in QP and BP you can use one formula for all items, although QP and BP is unique to each item.

Ex. Carbonum plate (if i remember correctly) has:
QP ~1,75k, BP of ~6k
(i'll update later with more price examples)

Using the same formula on, e.g. Nano Creation, a QP of 3,5k and BP 0 (as suggested price at Q100 was 3k and at 101 4k) would give:
Q10, 6k
Q25, 25k
Q50, 90k
Q100, 350k
Q125, 540k
Q150, 780k
Q175, 1mill
Q200, 1,4mill

The exact QPF and BP is a work in progress, but as a rule of thumb the Xk/qual at 100-110 is fine.
How does that idea sound?

Nano's
I think it is important to consider the price of Nano's from shops when quoting a price to a potential customer. If someone comes to me with a disk that he wants to make, but could buy in store, quoting 500k for a ql 125 will likely scare them away. I believe the nano itself would likely cost them 480k or so at a shop. I would ask 400k for such a service so that I make money, they save money. This pricing can spur positive economic activity for lower level engineers who are just developing their craft.

Were the nano to be disk only drop such as GA, i'd probably ask 5-10% of market price of the disk for the trust factor on big disks. On those nanos that drop by disk only but not quite so rare or low trading value, I charge 125% of what that ql Nano sell for in shops. So I just quote whatever i think that is typically after I round it to a nice smooth number.

Otherwise I do like the pricing schemes for higher level nanos and other tradeskill operations. Good work for putting it down on a forum and allowing discussion of the issue.

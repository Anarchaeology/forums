Random drops and Chase Items
I have mentioned some of these pts. elsewhere, but it seemed important enough to warrant its own thread.

Basically, I think that make only nanos (the grid armors, nullity sphere), should not be random drops. If you want to make them difficult to obtain, they should instead be drops from uniques, or rewards for static missions. But random drops, especially rare random drops, just breed frustration and loathing.

A good game should have two central features: it should be challenging and fun. (A good MMORP might need one more: cooperation) Challenging games reward skill, and give you a sense of accomplishment.

Now it is a good decision to put in some chase items into the game: it gives players some goal to achieve, to dream about, to plan for, and hopefully to succeed at. Since i play an NT, nullity seems like a ideal chase item -- something that would be worth striving for, even if it was extremely challenging to get.

Well, it IS extremely challenging to get in only one sense -- you can make no real plan to get it. I have spent hr upon hr area nuking grey and green mobs that regularly drop crystals. I must have literally killed thousands. The only skill this challenges I suppose is patience and perseverence. Recently, I have stopped even trying to get it, it is so dull and frustrating. So nullity, instead of being a feature that inspires and drives people to be NTs, instead becomes something that makes you want to drop both the class and the game.

The mechanic of wholly random drops is a terrible mechanic for chase items, for the items that everyone who plays the class would want. As a game designer, you have to be able to envision the play experience you are creating -- what are you asking the players to do, and what are you rewarding? Restricting availability is fine, but doing so via randomness defeats the very purposes you might have for initially incuding them.

One of the primary things that keeps mmorpgs different from traditional games is that as you advance higher and higher in level, the game usually adds more and more content to adjust to the advancement in the people playing. Everquest, despite its problems, has done a wonderful job with this. EQ has places to hunt if you are low level, but it keeps the "special" raid type of places to those that are high. It become the reward for putting work in. In AO, the only raid-style of dungeons are unfortunately for med-to-low level characters, those for whom the game still is fun and also those who should have simpler goals as leveling is still its own reward. AO needs to have more and variable high-level content in order to keep its player base happy and purposed. Please add more content for those where leveling is near meaningless (anyone over 125).

Swicky and Munch raise very relevant pts that tie nicely into my original post. As I mentioned, there seems to be a real lack of vision about what type of play experience is trying to be created. I realize that because of its premature release, the AO team has had to spend a lot of time putting out fires. But in order to sustain a stable player base, high lvl content has to be a top priority. I have talked to a lot of people who have quit the game, and at least recently, the number one complaint was not bugs, or even class imbalances. It was boredom, and a sense that the game had nothing left to offer.

A pt. that ties in with this deeply is the lack of difficulty in obtaining the best gear that the game has to offer. Blitzing and calming missions is just way too easy -- it just needs to go altogether. After you crest 100, and to a certain extent before, you can obtain anything in the game that is missionable, and without fighting a lick. Because of this, there is no economy, and none of these items hold much real excitement.

At high lvls, the game becomes deeply unchallenging. Unlike EQ, players are never forced to develop complex tactics, and/or organize to meet challenges.

Adding high lvl content, with unique loot drops and/or quests, would go a long way to solving these problems.

I have played a MUD (GemStone 3 for those who know there were MMPORPG ten years ago where reallocation was allowed once. Now, it wasn't because little nerfs left and right, it was because they did a whole revamping of the game mechanisms. Like they were using RoleMaster rules and lost there license. Were you could only get a *chance* to learn one spell per level, you could know learn two or three. Same with weapon skills. So basically, you had level 100 characters with the skills of a level 30-50... So they did allow reallocation during one week...
What happened? Well, players pulled off their spreadsheets. They quickly put down simulators, and ran about every possibilities they had. They looked at how it was possible to mini-maxing every aspect of their character. They completely ignored the skills that was only useful at lower levels but useless at higher ones. Like in AO, lots of things make life easier when you solo at low level (vehicule ground, HoT which become less useful in groups, weapons which are good at low level but not so good at high level...), but when you're forced to group at much higher level, they become less so.

Game balance was completely messed up, and still is at the moment. Monsters were balanced for these fine-tuned characters; to the point that any single mistake at low level could completely nerf you. Also, there was a whole generation of mutants : characters with such configuration that they wouldn't have been able to survive the lower levels but were completely uber at higher levels (like mages using two handed swords more efficiently than warriors, because high level spells made it viable)... Also, they could do all the trial and error using the reallocation, trying a route to level 100 in minutes, test it out in the field, rinse and repeat...

Now, if this happens in AO, this means *you* won't make mistakes... Every newbie after you who will not be able to reallocate will make them though... And face monsters tweaked to be challenging to *you*...
Also, AO is very forgiving. You don't have a limited number of time to train a given skill per level. You can catch up on a skill you never trained in in a few levels, and the IP cost will be exactly the same as someone who trained it from day 1 (AO skill cost is *not* level related! It's related to current skill level).

If we are going to bring up this argument, lets get something straight.

Skill costs are based on one thing and one thing only: class and skill. Ok, thats two but one is kindof redundant.

Lets take tutoring. Everyone has Green (base 5.0) tutoring. I doubt very many people besides newbies messing around raise it AT ALL.

So, next time you look at your char, notice the tutoring cost. Assuming you haven't raised it before (highly likely) a level 1 will spend 5 IP to go from 5-->6. Guess what? So will a level 200. Atrox enf, nano nano, it doesn't freaking matter.

Level only has bearing on HOW MUCH you can raise a skill. All skills are either 4 or 5 per level depending on class. (base 7.0 and less is 5 per level, 7.5 and above is 4 per level) Of course theres the title caps (15, 50, 100, etc 150? 190?) as well. Those title caps are also determined by color (base cost) which I believe has 2 or 3 divisors. (7.0/7.5 is one, I think theres a higher one, 12.0/12.5 don't rem offhand) The dark blue (16-20.0) seems to have a cap about 10-15% lower than a green cap. The middle cap seems about 5% lower. Its somewhat hard to tell with ability modifiers...unless you're really bored.

An atrox NT gets nanopool for 5.0, he just doesn't get as much GAIN as a nanomage for boosting NP and his level bonus isn't as much. Likewise for a Nanomage Enf. and Body Dev, 5.0. Breed does NOT affect skill cost.


There, we've cleared up two very common misconceptions that 90% of newbies seem to have, and quite a few "veterans" as well.

Overequip fix:

Item QL = True QL * ( Skill/Stat value divided by Requirement)

Bad thing, this adds a lot of extra procedures (computing power needed), as it has to be checked at debuffs, buffs running out, heck in so many different situations, for every piece of equipment...




What the simple line means in a concrete example:

You uber-equip *smirk* a ql 100 rifle with the huge amounts of buffs (+rifle, +riflegeneric, +aimedshot, eagle eye, trader). However, once all those silly buffs run out, you end up with (fictional numbers):

Item QL = True QL (100) * ( 400rifle skill / 600 rifle req) = 66QL efficiency rifle.

So instead of using stats of a QL 100 rifle, the weapon would use the stats of QL 66 rifle of same type. Same goes for armors and implants. Easy, simple, but a lot of extra computing power taken... but would be worth the computing power.

And most importantly, it would not make uber-equipping TOTALLY useless. It would actually make uber-equipping help casual gamers, muah-aha-hahaa!

How? Well, you'd only need to upgrade your equipment once in a while, since you could go high and uber-equip stuff with buff-o-mania, but your fighting statistics would ultimately give the "QL" of them. So you uber-equip, then you can play casually and ignore your equipment until you exceed the stat/skill requirements on armor/weapons/implants!

On what req to compare it, for weapons it would naturally be the skill the weapon uses, for armor a weighed average (60% dependant on higher stat and 40% dependant on lower stat), and for implants the _base ability_ score (unbuffed value), not the treatment skill of course. You start doing the checks from implants, so their +skills and +stats effect armor/weapon.

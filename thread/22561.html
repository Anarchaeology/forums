AO for Kids
Can we get most of these <expletive deleted> kids out of here?

1) Anarchy Online has a MA rating. (That's probably excessive. Neither the level of violence nor the amount of skin shown are probably enough to require above a Teen rating. Nevertheless, the MA rating is in effect.)

2) Anarchy Online requires at credit card, or at least a bank account and PayPal account, to play.

Now, if this combination isn't enough to mark Rubi-Ka as an adults-only space, one where adults can be themselves without having to offend somebody's parents, then I don't know what would be enough.

The other day, I was running through Newland City, on my way from one place to another. (Just about the only use any Clanner makes of it, of course.) As always (almost everywhere in the northern half of Rubi-Ka), there were the usual couple of strippers out in front of the Ordinary Basic Supermarket, working the crowd for tips.

As I was running by, I heard one guy yell at them to stop it - "I've got kids who play this game," and he didn't want his kids exposed to such things.

Because it's never productive to be rude, I had to choke back my instinctive response: if you bring your kids to a planet that has lacked effective government for several decades (in story), and let them hang out in the strip clubs when they're not out killing and robbing, then one of two things is true. Either you have decided that it's OK for kids to vicariously do those things. Or you are a bad parent. Period. Because we didn't volunteer to parent or babysit your kids. If you dump your kids in public "in a bad neighborhood" and expect adult total strangers to look after them and look out for them, you are a bad parent.

But like I said, it's never productive to be rude, so instead I drew him out into a discussion of what an MA rating means, and whether or not it's appropriate to have kids play them, and whether or not it's fair to the adults to require them to temper their actions, costuming, and language to the level that's appropriate for a 10 year old. His primary defense was that when his 10 year old son started playing the game was in beta, and didn't have an MA rating. Now the kid is hooked, and he can't fairly take it away from him.

That's not much of a defense, in my opinion.

But the would-be disneyfication of Anarchy Online is only one of the reasons why I want the overwhelming majority of these kids out of here.

Am I the only one who's noticed just how low the grammar and spelling skills are of the people who are complaining about the version 14.2 "overequipping nerf"? I am prepared to bet that the average age of people complaining about overequipping runs a solid 20 years younger than the average age of the people who are looking forward to 14.2.

And this makes sense to me, from their perspective. There is nothing in the world more powerless than a child. Anyone who's studied Adler knows this. By age 2, they're aware of their powerlessness, and as Adler argues, traumatized for life by it. Even if he's wrong, it's impossible to explain to a 12 year old or a 14 year old, why he or she has to obey orders from parents, teachers, cops, or any other adult. They feel like adults. They also feel invulnerable, fully educated, fully aware of their surroundings, totally trustworthy, and fully capable of anticipating the consequences of their actions. (Recent neuroscience confirms that all of those impressions are hallucinations; on the contrary, once puberty begins, they are scientifically and medically incapable of any of those things.) Yet despite their (self-perceived) adult competency, they still get treated like children.

Consequently, when you are 12 years old, there is nothing in the world more emotionally and psychologically satisfying than spending some money on eBay to illicitly buy super-high-level equipment for a multi-player game, then lie in wait for, ambush, and murder adults.

I ran into this on Diablo II, all the time. Every single compulsive player-killer I ran into on Battle.Net was demonstrably (and usually admittedly) between the ages of 10 and 15.

Finally, the least of my reasons is a cultural one. About every third kid on here uses "gay" as the generic insulting adjective. And I have way, way too many gay, lesbian, and bisexual friends to just let that blow by me. Every time, it makes me grind me teeth. Because I'm an adult and they're children, every time I force myself to calm down, and then politely ask them to stop doing that. It doesn't do any good, of course, not 99% of the time. If "these <expletive deleted> kids today" are around, this is just a piece of their culture that I have to deal with.

But of course, each time it happens, I also wonder why I'm paying money every month to be annoyed at least every other night by swarms of homophobic teenagers.

Despite Funcom having taken every precaution the law requires, and then some, parents are still dumping their miserable and annoying pre-teen and teenage yuppie-ettes into our adults-only game world. We then not only have to deal with the grief they bring, we then also have to take grief from the parents for acting like, well, like it was an adults-only game world.




Personal Note: I was 13 when I first became actively involved in science fiction fandom. This was 3 years before Star Wars came out, so SF fandom was still a pretty small community. Because it was primarily a literary community, it also ran mostly to adults, most of them college students or college educated. And very early on, I was taken aside by kind people who made sure I knew the rule: I was welcome, but only so long as I acted like an adult. If I acted like a teenager, especially like an obnoxious teenager, I would be Dealt With.

For me, this was not a problem. I was raised by parents who didn't actually believe in childhood. I was never encouraged to act childishly, even at age 4. By age 9, I already had a clear and stated preference for adult company over children my own age.

Then 1976 came along. A fun-to-watch but deeply moronic "sci-fi" movie called Star Wars came out in theaters. As I'm sure you know, it was a smash success. It also gave almost everybody the wrong impression about what science fiction was "about," namely zapguns, spaceships, and cute robots. As a consequence, science fiction fandom, as a subculture, got suddenly and massively swamped by children. Also, within a decade, the "echo boom" was well under way, and adult SF fans were bringing their own infants and young children with them into fandom - along with all the problems they brought.

Back when I got into fandom, none of this would have been tolerated. And you know what? Even for a kid, it was more fun back when everyone was either an adult, or trying hard (and mostly successfully) to pass for one.

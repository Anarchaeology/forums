How To Raid - A Little guide
I originally posted this as an answer to a post on this board, but once I started working on it and improving it, I thought I might just as well make a thread about it. I will continue to build upon this guide, adding information as I remember it and learn. This is mainly for the new guys who are curious how a raid works, how to behave, what commands to write and stuff. I hope this will help you all to join the great Raid Community

First of all, I would like to quote Sneakerplmp:
"Also whould like to add that the rules and guidelines for both raidbots you use in your examples are easily gathered up at www.schna.dk/ao Under the Guidelines section."
This is required reading folks, so spend some time reading here before you head out on the first raid!

Great! Head over to that link for concrete, correct information!

PS: This post describes Omni Bots on Rimor(RK2), but the basic is the same for every raidbot.

============================
What is a Raid and Basic Information
============================

Raiding is when a group of players gather up, use a common chatbot to communicate and execute the raid on a selected mob.
Basically, there is one major raidbot for each side(Omni and Clan) for each type of raid. There are different types of raids, but not as many as you would think. Mercraids and Tara is the two biggest raids, and they use a bot each. There is also smaller bots used for lesser things like private raids on mobs and towers.
Since I am on Rimor(Rubi-Ka 2) I can only speak for that dimension. Here are the two main bots on the Omni Side:

-Mercbot This Raidbot includes raids on: Supply Master Smug and Fred, Ian Warr and his gang in EFP and General Nirtox and her Dustbrigade. You need to be level 101 to join Mercbot(Registered 101 in the AO base). For those unsure, this is the bot you want to join if you want Azure Armor, Notum Tanks, Armed Forces armor, Reign/Pain of Patricia, Anything`s, Dust Collectors and more.

-Tarahq This raidbot includes raids only on Tarasque in Camelot. Beware, Raiding Tara very often includes PvP`ing!
You need to be level 130 to join Tarahq(I think). This is the bot you want to join if you want Living Dragon Armor, HPO, GPH and all that neat Tarastuff ^_^

Towers and Hollow Island and Unique mobs most often uses private bots, this is usually announced and spread thru guilds, and or the mentioned two bots so that people know what bots to join.

Before I start with how to raid on a typical raid, I`ll list some basic guidelines and things to remember:

�1 First of all. Make sure you read all the rules before you start raiding. You can read the rules by typing "/tell Raidbot Rules". Rules may vary from bot to bot, so the rules here should only be seen as normal guidelines, but not exact rules for any specific bot. Keep that in mind. If you are unsure about anything, ask a raidleader what to do!

�2 Always, ALWAYS follow the orders given to you! This is so important, I cannot stress this enough. Do not play hero or pull of a stunt in the raid, it will more often than not ruin the raid, and get you kicked! So follow the rules, listen to Raidleader, and everyone will be happy about another well done raid

�3 Points you have gained on one bot cannot be used on another bot

�4 Respect Raidleader. Raidleaders are nice people, but for the raid to work, its very important to listen to them and do what they say

�5 If you attend to many raids, its considered a good act to help out in Tower battles/defense/attacks. Nobody can force you to do this, but generally, if you show up on a raid, you should help out in Tower Battles if any bases needs defense. So generally, Support your raidfriends by defending their towers!

�6 You have to meet up at the location and get added to the Raidlist to get points. Only joining the chat will not give points. Also, you will still get points if you die under the raid, so dont be afraid of that

�7 Points are stored on Bot, so you will not loose your points when leaving bot or taking a long break. You can have points stored on several bots at the same time.

[b]�8 You CAN bid for another player, but only one who attended and completed that raid. You can not bid for someone who only joined chat/didnt get points/wasnt there.

�9 Last, but not least. Raids are fun! Join your friends, get the "phat lewt" and have a good time! Everyone is nervous the first time, but once you have tried once, its much much more fun and easier the second!


============================
Lets look at Mercbot(Omni, RK2) and a normal raid on Smug. In the following Example, I will be using "Mercbot" as the raidbot.
============================

1: First of all, you join a raidchannel, by typing "/tell (botname here) join" like this:

"/tell Mercbot join"

2: When you join the channel, you will get the status on whats currently going on, the "topic". Whenever there is a raid going on, its displayed in the topic. You can also check the status on the bot without joining it by typing

"/tell Mercbot topic"

3: Lets say there is a raid on Smug. First things first. If it says "CLOSED!" in the topic, its to late to join the raid. If it doesnt say closed, I`d recommend asking on the channel if its to late to join. If its not, get your yalm in gear and fly to the coords and zone descripted in the topic.

4: Once you get there, you will have to seek a Raidleader. I do not work as a raidleader myself, but they are the ones responsible for "adding you to the list" if I am right. Usually the Raidleader is all the way in front, and all you have to do is find him/her and stand infront of him/her. You will then see in the chat

"(Your nick) joined!"

Great! Regulary, you will see a link to a "Raidlist" in the chat. Click on it. If you find your name on that list, you are registered and will get points once target is killed. Then you are set to go.

5: Okay, the following is very important. ALWAYS, and everyone mean ALWAYS follow the commands given in yellow text(I am not sure if its yellow on other bots but we`ll use mercbot as example). Raidleader/tank usually give out orders there, and those are top priority. Failing to follow them will get you kicked, so stay awake Orders can be everything from "Buff up and be ready" to "STOP ATTACKING!", and are vital for a raid to work.

The fight it self, and how it usually works:
6: Okay, you are ready, tank is ready, docs are ready. What usually happens, is that the Tank starts hitting on the raid target. Just wait on yellow orders and follow them, its very simple once you see it. Here is some basic things about the battle it self:

6.1: The Tank starts first, doctors healing him/her/it. On harder mobs, healing professions(Adventurers&Martial Artists) might be required to "spam small heals". This will be announced in chat if required. Nobody else attacks at this moment, just stand still and be ready to follow next order.

6.2: After a little while, The Tank will then ask for additional damage. It often starts with "Send in the Pets!". When you see that, you can send your Pet to attack the Raidtarget.

6.3: After that, it is usually "Only Aimed Shot, Max Range!" They do mean only Aimed. Not burst, fling, full auto, so be careful with the triggerfingers Also, its important to follow the "Max Range". Always check your distance to the raidtarget, the further away you can fight, the safer it is.

6.4: After pets and aimed, its usually time for the rest of the raiders to attack. This will be announced by something like "25% melee, 50% Ranged, No Specials, Max Range". Explanation: The "%" melee/ranged means how much you should keep your Aggdef Bar, the "No Specials" means you must not use any kinds of special attacks (Brawl, Dimach, Fast Attack, Sneak Attack, Fling, Burst, Full Auto)

6.5: After a little more time, the tank will say "Full attack with Everything!!" Thats the time to just dish out all you can, but remember to keep your range, and healers should still heal tank.

7: Okay, mob dies. Basic rule number 2: Always, ALWAYS back up from the corpse when it dies. This is usually(or always said in chat) but its kinda important. That way, it will never be a question about ninjaing or anything like that. Tank usually stay with body, and checks its corpse.

Next part is about Points and bidding.

============================
Points and bidding
============================

1: Depending on the mob you kill, you will get a few points. Its about 3 points +/- for the different mobs. You will get up a message in the chat channel:

"Congratulations! Awarded 3 points!"

2: At any time, you can check how many points you got. This is done by typing:

"/tell mercbot mypoint"

3: The tank/leader then announces which item is up for "bidding". You can bid on a item by typing:

"/tell mercbot bid (points)"

One thing to remember. One bid costs 1 point. So if you have 90 points, you can only bid 89, since the last point is the "tax" for bidding.

Also, nobody can see how much you bid(Unless you win), so dont be afraid to bid any amount you want. However, remember that no matter if you win or loose, you will loose 1 point as long as you bid, so think twice before you bid. Its better to save that point to next time(and bidding higher) if you are unsure if you will win.

If you bid on an item, it will say the following in the raidchat:

(Your name) is bidding....

If you regret your bid, and want to unbid, just type:

"/tell mercbot unbid"

4: There is also a bonus systems. A Raidleader can set certain conditions/bonus for bidding. Example is Armed Forces armor, which is level 190+. Raidleader can set +10 bonus(Just an example) points to the bidders who meet the requirements. So example, there is one level 170 who bids 30 points, and one 193 who bids 26 points. the 193 wins because he gets 10 bonus points added upon his bid, so that his bid was 26+10=36 points.

Another bonus, is when a profession specific item is up for bid. The leader can/will then set a bonus for being that profession. Lets say the "Longshot" drops, which is a Agent only Rifle. Leader sets +20 bonuspoints if you are Agent.

In addition, bonus points gained by profession stacks with level. So for a profession specific item with e.g TL6 Requirement, you will have (your bid)+(Level bonus)+(Profession bonus)=Total bid point. Use this to your advantage!

5: Then its just crossing your fingers and hoping. The Raidleader will list up which players that are currently bidding on the item and how much bonuspoints that are added to their bid before ending the bidding, and thats a good time to think one more time about your bid.
If you win, you can go up to the corpse and loot your thing(always a warm, fuzzy feeling ).

6: There is also a "roll" function. Rolling is mainly used for items that isnt worth bidding on or that are very common. Examples(that you will see often) is Stetoscopic Glasses, "Pre Uni crap(as they always say ), Armed Forces Sleeves/gloves and Tokens. Once its out for rolling, you can add your roll. If there are several items you can roll on, you pick a one item by the number in front of it. Rolling does not cost points, so if you want the item, feel free to roll! Also a good way to get tokens
To roll, type following directly in the raidchannel:

"bot: add (number on item)"

When all items have been bidded and rolled on, the raid is over. They can then go directly over to another mob, by starting a new raid. Just stay tuned in the channel to see if any more raids are starting.

Whenever you want to leave, just type
"/tell mercbot leave"

============================
The Basic Commands
============================

To see the rules of the Raidbot: /tell raidbot rules

To see topic of a raidbot: /tell raidbot topic

To join a Raidbot: /tell raidbot join

To bid on an item: /tell raidbot bid X (where X is the points)

To unbid your bid: /tell raidbot unbid

To roll on an item(this is typed into the channel): bot: add X (The number of the item)

To check your points: /tell raidbot mypoint

To check earlier raids: /tell raidbot raidhistory

To check how much an item has been bidded on earlier: /tell raidbot raiditem (name on item)

To leave Raidbot: /tell raidbot leave


===================

Roughly in order:

Whatever its worth to you. You can look at the item, figure out how badly you want it, and bid accordingly. Simple.

Because while on the surface a random system is more 'fair', when you're dealing with large raids, the odds of you coming out with a piece of gear, much less a piece of gear you actually want (and then you get into arguments about who can use what, who needs what, blah blah), are very, very low. Not good at all. By bidding, people who put in time and effort are rewarded. Again, simple.

And last, how can you judge if its appealing or not until you've tried it? Login to a raid bot, join a few raids, try them out. If you like them, great, run them like missions when you're in the mood. Build up some raid points, get some cool items, see some neat fights. If you don't? No sweat, plenty of other stuff to do.

Responsability of Leadership
This is OOC, so don't try answering IC.

I've been roleplaying as leader of Mercury Dragons Clan for 14 months, first as the "father" and then with a second character which was the "son" to follow the legacy.

Now since day 1 I've made things clear to those who joined my org. You misbehave IC or OOC, you will either get me or another officer breathing down your neck. You break EULA, harrass people (after they've asked you to stop OOC, keeping in mind there are rplaying situations in which you are to harrass someone to gain something out of it), etc...you'll find yourself getting KICKED out of my org.

Now more and more I see clan/omni/neutral Players, who don't give a rat's ass about roleplaying and disturbing other Players rplaying for NO REASON. And they are in an org, and contacting his/her org leader gets NO RESULT.

What is the option?

1) Run away and try to continue rplaying elsewhere.

The idiot can follow and harrass even more.

2) Log off.

So your roleplaying is ruined, some rplaying takes planning to do, which means your time was wasted more or less.

3) You call an ARK.

They got better things to do than deal with an idiot, and the matter can stretch into days of e-mailing FC if it gets serious.

So WHERE do people who make an org stand?

Are you responsable for the actions of those YOU accepted into the org (as I assume leaders are told "so and so joined the org man" and is aware he'd got someone carrying his org's name).

There is the roster AO has, so more or less leaders KNOW who they got in their org.

Is a Leader responsable for the actions of a member? even if his org is 100% OOC, non-roleplayers...they carry a TAG, your group, your peeps, you are the main man.

Do you care or not?

I want to know since so much BS (and I don't mean Broken Shores) have been happening ruining other people's fun.

I'm asking org leaders (clan/neutral/omni) what their Point of View is on this.

I know where I stand and what I do, but I'm curious as to what others do.

I think it comes down to styles of leadership and orgs. Some leaders really see themselves as leaders, others just see the role as a service. As each org will have its own concept of leadership the main thing is not to approach an org leader with preconceptions about what they should or should not be doing .

I had the pleasure of leading the South Rubi-Ka Society for 8-9 months. I never really saw it as more than an administrative role. Conceptually we were always meant to be just a group of peers, and indeed for several months, after Lev left the org, we functioned without a leader. If some people turned out to have more influence than others that was due more to an individual's confidence and personality rather than rank.

I never did get tells about one of our members misbehaving and to be honest I haven't a clue how I would have reacted. The whole concept of having to discipline someone in a game makes my skin crawl. But of course we were always a very small guild, and by its very nature the SRS was not likely to attract troublemakers to its ranks.

I can't comment on leading an org in AO, but I have helped lead 2 guilds at the same time on different servers in DAoC for close to a year until I got tired of the game not the people and decided to come to AO. Both guilds were based from a single guild carried over from EQ, so alot have been friends for a long time.

In both guilds, if leadership was told about one of our members causing problems, that person would be spoken to, no matter how small the complain. If it was a little complaint, then one of the officers would simply mention it to the person that got the complain, just so they knew about it. Where as if it was serious, we'd go so far as removing them from our guild. The most important step in this process is the next time we saw the person that complained we'd send a simple /tell saying we've talk with the person that caused the problem and possibly give them info of how it's been decided to resolve the problem if the problem warranted solving.

The most unfortunate thing I've seen in the last year or so is a huge rise in the amount of people logging on to MMORPGs that simply don't respect anyone or anything. People that bacause they have the wall of anonymicity(not sure of spelling but meaning anonymous) that the internet provides, they think they don't have to treat people with respect.

I think no matter what the philosophy a guild hold, whether, 100% RP, 100% PvP or PvM, or some mixture in between. I feel leadership of the guild need to be partially responible for the actions of their members. Although, I also feel that it's the individual members responsibility to act both IC and OOC with the best interests of their guild in mind. With this in mind if both leadership and the individual members act respectfully to others at all times even if acting 'pushy' IC, then if any problems they hopefully will only be minor ones at best.

I do consider it the responsibility of organization leaders and officers to 'deal with' members who misbehave. This does not necessarily mean punishment, but assessing the truthfulness and seriousness of the complaint, and then taking appropriate action, is part of the job. By accepting a member into your organization, you are assuming responsibility for their behavior.

That said, removing a member is not always, or even usually, the best choice for dealing with a complaint. Every story has two sides, and generally speaking, people shouldn't be punished for honest mistakes. There is also the case of false accusation, overreaction, and exaggeration on the part of the person making the compaint.

Generally speaking I feel that organizations with high standards for accepting applicants will experience a low rate of complaints, even if 'rules of conduct' are vaguely defined. Good people don't have to be told to be good, or how to be good. On the other hand, people that are immature and/or amoral won't be instructed, or stopped, by all the rules in the world.

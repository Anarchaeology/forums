This is an ooc thread and so I will comment oocly

I believe RolePlaying should be guided by the game code and game history. For example, I honestly don't think anyone on Rubi-Ka gets the common cold, and minor wounds heal without any fuss - because everyone has nanobots that take care of them, somewhat like but to a lesser extent than the Omega from the novel.

To serve as a counter-point to Savoy, I believe that if a violent conflict can't actually be PvP'd between people, it should probably be avoided. Not entirely for the sake of realism... but because when you PvP a fight, the arbiter of the conflict's outcome is the game engine instead of people. It takes the "Cops and Robbers" aspect of "Bang!" "You missed me!" out of the picture, and yes... it does mean the more powerful fighters will usually win, if the other team isn't cleverer or better-prepared.

I don't have a problem with that. In Star Wars or in Lord of the Rings, the heroes are the under-dogs, and they try to avoid direct battles precisely because they know that they'd be crushed when going head-to-head. When they do fight they fight desperately - and their ultimate victories are won not through overpowering force, but through one, or a very few, people making an 'end-run' around the powerful evil armies and throwing the Ring into the fires of Mount Doom or the Emperor into the bottomless pit thingy.

I feel that a good rule of thumb as regards violence and such, for Roleplaying purposes, is to ask oneself, "If the suppression gas were 0% here, would I lose?"

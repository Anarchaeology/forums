Hi,
AO was designed and programmed by Funcom from the ground up. The only real similarities between the AO engine and the EQ engine is the fact that you have an avatar, and you are in a 3d world. These things are common game design concepts, and are included in MANY games.
As to gameplay similarities, EQ didn't "make" the system. It's a role playing game, and such rules are used to simulate the effects of various characters attempting to complete certain actions. If you play any MUD or MMORPG, you will find similar elements. Classes and skills are generally considered to be basic design elements in most RPGs.
As to the "considering" system, our system (Probability Of Death) is nothing like EQs. There is a basic look over that tells you what chance you MIGHT have against the enemy. Then, thru the use of various tools, you can take a closer look at the enemy, finding out more about their health/nano reserves, or their level... I think most people will find the Probability Of Death system in AO to be an interesting feature.

I've played both Asheron's Call, and Everquest. I prefer AC over EQ for a number of reasons. The reason I asked if AO used the same or similar engine as EQ is because AO appears to have more similarities to EQ than AC.

I'm extremely excited about AO because it seems to do better at the things I disliked about both games.

In EQ, it looked to me like if you were a wood elf druid, you're pretty much the same as any other wood elf druid of similar level at least as far as abilities, and usually items you can use, etc. AC is different in that it's skill based, and all checks are done using skills - not level. It's also easy to look different than any other person at least as far as clothing, etc. goes. However, there is a big tendency to play the flavor of the month. Classes are poorly implemented in AC.

Anarchy Online appears to do it right. There are classes, but I don't believe you're restricted to only certain skills. Yes, it will be easier for a Nano tech to use nano abilities but he can still use other skills... They might just cost him more.

In AC, race is really meaningless. Race picks your free weapon, and that's about it. AO does this SO much better by allowing attributes for certain races to be cheaper to raise than other ones. My gosh, this just makes so much sense.

Con'ing. In EQ, you con a monster and I think you get 5 different colors. In AC, you can see a creature level, but that's really pretty meaningless. My character at level 40 was able to kill monsters level 150. In AO, there are many different degrees of con'ing from what I've heard.

Please don't take my question as a slam on AO. I'm buying the game the day it comes out, and am anxiously awaiting Beta 4 to open. But from what I've seen - which isn't nearly as much as I'd like to have seen - AO has more similarities to EQ than AC. Not necessarily a bad thing at all. And like I mentioned, AO appears to do better than EQ in so many areas (quest generators that eliminate camping - How frikkin' cool is that?)

I'm hoping to convince as many people from my AC group to come check this game out. I've already added a message board, and some people are interested, but I had to correct someone that said "It's EQ in the future". I had to smack him around a bit.

Thanks for the quick response, Karma.

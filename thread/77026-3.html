The way it generally works in 150+ teams, is that IF AND ONLY IF a certain player has the ability to loot chests without grossly interfering with the pace of the team, then:
1. Any loot that is not contested that looter (ninja) gets to keep
2. Professions have the priority for their own equipment
3. If only one person asks for something, it is given
4. If more than one person want something it is rolled for. In the case of a VERY rare item that all 6 people want, some groups decide to sell the item in exchange and split the cash.

I myself try and loot as much as possible without interfering with the team speed. A good rule of thumb is to position yourself beside the chest as soon as you run into the room, and unlock the chest when the last mob in the room has 5-15% HP left. That way the chest will only open when the mob has just died. This guarantees that if a rat does show up, it will not stack on the other mobs, and you have time to get the stuff while moving out of the room. With some practice, it is possible to loot all chests and not fall behind at all.

playstyles
It boils down to play styles, regardless of levels.
Role-players want to act 'in-character', so looting may or may not be important to them but getting through the mission ASAP is not their priority.
Explorers want to find new places and loot and monsters, and they don't like missions much. They generally don't give two hoots about going fast through a mission.
Socializers are there for the group experience and will go for whatever the team wants to do.
Credit/Item Achievers want loot. Phat loot. Lots of Phat Loot to sell for credits or other items. They will want to loot everything off the mobs and chests.
Powergamer Achievers want to get as much xp as possible in the shortest amount of time. the goal here is to "win" by getting to 200 in the most efficient way possible. Looting is what they do after they "win".

Most players can work together as a team, but powergamers don't seem to play well with the other groups. They will ditch you if you loot and will kick you if you are not moving fast enough. My advice is that power gamers identify themselves as such as in "I prefer to level as fast as possible and I don't want to stop for looting." If that is ok, then fine. But Yazule is right that you must tell people up front how you personally play or else there will be problems. Going passive-agressive in the middle of a mission and demanding a roll when you don't want the item is just dumb. If you don't loot, don't expect to roll. Rolls should not be a penalty for people who do not share your playstyle.
Here is my rule of thumb (and apologies to those who have heard me say this several times before on the boards): If your tank is fighting you should not be looting. The tank must set the pace and hold aggro, that is the 2 main functions of a tank. The tank (regardless of level) should consider the group in setting the pace. Charging off to kill the next mob is NOT acceptable if there are looters on your team. Kill, heal, loot, repeat. That is the rhythm that a good team needs. If 150's and above feel the need to change this, announce it ahead of time and the team will either live with it or not. It is NOT about competence; it is simply a matter of style.

Hmmm...
I usually like to prepare well for things, and before I started to actually play the game I read a lot of newbie teaming guides.
Basically the guides can be shortened down to this:

"Your team members will expect you to have the latest nanos, the best implants, the most modern weapons and the toughest armor, and if you don't, you'll be kicked before you can say ooops. Need money for it all? Oh, spend a couple of months earning it with a mule..."

Needless to say, I wasn't very eager to team in the beginning, but reality proved to be different.

I'm slowly rising in the ranks of the great Clans, but so far I've been quite lucky in my teams. We may all make our mistakes, but usually we just laugh at them. The MA I've been teaming with the most had no idea what a crit buff was (even though he had the level to use it by far), and it took me a long time to find out that adjusting the aggro bar actually helped my own doc heal way faster

I can understand that you expect lvl 100+ players to know the basic things, but for us newbies, lowbies (and perma-newbies like me ) it's a bit hard to figure out.

I can understand your frustration, but since you all seem to know how the game should be played (or at least how it should *not* be played), why don't you join forces and write a short, yet comprehensible guide to teaming? For lowbies and grandmasters alike...

Yes, I've read the teaming guide from FC, but do try to tell new players how to actually *play* their class in a team.

It's way too easy just to say that every player should have the latest nano for their class and blah blah. And even easier to patronize and say do this in that way, or your team mates will kick you before you can say ooops...

Make a short guide for each class and compile them in a mini-compendium. Write TIPS and HINTS instead of just orders. And for God's sake skip all the "remember to have the most updated gear or your team mates will..."-crap.

What I imagine could be something like:

---
Doctor:
Team Window:
Always remember that the team window is your friend. It will tell you what team members who need your attention in a glance and at the same time enable you to select them with an easy click. You can, however not heal them unless they're in your Line of Sight (LoS) - yet another reason for a team to stay close together.
Aggro:
All healing of teammate while in a fight draws aggro - especially team heals. The same is, of course, the same with damage dealt to mobs. It's a balance to heal your team mates (and yourself) and still not get the aggro.
You can avoid a lot of aggro by not fighting the monsters, but most importantly your team mates can help you. The fighting classes all have access to aggro-drawing nanos, and though often ignored (sadly), there are efficient aggro-drawing devices out there (Aggo Enhancer and Da Taunter).
If you get aggroed, do what you can to get it off you. Don't fight the monster and put your aggro-bar to full def. for the increased defense bonuses. As soon as the aggro wears off, put it to full aggro again. That increases the execution speed of your healing macros vastly and contrary to comme belief, it won't add more monsters on to you (if you don't fight them, that is).
Damage:
Some teams require the doctor to fight, some prefer a doc that considers healing his most important task. The latter is quite reasonable in my eyes, btw...
Many doctors use shotguns for their high crits and the fact hat they're one-skill-dependant, but some ignore weapons altogther. It's up to you (and your teams). Just remember that you can't use your nano rechargers while fighting.
Doctors also have a nano line, that deals Damage over Time(DoT). Aagain: some use them, some do not. In general they're not considered effective enough to use at lower levels.
Your Team Mates:
Some players are really hysteric aboit their health and will start screaming for help when their health goes below 70%. Others reason that when you're low on hp, it might be time to start trying to fix the situation instead of using time writing "helpp dovc!!!". If they even have a "Help, Doc!" macro, they either have 1) met a lot of bad doctors 2) the impression that they are the most (only?) important member of the team...
On the question of the doctor sacrificing himself, not all players agree. For some reason some players think it's the doctor's noble duty to keep healing his team mates even if it costs him his own life and will scoff at you if you use precious time and mana to heal yourself. There must be a point somewhere, but before you choose the path of the martyr, ask yourself how long the team will survive after you're dead...
---
More should be added, but I think I would have found this very helpful when I started out...

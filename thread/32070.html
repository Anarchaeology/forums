Rompa Bar Impressions
Amazed, stunned, intrigued. Those were the words that described Ehrynns second visit to the Rompa Bar. Her first visit to the establishment had been unremarkable. It was quiet and easy going. If it hadnt been for a chance meeting with Racius, a member of an elite Omni-Pol faction with a sympathetic ear, she might not have given the place a second thought.

Last night was different, Rompa was bursting with activity. Dancers, waitresses, customers, security, and a remarkable woman named Aktrez. The wait staff was efficient, solicitous of customer needs, and exceedingly polite. The dancers, well they were energetic and seemed to double as additional help for the wait staff or it might be the other way around. Ehrynn had never seen a spot in Omni quite so lively and engaging. However, she heard some speak of a place equally exciting in the neutral area, called Reets.

Through casual conversation, Ehrynn learned that Aktrez was the director of Vixen Entertainment and that the costumed dancers and wait staff were from her organization. While taking in the atmosphere of the place, Ehrynn discovered she was a bit in awe of some of the dancer's ability. Although she could not see herself jumping up onto a table to dance, it was fun to watch them and imagine what it must be like to be up there.


OOC: my compliments to Aktrez and the Vixen entertainment staff. It was quite the roleplay experience to see a nightspot alive and kicking with PCs running the show. Your wait staff is well trained and the dancers are fluid. I was especially impressed with Alun and Kinkajo (hope I got thier names right.)

trainwreck:

You can't have it both ways - are you debating IC or OOC? First, you say that no one can own the bars because no one has the right to (or power to) as a player. Now, you are saying that these GMs can own them (or manage, whatever) the clubs. Now, if that's true, and a GM grants a guild the "right" to run a club, then that should be fine with you. I don't know if Aktrez's guild has an arrangement with a GM to run Rompas or not, but I do know that they do a great job of bringing some atmosphere into the bar and keep it from being a place like the Bronto Burgers where people spam for buffs and trades all day long.

If a player who is not in Aktrez's guild goes into the bar to work the place, then that player is rping. Aktrez, as the one who "runs the bar," should be looking out for her employee's best interests and the business of the bar by preventing renegade drifters from just walking into the place and working over the place. So, by asking them to either join her union (guild) or not work the place is very much in context of rping which is what the other player was doing in the first place. At that point, the other player has the choice of either joining the union, staying and patronizing the bar, or leaving to pursue something else (perhaps try another bar?).

Is this fair? I think so. Aktrez and her guild consistently work Rompas and do a great job of it - they put the time in. Obviously, there are a limited number of clubs so the question you need to ask yourself is, "Do I want good, consistent rpers staffing the clubs or drifters who wander in with female alts that they made for the specific purpose of milking tips and will be gone as soon as they've soaked up what they need?" By pressuring the drifters into either joining the guild or to stop working the clubs, a higher standard is maintained and the clubs are more fun for everyone.

Obviously, none of this is enforcable, but if the drifter came in to rp, then this sort of confrontation offers lots of possibilities. At that point, there could be an opportunity to join a good guild, an ensuing argument about strongarming, a turf takeover by the drifter's guild, etc.

Franchises
Frankly, I don't see a problem with a guild willing to dedicate their time to making a static and sterile bar come to life owning the franchise to the place.

Of course, such success generates envy and competition. Which seems to be what is happening. Once a customer base for such an effort is established, competitors will want a cut. But there would have been no customer base to compete for if the original guild hadn't gone way out of their way to make it happen.

Does, that justify ownership? In my book, the answer is YES!

And there is an alternative, even with the limited number of bars. There are plenty of level 1 static dungeons in Omni Trade. Clear one out and have bouncers to keep it clear. Staff it, stock drinks and food on mule characters, and build a customer base from scratch. Advertise it as a 'private club'. There are plenty of good room layouts in missions to fit just about any need. It might not be perfect and the background music may be lacking. So run in window mode and get real player up and listen to Gridstream Productions. And party on, dude!!

trade skills...
Trade skills. you hear things like 'trade skills are broken' and 'trade skills suck!' when you mention the name. Trade skills aren't broken, they work perfectly fine - you can build guns or anything with no problem now with the new tradeskill shops. I just see two major flaws:

1. Value of weapons made. I have only seen two or three unique tradeskill-only weapons. Two are rifles, the others are melee weapons. One of the rifles cannot be made, the other, well, sucks. I haven't tried the melee weapons. If you want trade skills to be commonly used, you need to make lots more unique and powerful weapons. You could also put a makers mark on them like UO, and a variation of the quality of the guns dependant on skill, like in UO. This would make tradeskills much more viable, profitable, etc.

2. Tradeskills should mean 'I dont have to make a single kill to advance in game.' In UO (which was the best system yet) you could start a blacksmith, and never have to even wield a single weapon or ever kill a single soul and still get rich and famous. In that game, if you chose to start with trade skills, you would start with tools and resources for using those skills. If you chose to be a miner, you would start with a pickaxe, a blacksmith, you would start with a hammer and some metal. In this game, you start with nothing except a couple of nanos and a weapon. That means you must kill in order to start using trade skills - the only way to make money in the beginning is to do killing missions or by killing monsters. This makes it so that no one would even think about tradeskill until amassing a good fortune, unless, of course, they were a twink in a twinking guild... My solution to this would either be to give tradeskill related classes (engineers, fixers, traders) enough parts to begin a tradeskill career, or to give them tools to build those resources. This way, trade skills would actually be an option for lower level characters, and a way to escape the killing/mission/leveling treadmill.

Many people have mentioned that trade skills need to give more exp - but, if you have counted, a level 30 gun built gives you about 1800 exp, which is more than twice what a kill that level would give you. I wouldn't mind if more exp were given, but I don't think it's necessary.

People also have been requesting missions involving tradeskills, like building a certain item to give to an npc to get the reward. This is a good idea too, but I shant go into more detail, as I'm sure you've read thousands of posts involving that...

Thx for your time.

A friend of mine once purchased a Chevrolet Camero. After about a week, his new car did not perform as well as the dealer had promised. The gas mileage was somwhat less than advertised and the dealer quickly corrected the problem.

Another aquaintance of mine purchased a brand new home. The roof leeked right at the junction of the west wall and the eaves. After several attempts to have the builders replace or repair the building, this aquaintance sought legal counsel and was quickly awarded full reimbursement for the home, moving expensives, and future relocation.

Today, in MMORPGs, it appears that "persistant worlds" equates to "buggier than can be imagined" or "a work in progress". In either case, the content of the actual game falls far short of the marketed "features" in either technical, playable, or even existance concerns. "We're working diligently on it" is not what is on the box I purchased. Since when this this attitude become acceptable - or even legal?

Trade skills, among a myriad of other issues, simply do not work as advertised in any perspective. Let's suppose for a moment, using the SMG kit as an example, that one could argue the point that SMGs were never advertised as possible for the tradeskills. That would be fine. But to leave them out for purchase with all, or most, of the materials when they are known to be buggy, not functioning, or "a work in progress" is simply wrong.

If there are components of the game that are not working, then fix them or remove them. But please do not ask the world to pay for these "features" when they do not bloody exist. Use the test server or other testing models prior to release on subscriber systems. Of course there will be bugs on even the most heavily tested systems, but roll those features back to test until they are finished. Otherwise, it appears to a great extent, that Funcom (and many others) are simply milking subscribers with marketing bait and them spending a great deal of support-related overhead costs trying to put out the fires.

NOTE: I am not angry or upset. I am only presenting a perspective that perhaps some have not considered. I am not a power-gamer and do not camp games forever. I play for entertainment and that's about it. I never participate on these boards for many reasons (mostly it spoils the surprises sometimes - hehe) but did so today in hopes that Funcom might lead the pack in producing solid releases - something that they are not doing now and something that really nobody is trying to do anymore.... /me sighs

I agree that it would be very cool to pursue the trades without ever killing anything at all. Good concept and it fits right in with the environment (nano-based items, etc.).

But where I loose sight of the logic is when a particular item or feature can be purchased with full knowledge that it does not work. This just boggles my mind. If SMGs (or whatever) are not currently "implemented" then "un-implement" the buying of the base kits and recipe books until they have been tested and verified prior to implementation on paying subscriber systems. That's just common business.

Now I know the EULA states that we have agreed to pay for the game in it's "AS IS" state. But "AS IS" indicates that there may be some problems with the game that Funcom is not currently aware of. "AS IS" does not mean that we agree to pay for things that are known to be faulty and/or contradictory to any advertisements.

Again, all I'm trying to say here is just pull the SMG kits out of the booths until they are fixed. I use the SMG kits as an example but know there are several other situations even outside of the tradeskills. Why let people waste time with bugged systems that are known to exist? Legal or not, it's just wrong.

Going back to the first part of this post, I think the ability to do tradeskills without having to kill anything is a great idea and one I would love to see. Implementing it may be some work but here are a few ideas...

-- each item made could yield the appropriate XP based upon that item's quality level and it's difference to the level of the person creating it. A QL 20 pistol might give max XP to a level 15 player, about the same as a green MOB's XP to a level 25 player, and 1 XP for a level 30 or higher (sort of a "con" system). Most of this is in place and it should not be a problem to adjust the numbers a bit. Essentially, keep the rate of progress the same as killing MOBs. There are arguments for and against this idea.

-- booths should be placed in certain areas where you can sell your stuff on "consignment". Instead of sitting and dropping creations on the chat window, you could place the item in the booth and set a price (a contact name should be in the info window with who made the item in case someone wants to haggle price). When you log in a few days later, you might get the message that your item was sold and you recieved xxx credits.

-- the items themselves should be ones that are commonly dropped, quested for, or tripped over. But they should contain a certain special advantage that attracts others to buy them. One advantage might be attaching some already existing nano-clusters (1 of each) just like an implant. This could yield some very cool stat bonuses and literally make for some great combinations if any cluster could be used.

Anyway, sorry for the book here. I hope FC can control the bugs wisely by not making the buggy features available to paying subscribers. AS IS or not, there is simply no reason to sell SMG kits (and others) especially when they are known to not be working. "We're working on it" is only heard when the subscriber has already suffered. Support costs go higher than needed that way.

Keep at it though - the game has great potential, but potential doesn't pay the bills. hehe.

First off, I would like to say that the "leave us alone" post was just something half-ironic I wrote in a moment of frustration over the anti-tweaking side's lack of depth to their statements. ;)

In fact, your argument is one of the only arguments from the anti-tweaking faction I have heard that is based on some degree of sense. Unlike all the others who think that it is unfair that people who worked hard with their character is more powerful than those who didn't, you are saying something else, namely that all players MUST have the same CHANCE of becoming good, and that I can totally agree with. Moving on the second part of your statement, you're saying that this is not the case, and you're basing that upon a comparision between how a soldier-type character and a nano-type character works.. so let's just analyze that.


You are saying that the game is unbalanced because as a Doc you can never cast a buff without meeting its requirements, and that an Enforcer can keep using an attached weapon even after a buff runs out. This is true. Can you however, from this alone, derive a conclusion that there is an imbalance between Doctors and Enforcers?

Not at all. An important realization is that in order to compare two classes to see how they are balanced, you need to compare them when they are both on the same level, and in their respective ways maximized as far as weaponry, skills, nanos, armor and other aspects goes. Remember that different professions fundementally work in different ways. The enforcer may have the option of using weapons way above his level, that the Doctor doesn't have, but then again the Doctor might have access to an arsenal of nano programs that the Enforcer doesn't.

IF, through thorough testing in the way described above, one profession would turn out to be more powerful than another, it would be reasonable to enter a new discussion, focused on how to even out this imbalance. In such a case, the possible solutions would be many, but basically this will traditionally be about nerfing the one or boosting the other, in a way that makes their different skills meet, balance-wise. One of those ways, in certain cases, might be to limit certain buffs or enhancements that makes over-equipping possible, yes. All this is, however, another debate.
Because we have now moved onto the true debate of balancing, which is a debate that should be centered around the results after measuring and testing and comparing various types of characters and settings. That is really Funcom's job, and something that initially would be to assume that they did as thoroughly as possible when making the game.

I am willing to listen to any opinions on the game's balance. However, it makes no sense in itself to simply suggest that the game is unbalanced based exclusively on the fact that certain character classes are designed with a higher "tweakability" than others, and to use this as as an argument to even out the professions' tweakability. In fact, if Funcom really did their job properly when balancing the classes, reducing certain classes' tweakability would create an imbalance.

WE CAN ONLY ASSUME, for general debate's matter, that Funcom has done their work and compared the different classes to eachother, in their ultimate state, on a broad range of different levels, and done their best to balance them to eachother. How well they really did this though, is another question. And of course I know that it is impossible to ever get the balance 100% right for any kind of game like this, the goal will in fact forever be to try and come as close to that as possible. I am sure there are a hundred good ideas out there as to how FC could balance the classes better, but the point is that all that doesn't really belong to this discussion. Because what this discussion is about is whether over-equipping is a general problem or not, and whether it should be nerfed. And I have still not seen any logically valid proofs that that is the case. The best thing I have seen so far is a suggestion that not all character classes are 100% balanced to eachother, and in fact I will suspect that to be the truth, but as I said, a) you cannot derive that conclusion from a lack of material, b) it may have a broad range of solutions whereof nerfing tweakability is only one out of many and c) it's another discussion.

Final Answer
There seems to be lots of disucssion but much is wide of the mark, here - the term overequiping refers to using equipment above your lvl. That was designed in the game as you can all see, there is nothing wrong with that - the game was deisgned to be played that way. IF you wish to spend the time, effort and points you can use equipment higher than your lvl, and it was SUPPOSED to work that way.

The debate is not if overequiping is good or bad, but how to RESTORE the balance designed into the game that has been disrupted by players using an EXPLOIT (not something that was supposed to happen). As a result, the game balance is changed by some players who have used this bug to achive what they should not have been able to achive within the structure of the game.

The real debate is HOW to restore the game balance that has been disrupted by some players. The best and least damaging suggestion to date is a one time de-equip. Anyone who has built up their character within the designs of the game will be able to fully re-equip. Yes it will take time and be inconvient, but I really think that everyone has overequiped a bit - so it really will impact all of us.
Those who have exploited will not be able to re-equip to the lvl that they were before, and the balace will have been restored.

To those that claim that this is unfair, it's not, using an exploit that is no longer available is unfair - the game is ment to give EVERYONE the SAME chance, those that are better players or are clever and can use the resources of the game to their advantage deserve the bonuses that they recieve. The goal shoul not be to make everyone equal, just have the same chance, what you do with that determines how well you do.

The de-equip is also the best because it has a minimal impact on the game mechanics and will not disrupt the over game design, every time there is a adjustment to the game design you run the risk of seriously disrupting the game balance and leaving someone on the short side. Also remember this is a dynamic game, not a static one, we all knew that changes would be made to the system, hopefully with the improvment of the game. That's one of the things that makes it fun.

Final Answer:
The one time de-equip will restore the inbalance created by the bug and allow everyone back to equal footing, either that or put the bug back in and let everyone use it, that would also create a lvl playing field.

The true but smart a** answer to your question, "why do you play AO?", is... we play for "fun".

I suppose on the one hand we can look at this type of game related feelings and say, "well we had some great times and made memories and used our real life free time having fun playing a game". Therefor we should not really feel that bad when a game starts to come to an end.

Maybe as we all play more games, (Cus let's face it, once you have played a game like this or UO or EQ, there isn't any going back to TV or video games for entertainment.) we will get more accustomed to leaving them behind and moving on to the next one that excites us, without so much hard feelings.

I don't know. I'm just rambling this morning before I have drank enough coffee.

What a bargain
I agree with Swavena. I think we should all do a cost benefit analysis on these games. You play $40 for the game and get the first month of server time for free. It's the same amount you play for any other game and it's a month's entertainment. You can't really say that about many games now. I finished Max Payne in a couple of weeks. Same with Jedi Knight II.

After the first month, you pay 12.95 and get another month's worth of entertainment. Seems like a bargain to me. Other games are giving us less and less for our dollar while AO and others like it are expected to deliver more for a quarter of the price.

After awhile it's going to get boring. If your lvl 200 and not bored with this game there must be something wrong with you. Do anything for that long, I don't care what it is, and you'll lose the original excitement it brought you. Never the less, by that point you've gotten hundreds of hours of fun and frolic. All for the price of a movie and a coke. What other form of entertainment gives you such a high return for such a modest investment?

I'm still new to AO, so I play for many of the reasons you played. Now, I am not a newbie to online games. I played EQ for 2 years, and DAoC since release. My feelings about this type of games have changed considerably since my days in EQ.

I was very addicted to EQ, like probably most people were/are. So addicted that I simply couldn't imagine a life without it. I was thinking that I would keep my characters forever... I did not ignore RL because I couldn't afford to - I have a child and a serious job. That stopped me from being able to do everything, like camping, long raids, etc. in the game, which in turn, made it so that I never felt I could get to the end game, meaning the day when I said "Man I have done everything, now what?".

But it came gradually. I found it more an obligation to log in than enjoyment. I was more worried about my friends wonder what happened to me, or why I was not helping with this person or that person's epic,...than about whether or not I would have fun. I tried just about everything to boost my interest to stay in the game, including everything and more of what the previous post listed. And yet, it simply wasn't working out.

DAoC came just in time. I bought a copy and was just toying with it. The new look, the new world, and the complete new feel brought back the fascinating aspects of MMORPG. The game mechanism sucked but that is a different story. What matters is that it is NEW. So we (I and my husband) bid goodbye to our bodies in EQ and started to play DAoC seriously, formed a guild (very good one), did just about everything that one could do in the game, got to 50, and got bored. That last 3 - 4 months. Then it is no longer NEW.

We were at a loss as to what to do. We struggled for entertainment. TV is no longer the same. Books are no longer the same.

We finally settled on AO. That was about two months ago.

And I observe a change in myself. I like AO. I agree it is a fascinating game. If It were the very first MMORPG in this scale, I have no doubt that it would have been as addicting as Everquest.

But 2 games later, an MMORPG of this scale no longer has the intense appeal and hooking power over me.

You know what? I think it is good. This is how entertainment should be like: Nothing you should not be able to do without but when you have it, it feels kind of nice. When you cannot live without it, something is wrong.

So, it's not the game's fault, neither is it the players' fault. It's just human nature.

I think it will soon feel normal to everyone to move from game to game, just like you used to move from PC game to PC game. I'll congratulate the first company who can come up with a game that ties players in forever. Maybe if they inject drugs to your brain when you play

My advice: Let go and enjoy life!

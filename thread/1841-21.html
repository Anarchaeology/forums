Just because something is possible within the game mechanics doesn't mean that it's not an exploit... That's basically what the word is for, isn't it? Defining problems that aren't really bugs, but are more like design errors?

MMORPGs often require the players to observe the 'spirit' of the game, after all: the trivial example of this is reporting that duping bug instead of making insane amounts of cash out of it, but there doesn't even have to be a coding error present for something to be considered an exploit. Anyone remember 'kiting' in UO? A simple procedure involving pulling insanely dangerous monsters into crowds of people and then hiding until everyone else was dead, then looting their corpses. Perfectly viable under the game mechanics, since it was possible to run from monsters, possible to hide from them if their LOS was broken, and possible for the monsters to target other people if their original opponent escaped. The catch was that this could be done in the non-PvP area, where the puller could not be attacked, forced out of hiding, or even damaged in any way by his victims. Thus, it was a clear case of exploiting the game mechanics to achieve something that was never intended. Hence, it was an exploit, and a banning offence at that.

This, you see, is the counter-argument to all these claims of 'It's possible, so it must be intentional'. Overequipping is possible, yes, but I don't believe it was ever intentional. It's not a bug in the code, it's an oversight in the design, and although Funcom seem to have been content to leave it alone so far, as many people seem to like it, it has finally gone out of control. Overequipping is the reason for the insane power of the mobs. It's the reason why so many people are now unable to do even 30% difficulty missions. It's the reason for all those ridiculous bandaid 'fixes' to PvP. Quite frankly, it's destroying the game.

Something must be done.

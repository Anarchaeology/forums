Figures, the redheaded Martial Artist whispered as she took in the reply from her wrist communicator. Always the tight ass she mumbled as she made her way through the west gate of Tir, staring at the Sentinels sign that was firmly riveted to the gate.

What the hell is that supposed to be a bull skull? she wondered as she passed through the gate.

The west side of Tir was quiet today, no one within her vision was starting teams to assist the clans, and there wasnt any yelling in the streets from people looking for friendly Programs, it was almost serene. The buzzing of the transports were an ever present comfort sound that let Munkie know that everything was still Ok in the city. She watched one vehicle in particular that nearly clipped a half out of control pilot that was flying to high in his burnt orange Yalmaha XL. Mesmerized and infatuated she was staring intently at the close call in the sky; suddenly she was startled back to reality by an unexpected shoulder tap.

A large Atrox adorned in a white robe was gawking at Munkie, to the point that she could almost read the expression on his face through the Worn Metaplast Armor Helmet. He stared for a moment at her, almost daring her to ask a question, however before words could slip through her lips, he pointed upwards, and uttered Moon.

Munkie, following his lead looked in the sky, almost believing that she might find the moon at a time in the day that this would not be possible. As she had suspected, all she found was a clear sky, void of anything resembling the moon. The Martial Artist turned her vision to the Atrox and shrugged, Moon, I do not see the moon my friend.

Again he pointed skyward, Moon.

Munkie, now in the mode of placating the stranger looked once again, still unable to find the moon. Her face contorted almost trying to verbalize the thoughts of confusion through facial expressions.

The kind Atrox, still showing no level of frustration at the communication breakdown, continued. He motioned, Follow me? Munkie, in a mood of indifference, was not on her way to anywhere in particular, so she followed, happy to accept a diversion. The Atrox pointed at the closest door he could find, which was perpendicular to a tan awning that moved with rhythmic motion in the gentle breeze.

How wuld I go tru da door, his first full sentence came out, in a simple manner that was both calming and soothing to Munkies ears.

At the Atroxs question she set in to motion to walk through the door, and with a woosh it opened and allowed entry in to the small room. The rooms stucco walls were bathed in the green glow of the incandescent lighting, with the far wall housing a vent that gave a low pitched humming noise. In the rooms entry way was a small counter with an orange light above it; apparently this was used for a hostess in this abandoned restaurant. Two tables completed the room; with each owning a scattering of chairs, and an individual spotlight that beamed down on their respective dirty tablecloths, from recessed portals in the ceiling.

The door once again wooshed behind Munkie. She turned to the Atrox, and with a that-was-easy tone, said, See, nothing to it hon, ya just walk through and it opens, Munkie smiled now convinced she solved the second part of the Atroxs riddle.

No, no me not stoopit, me know how door works, how you go through if not open? He asked still with no hint of agitation.

Munkie was stupefied, now she had to think, why was he asking this of her, what was he getting at, and further, why her and why today? Why, why why, did someone put him up to this, perhaps it was some kind of ambush. Against her better sense she decided to go a bit further.

She repeated his last statement, So, you are asking me how would someone get through a door if it would not open for them? He nodded as if he was now looking at her as the stupid one of the tandem.

A key? She said almost questioning her own answer.

Yuh, a key, gud, now follow please, his voice trailed as he once again went through the door with the slim red head in tow.

Now remembu wut you lern in der, he asked.

A key she replied again, almost expecting him to blurt out Wrong stoopid Red Head, however he didnt fulfill this fleeting thought.

Gud, now luk, he pointed up again and said, Moon.

Munkie was feeling a bit crisp in the mind, and stared for what she hoped was the last time up in to the cloudless sky, praying that the moon would miraculously appear so the pain might end.

And then it hit her, she felt at once relieved and as if her IQ dropped 100 points from the beginning of the exchange. She cast her eyes at his helmet, smirked, and indulged the large being.

Moon  key Moonkey Munkie, she couldnt hold back the smile, yet for reasons unknown to her tears, started to well up in her deep green eyes.

You not to bright for Solitus, he said with a caring, comedic tone.

Yeah my friend, I suppose you are right, I am not to smart at all

With this, Munkie knew what had to be done.

Ervin awoke to the sound of chattering beeps, grinding noises, and a gun muzzle nestled up against his cheek. For any other person, it might be an alarming way to start the day; for him, it was business as usual.

Or so he thought.

A loud, metallic voice grated out slowly, "Message for you, chubby. Quite a few of 'em, actually...and more'n a few pertain to that little 'subject' you had spoken with me about."

That got his attention. Swinging his legs out from under the covers, he rolled over, shoving the gun muzzle out of his face perturbedly. "This better be worth waking me early," he rasped out sleepily.

The normally-mouthy droid hesitated a moment in reply, an uncommon occurance for him. Ervin crooked an eyebrow, and the only reply was a grinding, "See for yourself."

The robot had already plugged himself into the datajack port, another bad sign; normally, he resented being reminded as to just how much machine he was. Running a hand through rapidly-thinning hair, Ervin finally kicked up to his feet, fought off the last of his drowsiness, and had a seat at the screen.

"Not now," he murmured as he thumbed through the built-up comms. "Why now?"

There were two bad aspects to the messages, lurking below the surface. The first was, obviously, that they aroused far more questions than they answered. The second was the sheer irony of the question that seemed to be on everyone's lips, these days.

"'Are you a hawk or a dove?' Anyone can be either or even both, given the circumstances." The words were murmured aloud, without concious thought.

The robot cocked a mechanical head to the side, then finally spoke up. "You see why I felt you needed to see this?" He was strikingly formal, now; another bad sign. Too many bad signs for this hour of the morning.

"Oui," was the only reply from Ervin.

The giant robot tilted his head back straight. "So what do we do now, boss?"

Thinking a bit, the chubby man scratched his head. "We watch, and we wait. And we make sure we keep the doves out of the maelstorm that seems to be coming to sweep us away."

The giant claw on the droid's right arm flexed; he crouched, just a bit, more at an eye level with the rotund engineer. "And if these 'hawks' continue to push things?"

"Then we see how these hawks react when they find that they've roused a sleeping bear to protect its den."

That old, steely light was back in his eyes; flames of determination danced within the depths of those orbs. It was a look the robot hadn't seen in some time.

He would have smiled wolfishly...if he were able.

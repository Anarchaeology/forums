Tongue tied-and-twisted, just an earthbound misfit, I...
The evening air had an almost crisp feel to it, the stars emerging one by one as the last vestiges of light receded. A reet, confused by the street lamps, landed in a nearby tree, and started singing exuberantly. After a moment, it realized its mistake, and flew away.

She watched it disappear beyond the buildings, and smiled to herself. In a moment, she was going to follow its path, or at the very least, try to clear those high-rises...

She turned her attention to her aircraft, gleaming a dull silver in the artifical light. It had been her wedding gift from her husband, and several of his AF colleagues. The first time she'd looked at it, she'd been terrified almost beyond speech. A few weeks and many flights later, she'd come to adore her little yalm. She'd christened it "Little Wing", after the song by the ancient bard Jimi.

She walked around the outside, doing the pre-flight, just as Edwin had instructed her. Everything looked good, and there was plenty of fuel. She climbed into the craft, and pressed a button.

There was a cool rush of air as the climate control came on, and a soft voice spoke from one of the monitors, "Good evening, Kiyoko. This is your automated assistant. Please make your selection now".

Whoever had come up with this program was a genius, Kiyoko thought to herself. While Edwin was busy working, she had been able to teach herself some of the finer points of flying. Using this computerized assistant to monitor and make corrections, she'd learned to make smoother landings. And it made the whole idea of flying less intimidating...

Of course, the program wasn't foolproof. She made a face as she remembered the long scratch and huge dent, when she'd tried to clear a tree several days earlier. Silly tree, she still could have sworn the thing had lifted its branches on purpose. Good thing the craft was under warranty and heavily insured. Those Yalm mechanics cheerfully and graciously repaired it. It almost looked better than it had in the showroom...

But, come to think of it, her landings could definitely be improved. "I think I'll practice touch-and-gos", she murmured to the computer. The soft voice responded "Excellent choice. Enjoy your flight."

She flipped a switch and the engines roared to life. Pressing another button, the canopy closed slowly over her head. She slowly maneuvered the aircraft around, letting it hover for a moment, as she waited for the Tower to clear her for takeoff. That was another thing she hadn't ever realized - outside of the city, you were supposed to get clearance for takeoffs and landings. After almost colliding with another aircraft, it had taken a lot of charming smiles and heartfelt apologies to get herself out of hot water...

Ah, there was the clearance. She was to use the east runway, 090, climb to 10,000 feet, then make her turn. Easy enough. She programmed the information into the computer, so that it could double-check her.

She increased the power to the engines, and shot up into the evening sky.

She continued the climb as she gradually banked north. There was the moment of queasiness as she gained altitude, just that little bit of fear. She soon forgot about it as she levelled out, and cruised over the Omni-Forest...

The larger of the two moons had appeared over the horizon. Its light seemed to make a path over the Pleasant Meadows. Impulsively she decided to follow, forgetting her good intentions of practicing...

It really was a perfect moment. She was thrilled by the feel of the air currents beneath her, the hum of the engines. The moonlight had turned everything to silver, and from up here, everything appeared tiny and exquisitely made. Trees, hillocks, buildings, that - what *was* that?! Some bizarre-looking creature floating along... she eased down to take a closer look...

Oops, the moon was playing tricks on her.. it was a fatestealer. She felt the ship shudder as a wave of destructive energy slammed against it, and she quickly climbed up, out of harm's way. Phew! Better to just soar up here...

Still looking down at the still planet beneath her, she sighted the Stret River, and followed it north...

Several times she'd considered heading back home. But each time, something new and interesting would appear on the horizon, barely out of view, and of course, she had to continue on. The landscape had changed, the trees were more dense, and of a different sort than the ones in the Omni Forest. She was still following the river, although she didn't have any maps or charts of the area....

The darkness was beginning to lift, and she realized with a start that the night was nearly over. She hadn't meant to come this far! She rubbed her eyes for a moment, and in that moment, it was suddenly dawn. She laughed, and set the controls to auto-pilot. She was just reaching for the thermos of coffee, when she spotted something moving fast, toward her. She sat back up. It was another ship, larger than hers. Sleek design. She didn't have time to observe anything else, because just then, there was a blinding flash of light...

Time seemed to grind to a halt, as the events unfolded in slow-motion. She watched, horrified, as the other aircraft fell away, veering towards the ground. "Pull up pull up pull up", she murmured under her breath. Even though there was nothing she could do, she turned her yalm, circling, and brought it down toward the ground some distance away.

She had her landing site in view, when there was another flash. Lights went out, screens went blank, engines were suddenly silent. Frantic, she grabbed at the controls, and managed to glide the rest of the way. As her ship reached the ground, it seemed like a thousand trees were waiting, hungry for her pretty ship. She managed to steer around most of them, hearing limbs snap, and the screech of metal as the yalm scraped against thick trunks. Her last thought, just as an ancient oak appeared directly in front of her was, 'damn, there wasn't a scratch on this ship'

She could hear the alarm clock, and her mother's voice. "Kiyoko, darling, it's time to wake up".

Don't wanna, she thought to herself, trying hard to shut it out.

Time to wake up. She opened her eyes, momentarily disoriented. Ow, her head hurt. She put her hand up to it. There was a big bump on her temple. Where was she? She looked around.

The ship. OH! The ship!! She looked around again, dismayed. No lights, no power, the navigational systems and computer were down... and she could smell something burning...

She reached up and released the safety harness holding her to the seat. Pressing a button, the canopy slid open and she climbed out. She examined her ship for a moment. Actually, the damage didn't look all that bad, mostly scrapes and dents. Oh... and the big dent in the front, where she'd struck that tree.

She caught a whiff of that burning smell again, and looked around. That other ship, it had crashed, she remembered. There might be people trapped inside...

She spotted a plume of smoke rising above the trees, and plunged through the woods.

About a minute later, she had reached the wreckage. The craft lay mangled, smoke rising from the tail, but she could see somebody inside. The person wasn't moving. She tried to pry the canopy open, but it wouldn't budge. She heard a crackle, and glanced over. A tongue of flame appeared. She had never used dimach on an inanimate object, didn't usually even acknowledge her ability to channel her rage and energy into inflicting harm on a person... but.... would it work on the canopy? She concentrated for a moment, then pulled against it.

It gave a small screech as it reluctantly slid open a few feet. Big enough for a person to fit through, which was good enough for her. There was a young man inside, still strapped into his seat, and unconscious. His hair was matted with blood, but it was his leg that concerned her. The bone in his upper leg had broken and pushed through the skin, about three inches above his knee. Looking at it, she felt suddenly weak. A glance at the back of the ship made her pull herself together. "We have to get you out of here", she said to the unconscious man.

She would never be able to explain how she'd managed, but she'd somehow gotten his safety harness loose, and dragged him out. Once she'd gotten him to what she considered was a safe distance, she turned and ran back to her little yalm. Fishing around frantically, she found a first-aid kit, more-or-less undamaged by her hasty landing....

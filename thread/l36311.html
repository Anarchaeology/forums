1) A simple worm like this is not able to harm your PC in any way if you do not open the attachment.
2) Don't open any attachments if you are not sure that you were intended to receive them.

Side note: It is possible to get infected by a clever designed worm / virus / etc. without ever opening an attachment. This is due to security flaws in e-mail programs (some of them are known for some time by now, others possibly not).

Viruses...
To start off with, while viri is a word, virii is not, and neither of them have anything to do with viruses whatsoever. Viri is the latin word for men. To say virii would be akin to saying mens ... or womens, deers, carses, houseses ... it's going overboard in a goofy way. So unless you consider men to be a virus (many of us women do at one time or another ), the two words -three if you include virii- are unrelated. 'Viruses' is the proper plural of virus.

At their most basic, there are two types of viruses: file and boot. A boot virus infects the 'boot sector' of a disk. File viruses, the sort of virus this one is, are executed in some way. Be it a .dll, .exe, .bat, .pif or any number of other executables, or a template, macro ... even .xls data, these files only become active when made so. As a rule of thumb, don't even trust an email from your very best friend until you've scanned it with a virus scanner (NEVER open the file to do so, there are 'scan attachment' functions). Email viruses will spread by finding a list of every contact in your address books, sending itself across LANs, even posting to newsgroups under your name. The main idea behind a worm virus is to propogate itself, to infect as much as possible.

This is certainly not the full range of situations during which you should use a virus scanner. There are numerous places across the net to learn more about viruses, what they are, and how to defend yourself/your company against them. Take a look, who knows, maybe it'll interest you and you'll be the next person to write a whammy of a virus.

Finally, please be aware that this isn't some devious plan by Funcom. While it's likely just a matter of incompetence or lack of knowledge on the part of one of their employees, this doesn't necessarily have to be so. There are loopholes in every system, finding them is only a matter of time and patience. AOL, MS, IBM and many, many other companies, large and small, have been infected with viruses at one time or another, as have millions of home users.

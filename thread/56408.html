The long road to Rubi-ka

The monitor buzzed into life, the holographic display forming the seven characters Logan Dkystre had grown accustomed to RUBI-KA.

All roads it seemed lead there, at least for the time being. It didnt matter which way he crunched the numbers, facts and statistics that had been captured everything started there.

It wasnt altogether unexpected either, not that there was any logical explanation, it just seemed, well, inevitable.

Logan slumped back in his chair and sighed, the kind of sigh best sampled at the end of a long day. There was no denying it had been a very long day. Days tended to drag seamlessly into weeks with relative ease when you worked for the Interstellar Confederation of Corporations, but right now he wasnt sure whether hed slept last night or the night before.

This was important to someone though, it wasnt often that he got access to priority A3 resources. Even as a senior analyst he was usually restricted to the relatively feeble power of the local sub stations computers. Getting to use the primary mainframe was like the proverbial Valhalla of the Analtech department.

This kind of thing was the reason he joined as a bright prodigy, adept at analysis and with an uncommonly good, if not rare, ability to find the needle in the haystack.

He knew he was good at his job, damned good even if he did say so himself. Being good at anything in a bureaucracy though wasnt necessarily a good thing, thats why he wanted to be sure.

He need every conclusion to be sound, every i dotted and every t crossed.

The calculations fired through faster then he could enter them at first, it was only when he realised exactly how much information they wanted him to process that he knew it wouldnt have been possible without the access they had provided.

He sighed again, he had a nasty habit of second guessing his results. He just couldnt help speculating as to why all this was necessary, its like he was being asked to provide ammunition for a war that already been won.

Looking for the answer wasnt made any easier by not knowing the specifics of the question being asked.

He tapped a command into the video display and brought up the news channel. He knew that the top six stories at the very least would be dominated by events on Rubi-Ka.

For such a small chunk of rock at the ends of the universe it was just about the only thing people were talking about. He remembered asking his tutor at the learning centre as a child why everyone talked about this place so much.

He remembered listening intently as the old man he only ever addressed as sir asked him if he knew what nano-bots were.

Of course he did hed replied, he was eight after all. Even eight year olds knew what nano-bots were, they were the so tiny you cant see robots that were everywhere around us and inside our mothers and fathers that made our lives easier. They fought disease, made us stronger, improved us, even cleaned our teeth. That was what an eight year old knew.

He knew now that wasnt a universal truth in that you had to be able to afford the benefits of nanotechnology even in this supposedly enlightened age. Is parents had been rich enough to afford him a fairly rosy view of the world as a eight year old.

It was the tutors answer that had first triggered his interest in science, he could still remember that day as the first time he heard the words notum. Notum it was explained to him in the same monotone voice that explained everything, was the thing that made the nano-bots work, it was their blood, oxygen and air all in one.

Rubi-Ka was the one and only source of Notum known to anyone and it was owned by a corporation called Omni-Tech

The fact that someone could own an entire planet actually sounded quite appealing to an eight year old, it wasnt until later on when he went to university that he grew opposed to the market control of a substance that was of such benefit to humanity.

That was why he had joined the ICC and not accepted any of the offers from the corporations to join their R&D teams. Even Omni-Tech itself had put forward a handsome incentive given the work he had done during in studies. They were eager that his research and analysis of Notum should be applied within their corporate architecture.

Of all his peers he was the only one who stood by the morals theyd all developed while studying. The usual student thing, oppose the control of information and fight corruption or at the very least highlight it as an issue.

He hadnt bought into the whole sell out thing though.

The news broadcast was drawing to a conclusion as his thoughts snapped back to the present and he wandered over to the Procol2000 station that was currently running over three hundred thousand million calculations for him. He wondered if three hundred thousand million had its own name, and that if it didnt it should.

He ran his hands through his short black hair that was badly in need of washing. Still only seventy five complete. Maybe, just maybe it was time to take a break and go for a walk, or even better a walk ending in a drink.

There wasnt all that much to do on a deep space battleship except work, sleep and drink.

He tapped the communications panel and tapped in a key code he knew almost instinctively now.

Chrissie? You there?

Yeah, thought you were deep in calcs Logan, conspiracies to unearth and all that. A female voice answered.

Enough of the sarcasm, you off duty?

Yeah, detail ended an hour ago, Im just out the cryo shower.

Mess hall fifteen minutes?

I can do that. The voice replied.

With that Logan switched the com panel off and grabbed his jacket from the back of his chair.

The voice belonged to Lieutenant Commander Christobel Chandler; it wasnt like him to develop any type of friendship with military types, but Chrissie was about the only person hed meet that tolerated his dedication long enough to discover that there was real person beneath the abilities as a human computer.

Shed been assigned as a bodyguard when he was transferred here to the Guardstar, the largest defence cruiser in the ICC fleet. He was fairly sure their hormones had a good degree to do with the friendship that developed, but he was glad of the company.

There is only so long you can spend with a series three alpha processor before it becomes unhealthy and he spent enough time staring at them for it to be positively fatal, at least for anything resembling a social life.

He rolled his neck to get rid of some of the stiffness as he left the main processing area and wound his way down through the checkpoints to the junior officers mess.

At each plasteel coated door the helmeted guards nodded and waved him through. Everyone on the ship knew who he was it seemed.

Chrissie was waiting for him by the bar, her shoulder length auburn hair still wet, but sleeked back and glistening in the artificial light, her slim cheeks rosy from the bacto-water used in the military issue showers.

Her sleeveless casuals stained darker where her still wet hair rested.

She smiled and beckoned him over.

Long day? she quipped cheekily, knowing better then to expect an answer.

Very funny commander, and how was the bridge today? likewise he knew she couldnt answer, him officially being a civilian and all, exactly how far from Rubi-Ka are we now?

My day wasnt bad, were about three, maybe four days away at current speed and heading, but you probably knew that already.

I have better things to be looking up in the mainframe then our current course. He replied slightly put out at the accusation, even if it was in jest, maybe Ill look up your service record when I get back though.

You telling me you havent already? she smiled coyly.

Of course he had, he just avoided the question and took a slow sip of his beer. Loses the effect when you know the damn bots are cleaning your system doesnt it?

Chrissie laughed, good subject change, almost subtle for you.

Shall we take a seat and get some food before I give away anything more?

Sure, want to try one of the local delicacies? They have started sending shuttles from Rubi-Ka with foodstuffs to get us acclimatised.

You know Im a fussy eater, he said, eyeing the contents of the buffet table su****iously, whats this when its cooked then?

Thatll be fried rollerrat tails I think, a popular dish by all accounts.

Im not sure what a rollerrat is, but Im equally sure I dont want to try any.

You sure, taste ok, if a little crunchy, Chrissie teased him taking a bite of one of them.

Ill stick to safe sanitised rations thank you very much, Logan said as he placed some food on his plate, you might be used to roughing it out in the wilds of space, eating whatever was stupid enough to get the wrong side of your sidearm, but Ill stick to what I know thank you.

Suit yourself, she smiled, taking another bite and rubbing her stomach playfully, hmm, its good.

They sat down at a table by the large porthole view screen that dominated the far wall of the mess.

You can just about see it now. Chrissie said, pointing the vague direction of a cluster of stars in the distance.

Rubi-Ka? Really? Logan wasnt used to deep space travel and didnt know if that was normal.

Yeah, here, Chrissie leant over and direct his view to three stars close together just under some kind of gaseous feature, the middle one, thats Rubi-Ka.

So thats it he thought, almost there.

He wondered if it was going to be like anything hed anticipated.

Level order wrong?
Personally, I think it is wrong to remove the xp loss prior to level 30. Having the xp loss kick in at level 6 means people learn it without too much impact.

How long does it take someone who is playing for the first time to recover 2000xp? Answer: probably less than 15 minutes.

How long does it take someone who is playing for the first time to recover 20000xp? Answer: probably longer than 15 minutes.

Sure xp loss sucks, but better to learn about it and its impact early when the effect is less and you can recover from it faster. It teaches new players the value of saving regularly.

The best solution (if possible) would be to introduce 'staged' xp loss. That is, from level 6-10 you lose maybe 20% of your unsaved xp, then 50% from 11-15 and the whole deal from 16+

I would rather see xp loss kick in earlier than res. sickness. Res. sickness really only becomes necessary from a game mechanic PoV once you start getting beacon warp as part of your strategy. Prior to that you still have to run (or drive or fly) back to wherever it was you were playing...

Feedback from another newb. Although not precisely a complete newb, having played the game at release, but soon abandoned my level 39 adventurer. But I've been gone long enough to have forgotten nearly everything.

I've been back for 3 or 4 days now. Leveling is not hard in this game. In that time, I've developed, all untwinked, a level 4 adventurer, a level 9 soldier, and a level 6 doctor. The absolute biggest frustration has been trying to find the newbie shops (at least for Omni, I was Clan before and don't recall this difficulty. But I also started the game with several of my EQ guildmates.) I spent hours, along with a friend, running around in Omni Entertainment, finding plenty of high level shops before deciding to take the whompas just to get the heck out of there. And of course, I found the basic shops almost immediately.

I don't mind spending several hours learning the ropes, recovering from set-backs, and learning from my mistakes. I do mind wasting several hours running around blindly just trying to find basic weapons and armor. I've RTFM. I even paid extra for a book, which has a map of Omni Trade. I didn't find anything that explains how to get from Omni Backyard to Omni Trade. And if there is a basic shop in Omni Entertainment, it's not obvious where it is.

This is pretty critical for newbie retention. I almost abandoned my adventurer once again in frustration that nothing has been done to fix the class or more precisely, the ineffectiveness of the weapons they have an affinity for. It took me awhile to realize that my frustration was partially driven by my newbie advent still being stuck with her newbie weapon (I was thinking "Wow! I had forgotten how much advents suck!"). In contrast, my doctor started out with a DoT nano in addition to her solar powered pistol and was lucky enough to find a decent weapon up-grade in her first mission. My soldier *started out* with a decent weapon.

So my suggestion is to either make the basic shops easier to find or start all classes out with some damage potential beyond a solar powered pistol or give them a better weapon. You can't depend on random loot drops. Only one of my 3 characters found anything worthwhile.

Come to think of it, it was probably my prior experience that made me so determined to find a basic weapon/armor shop in the first place. I wonder how many people get frustrated and quit, not knowing that relief from frustration was just a store front away, if they had been unlucky like I had been with no decent weapon drops? Even if newbs don't have enough credits to buy a decent weapon immediately, finding a basic weapons/nanos store immediately, thereby discovering the reasonable prices for a substantial up-grade, would make it more clear to newbs what they need to do in order to survive and level up (or make them beg harder, but there's not much anybody can do about that. )

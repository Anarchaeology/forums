My given name is Vern Cervenak, but my friends call me Drusillus. I was "born" on July 31, 29450, in an Omni-Tek laboratory in Argentum. As is the way with many homo nanos, I was placed as a "child" with two homo nano "parents"; the solitus call this "adoption", but somehow that word seems inappropriate for us.

My parents, Zane and Erminia Cervenak, were loyal Omni-Tek employees, working in the mines of the 4 Holes region. Our kind is not suited to the grunt work of the actual mining process (this is meant for the Atrox), but, because of our inherent awareness of nanobots, we are helpful in detecting notum deposits. My father was always a tinkerer, though, and in his spare time we would work together on little "projects", like homemade radios and NanoBlox kits. Deep down I think he always wanted to be an Omni-Tek engineer, but my father was not one to question the position given to him by Omni-Tek.

At the age of 10 I was old enough to start accompanying my parents to the mines scattered around 4 Holes, and one year after that came the event that changed my life forever. While travelling between Argentum and Ahenus, the three of us were attacked by a pack of Bloodcreepers. Before the Omni-Pol guards could reach us, the mutants had killed my mother, and seriously injured my father and I. My father lost the use of his left arm, and my face was forever scarred from the attack.

From then on my father and I became even closer. While I was fairly good at the jobs my parents had mastered, it never really held my interest for very long. I would find myself thinking about the amateur electronics and engineering projects my father and I had worked on the previous night, thinking of ways to improve them or new techniques we could try when we got home. And so, at the age of 15 when it was time for my profession path to be assigned, my father recommended to the Omni-Tek Profession Office that I study Engineering, to which they accepted. I enrolled in the Argentum Technical College the day of my 16th "birthday", built my first android at 17, and graduated at the age of 20. At 25, I joined the Omni-Tek Engineering Corps (OTEC) under the leadership of the well known engineer Elyse Persichetti, better known as Virral.

In February of this year, my father passed away. On his death bed, he gave me a badge that had been given to him by his "father". It had the image of a blue wolf imprinted on one side, and an encoded blueprint on the back. It didn't mean very much to me until I talked to some other engineers in OTEC in April, who surprisingly had received identical heirlooms. Five of us had been given badges passed down from the vanished engineer guild "Sons of the Blue Wolf". On the back of the badge was the encoded blueprint for the famous T70 Khan pistol, which is today replicated by Galahad Inc. The message was clear: we were to be the new Sons of the Blue Wolf. We separated ourselves from OTEC and reformed the guild.

Today, I am a loyal Omni-Tek Engineer. For obvious reasons, I have a special interest in nano technology, and have become a quite well-respected nanocrystal manufacturer and salesman. I like to think that I'm living the life my father missed out on, that he dreamed about on those nights in our workshop.

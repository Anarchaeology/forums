(THE MOBS ARE NOT PAYING $12.95 A MONTH TO PLAY THIS GAME! REMEMBER THE PLAYERS.... P-L-A-Y-E-R-S... *ARE* PAYING! LET THE GAME BE FUN FIRST!

SCENE: Somewhere in Uncharted Rubi-Ka. A large conference room, filled with men, women, and creatures. The Iron Reet is in deep conversation with a Primordial Chirop. Rhinomen and Rollerrats mill around. Omni, Clan, and Neutral mingle freely. A deep hole has been cut in to allow the head of an ancient bronto to peer through.

The din of humans and creatures is interrupted by a gavel banging sharply.

This meeting of the Concerned League Of MoB and NPC Individuals is now called to order. Take your seats. An elderly, heavyset man stood at the central podium.

Beings of Rubi-Ka, as you know, the constant attacks of PCs, (a brief screech from a rollerrat matriarch echoed starkly in the silence), has completely changed the landscape of Rubi-Ka. Hunting has left empty forests, inflation has caused many citizens of Rubi-Ka to starve in the streets as they are pushed out of housing by wealthy noobs.

CLOMNI was formed a bare six months ago to combat this evil menace. We were able to form covert communication with the caretakers of the gateways, the Developers, in a variety of ways. We will now hear from division heads. First, Agent Nadlus, Director of Covert Ops.

A gray-haired Atrox stood. One of the first things we learned is that these Developers do not have nano-technology. However, through a set of fortuitous circumstances we learned that there is a market for Carborich Rock. As you know, for a while we were able to mine every rock that formed on the planet and shift it through contacts to Denmark. Our first shipment, you may remember, resulted in 12.6, and was probably our single most successful venture to date.

Heads in the crowd nodded, and one person called out, What do they use the rock FOR?

The agent smiled thinly to the crowd. For unknown reasons, they like to grind it into dust and inhale it. Our scientists do not understand it, but this single trade good is our lifeline to a successful defense from these marauders.

However, a new problem has emerged. Because of counter-bribes from these PCs, called Monthly Fees, these rocks are no longer appearing in the deep mines. One of the richest sources is in Southern Avalon, and I will let LifeStealer Chtop address this issue.

A young LifeStealer with old scars and a fresh burn across his base floated gently forward. Defend we do, the creature hissed. But fight we cannot when their pets are twice their level. Slayerdroids they become at level 100. QL 200 guns they use before reaching level 110. Die my brothers do to protect the rock from theft. Fail we do. The LifeStealer slowly floated back to his space. A Rhinoman Matriarch gave him an encouraging pat on the back.


The old Atrox stood back up. So thats it. Our carbonrich rock supply, the only thing we have to purchase the developers loyalty, is in jeopardy. Furthermore, there is no good reason to collect these rocks. Trade and Negotiations has depressed the true prices of nano-crystals in the cities so that there is no reason to ever create a non-crystal from scratch. Nanos that must be built, like Grid Armor, are so rare that the odds of it being found are infinitesimal! There is no reason for these rocks to be collected. And yet they do. We collect what we can, however in the war of outbidding the enemy for the developers favors, we are on a cusp.

The Atrox bowed his head and sat. The moderator stood. Next we will hear from our Enemy Communication Division, Agent Llyndel. A beautiful woman with flaming red hair strode to the podium.

Our division is working on three separate operations. The first we call Operation FanBoi. We have been able to infiltrate their communication pathways with the enemy. These message boards allow us to anonymously post glowing praise of the developers when their actions meet our wishes. While having some success, our enemy is much more vocal than we are, and often we are lost in the flood of their speech.

Our second op is our most successful one. Female Agents using FP: Budwiser Swedish Ski Patrol have been able to keep Cosmik and CZ from reading much of the boards. When they do make promises to the enemy, we are able to delay fulfillment, or make them forget the promise. We have had great success in harming enemy morale by causing the breakdown of the Daily Update. We have also been able to impeded the Full Patch Note promise, though I must give a nod to computer ops there as well. She nodded to a thin nano-tech sitting to the side, who returned a thin smile. We have also managed to wipe the boards twice. Each time the population of intruders has been reduced.

Our third operation, Operation Death Star, is the most risky. Our trade relations with the Star Wars worlds are minor, but we do not want a war with them. If they learn of our meddling with their developers to hurry their game out the door, conflict would ensue. If the Developers at Funcom learned of our complicity, our connections there could also be severed. However, we think it is worth the risk. A few words here and there, a little bit of carborich rock dust, and the release date has been pushed forward. In addition, we are trying to add the things that will pull players from Rubi-Ka and thrust them into another world. I will not get into our exact operation at this time; the fewer people who know our work there the better.

The moderator stood. Our final report today comes from R&D, also knows as Bugs and Balance. Rodger?

The thin Nanotech rose. We have two prongs ongoing in our division. The first, as we are prone to call it, is Bugs, the second Balance.

First, Bugs. As you know with each patch we are usually able to sneak in a few bugs. If the developers catch us then our entire department is in jeopardy, so we must act very carefully. Every patch has had at least one bug introduced; however, some of these are so minor that they are almost useless. Parry shields still dont work. And we have kept the tradeskills so messed up that no one who wants to do only that will stick around for more than a day or two. This has had the unfortunate side effect of moving everyone into hunting and missions. We have yet to determine a solution for that problem. He drew a deep breath.

We think that we can keep tradeskills unusable for the foreseeable future. Also, some of the high-end nanos of some classes are still useless or impossible to find. We are struggling to keep developers from allowing Grid Armor to become more common, and Adventurer Morphs are still a boon to the mission defender.

Our greatest coup so far is pet pathing. While we have been unable to decrease the power of those monsters, at least they get stuck a lot. When that happens it is usually possible to take out the owner.

Secondly, Balance. Here we are making decent strides. 14.2 offers us a great hope of ending overequipping by many people. This will give us a fair and even chance when they invade our mission areas. While we would like more than fair, it is a start. We are trying to get the overequip change in before they reduce our own armor and hit points. This is still an unknown, but we are working with Chtop to bring a massive shipment of ground carbonrich rock to them the week before release of 14.2.

Another good change we got was forcing doctors to have LoS to heal. We didnt even have to bribe the developers on this one. However, Agent Scott was able to volunteer to help write the code, and chairs and tables can also impede LoS. Definitely a victory there! Additionally, mission agro has been greatly increased. We are more able to call for help, and pick the obvious target. We are still unable to mez well, and roots can still be broken by a self-cast debuff. We are going all out trying to get rid of that particular bug, let me promise you!

We are working on several surprises for 14.2, but I would rather not comment on them until we have a more definite update. Full projected patch notes were distributed before the meeting; we are in constant contact with our agents to revise them. Any ideas you have on small changes to upset their update are always welcome. With a small bow, the tech shuffled back to his seat.

The old man stood, Friends, we have weathered much, and will weather more before we are through. Remember that in the end, we will prevail.

We must.

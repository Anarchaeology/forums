((I'd say you're off to a good start by making your intentions known here as there might be other roleplayers who are interested.

Actually creating the org and getting a little momentum going at first is the hardest part. You can look for people who, while they might not be interested in joining your guild permanently, might be willing to act as a placeholder long enough for you to actually form the guild (you need six people in a team to create a guild) and they'll leave after it's formed.

I think people are the most important factor in a successful guild. Find like-minded players with the same interests who will participate and want to make the guild better. In order find these people, you need a process to see who will be a good match for your guild and if your guild will be a good match for that player. My guild, Devil's Advocate, has an application on our web site that all interested players have to fill out. After that, the officers look over the application, and if it looks good, we set up an interview with the player. The final step is voting after all officers have had a chance to go over the interview. So, by having more than one person there for an opinion on an applicant, we have a process to discuss potential members. It tends to be a slow process, but very effective in finding good matches.))

***How to take a picture of a MissMaul in the wild***

This is an urgent message...dear citizens of Rubi-Ka, when looking to take a picture of a MissMaul in the wild, One of the best things to know is that youve got to keep non-aggressive body language, knowing that MissMauls are totally keyed to that. I keep my shoulders down and dont make eye contact.

Be very passive. I slowly bring the camera up and shoot, but I dont stare. I can stare through my camera, because they cant tell Im staring, but I dont want to stare with my eyes. Theyll tell me with body language and barks if Im doing bad things. If a MissMaul doesnt like what Im doing, shell definitely bark and look at me like, okay, the next time you mess up Im tearing your arms off.

I wouldnt recommend using flash photography with any of the MissMaul's. First, I dont think they like it. I tried it  I asked Nevver if I could use flash, and she said lets see how the MissMaul's feel about it. Well, every time I flashed MissMaul, she looked at me like, the next time you do that, its your ass. Or your head, but somethings coming off.

MissMauls in the wild spend a huge amount of time on the ground, so youre photographing them close to you. You can get within 15 feet of them as a tourist  and you wont even need a 300, youll do fine with a 200mm lens. You can sit with them and be part of the family with MissMauls. Youre like a street shooter, but youve got to keep your etiquette correct, because youre with the family and the boss is letting you be there.

Once a baby MissMaul stole my gloves  itd been doing it every day, and the environment really stung your hands if you touched things. I thought, Im not going to let you do that again, so I grabbed the glove away from him. Which was a really stupid thing to do. I mean, I had 20 MissMauls on top of me in about thirty seconds, all looking like, okay, now were going to rip you to shreds. I just laid down and whimpered, and it all worked out okay. But I realized you definitely cant mess with the baby MissMauls. The only ones who can discipline the kids are mom herself, and the big boss MissMaul. So youve really got to pay attention to the etiquette of the group. Thats the key to photographing them.

So i asked Nevver, How do you show respect to a Wild MissMaul? " She told me To show respect to a MissMaul":
Don't stare directly at them;
Do not stand with your body facing straight towards them;
Always look down and only glance at them briefly. Avoid eye contact;
MissMauls prefer to be in a superior position;
Try not to mention the word 'Moose', as it can ilicit a homicidal craziness in the MissMaul.

Dear citizens of Rubi-Ka, follow these simple rules and you too can return from your next trip in the wilds with a picture of the MissMaul's. But remember, your most likely going to perish on your first, second and third trip. MissMaul's tend to be a fickle and are prone to spychotic outbursts that usualy end in violence.

one final word of warning dear citizens of Rubi-Ka, NEVER...and i mean NEVER try to photograph a MissMaul in the wild when it's 'that' time of the month. You'll find yourself back at the reclaim terminal faster then you can say MOOSE!

Good day citizens, and happy photgraphy.

Dear Miss Barlau,

Thank you for your kind comments. It warms my heart to know there are others out there with a distinct fascination for the more 'reclusive' creatures roaming the wild lands of Rubi-Ka.

In regard to your letter, i will try to answer as many of your questions as i can. Even though i would concider myself the foremost expert on the MissMauls, i find that the more i learn and study them...the less i find i truly understand and know them. But enough of my ramblings, let's get to the 'meat' of the matter.

To your first question: "And I am most impressed with how you successfully managed to persuade the herd (tribe? flock?) to accept your presence."

If i had to use a term to categorize the MissMauls by, i would probably use all three that you mentioned. They are indeed like a herd, for they are a number of wild animals of one species that remain together as a group.

~At this point i must digress for a moment, for to label the MissMauls as animals would be like labeling the tarasque as a big lizard. We know they are much more then that, yet as to which extent i would go to clasify the MissMauls is something i would rather not comment at the moment.~

The MissMauls do show carachteristics of a Tribe, in the sense that they are a unit of sociopolitical organization consisting of a number of families, clans, or other groups who share a common ancestry and culture and among whom leadership is typically neither formalized nor permanent.

~To digress for a moment on the matter, i have found that all MissMauls seem to share on, let's call it genetic, trait. Take for instance a venus fly trap from the remaning botanical records of earth. Now this 'plant', could appear as a dormant tranquill tulip. It's passive and calm demeanor only used as a coat...an armor shall we call it. Hidding beneath lairs and lairs of this armor, you would find the true voracious being underneath. In that sense, the MissMauls may be calm and gentle one moment, and quite vicious and deadly the next. There nature and demeanor are two different things. They are fickle beings at best.

I have tried to find a reasoning behind this behavior, and have found some staggering results in from my research. The weather paterns of Rubi-Ka seem to shift and ondulate as much as the moods of the MissMauls. They're eating habbits are quite amazing. In certain occasions, a MissMaul can be quite picky about what it wants to eat. Other times, it is a voracious being consumed by it's hunger. Many a time did i have to leave my equipment behind and run as the MissMaul's would eat and destroy everything i had with me. Be it vegetable, mineral, organic or inorganic...there crazed ravenous bits of hunger rarely occur...and tend not to last too long. But woe be to the traveller that happens upon a group of MissMauls during this time.

But i am getting off topic. The one genetic trait i have found among all MissMauls is a distinc prone to madness. At first i believed they were inflicted by a disease. But my research has found something much more...interesting. There is a method to the MissMauls madness. The more i seem to understand it...the more i find my own mind slipping towards insanity. So i have left this subject of my research rather empty and vague at best. For i truly believe that any person that manages to understand the method behind the MissMauls madness, will truly have lost his mind to achieve such a conclusion. Kid yourself not fellow explorer, the MissMauls are an integral part of the well being of Rubi-Ka. I haven't figured out how yet. But i am positive that they bring a form of stability to the wild life and well being of Rubi-Ka itself. Perhaps that is why the MissMauls are completely insane? Or perhaps, they are not insane at all. Perhaps...nevermind, this is a topic for another time.~

To get back on topic, you mentioned the term 'flock' for the MissMauls. Well...in a sense, that is also true. The MissMauls are a group of "animals"(for lack of a better word) that live, travel, or feed together. I am still not completely sure as to why the MissMauls remain together, but i can state for a fact that i have never witnessed one MissMaul attack another. Even in there moments of insanity, they seem to work as like minded beings rather then delusional psychopaths that rampage and destroy anything in there path. (Like, for instance, most of the clan population).

I am not sure if they identify with one another by smell, sound, touch...or perhaps another method of identifying one another that we have never come across before. My own personal opinion is that MissMauls are in fact...linked in some way. Like Bee's, or Ants. Each one knows it's role. And indeed, even though a direct leader is not established, the biggest MissMaul of a given tribe of MissMauls acts as the custodian, currator...let's say 'boss'. Directing the smaller MissMauls on certain errands and ensuring the protection of the younger MissMauls.

I am sorry...but my description on the exact categorization of the MissMauls seems to leave me more confused then anyone. They are...a collection of like minded individuals...and even though they're intelligence seems to be a primitive...animalistic one, one can clearly see that they're way of life is more complex then we can understand. As i have stated, the MissMauls, i believe, provide a distinct ecological stability to Rubi-Ka. In what way i cannot say...what proof do i have of this i dare not mention...but rest assured that a terrible cataclysm would befall rubi-ka should the MissMauls cease to exist.

*Coughs* Where was I? Oh yes....You asked: "So there is one matriarch in the herd? And how do they reproduce? I note there has been no mention of males... are they nomadic? Or do the Missmauls mate, then kill the males?"

First off...as i have stated above, there is a sort of collective unifier for the group, but in no way do i find this one MissMaul to be the matriarch. That MissMaul happens to be the biggest, and the MissMauls, i believe, as a whole decide that it should be the 'enforcer' of the group. They are too volatile and unpredictable to be led by a single individual. The very nature of the MissMauls makes them unable to obey to another being. So i guess the Enforcer MissMaul could be the quote-unquote leader. But as i have stated, it appears more as if the MissMauls are guided by instinct rather then the will of an individual.

The question of reproduction is a very interesting one. At first i believed the MissMauls to be a self-reproducing species. With both male and female components. But one night...a bottle of whiskey and some of the MissMaul's special 'white powder'...i can safely say the MissMauls are not a self reproducing species. I...my research found no male organs on a MissMaul. That part of my research was long and ardous, but one must set aside moral dilemas for the sake of science.

I am still unsure as to exactly 'how' the MissMauls reproduce. I see no males in there collective gathering...yet they have baby MissMauls. That can only lead me to believe one of two things. One...that the MissMauls mate with any species that appeals to them during a certain period in the year...or two, that the MissMauls mate with themselves. How this is possible still eludes me, but i can safely say i have seen MissMauls attracted to females of a species as much as males. From a biological stand point, i can only conclude that under instance of extreme arousal, the MissMaul's somehow become impregnated with a youngling. I do not think that an exchange of fluids is required for the MissMauls to reproduce, but then again...they have not allowed me to view much of they're sexual habits. So i am still left in the dark in this matter...for now anyway.

As a side note, i have seen MissMauls kill there mate's. And i have seen them let them live. I would assume this depends entirely on the MissMauls state of mind. If the mate had aroused the MissMaul enough to produce extreme arousal, and then followed by extreme pleasure...the MissMaul might let the mate live. If not, they are killed and devoured on the spot.

One last thing, i mentioned a 'white powder' above. The MissMauls seem to produce this, how i still know not. This white powder is a highly potent narcotic. From simply licking one spekle of this powder, i found myself in a severe dilusional state. Hallucinations, dizziness, shortness of breath and bouts of psychosis followed shortly after. It is something i am currently looking into right now. I will get back to you on that matter.

I hope this information helps you understand, and appreciate, the MissMauls more. They are a wonderfull species that we should learn more about. But we must be sure to leave the MissMauls alone...and let them remain in the wild. Such a beautiful and exotic race of beings...i find them to be the most interesting, beatufiul...and at the same time terrifying species on Rubi-Ka.


As for wether or not i will be addressing the science convention in January, that is still up for debate. As much as i want to enlighten the population of Rubi-Ka on the wonders of the MissMauls, i fear that over exposing them could cause certain over zealous hunters and scientists to bring harm to this rare and exotic species. So for now, i will have to keep my research and findings confined to myself. Perhaps one day i will compile a complete book and send you a copy. From one fellow scientist to another.

I hope this letter enlightens you a little more to the behavior, way of life, eating and mating habbits and ecological importance of the MissMauls. I hope everything is well with you.

Wishing you the best,

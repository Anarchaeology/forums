Team mission patrollers: solving outdoor vs. missions dilemma
As I posted in another thread, simply raising outdoor xp is not going to get people to come out of Home missions to any extent. Players look at the xp they gain per hour, not per kill, when making decisions on how they hunt.

The "canned meat" ease of team missions makes the xp per hour gained out of them too large a reward to pass up by 99% of high lvl players. You simply can kill relatively easy human npc's much faster than you can at any of the lovely, though useless, high level camps that have been created all over Rubi-ka. A 130ish team can Sprint through a 190ish lvl team mission only pausing to get the Big buffs renewed. They worry little with what the enemy can do. Why should they? The biggest threat will be from rooms that have two easily calmable villains and if they arent Enforcers, calming might not even be an issue. The only way to fix the problem is by making team missions harder.

Here are my suggestions. I dont know if they are feasible, but I am desperately looking for changes that will keep me interested in Rubi-ka.

1. go ahead and raise xp from critters 10-20%. They have better attacks, more hp and the high lvl ones are insanely more powerful than they 'con' or register as(sandworms,erimites, anuns, etc).

2. Create Patrollers. Wandering npc's in team missions only. These bad boys and girls ignore territory boundries looking after the poor minions that are stuck guarding only one room. They could be slightly higher level than the average npc in the mission, be more difficult to calm, and perhaps have a slightly bigger Help! radius(if that applies in AO). These could show up in numbers dependant on the difficulty level of the team mission. Say, zero to five, on each large dungeon area and maybe zero to one on the smaller levels. They would give the same xp/loot as their difficulty dictated.

3. Because of the added difficulty of team missions, solo mission keys would have to change so full teams couldnt enter to get around the evil new Patrollers. Perhaps instead of the ability to duplicate solo mission keys, they were changed to having 3 charges. They would be splittable so that a solo player could ask up to two of her friends to join her, but couldnt get a full team. So people that like to just hunt with their best friend or mate wouldnt be penalized by the changes.

4. Increase the radius that critters in camps can travel. As they stand, the higher level ones tend to bunch up far too much. Again, the KEY to MOG player behavior is not reward(xp) per kill. Its reward per hour. The main thing that slows down high lvl camps is that too many truly EVIL critters are too close together and a great deal of time is spent waiting for the cyborg major to get his butt away from the two cyborg colonels. The issue isnt two critters being too close together. Its that three or four of them are too close. I would only increase them in most places by 10-20%. But, that small change could shave minutes off of each pull.

Anyone else have any ideas? Ill repeat, Im desperate to find something to keep me on Rubi-ka. I cant do Home missions night after night after night. There is no purpose in going from lvl 150 to lvl 200 if the only real change is that the enemy I fight every time's title changes from veteran to real mean. Ill increase in power but my foe will as well. Thus my perception of the fights at 200 will feel like I have treaded water the months it took for me to get there. The simple fact that I would graduate from Biledrinkers to cyborg generals WOULD change my perception on levelling. If outdoor hunting could attract teams enough that 30% of high level players would find the outside rewarding, it would be a whole new game to me.

Variety and fun, Vydas. That's what is outdoors.

Fighting outdoors requires attention and the application of tactics. Teams fighting outside have to coordinate their responses and deal with far more adds and far more threat of adds. Outdoors has more variety of critters, too, and they are generally more dangerous, which gives healers and tanks added emphasis. Generally speaking outdoors should have more reward than missions, because it is far more risk/difficulty.

In terms of improvement I think it would work if killing an X-level monster-type outdoors yielded 130% of the exp of an X-level NPC in a mission. So if you kill a level 180 'veteran robotbuilder' in a mission for 40k exp, you should get 52k exp for killing a level 180 biledrinker in omega swamp.

I would like to see missions changed in the following ways to make them more interesting. Yes it will raise the difficulty... and the risk. But if the main draw of missions is the private playfield on demand with easy access, this won't change that at all.

1) Mobs randomly patrolling through multiple rooms. Let them patrol a range of say 3 to 4 rooms away from their spawn rooms. This is to increase the randomness of the mob distribution and to keep rooms from being 'totally safe' once cleared.

2) Mobs getting a "help me" radius that can extend into neighboring rooms. Right now a creature will only aggro from another room if it happens to peek through the door and spot you. But the veteran botrunner you are beating up should be yelling for help to his real mean torpedo friend in the next room. "TRAIN!"

3) Human NPCs and 'intelligent' monsters in general should try and run away when they get under 20% health, both inside and outside. Again... "TRAIN!"

Then you'd have relative equality between missions and the outdoors: monsters would yield exp more tuned to their increased difficulty, and indoors-fighting would have a higher chance of drawing adds. Crowd control would take on more of the day-to-day importance it had in EQ (every group wanted an enchanter and snared or rooted runners). It would just be running amok from room to room beating the opposition to a bloody pulp

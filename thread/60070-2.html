Asheron's Call has evolved an interesting solution to the problem of buffing... players have written macros that perform all buffing automatically.

You stand in front of a "buff-bot" which is a toon that is good at casting buffs (a mage with the right schools trained) and ask for a bunch of buffs... it will then cast them all on your toon. The buff bot software understands your requests and casts buffs.

Some of these bots charge for their services, but the majority of them are provided as a guild service. High level players in the guild tend to supply the bot with anything it needs to keep casting.

Of course, in AC a single toon can cast ALL the buffs, very different to AO where most buffs are spread throughout the professions.

This has lead to less buff begging, but newbies expect complete high level buffs at all times when hunting. And because it's a machine buffing them, they sometimes get buffs, sit around chatting letting them expire, and forget to even use them. Anyone manually buffing them is barely thanked, because they rarely do as good a job as a machine.

Going against the tide of opinion here a bit but I would actually prefer it if people did start charging uniform prices at the "hawk your wares" areas such as BB and FT.

The point of these places is to make money buffing. If you want to waste your time hassling people for free buffs go ahead. But if you want to go somewhere where you can guarantee getting a buff at a low upfront price - even better.

If people hawk their prices along with their buffs instead of feeling they might get mobbed by the "OH I NEVER CHARGE FOR A BUFF" fraternity, the prices themselves will drop.

How can I charge 100k for a +80 when the guy next door is doing it for 50k.

The lower the price drops the more willing people will be to go get buffed as a service and the more the buffers will make. Why hasstle a guildy when I know the guy in the market will do it for a very reasonable price.

I always wonder whether I'm over tipping when I pay 100k a plant or whether I'm giving a paltry amount. I've had people tip me insulting amounts in the past.

Time and tide what for no man, and no man is an island.

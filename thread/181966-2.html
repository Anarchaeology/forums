What's the point of PvP without twinking?
I'm not a PvP'er and I don't have an axe to grind either way, but I'm trying to understand the point of the thread.

If twinking weren't allowed in PvP, then what would be the point of it all? If we assume the same amount of interest in PvP armor and weapons by both parties then they would be identically matched, everything else being equal.

If they are of the same breed and profession then, it is likely that both will be a fraction away from death at the end, and who wins will be dependent on who fired the first shot, and/or a bit of random chance.

If they are of different breeds and/or professions, then you could draw up a spreadsheet of who will win, just about every time.

That's for one-on-one person encounters. In tower raids, the same applies for one person versus one tower, or one person versus two towers, or two people versus one tower, etc, etc. Again, you could pretty much draw up a spread sheet, parametrized by player levels or tower QLs of course.

In full tower raid situations you could no longer do that, because the many-body problem and sheer human wierdness means that encounters are largely random affairs. On the whole though, since any given attacker or defender would have a predictable offensive and defensive capability, and towers even moreso, you could certainly estimate the likely outcome to within some degree of probability on the basis of people numbers of each profession.

That's for players of equal equipment and equal interest and PvP skill. If interest and PvP skill are below par then the predictability of the outcome rises if one party is top notch and the other not, but in that situation what's the point of PvP? Is a top-skilled player happy beating less skilled players? If they're both below par then the outcome is almost entirely unpredictable, so I guess here there is some small point in PvP, since it's about as much fun as rolling a die, and a lot of people like games of chance.

All in all then, except for the very last case, I see no point in untwinked PvP.

When twinking is added to this mixture, suddenly the predictablity evaporates, and the difficulty level rises massively even for highly skilled PvP players. This can be very challanging, and certainly interesting, especially since any given character will probably never have the same set of high-level buffs twice.

So, far from understanding the call to stop twinking (not that it could happen anyway), all I can see are its benefits to PvP. Pretty odd.

Are you seeking org self-sufficiency?
Donna, Haly, and others, it occurs to me that nobody else understands the point that you are trying to make because you are stressing the wrong part of it.

What I believe you are after is self-sufficient, limited-range orgs (for example limiting members to levels 20-31 if you have a 25 Control Tower), so that in an attack by a nominally level 25 org on a level 25 base you know exactly what you are getting into. And then, once two such orgs are in war status with each other, that no outside help to their members be permitted, for self-sufficiency.

Well first of all, let me point out how excruciatingly boring this would be. Who would win would be predetermined by numbers of attackers and defenders in the main, and for equal numbers the defending side would always win owing to the contribution from its own towers.

Secondly, what possible future would such an org have, seeing as its members could not level or they'd no longer be allowed as members? OK, you would probably counter this by saying that you don't want to limit the levels of org members, but if that is so then clearly the higher members will be able to help low traders equip 5 and 6-slot belts, and once that is done then obviously high-level buffs in those NCUs follow. I cannot believe that you would want to bar high guildies from assisting low guildies, so I won't examine that.

Or do you want to outlaw the use of high numbers of NCUs by traders? If so then you're asking for a fundamental change (ie. collosal nerf) to traders, since that is a key defining ability of the profession. There is no point in having high NCUs except to hold high-level buffs, so even if you don't explicitly say that you want traders to be nerfed or fundamentally redefined, that is what you are requiring.

Either way, if you look at the ramifications of that you are proposing, it quickly becomes apparent that it would either entail a collosal nerf to one profession, or else a fundamental redefinition of orgs. And whichever way you do it, the end result would be extremely predictable raids and boring play.

The current system isn't perfect nor anywhere near that, but at least it makes for interesting raids, as long as the defending side is willing to put the same amount of effort into it as the attackers.

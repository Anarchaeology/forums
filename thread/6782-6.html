I think the only true path around the problem is to make AC's effect non-linear - increasing fist damage just means we're using fists with the same stats as big heavy hitters, then I find myself wondering "What's the difference then?"

Raising min damage just means players are still hitting for the same damage over and over. We're doing more, but MA's would NEVER EVER do variable damage beside crits. Kind of dull.

And only solution is to re-gear AC to be non linear so that it takes less off the low-end damage. This is very tricky math, but a hell of a lot less tricky than trying to make fists A) unique in damage and B) not suck.

A simple way to try to gear damage would be to take some of the high-end damage in the game (not including crits) and re-gear AC to take the same level of damage from those hits, and try to curve the damage so a similar percentage would be taken from smaller hits. IE, if Big Mondo Weapon X hits for 2.5k against 4000 AC, and FunCom decides that's the new medium and keeps the current rule applied there - 400 damage chopped off, down to 2100 damage. This can be applied in a linear percentage or a curve if FunCom finds the need - but this 4000 AC would -NOT- take off 400 points of damage from a 500-point hit from the same weapon - in the linear percentage rule, with 400 points being taken off at 2500 damage, it would only take off 80 damage for a 500 point hit.

Let's face it, AC's non-linear damage reduction means frequency vs. damage does not work and will not work unless if this is changed. To minimize balance effects, you just find a high-end medium for damage (look for the super-slow high-damage weapons in the game to act as a guide), take the AC effect there and try to apply it to weapons below and above that high-end medium in a linear percentage fashion.

If a Sledge hits for a non-crit 1k, some variable AC would take off 200, if it hits for 500, the exact same AC should take off 100 - not 200.

While it's pure opinion on my part, crit hits as an example should have something like half the normal AC effect under this linear-percentage system so they stay nice and critical in terms of damage. If they were just tallied normally they'd take a big fat AC hit when crits are supposed to be critical.

This is a pretty drastic change however, and it might actually fix low-damage/high-speed weapon types in the game - so count on FunCom just raising fist damage instead.

The AC subtraction percentage in the examples I gave is linear, and thus damage would not slow down with higher quality levels of weapons.

A weapon dealing 1-500 damage after all the mods hits for 500 damage against an arbitrary AC value that has been assigned a value of 20% absorbtion.

500 - 20% (100) = 400.

1-1000 damage weapon hits for 1000 against the exact same AC.

1000 - 20% (200) = 800

The damage range of the weapon doubled, and hitting for max damage, the damage is... wow, strangely enough doubled. Let's hit some math again.

1-2000 weapon hits for 2000:

2000 - 20% (400) = 1600

Heh, the damage absorbed is even higher now - even though the weapon's damage range doubled, what it hit for with this linear percentage didn't double!

Oh wait, it did.

On the other hand, let's say all these hits hit against a much higher AC rating - let's say this AC rating was double the AC, resulting in a 40% hit:

1-500 hits for 500 some how: 500 - 40% = 300

1-1000 hits for 1000: 1000 - 40% = 600

1-2000 hits for 2000: 2000 - 40% = 1200
1-2000 hits for 1000: 1000 - 40% = 600
1-2000 hits for 500: 500 - 40% = 300

Here's how it works now - we'll use an arbitrary AC value of 5000 for this. Mobs hit this value long before level 200, so you know.

1-2000 hits for 2000: 2000 - 500 = 1500
1-2000 hits for 1000: 1000 - 500 = 500
1-2000 hits for 500: 500 - 500 = 1
1-2000 hits for 45: 45 - 500 = 1
1-2000 hits for 392: 392 - 500 =1

Not a very logical system is it?

Weapon 1 is capable for hitting for 1-500 damage twice in a ten second period. Weapon 2 is capable of hitting for 2-1000 damage once in a ten second period. Let's pretend like they're hitting 2.5k of AC.

Weapon 1:
Let's say it magically hits for 500, twice in a 10 second period.
500 - 250 = 250
500 - 250 = 250
250 + 250 = 500

Weapon 2:
Let's say it magically hits up for the max damage as well in it's 10 seconds:
1000 - 250 = 750

Well gee, one's hittin up for 500, the other 750 against the same AC. Let's try that with a linear percentage applied:

Weapon 1:
500 - 20% = 400
500 - 20% = 400
400 + 400 = 800

Weapon 2:
1000 - 20% = 800

Well gee golly, look at that. Weapon #1 - which in a numerical sense does the same amount of damage in a given period of time..... now actually does the same damage.

As it is, the system doesn't function at all for any weapon based on speed vs. damage. It's hit big, or don't hit. If you can tell me it makes sense then by all means just get rid of every weapon except the big hitters - there's no reason for other weapons except to prove slow weapons work better.

I've personally had 5 level 60+ MAs since this game has gone to release. I guess I'm just one for punishment, but this is the only class that I really feel right about playing.

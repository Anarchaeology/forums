Role-playing in MMORPGs might not be second nature to everyone, but I don't think it's a lost cause by any means. There are several things that must occur for the average player to be interested in RP, and they all tie together.

The MMORPG must have a dynamic world. If it's static, the players have a very hard time immersing themselves and feeling that they can actually make a difference. Static worlds tend to pigeon-hole the play styles dramatically.

The game must include the items and tools necessary for role-playing. This means that you must have roles that are not only the standard hack and slash, rinse and repeat. There must be a variety of ways for people to participate, so they can create a real community.

The dedicated role-players must not be stifled. They must instead be supported and encouraged as they build a RP community.

I believe that AO is on the right track when it comes to laying the foundation for an excellent RP community. Rubi-Ka has a rich history, and since the the actions of the players will have a real effect on the world of Rubi-Ka, it will have a very dynamic future.

Although combat is a big part of life in Rubi-Ka, there are a variety of other interesting roles to fill. Our events team is very creative, and has been working on stories and events for quite some time. The entire AO team is very excited about the future of Rubi-Ka, and is dedicated to fostering an environment that supports both established and new role-players.

We also have an organization of volunteers, known as the Advisors of Rubi-Ka (ARK), which you will be hearing a lot about in the near future. Stay tuned for more about this amazing group of people. (I hear there's some freelance reporter snooping around asking questions about ARK.)

What I am saying is not that it is impossible to roleplay in a mmorg but that currently only very small minority do and any game company that wants to instill it in their game community will have to swim upstream to do it.

Basically what I am trying to get at is that if Funcom really wants to put roleplaying into AO they are going to have to find some ways to encourage players to do so and ways to discourage those that do not.

I went to a couple of gm events in EQ. What I saw was lots of people shouting and just killing mobs. GMs were shouting the story line and were trying to roleplay with the small amount of people involved while around them a mass of uber guilds and power gamers killed everything that moved.

There are several ways around this I think. One when holding a gm event in a area have a message sent to each and every person that zones into that event area stating that a event is going on and to try not to disturb it. They are welcome to join in but only roleplaying is allowed if they participate. There will need to be GMs there that will immediately bar any access to event area to anyone who disturbs the event in any way.

Also there needs to be several GMs responsible for running dynamic events. And there need to be several types of events. Some that advance the story line and impact the world as a whole and others that are run almost like the old D&D modules.

Now the large storyline advancing ones are going to be pretty much one shots and should be large and involve as many possible so that everyone gets a chance to feel important. These "Epic Quest" may involve several large battles that involve large numbers of players and several mini quest that are run simultaneously by other GMs. These types of events require several teams of GMs working in concert with one another. In order to make sure the beginning and ending of the larger and smaller events occur in the right order they would need to be scripted and thoughly planned. Also there would need to be one overall gm that controlled when the other GMs could start various parts of their events. All in all I believe this is the way most events should be done.

The second and smaller type of events I talked about are the module type. These should in theory be fully planned very much like modules of old. They should be designed for around 3-6 hours of game play. These would be the type any guide or gm could log in at any time and initiate. These quest would really only be dynamic quest with the gm or guide playing the part of the villain npc. These smaller events would have no large impact on the world but if many of one type were successfully could make some impact. Example of one would be lets say a mission were a team needs to rescue a renowned doctor and blow up a ammo dump at the same time. If enough people were successfully in doing this mission then you might eventually see ammo prices rise for the opposing side while implants and medical gear got cheaper for yours. Also this might be a good way to make good equipment available to players. In this way GMs could better control what gear was available to players as a whole.

There would need to be a large selection of these smaller events available so GMs and guides would be able to run them on a regular basis without having to repeat them to often. If these smaller events were run on a regular basis it could also avoid the overcrowding of the large ones. By holding events on a regular basis and by enforcing roleplaying during them I believe it would do much to improve roleplaying in the community.

Also there is one more idea I would like to mention. In games I have game mastered I have made sure each person has a alignment and a sheet detailing their personality. When I notice someone doing a particularly good job of staying in character and playing their alignment correctly I would give them extra experience or a perk of some sort. Example if you are playing a kind hearted generous character you would be penalized for killing a little old lady and selling her grandchild into slavery. While on the other hand you would be rewarded for finding her and her grandchild a place to live and a way to survive. I have only seen one computer game that ever did something like this and that was Baldur's Gate. In quest there might be several ways to do it. And if you chose the way that agrees best with your characters personality you might get merit points. And these points would increase how much experience you got and may determine if you got invited to participate in the larger event.

These are some of the ideas I have had in the past and hope they get some use. I do not think any company can really devote the time and resources needed to do some of these things but hopefully Funcom can get the ball rolling in the right direction.

Sorry about the length of this post. Does anyone have some other suggestions on how to promote roleplaying in AO?

Lots of hope!
Go check Ultima Online, it has the biggest and best Roleplaying community among "the big three". There are lots of reasons for this, but the first one is that Ultima Online has a lot more depth than any other MMORPG (I hope AO will up the ante on this). You can actually chose other ways to develop your character in UO than the wellknown formula; Camp, Kill, Loot, Level..As most people know, these four words represent ALL the depth that is present in EQ. ( I am still confused as to how the shallowest gameplay in any MMORPG can generate a userbase in excess of 350k )

To Roleplay or not, should be a personal choice. And it should NOT be dependant on some GM generated quest or event. If the other players in the MMORPG is cramping your Roleplaying style, then you should try to avoid the other players, or change your style. I am what you could consider a casual Roleplayer, meaning that i dont do it all the time. Sometimes i make a character who i plan on Roleplaying to the max. Some of the most fun that can be had with this kind of character is when you try to Roleplay "around" the ocasional Kewl Dewd... When the Kewl Dewd leaves shouting obsceneties intermingled with the obligatory "Nuubie" ..i consider it "mission accomplished".

The most fun you can have Roleplaying in a MMORPG is getting a group of friends together and make some foundations for a true RPG experience. In Ultima Online i have seen a great diversity in the different Roleplaying groups, from undead, over Pirates, to the famous ShadowClan Orcs on Siege Perilous. They all do quite a good job, with the ShadowClan standing out, as the Roleplaing highlight of Ultima Online.

It is up to you on a personal level if you want to roleplay or not. The other players, the GMs and the environment should only have minor impact on your decision to roleplay or not.
To me a good MMORPG is like a lump of clay, YOU chose to do with it what you want.

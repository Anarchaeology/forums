Thoughts about the new Fixer stuff
As much as I like the new fixer stuff in 14.4 I'm left wondering if the Fixer isnt being polluted a bit (and guilty of the same problem the Agent were with the whole Fixer shop thing). I wonder if, in their haste to get the Fixer community happy (and off their backs) FC hasn't thought these through very well?

The NCU compression nanos:
Don't these strike you as more in the NT's territory than the Fixer? I think it would have been better to have introduced a 'darker' line of nanos based on the Trader (since we're kind of their dark cousins). Nanos that steal health and nano from the target and add it to your (and kind of offensive HoT). Perhaps nanos that would allow the fixer to 'piggy back' his programs into other people NCU's or nanos that allowed him to steal the benefits of his opponents running programs (or dimish them, or steal a fraction of the bonus or something)

The ability to strip down implants:
This seems to me more in the Engineers domain than the Fixer, or perhaps the Engineer should be able to retrieve the clusters as well since it's not so much a 'back yard chop job'.

quote:
Originally posted by EvilSpork
Errr... that's what 'fixer' means.
Hate to disagree with you, Spork Ol' buddy, but the term "Fixer" originally comes from Cyberpunk, a genre developed by William Gibson ("Neuromancer", "Johnny Mnemonic", etc) and which evolved into such Table Top RPGs as R. Talsorian's "Cyberpunk", and FASA's "Shadowrun". The Fixer was the man with the connections. He's the one that if you wanted something, you came to him, and he'd find the resources to get it, one way or another. In "Shadowrun" a Fixer was usually referred to as a "Mr. Johnson".

Example: (Cyberpunk Universe): Styles is a Fixer. For legal purposes, he runs a bar. "The Iron Hammer" near the combat zone in Night City. This is merely a front for his operation of "Buying and Selling". "Jake", head of a R&D division at the Arasaka Corporation, needs to reaquire some data recently pilfered by the Rival Millitech Corporation. As he can't let his superiors know the data was actually stolen, he turns to Styles. Styles, in turn, makes a few calls and gets a team of Edgerunners together to pull the hiest. In a couple of days, the data is in Styles hand and he gives it to "Jake"...for a price. He in turn pays out to the Edgerunners....far less then he charged, of course.

Now, let's convert this example into AO terms.

Example: (AO Universe): Styles is a "Fixer". For legal purposes, he runs a bar, "The Iron Hammer" near the 25% zone in Stret West Bank. This is merely a front for his operation of "Buying and Selling". "Jake", head of a R&D division at Omni-Pol, needs to reaquire some data recently pilfered by one of the Clans. As he can't let his superiors know the data was actually stolen, he turns to Styles. Styles, in turn, makes a few calls and gets a team together to pull the hiest. In a couple of days, the data is in Styles hand and he gives it to "Jake"...for a price. He in turn pays out to the team....far less then he charged, of course.

This would make the Fixer the shady equivilent to the Mission Terms we have in AO. In the recent patch, I hear all classes have this ability. To an extent from what what I hear of the Mockers that's close to what you do sometimes, but imagine spending your entire AO day being a mission booth, simply making and handing out missions. Fixers in Cyberpunk seldom got their hands dirty anymore, unless something went wrong. They have to make deals in both the legal and illegal world, much like a mafia don.

To ****e your life up, FC has given their interpretation of fixer another ability, the ability to use the grid on a higher level, and now more into cyberhacking, which the closest thing that comes in Cyberpunk terms is "Netrunner" (Decker to all you "Shadowrun" fans :-P)

Here's what a Netrunner was:

In the previous example, Styles hired a Team of Edgerunners to aquire the data for "Jake". One of these Edgerunners is a Netrunner by the handle of "Weefleboy". Weefle gets the layout and a slicer code containing a autosearch tool for the data "Jake" wants. Weefle then sits back fine tunes his Cyberdeck, does a few test runs in the area of Millitech, then waits until a few minutes prior to the run. Then he logs in.

First, to throw off traces, Weefle bounces through several cities and nodes throughout the world. Then, after he's convinced he's suffuciently masked his trace, he enter's Millitech Data Fortress.

What happens next is very similar to an AO mission, only the MOBs are hostile programs (ICE). After all, this is happening in Net space. Weefleboy, uses a variety of code crackers, stealh programs and combat utilities to get to the datastore where the stolen data is being held. If Weefle is particularly unlucky, he may bump into a Corporate SysAdmin which would end his run quickly and rather painfully. Generally, somewhere along the way he might find "Black Ice", programs that can cause enough feedback on Weefle's deck to actually hurt him physically. Finally, Weefle uses the slicer to get the data, makes his way to the securty controls, and unlocks everything for the meat team to go in and steal the "hardcopy" and generally make a mess of things. Then Weefle logs out (the tern is refered to as Jacking Out or Pulling the Plug a term brought forth by the datajack implant on a Netrunner that plugs into his deck) and goes to meet the meat team and collect his pay.

Frankly, THIS is what AO is missing, IMHO. More grid stuff! ATM, it's just there to get from A to B. Let fixers be able to run missions in the grid! Let them be able to hack into Omni-1s computer system, or infiltrate Clan Security. Come on, people! Where's the cyberhacking of the future!

However, I must admit to some error. I spent Friday talking this over with my old Cyberpunk GM, and a large part of yesterday looking through my collection of Cyberpunk and Shadowrun books (I had a whole set at one point) and yes, the fixer does a have a broader definition than I remembered.

The following is a direct quote from R. Talsorians "Wildside" Fixer Sourcebook for Cyberpunk. Please read and visualize how this would apply to the Fixer in the AO Universe.

"...A Fixer is a power broker of the Street, a man whose business is to make things happen in the semi-legal swamp that is the cyberpunk's world. A Fixer needs to know many people, and have a lot of information; that way, he can bring the right talent and resources together and accomplish something. Perhaps he's trying to run a successful drug ring; perhaps he's trying to put together a black ops team--it doesn't matter. Smart business is what a Fixer is all about, because business means money..."

"...While stealing and selling is good work if you can get it, the true center of a Fixer's power comes from his contacts, so it's only natural that many fixers find their calling as skill brokers--acting as an agent for talented people. Any major city has a pool of high priced Solos (AO equiv - Enfs, Solds, and MAs) and Netrunners (no good AO equiv, see last post) who need representation..."

"...In any line of work, people are a Fixer's most valuable commodity. A Fixer's contacts and allies can form a potentially vast web of information, intrigue and coercion, allowing him to pull strings without acting directly. Through his network a Fixer can buy and sell favors and make connections in any kind of business, deals or even policial groups. Fixers are ubiquitous characters; they buy into hot nightclubs, smuggle military-surplus weapons onto the street, and play negotiator between sides if a Corporate War is going down (with a definate eye on the main chance)..."

So, while a fixer can be a hacker, or a thief, that's not the sole definition of a fixer. He's the mastermind, the one with the information to know what's going on and the resources to make money from it.

I'm starting to think I should obtain another copy of this book and mail it to the FC development team.

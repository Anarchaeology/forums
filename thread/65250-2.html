quote:
I have to agree here. No 1 on 1 battle between classes should be a sure thing.
The guy putting rocks in a catapult vs. a knight on horseback.
A field medic vs. a Navy SEAL.
An enemy cargo plane vs. a F-15.

I don't know about you guys, but those are real life situations that are sure things.

But only because they are taken out of context. You cannot solo out pieces in a chess game for one on one battle if the ultimate objective is strategic gameplay.

Doctors for example should do jack for damage, yet have incredible healing skills. Almost an EXACT opposite of soldiers ability to do jack for healing, yet have nice alpha strikes. Traders should not be able to do that much damage. They should be there to augment other classes. Soldiers should be able to offer themselves as meatshields doing damage and nothing else. Engineers should be able to build temporary walls, fences, dig moats, and other things to improve terrain advantage. A bureaucrat's presence should lift morale and add significant offensive and defensive bonuses.

Some of these things are in effect today, some are not. But my point is that a classes' worth should be measured in context. Not in singular combat.

If we are to pursue a truly team-oriented game.

I have mixed feelings here
First of all, I have mixed feelings on this. While I do believe strongly that certain professions "should" do better against certain other professions, I also feel that PvP as it currently exists is fubar.

All of the professions have valid concerns about how they PvP, what advantages they have, what disadvantages they have, and how things weigh against what weapons/defenses they choose because of limited IP. Having to spend IPR points to fix a choice that turns bad due to a new design or implementation is a great concern.

Rather than jump on the bandwagon and say I dislike it, I would really enjoy seeing exactly what the designers are thinking about the current state and even moreso what vision they see with regards to current PvP and how it works within Notum wars.

I've also been wondering just how many of the designers actually play the game for an extended time, how many have characters over 100th or even over 150th for that matter, and whether or not they take all of what we say into consideration before making the changes.

Khyrene,

Stun: Freezes Mobs and Players completely even while you continue hitting it or casting nanos at it. It's a total freeze for a very short duration (2 to 5 seconds) It uses the mechanics effects:
  • Restrict Action Movement
    Restrict Action Fighting

Root: Stops a Mob or Player from moving. The mob can still attack. It has a chance of breaking when the mob is hit or cast at. It uses the effect:
  • Restrict Action Movement

Mez/Calm: Mez is a crat thing Calms are for the other professions. They are identical in effect. The effect is that the Mob stops attacking and stops moving. This effect does not affect Players in PvP. The effect is created by the mechanics effect:
  • Pacify Target.

The Psychosis Pet is intended to have the same basic effect as mez/calm. It uses the mechanics effect:
  • Pacify Target.

So the Psychosis Pet effect is a mez/calm. They are intended to have the same mechanics effect and in fact use the same effect. They have the same graphic etc. It is this effect in the mez pet that is broken. It is the intended mez/calm effect that does not work.

Player Calms/Mezzes suffer from the same problem with the 'Pacify Target' effect as the Psychosis pet. There are a series of situations where the calm/mez doesn't actually work and the mob continues attacking incorrectly. In most situations though, the player calms/mezzes' extra feature 'WipeHateList' counteracts the bug and the mob stays passive and doesn't attack.

The WipeHateList functionality in AO does not place the caster high on the hatelist. It truly wipes the hatelist and when successful the mob will not aggro again if you are outside range. It does not Taunt (raise in the hatelist).

The Init debuff was added fairly recently to make the Player Mez/Calms function in PvP. Previous to that calms/mezzes had no effect in PvP. The Psychosis pet mez effect was not updated with this PvP functionality (a subject of some annoyance to MPs).

So - when you mentioned seeing that the mez effect was a stun or a root,. neither was correct.

They use different mechanics effects in AO to the Calms/Mezzes/Psychosis Pet. The Psychosis pet does a mez/calm 'light'. It's not functional in PvP (lacks the init debuff) and doesn't wipe a hatelist but is intended to have the effect of stopping the mob from attacking you while still being breakable and uses the exact same 'Pacify Target' effect as every other mez/calm in the game.

There are no nanos that reduce aggro radius in AO.

In AO there are two basic ways of making an effect happen on a target.

Nano Formulae: These are cast using nanoskills. They have an attack and a recharge time. The nanoskills are checked against the Nanoresist of the target. If they win the roll, the nano is run in the NCU of the target and has its effect.

The heal pet functions by casting a nano. Each heal pet has its own nano.

Proc: These are effects that are run on the target as a result of a successful action. For example, a weapon may stand a chance of stunning you when it lands a hit. Here the weapon skills are checked against the correct defensive skill for the weapon. If the roll is successful, then the effect is cast on the target. It does not check against nano-resist.

This is how the Psychosis pet is built. It uses an MA fist, which checks against Evade-Clsc. When the fist lands, the proc should run the mez nano in the NCU of the target. Unfortunately, the proc mechanism doesn't work.

The current mezz pet uses a proc to place the pacification nano on the target. However, because of the way this system works, this can mean that the nano is placed on the target even if the mezz pet's attack misses.
Saying that the Proc process doesn't work correctly to fire of the Pacification effect. Pacify Target is the effect being discussed and is what makes the mez a mez.

Then they say:
quote:
We need to investigate a new AI script that has the mezz pet actually cast a mezz nano on its target.
Which is to say they're looking at changing it to be like a heal pet that Casts a Nano rather than using a proc in order to get around some of the issues.

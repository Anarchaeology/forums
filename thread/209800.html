We have those already. They're called Fixers.

Ever wondered how a Fixer can evac-to-grid so fast? Fixer evac grid nanos have had almost all of the safety protocols stripped out, that's how. The actual encoding/decoding modules aren't that computationally intensive, it's all of the checks, balances and safeguards that are necessary to make sure that you encode safely that constitute most of the overhead in a typical grid encoding nano.

Contrary to popular belief, Fixers do actually have a perfectly normal set of grid nanos available to them. It's just that most of them are in such a tearing hurry that they can't be bothered to use them and will evac even from perfectly safe areas. It's a testament to the robust nature of the core code and the safety features built into the grid hardware itself that we haven't seen an accident like this much sooner.

Did I ever say that I didn't like evacs?

Regardless of whether you like them or not, the lack of safety protocols on them is the reason they can be cast so quickly. It's also the reason why evacs are called evacs - they're a dangerous, last ditch escape method intended only to be used in the direst of circumstances. Just look at the damage it does to your pattern to initiate a grid session in such a rough and ready manner.

Now, if it's a straight choice between evac or reclaim, I'll take an evac every time. Don't get me started on the things that can go wrong with reclaim technology. Have a look at the poor, pathetic creatures roaming around in the depths of the abandoned subway workings and draw your own conclusions. There's no way I want to end up like that, thank you very much.

There's no confirmation one way or another that an evac was the cause of this particular accident. However, it does look like at least one user's grid session was cancelled without the proper procedures being followed. Evacs are known to work this way, so it's at least a possibility that one or both users had initiated their sessions via evac and unwittingly caused a glitch, or that their sessions were hijacked by an evaccing user while they were partway through their exit protocols, causing one or both sessions to crash. By no means conclusive proof, but at least worth checking up on before we all start panicking about anti-grid bombs and such.

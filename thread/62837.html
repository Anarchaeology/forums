Yeah, it would be cool. BUT, if it were me next to Elena Oak when Corky blew up, then I would be depriving 20,000 other people from playing that part.

We had equality before where we all were 'Citizens of Rubi'ka' and we watched and waited on our powerful leaders to make their moves.

Then we had absolutely nothing for a while.

Now, we have some who are apparently more equal than others. I didn't see the option to pay an extra five bucks to be provided with greenies. I would pay it.

I would love for the story to evolve in-game and be at the fringes of it and actually have it impact me full frontal at times. That would take a complete re-write of the NPC code and the AI for key personallities and monster behavior models. The changes in guard behavior through the evolution of the story is like step 1 of 1000 that needs to be incorporated in your world to make this a reality. As I hear Asheron's Call is much better in this regard. Likely they'd still need 60 or so paid actors to keep everything moving.

The above assumes of course we are talking about the Story and not simply 'events'. Events can be run by anybody. Story is twofold. One is continuity and believabillity when it comes to your setting and plot. Two is interest. People actually have to care enough to turn the page. The animated series had both elements as far as I'm concerned. Some demon played by a greenie had neither for me and about 19,950 other people.

Tower capture-the-flag games I'm sure will have as thrilling a plot as reading the PvP boards and following the pissing and moaning regarding bot scams and ninja looters. Somehow Midguard capturing Albion's keep in DAoC didn't seem like a Pulitzer prize winning Story to me.

I wish you luck with your programming effort. I don't think it's as hard at the core as you think. You just have to anticipate everything you might need at your core object model. (Dialogue library, mood, allowable range, capabilities, attributes, faction, allegiance, friend list, enemy list, etc.) The system isn't that hard. It's the content that'll kill ya.

But when and if such a system is finally implemented, and you have to do a double take to determine if a character is PC or NPC, then we will finally see a stage where a story can be told in-game that can be experienced by and affected by all players.

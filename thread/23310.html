Question about Mission Booths
OK, with AOMD broken, once again, on Test, I'm reduced to reading the mission texts. And I'm not going to attack y'all for breaking AOMD; this time there really was a good reason for it. It would improve the color of the sky in my world, in the short term, if y'all would release the compression algorithm and publically support the AOMD-alikes, but that's neither here nor there. Here's my real question:

Is it Funcom's opinion (by which, I guess, I mean Gaute Godager's?) that mission selection and mission rewards work just fine the way they are?

Because, see, I don't see anything in In Progress or Envisioned to suggest that y'all think otherwise. And if Funcom thinks that their existing algorithms for generating mission rewards, and their existing interface for selecting missions, is anything like acceptable, then they are wrong. And that's not one man's opinion. The whole AOMD issue wouldn't even have come up if the whole mission generation process were not so badly designed. Otherwise, why would so many programmers be willing to beat their heads against the wall, packet sniffing like mad, just to improve the user interface?

People like me use AOMD for three reasons, from least important to most important:

  1. To get out of having to read the same 20 mission texts over and over again. Look, let's assume that you weren't going to mission booth camp, and that you were willing to take almost any mission, almost anywhere. On average, it takes about two missions to level up. Even if all you do is read the first five mission texts, by the time you get to level 20, you have read a minimum of 200 mission descriptions. And guys, you don't have 200 mission descriptions. Most of the ones you do have are interesting. Once. Or maybe twice. After the 10th time, each mission description is just one more really boring, really annoying obstacle to actual fun.
  2. The existing info window layout for missions makes it almost impossible to actually see, at a glance, what most of the rewards are. About a quarter of the rewards are implants. Can you tell from looking at the icon what clusters are in an implant? About a quarter of the rewards are nanos. Can you tell from looking at the icon what nano it is? About a quarter of the rewards are weapons. Given how many weapons have the same icons as each other? Given all of that, to tell what the reward is, you must mouse over it - at the absolute bottom of the window, and that only if you resize it as big as possible. This means shift-click near the upper right corner, mouse down to a specific spot in the lower left corner, repeat four more times, then mouse around to generate more. The process could hardly be made more aggravating.
  3. 99% of the mission rewards offered are total trash, of absolutely no value or use to anyone, the sort of thing that nobody would even want if you offered it to them on the street for free. It wouldn't be worth their time to click "Accept" in a trade window. And you think you can get people to run a half-hour to 90 minute mission for implants with poorly chosen combinations of clusters? High-level versions of low-level armor types? Other professions' store-bought nanos? Very unpopular weapons, like high-QL water balloons, the fast chocolate surprise, or the more gimped assault rifles? Super-cheap items like Mission Key Duplicators and Application Forms? An endless supply of the same solid-color cloaks? NCU belts and memory upgrades at half the QL anybody needs them to be?

    No, of course not. Nobody wants any of that stuff. They want their own breed's armor types, but only in styles appropriate to their level. They want their own profession's nanos. They want specific weapons at a minimum of 25% above their own level. (If it's fair to offer nanos at that distance, why would it be unfair to offer weapons?) They very specifically want the mission-reward-only items. At very high levels, they might not snear at empty implants or implants with just one cluster, and they might not sneer at first aid supplies (which are totally worthless as low QL rewards). They'd love stuff you don't offer as mission rewards, like rings, bracers, and vehicles. At very low QLs, they're mostly OK with the tattoos and sunglasses. But that's pretty much it; the rest is just garbage to skip over.

    So to weed through the 99% of the rewards that are worthless, and the half of the remaining 1% that are in locations that are too dangerous to get to, means that by the time you find a mission worth running, you've looked at at least fifty to a hundred of them - before each mission. That way lies madness.


So yes, by all means, break AOMD. Break it permanently, if you like, by not just compressing but also private-key encrypting the mission packets. This would be great. And it would solve all of the problems related to auto-generation of missions, which I consider a borderline exploit myself.

But for God's sake, don't do any of those things until you fix all of the above problems. Please?

OK, I'm in a poor position to make demands. But if you won't announce that you're willing to do something to make missions more rewarding and/or easier to browse, will you at least aske Gaute Godager to explain why not, and why he thinks it's fine the way it is?

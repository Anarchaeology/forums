Interesting topic.

(The following is based on my hazy memory...)

The alcohol issue is much more involved in EQ. If I remember correctly there is an alcohol skill that would start out a zero. When you drank even one drink the swaying would start but you would increase your alcohol consumption skill. If you keep drinking, the skill increases and the more drinks it takes to start the effect. The alcohol would lower your agility skill and was offset by some attack benefit. If you maxed your alcohol skill you could really chug some down.

One think that EQ and DAOC had in common was the advancement of skills by actual repetition, not through the use of IP. In some ways it was interesting.

However,
In EQ as a warrior you had to use a certain weapon, such as a 1 hand edged sword for many levels to get the skill high enough to be useful. If you neglected 1 hand blunt for example, then wanted to use a club it was real bad because your skill was so low that you couldn't cause much damage for a long time. Talk about a way to become very unpopular. Trade skills such as jewelry making and alchemy also depended on this principle. I spent many weeks and a large fortune to max alchemy on my shaman and in the end they changed the game and made it worthless.

In DAOC you had to practice trade skills to increase them. I spent so much time and money to max out armor crafting. Eventually it was handy but worth me time? No.

So there is a balance here. We all want more content. EQ rules when it comes to content and DAOC sucks.

The lack of AO content is causing the loss of the most talented MMORG players who find it too repetitious for enjoyment.

If Funcom can find ways to increase the content without creating time wasting hindrances everyone will benefit. Maybe alcohol consumption is the place to start?

Content is, in many ways, a dirty word.

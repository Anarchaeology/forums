quote:
Originally posted by Hypos
I think we need to seek out a compromise position. In addressing this contradictory game mechanic, let's consider the following possibility.

A level 200 interacts with a level 50.

In the current rule set, the level 200 can perhaps buff and twink the level 50, but that's about it. This still results in high level chars determining the outcome of low level combats. This is no fun for the low level char.

Under the "protect the lowbie" rule set, the level 200 would preferable have no influence on low level combat at all. This is not fun for the high level char.

Under my level scaling concept, the level 200 would interact with the level 50 in every way possible including rooting, nuking and smaking him with a bronto burger. This is potentially fun for both chars.

Now to address your contradictory game mechanic, perhaps we dont reduce the level 200 all the way down to level 50. Perhaps reduce the level 200 to level 70 or so. A level 70 can be beaten by a couple level 50s, but a group of level 70s would still be more powerful than a group of level 50s. So while we are reducing the strenght of the level 200, we're still permitting him to be stronger than the level 50 - thus maintaining the advantage of levelling. In fact, this idea increases the power of some profs by permitting them to take a more active role in their low level friends' combat. The healing power of a level 200 doc in this situation is nerfed, true. But now the doc can also attack, debuff, and DoT the opposition.

I think this option still allows everyone to have fun while still allowing the high level people to be more powerful.
That would be fine, Hypos, if the exp-to-level curve wasn't such that it took a fraction of the exp required to go from level 199 to level 200, to go from level 1 to level 70.

So instead of creating a situation where leveling excludes you entirely from many notum wars, it would create a situation where leveling - or more specifically, leveling past the point where gaining levels becomes a time-consuming, boredom inducing grind at around level 135-140 - is simply not worthwhile.

You get "temporarily pvp nerfed" if you participate in a battle against lower level people, it's hard to get stronger, and even if you do get stronger, you just get more nerfed for it. The stronger you are, the more strength you lose in a scaling system, but the stronger you are, the more time consuming it is to get stronger still. It creates diminishing returns more than title caps and the geometrical exp curve already do.

Why gain 30 million exp to level from 175 to 176 when it won't make you any more effective?

quote:
posted by Jynne

Why gain 30 million exp to level from 175 to 176 when it won't make you any more effective?
Well, going from 175 to 176 is a poor choice of examples. Due to level caps, making level 176 is already pretty irrelevant.

To answer your point, though, you are still becoming more effective as you level. In my previous example, a level 200 char in a QL50 area would have been reduced to roughly level 70. A level 125 (halfway between 50 and 200) would go down to level 60 or so. So the level 200 is still more powerful in the QL50 area than a level 125.

However, it's insincere to suggest that characters will only participate in QL50 areas. These advantage of being level 200 would increase in higher QL areas. For instance, a level 90 char in QL50 area would be reduced to roughly level 55 - only 15 levels lower than the level 200. But in a QL100 area, that level 90 char would be level 90; but the level 200 char would be scaled to maybe level 140 or so. And, of course, in a QL200 area, level 200 chars would be level 200 still, that level 50 char will be level 50, the 125 will be 125.

So when the high level chars go play in the high level playgrounds (or in any PvP area that's not a land control area), then the advantages of their level become more pronounced. When they go play in the low level playgrounds, their advantage does get muted for the sake of keeping it fun for all, but there is still an advantage.

The high level chars are still more powerful than chars lower than them, the opportunities for interaction with low level characters is increased, and the low level characters are still relevant. The only thing that's missing is the concept of a single high level char single-handedly stomping an army of low level chars. While I agree that such a stomping is in theme with the concept of RPGs, it can only be fun for all involved if all the low level chars are computer controlled :/

You're not just losing here, Jynne. This level scaling is a give and take proposition. You're losing the ability to heal people with little or no danger to yourself, true. But you're gaining the ability to step in and fully engage in combat with the lowbies. This should be more fun.

If you see 2 level 30s fighting, under current rules you can heal one and determine the outcome. Under my proposed suggestion, in a land control area, you could still heal one (with smaller heals) and simultaneously blast the other with your weapon of choice. So you'd still determine the outcome. It's just not as overwhelming. Once the numbers on each side start to grow, then you stand the chance of being killed by a group of lower level chars. But it should be more fun than just standing there chain healing.

Now consider that instead of a doc, you had decided to play a non-healing prof. Now what? You happen on those 2 level 30 guys and what do you do? You could try buffing one. Maybe that would turn the tide of battle, maybe not. Maybe you just get "target has insufficient NCU". But in my proposed change, any high level char of any prof could jump in and do their thing.

I think it'll be more fun for both the low and the high level chars.

I'm trying to think of who'd get hurt most by my suggestion. The worst case I can think of is the pure healing doc. A high level doc with only healing skills would end up casting lower level heals and get nothing in return. ... ... ... I'm not fond of that part, I admit.

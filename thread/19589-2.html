All roleplaying really needs is a bit of thought. Generally using the 5 Ws works. (Who, what, where, when, why) If you suck at memorizing stuff, you can write it down and tape it to your monitor or something.

And about acronyms and stuff: Nobody's really found out how to say you'll 'be right back' in character, so you might as well do it ((in double parentheses- fear my horrid spelling.))


In my opinion, all RP really takes is thinking about what you're doing, and forgetting it's all just a game. This is easy for some people (like me, even though I only lightly roleplay). And, if you don't feel like spending a ton of time making up background history, you can RolePlay Lite(TM) and not worry about as much.

I've only been playing AO since about 10 days (these days including annoying things like "job" and "school" in-between) but I'm a table-top RPer IRL too, and when I had first tried the game in autumn of last year, I had simply quit after the first week, waiting for more patches and thingees to be put into the game. It could seem od to many people, but I found it sad that you couldn't enter a bar, order a drink, talk to Fixer sitting in the corner, be addressed by some soldier looking for someone to complete a mission for him, etc etc.

I still don't know if this kind of things can happen in the game at current hour (I guess i've just played CyberPunk too much... ah well) but I'd be very glad to see such things. What us eis it to buy food or drink in the park at Omni- east? Nothing, except that when you're IC, you don't even notice it as 'odd' - it's natural. This comes with time, I think - time, and more or lessquickly depending on the player - but same for the use of macros and commands like /bow and such... You don't need to know them ALL (*I* don't),, even a few of them to begin with is enough.

"Adventurers talking to trees... Crats asking for forms... I have an Opifex Soldier who has 2 bars of macro shouts that are basically one liners like in action movies. She shouts them out when she does something fitting."

So now will I sound like a freak or not, but one of the last evenings, I was roaming streets in Rome Blue, slowly going to the basic shop to meet my b/f. It was something like 23:00 , the sky was getting darker, and when I climbed the stairs to sit and wait in front of the shop, I could see that it had taken on a reddish tint. I found it beautiful, really, and without even noticing it... a haiku came to mind... and all of a sudden I realized that my char had just said this haiku out loud (yes, in old Japanese). Sitting there in a dirty town, but looking at one of the most beautiful skies you could see from Rome.

Of course people in Vicinity may just have found it strange, but I don't mind. If one had came to me saying another haiku, I'd have been very glad. I haven't really met RPers for the moment, but there are players that easily enter this game if you begin to walk the RP path first...

So, no, my Adventurer may not talk to trees, but she talks to the sky and impress pictures (read: screenshots ) of the beautiful landscapes I happen to see while wandering around.

It is so funny and interesting to find yourself facing someone and beginning to tell your tale, like "you know, this battle that had raged two weeks ago at xxx? Well, *I* was there! And it happened this and that," etc etc.

Hmm, I realize this maybe wouldn't be of great help for RPing... but well. Advice I could give are pretty much the same than in "IRL roleplay" (bad word, I know, I beg your pardon):

- Get a few knowledge about the world, but don't worry if you don't know MUCH. Asking an "older" player about the wonders that lie there outside of town IS roleplaying, and if the other player is willing to, you may quickly find yourself in a "old experienced adventurer to young adventurer willing to discover the outside world" kind of setting.

- Do what you have in mind. Don't bother about "what will players around think?" and other stuff. I've sen IRL a few table-RPers that didn't "dare" acting their chars in fear of being ridiculous, but once they went past this, it became really wonderful. If some players mock you, then too bad for them. If not, you may either meet 'true' RPers... or bring some players to find this funny and enter RPing as well.

- Don't worry about commands, either. As said, learn a few ones, maybe experiment on one or two macros, try them out in the wilderness if you're not sure about whether they'll work or not, but if you don't know them all... "well, and then"? IRL my friends use to tell me that if one cuts my hands, I can't talk anymore (LOL) but not all people are like this. You could decide that your char don't make many gestures. Or the contrary. Oh well.

- Have a little story in store for your char. It doesn't need to be "great". Just something to give more depth, and that you can improve day after day. For instance, your char could have joined the Clans because Omni-Tek soldiers killed her family. But then two weeks later you can meet an Omni player that will act sympathetic, and your character could temperate her position then. Etc etc. This may seem lame, but even if no one else will ever know this char's history (because she won't tell, for instance...) it WILL help you acting.

And time for me to stop here before it becomes a whole novel ^_^;

Where to find people
In my experiences I find that most people on the streets in Omni-1 aren't interested in what people holler at each other. Most people in Omni-1 Entertainment are not there for the entertainment or to trade, but are instead mostly clustered near the Whompas looking over the Omni-Tek approved mission and task listing for that day, or using the area as a common meeting point for associates.

Omni-1 Trade is somewhere you'll fare a little better if you have things to trade, but only a little. To be honest, if you're looking for trade you are best off posting a bulletin on the trade network provided.

If you're looking for advice or you're looking to make contacts and you're in Omni-1, then my suggestion is head down to Rompa Bar in the northeast quadrant of Omni-1 Entertainment, buy a drink or two and ask around. Shouting in a bar that you've got things to trade is not likely to win you any friends, but a few discreet enquiries may reap big rewards. This would be, in my opinion, the best bet if you've an aversion to using the Omni-Tek Shopping Channel.

The easiest (but not the quickest) way to find Rompa Bar is to head northeast from the Arena (in the middle of Omni-1 Entertainment) - along a street with walkways crossing it at regular intervals. Rompa is on your right. If you reach Baboons, you've gone too far.

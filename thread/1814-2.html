Hard QL limit (level + x) won't work, because it overthrows the game balance. Some classes are *meant* to equip higher QL stuff in their specialities (enforcers with 2HB, Soldiers with guns, Fixers with belts and NCU...). That's why they have bufs to do it, and that's why they have light green skills in them. The hard QL limit, especially if badly done, would only make it so that a NT and an Enforcer would be able to equip the same weapon at the same QL (NT from external buffs, enforcer from own buffs).

The solution of decreasing the QL of the item to meet the current stats is nicer. Though there should be *some* advantage to putting money in better equipement. Just not as large an advantage as currently. My idea is that an item has built in capacities (ie, a gun has a damage potential), which doesn't depends on skills. What the skills do is enable you to make full use of it. It would need a per item adjustement :

- Nanos : increase the fumble chance if the req are not met. Including down to 100% fumble if the difference is really too high. Also, casting time gets longer and you can be interrupted more often. Makes sense, you have managed to learn the program, you can attempt to run it. Only, it's a bit too much for you : you can just plain fail, and you have to concentrate more to run it (hence longer cast time and easier to interrupt).
- Weapons : min damage and max damage stay the same (it's the capacity of the weapon). Critical chance decreases, down to 0 if the difference is too high (damn hard to handle such a powerful gun! Too much power = too much recoil). Attack skill decreases (hence, you're easier to evade, because you don't aim very well). Recharge time increases (have to recover from that recoil you're not skilled enough to handle).
- Armor. If you read the cyber armor description, it's actually stated something that "you could use it with low int and psy, but not make full use of it". Maybe slightly lower AC (the armor still acts as a good quality physical barrier, so it should be midway between actual AC and AC for the QL you can handle). Either give a penalty to some skills (armor too heavy = can't run as fast, can't evade as well...). Or the armor makes you have to focus extra on its req and lowers your stats (ie, using a cyber armor with 100 int while it requires 120 int would lower your int to 90) and all the related skills.
- NCU, belts. I would let these unchanged, because right now the only comp lit buffs are general buffs.
- Implants. Set QL midway (ie, wearing QL 50 with skills for QL 30 => QL 40 implants). Don't check treatement (would make the static clinics useless).

I think it both saves the usefullness of equiping higher QL stuff (and the fact that game balances gives more abilities to some classes to over-equip some stuff than others). But it doesn't make over-equipping THAT powerful. The troubles now is that there is little adverse effect to equip a gun 100 QL above your level. With these changes however, you would be trading sheer firing power for loss of criticals, and even downright misses. Brings more strategy into the game...

On a side note, I also like some changes that have introduced the need to be able to unequip some stuff. Like :
- Omni med stuff. Can only use it if you're able to remove your armor without too much hassle.
- Gun switching. Cannot do that if you massively over-equip your guns.
- Different sets of implants (fighting, tradeskills...), which are impossible to use if you need half a dozen high level people to fit in yours...
Maybe a solution would be to bring more incitatives like this in the game. Like :
- Tech clothes, which raise your tradeskills (but are social armor)
- Locksmithing device (raise breaking&entering and locksmithing, but must be held in right hand)
- More gun variety, so that ability to switch weapons gets so interresting that everyone wants it.

Well written
I agree completely. People who have invested substantial time an energy to tune their characters to the maximum extent possible should have an advantage over those who are not willing to put in that effort.

When a game designer builds in rules to which players invest a great deal of time and forethought to plan around, it is extremely unfair to reduce that player's effectiveness or viability after the fact. As Ani put it, it is punishing success.

I am not saying that all nerfs are unwarranted -- some things are too powerful, and should be brought in line, but *with moderation*. If something is truly unbalancing, bring it down a peg or two; there's no need to destroy it, or drastically alter the game.

With regards to keeping players, continually making the game harder is not the way to do it. People LIKE to dungeon-crawl. That means killing monsters at a reasonable rate, improving one's character, and occasionally dying because of a tactical error. Zoning 10 times to kill a single mob is not fun!

Also, it should be possible to play this game solo. Many of my friends are unhappy that the game is no longer soloable past the earliest levels.


Now, Some other comments:

Regarding NTs: NTs need some reasonable compromise so that they can use their NFs when debuffed, perhaps at a dimished capacity (in the same manner that debuffed attack rating reduces weapon damage). One MAJOR point: NTs outdamage most every class using ranged weapons.

Regarding 2H melee weapons: These SHOULD do a lot more damage than any other weapon or NF. They are poor for pulling, slow, and have no range. They put the user at tremendous risk because it is very difficult to pull a monster off once they engage a 2H melee enforcer.

Regarding MAs: If you take out traders and agents, most characters that overequip will have an advantage only to about level 80. After that, various caps in the game make it such that their damage output is not really any greater. MAs have a substantial advantage in that they don't have to find weapons at higher levels. A QL 150 or 200 gun sounds really nice, except that they cost between 5 to 80 million credits in a trade channel. There should be a penalty of less damage comparative to a 1HE/1HB enforcer, if only because the weapon is "built in".

A level 150 MA with level 200 implants will do a HUGE amount of damage over time -- a lot more than a soldier, for example. MAs also get a crit bonus of 8%-24%, which stacks with an LLTS. At the high levels, that means that 1/3 of the hits will crit.

I think potoole that you missed an important point...
First, potoole, that was a well written opinion about the 'socio-economic' stratification, in other words the struggle between the haves and ever complaining have-nots, that has been created in Rubi-Ka. But I feel that you missed an important point.

People (or RPGers) should have the right to equal opportunity but not all people (or RPGers) have equal talents. Fair statement?

What is missing from the land of Rubi-Ka is the equal opportunity. At some point all classes/races should have an equal 'upper limit' or have no boundaries to continual improvement (I image it would be hard for a Persistant State World to have no boundaries at this point).

It should be possible for each class/race, when 'maxed out' at any given level, to be an even match. Do you believe this is true in Rubi-Ka now? I hope not, then my point is moot.

In order to maintain the state of haves and have-nots (bet you love those gaussian curves) the method(s) required to reach a maxed state, for a player, need to be reasonably difficult to achieve but public knowledge and fair (to maintain an equal opportunity environment). It would be really boring if every level 50 AO'er always killed each other on the last hit, everytime. Obviously, evenly matched players in a maxed state should have to contend with different PvP strategies.

Currently in Rubi-Ka, there is no doubt in my mind that there are several classes who, using all legal and illegal (i.e. exploits) methods to reach a maxed state (now a major 'have'), maintain an extraordinary advantage over other classes in their maxed state. This is not a good system to have if it can be avoided, since it violates the equal oppporunity paradigm.

Unfortunately, I can't offer a solution for Funcom. Setting up a fair equal-opportunity model in a complex system like Rubi-Ka is not trivial.

So good luck Funcom and keep trying. Also, I really appreciate the effort you are taking and the latest comments (11/25) from one of the developers.

My only advice is be wary of the very vocal minority.

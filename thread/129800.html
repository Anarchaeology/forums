Make assist work outside of combat
Pretty simple idea:

Make /assist work when the target you're assisting isn't in combat.

Reasons:
1. This will let people assist non-fighting classes (lots of calmers don't attack, for instance) so that the calmer can pick targets from a pack of calmed mobs.
2. It's handy for general targeting (assist me to get a buff target, assist me to invite the doc, etc).
3. In PvP it lets people all stay on the same target or pre-select a target without having to start attacking.
4. It's just generally handy, and there's no reason to restrict it that I can see.

Anyway, thoughts?

This isn't as simple as you think. First, you would need an all new command. /assist switches to the player/mob attacked, not the one targeted. These two are often not the same. Second, I don't think the player/mob targeted is something the server knows about (and why should it). This is a thing for the client, and the server probably doesn't know about it until you tell it to do something with the target.

So for this idea to be implemented, first a new command must be made and second (I guess) more data would have to be exchanged between the client and the server. And judging from the amout of lag in certain situations I think the data flow is high enough already

This isn't nearly as complicated as you're making it out to be.

%t returns my target. %f returns what I'm fighting. /assist sets your target to whatever your CURRENT target's %f is. It's not a matter of broadcasting targets to clients in the area or bandwith issues, trust me. It's literally a code issue that checks for the existance of %f for a particular person and if it returns a null result then retruns %t.

I guess what I can't figure out is why you think it's a bad idea beyond bandwidth (which is a ridiculous concern). It might be a bit confusing, but no more confusing than anything else in a game where you can effectively have two targets at once.

I develop games for a living, specifically as a QA and project manager and producer.

There are lots of reasons why it's "bad" for a client to control information like targets, so any time you switch targets/open fire it has to be server validated, at least before any actual effects happen. (You might go into an animation, but it's not going to have you start to do damage/shoot before it makes sure you have, say, line of sight, obviously.)

We can assume that your client has a the information on targets you have access to via Tab/ctrl+Tab/etc ("targets in your area") but even that has to be server validated every time you actually want to DO anything with one of those targets. You'll notice that sometimes you can target someone at a tower battle but you get an empty health bar until the client updates their health. So it's not appreciably more overhead than goes on now. Maybe one more packet send/receive cycle, which isn't huge.

My point, really, is that implementation concerns should be dealt with by the dev team. Players should be more concerned with play balance, exploits, user friendiness, etc. The fact that it might be confusing is a valid point, and one that people would have to address on their own. (I don't think it's any more confusing than the auto targeting switches, but how many people use those?)

Pluralisms
quote:
Oh yeah, Viri(I dunno how it's supposed to be spelled) is plural for virus


Nope.
Observe http://www.dictionary.com/doctor/faq/v/virus.html , http://language.perl.com/misc/virus.html , and http://info.astrian.net/jargon/terms/v/virus.html (while the third link doesn't contain information directly related to the battle between viri/virii vs. viruses, you can observe the word used in plural by someone who's both an etymologist and a hacker)

The word viri was popularized by writers who either knew better but wanted to add a flair to their works, knew too little, or copied others of the same ilk. The first computer worm (virus) was written by an extremely smart man whose unfortunate progeny are what are popularly called 'hackers' by the media. (see The Jargon Dictionary - http://info.astrian.net/jargon/ for further information about the difference between crackers and the -true- hacker) These crackers also know no better. And tell me, honestly, are you more willing to trust that a group of people who invented d00d sp33k and still use it, thinking it's cool, know more about the word than etymologists or someone who actually speaks Latin?

Also, I'd like to point out that, strictly speaking, a worm isn't a virus. A virus can infect other computers without assistance. Worms, on the other hand, require direct human interaction to propogate ... such as the active exectution of a file.

Beware that these .doc files could have macros in them. Also, and more deviously, they could be of the 250 character name variety. What this means is that your support policies.doc file could really be "support_policies.doc .exe" Windows will ignore all but everything that comes after the last period as long as it's within the character naming limit, so this file could really be an executable with an exceedingly long name. Handy trick for infecting newgroup frequenters, "bigboobs.jpeg .exe" The cleverer virus script kiddy will even include a popup picture, just to throw you off track. It's even worse through ICQ, since ICQ is unable to display so many characters in a file name through the file transfer window.


Oh, and as far as viri vs virus goes, all the paragraph you quoted proves, Acobar, is that there's no Latin plural. (and it doesn't even prove that, in fact, it only 'proves' that viri/virii aren't the plural of virus) It doesn't preclude a modern or Latin plural for the word. The second link is a treatise on both why viri/virii is incorrect and why viruses IS. So, if you somehow missed the big, bold 18 point letters on the first page:

What is the plural of virus?
Viruses.

then the second link will make the matter painfully obvious. Take a peek.

quote:
Originally posted by Naughty


3) if you have outlook as your email program, change, now. Outlook is the biggest target for viri since it has things like an address book that someone can actually write a virus for which sends to everyone in the address book. Its also often set up the same way on everyone's computer in the same folder.



This isn't true in the slightest. If you open an attchment that is infected with a virus (no matter what E-mail client you are using) and you don't have proper virus protection, you WILL get infected. It doesn't matter what E-mail reader you use. Sometimes the Virus will only spread itself by sending to your Outlook address book, but you will still be infected, just not propagating the virus. Besides, most modern viruses will scan for ANY kind of address book's contacts, not just Outlook, to send itself to. Including this particular virus. It scans for HTML address books as well as Outlook and several other kinds. Netscape, Pegasus and Eudora all use HTML address books, so not using Outlook isn't gonna keep this one from sending itself to your contacts.

The only way to truley be safe from a virus is to only open attachments that you are expecting to recieve (and even then you may want to have your e-mail scanner on). Even if it doesn't have an EXE, VBS, BAT, PIF and other kind of extension. Even if it apperars to be a picture file (jpg,bmp,gif). Also make damn sure that you don't have any folders shared on your network without permissions set on them. Especially make sure that your C: drive isn't shared to anyone but administrators. Worms love shared C: drives.

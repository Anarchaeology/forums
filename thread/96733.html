For the best part of the morning Dabz poured over the various robotics and technical journals. The RATFE droid had shown signs of extensive and rather nasty modifications to the robots behavioural routines. But as none of the standard input devices had been accessed, Dabz could not on her life figure out how the reprogramming had been effected. It was driving her crazy.

It was then she remembered reading something back in college about a very unusual method hacking into robots. Having searched all the more prestigious academic publications, she finally found the article in a small, underground robotics magazine. The author of the article identified herself only by the handle "Slotine". It read:

"It is a well established fact that if you take apart any two random robots on Rubi-Ka, on average 87% of the components will be the same. That is to say, regardless of make or model, all robots are made up of essentially the same bits and bobs. The standard components found in all these robots come from OT Cybernetics, or one of its sub-brands. They are cheap, reliable, and have not really changed at all in over a decade. They are also flawed.

"The majority of OT Cybernetics standard robot components come with a dedicated micro-processor which auto-regulates the component and provides the cpu with diagnostic information. This micro-processor is a closed system and is universally considered tamper-proof. This is not the case.

"By accident more than design, I recently discovered the work of an ingenuous hacker. Using a simple mini-broadcaster at close range, the hacker was able to activate test routines in the robot's standard component micro-processors and in turn fool these into accessing and even altering programs in the robots main cpu. In a nutshell, we are talking about potentially taking control of any robot with nothing more elaborate then primitive radio signals.

"I cannot go into the specifics of this case. I learned enough of this hacker's intentions to know he was planning something big and in the end I turned to an old family friend, Baxie, who at the time was in charge of the OT security agency Enigma. He later told me the situation had been handled, though I never found out any details.

"What I do know however is that these same, flawed robot components are still on sale today and still found in virtually every robot you see on this planet. It is just a matter of time before someone else rediscovers this technique."

Dabz finishes reading the article and frowns. "It's not much to go by, but
it's still the best lead I've got. I wonder if this Slotine and Baxie people are still on Rubi-Ka?"

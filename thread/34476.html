bBb uUu mMm pPp !!!

It was said by the nano designer of the game that level reqs need to be there to make sure that their expansion of the skill budget doesn't trivialize the rest of the game. So they put level reqs on all new nano formulas. Bad Bad Bad!

LEAVE THE SKILL BUDGET ALONE!! Putting all of us through the slow ass irritation of having to level to use a nano regardless of how much our skill exceeds the reqs is like telling us "Go away, we don't want your money.". Alot of people that play this game now won't be here once this goes into full effect as there will be other games out there.

It was also said by the same person that it is not the intent to allow characters to use any nano in their library without outside buffs. So why the level reqs? Everyone in the game can achieve level 200, but depending on how they've developed their character their skills are different making some nanos available and some not. It would make much more sense to fit the new nanos in with the existing skill set. You think the high end players won't like this because there's nothing new for them? You're barking up the wrong tree.

The high end game is just that....the high end game. A character is supposed to be done with development and ready for war at that level. You need to give us the ability to determine which territories belong to which factions through PvP / RvR and those territories should give a skill boost to the faction that has possession of them. You don't think that a dozen level 200s raiding an outpost in 4 holes or something wouldn't be fun?

Aside from that, how can you expand on something that isn't fully working properly yet? HP bar bugs, nanos that you can't run in mission environments, horrid pet pathing, screwed up PvP level restrictions, yalm bugs like being ultra small when getting out of the yalm and being able to equip the yalm when sitting, nano resist being totally screwed up.....the list goes on and on. When you add more features to a flawed program of any kind, you potentially end up with more flaws. I know this first hand as I write code for a living. Fix the base before you expand upon it. Less emergency patching to do, less downtime, less unhappy customers.

You have to remember this FunCom....the people who pay to play this game are the ones that keep you working on it. Shape the game how WE the PLAYERS want it, and you'll KEEP the players no matter what game comes out.....

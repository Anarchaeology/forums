Notes, notes, notes!!
I find it inherently advantageous to be able to use something as simple as a notepad for passing msgs, yet at the same time you can RP the passing of a note. Whenever a game is limited in the type of interaction between players, and concentrates on a combat based xp system players are what must become inventive rather than relying on the system or world you live on ie Anarchy Online - RK1 or RK2.

Game balance, "power" gamers, bad players, young players, old players, classes, mobs, and everything else that helps create a rich world is all based on one thing: The individual at the keyboard.

Paper based tabletop RP games have a simple pro/con out look. Pro: you are guided by another live human with whom you interact, and who tells you the results of your actions. Con: You can only "see" the world in your own imagination. AO has a similar pro/con out look. Pro: The world is completely viewable on your computer monitor, there is very little imagination needed. Con: Interaction, or changing of that defined world by either one individual or group of individuals out of millions.

If you want to be able to create missions, I totally support that. As I support the ability for written, or digital information to be able to be passed between players, but I also advocate understanding and tolerance concerning the immensity of the game world in which that interaction will take place.

Also, with regards to the Bounty/Party ingame forum. I honestly dont' see the difficulty in having something like that implemented. It is already impossible to loot a mob that you havent killed, so why would it be any more difficult to have it detect a bounty killed player having to sit at a regen term, and the player that just killed them recieve the bounty that was posted along with the "hit"?

To further explain: I walk up to the bounty term, I right-click to activate and then I click on "Place Bounty" I then have the option to input a username. That shouldn't be too difficult consider you can shift-leftclick and find out any info that you can detect already. THEN before the bounty is posted, you have to add a cred amount out of your account. It gets deducted, and you see the msg "Your Bounty on (player) has been posted for (XXX) Credits. Thank you."

NOW, this player is running because anyone who is able to team up for xp is able to accept that mission. I think the current system allows +/- 3 lvls for everyone to still recieve xp.

Oh, and of course WHO placed the bounty is completely visible, so now it works both ways.

I just thought for a second that perhaps a lower lvl could go after a higher lvl char for assassination purposes, but the PvP system would have to be majorly improved first. There is no way my 20lvl Enforcer can take out any char 10 - 15 lvls higher than me. Period.

I think that about covers my thoughts on this particular thread.

As much as I love this idea, and wholeheartedly support anything that encourages more RP, this really isn't feasible. Here's why.

The item database is quite large as it is. Every item in the game has a specific place in this item database. Any minute changes to these items grants them their own entry. Now imagine that everyone on RK1 decided that they were gonna write a note to a friend to try it out. They go to the store and buy a Notepad. This notepad's item database number is something like 1Q2456. As soon as that player adds his message and saves it, there is now an entry in the database called 1Q2457. The next person that does it generates a 1Q2458, ad infinitum.

For this to be a workable idea, the database as it is now would have to be reworked to be a dynamic database, with players, or rather certain items, being given a read/write capability for the database. Can you imagine how large it would become overnight? Everybody would want to try out the notes for themselves, so you could very well have 50000 extra database entries over a period of a day or two. I highly doubt Funcom has the resources to program a dynamic database with selective entry control, much less the resources to house one.

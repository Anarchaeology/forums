Harassment?
I can't recall any point where any behaviour towards me could be considered harrasment. I think that most online RPGers (not just in AO) are civil and courteous enough to know where the line is, andk know the difference between flirting and harassment.

I'm really surprised that anyone saw 'male behaviour' in 'touch my breasts again and I'm going to kick you in the nads' - that seems like the most sensible response to that kind of thing to me.

When I played EQ I ran a bit of an experiment with gender and observations. What I found was that (surprising results here!) males and females get treated differently.

You are more likely to get a favourable 'first impression' from another player as a female avatar. You are a little more likely to get gifts or handouts, and there are a subset of players who will make more effort to be your friend than try and KS/whatever you out of an area. But the difference is negligible - mostly it comes down to how you actually behave.

Flirting is a fact of life. In real life, and in game. I guess this may come as a surprise for most male players to find out 'life on the other side of the gender barrier' - and maybe it'll be an interesting social lesson - but on the whole flirting tends to stop there.

For me, in all my roleplaying experience, I've only once had an instance where I've had to tell someone to back off. And they did. In fact, they were rather apologetic when they stopped and thought a bit.

As for the whole dance debate? Dancing can be (but isn't always) a very arousing act. Table dancing in very skimpy clothing can be arousing for both the dancer and the viewer - without it being 'exotic'. However, just because someone looks good and dances well and is sexy doesn't give anyone the right to 'have a feel' without first getting the consent of the dancer. Which usually involves a bit more than asking 'can I feel yer tits?'

These kinds of games provide a rather interesting opportunity for many people - the chance to 'experiment' socially with the cover of anonymity to hide past mistakes. While some people abuse this, in many cases it's preferable that people make mistakes in a virtual environment.

Having someone emote feeling your tits without consent is unpleasant, but I'd say a lot less unpleasant than someone actually doing just that in real life. Better they learn proper social behaviour in an environment where the amount of harm they can cause is limited to words on screen.

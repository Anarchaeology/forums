more healer tips

make a macro to assist the tank (on name)
then make a second macro that just says /assist

1) Hit the tank assist macro
2) Hit q (this will prevent you from whacking possible adds that might go after you so that they can be calmed)
3) Hit the general assist macro to see who is getting whacked by the mob you are fighting.

Repeat 1&3 while you are fighting the same mob, in combination with the F2 to F6 keys and the team screen (<ctrl-5>) and there should never be any casualties that can be blamed on you.

A good puller will also check to see if everyone is ready, before taking off to pull. Kind of like a pitcher in a baseball game performing a quick practiced scan of the other players. Performing a quick scan of at least the health bars will tell you if the doc has his nanos charged up, and maybe if everyone is healed, or if anyone is absent.

You have to admit that most teams are just pick-up teams. Too often my MA or Trader is supposed to be healer, puller, and meatshield. Don't know how many times I have asked, "Who is pulling?" and get no response at all.

The most common cause of deaths are adds. The easiest way to draw adds is to open doors to unknown areas. My tip..... do not stand near doors while the team is engaged with a mob!!

I can't even count the times a team member will stand next to a door to an unexplored area, and the door will auto open, while the team is engaged in combat.

What most always happens is the mob in that next room, now has a clear LOS (line of sight) and can se into your fight room.

So he either rushes in, and/or starts calling out to other nearby mobs at the same time, this may become one of many adds.If you have made a macro the says "add", maybe you had better see what you or your teammates are doing to cause so adds you even have a macro ready.

Another way of drawing adds, is to fight mobs in the big main room of a mission. Same deal as above. Mobs have a way of also auto opening doors as the pass by them. Fighting in the main room, will almost always add a mob, or two, or three or etc... to the fight.

A good idea is to have the Soldier be the one carrying the mission, especially if you aren't going to kill the boss. Before you can kill level 185ish bosses regularly you might often as not end up with an unbreakable boss room. A team of 6 can kill single level 185 mobs all day long way before they can break a level 185 boss room.

Idea:

Soldier grabs a mission.

You all head there, clean it out. (as described above.) Soldier cast his highest mirror shield, pops up to boss room and looks at it. (Wearing health buffs, hot and what not to ensure survival no matter what.) If it looks reasonable (ie not four Real Mean Enforcers) he goes down. Team waits for shield to reset and go up. Clean out the normal mobs.

If it looks insane, he just finishes the mission off.

Definition of insane: 4+ enforcers with high mods. (Real Means, Seasoned, what ever makes YOUR life difficult.) Mobs that hits an AC where tanks are bad. Meta phys are often like that early on. And before aggro is established randomly debuffing mps can put a dent in any docs day...

Newbie team etiquette
Here are some thoughts from someone who's played mostly solo up until recently (i.e., I'm a teaming >>> n00b <<<):

Etiquette: if you're the newb on the team, someone is bound to lose his/her temper at you, because you'll do something foolish/fail to do something smart. Give the more experienced team members the benefit of the doubt: fess up readily if you recognize an error when they start shouting at you. That usually has a way of calming tempers a little; generally, it's a sign of maturity. If they continue to shout or start insulting, leave: you probably don't want to team with that type to begin with.

Also: if you're shopping for a team and you get an invite, warn the team that you're a newb and ask if they still want you on. If they don't have the patience for newbs, they can say, "sorry, no newbs thanks; nothing personal" and spare everyone a bit of grief (that's an admonition to the more experienced players, BTW.) Maybe even advertise on the team channels that you're a "~newb~ lvl xx <prof> lft." Might force you into a catch-22; but you'll avoid teaming with experienced players with little patience, and you might inspire other experienced players to "take you under their wing." In short, be as choosey with teaming as anyone else.

If you're daunted by going on team missions, do some co-op hunting to get the feel for team dynamics.

One other thought: my guess is that asking for advice in the middle of a team mission is a bad idea. Some matters might be appropriate; but I suspect that general expectation for team mission participants is that everyone has a good general idea of what he/she is/will be doing.

To summarize: being up-front and calm, and giving other players the benefit of the doubt should go a long way to avoid bickering and propagate playing. Going in as prepared as possible should reduce occasions for bickering in the first place. But if it comes to the point that you're just taking crap, walk away.

Comments from experienced teamers appreciated

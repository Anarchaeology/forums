I'm not saying that the polls are a good tool for pinpointing exactly what's wrong and what's right with a patch (to continue with your example). Those things will show up in the forums. But they are a far better tool to measure the general feeling about a subject.

If you read the forum, you'd sometimes think there's nothing good about this game at all. The problems are addressed by a very vocal part of the population, whereas the things that are good are either not addressed or overshadowed by the sheer volume of complaint posts.

I think polls can offer a more balanced insight into the entire playerbase's general feelings about certain subjects than the boards. And I for one like seeing the results.

Yes and no.

I sure agree that if all you did was read these Forums, you'd shut the game down as totally unplayble and totally beyond repair. I for one think that would be a big misunderstanding. And I also agree that the Forums are used by a special segment of the population, and that people are most likely to report their problems here rather than the things they like and the things that keep them coming back.

I also agree that you want a balanced understanding of your playerbase's general feelings about the game, and the various aspects of it. Last, I also like to see what the playerbase thinks of various things.

However, in my opinion polls are not the vehicle to give you what you want. Further, Funcom already has the best tap into people's general feelings of the game, and that is the subscription information.

I admit, though, that polls are not totally useless. If you're looking for an instantaneous public sentiment thermometer, then they may work for you. The point is that is all they are, so Funcom cannot (sensibly) use the information to change anything they do. Which means, they're a kind of PR tool.

Myself, I'm interested in tools that give you information that you can use to understand what's going on, and therefore can be used to change what you do. This is what I'd like to see Funcom using, because it would indicate that they're operating in some sort of scientific manner.

In short then, polls are for fun. And that's just fine. But I get the impression from some of these polls, that somebody in Funcom thinks that by running them they're gaining information that they can use to change what they do. And that's totally dumb.

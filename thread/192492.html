An open letter to Philip Ross and the Omni-Tek Board
Times change, there is no denying that. Since the opening of the Shadowlands portal, both sides of the conflict on Rubi-Ka have washed over the lands of Nascence, Elysium, Scheol, Adonis and even Penumbra, hoping to add the secrets of the ancient Xan to their respective arsenals. Soon I believe that notum mining technology will be adapted to be able to harvest the valuable liquid notum that permeates the Shadowlands. Perhaps soon mass production uses for the mysterious novictum will be found and that too will be the subject of a new chapter in the Rubi-Kan land war.

But the hidden technologies of the Xan are not the only secrets that have been found in the Shadowlands. Other, more modern secrets hide there for those willing to look. These secrets will build until a critical mass is reached, a point where truth can no longer be denied. I write this letter to hasten that time.

Policy-makers of Omni-Tek, I ask you in good faith to answer my questions. Your employees deserve these answers; they deserve to know where their company stands.

On the subject of the Unredeemed/Inamorta/Grand, it is obvious to anyone visiting the Shadowlands that these dark beings have chosen to ally themselves with Omni-Tek, lending their support and knowledge in return for manpower and reconstruction. Surely the status of this relationship warrants a statement? If not to the world then at least to your employees.

I see many Omni-Tek employees assisting the Unredeemed/Inamorta/Grand, seemingly accepting orders as if these beings held rank in the company. I see other employees hesitant to obey; after all, they have received no instructions from the hierarchy as to the protocol in these situations. One would think that blurred lines of hierarchy such as this would be counter to the discipline and order that Omni-Tek prides itself upon.

However, I can understand Omni-Tek's reluctance. After all, it appears that the Unredeemed are not the only immortals frequenting the Shadowlands. I speak, of course, of the fabled Omega. As I have seen with my own eyes, the Omega of old are not only still alive, but in the employ of the more powerful Unredeemed/Inamorta/Grand. Regardless of how you view history, this is no small matter. Perhaps you trust Omni-Tek's association to be for the better good; perhaps you do not. Either way the re-appearance of such ancient beings cannot be taken lightly. Neither should this matter go uncommented upon by Omni-Tek. Surely your well-educated employees deserve to know how this fits into history, how it applies to them as descendants of the race these very same Omega enslaved so long ago.

Moving to more recent history; the first exploration of Rubi-Ka, and the speech given by Philip Ross at the Planetfall Address. I quote:
quote:
Originally posted by Philip Ross
Nay, heroes we must not forget. [...] Let us remember Min-Ji Liu, the first human to ever set foot on Rubi-Ka's soil
And yet, Min-Ji Liu still lives... after a fashion. Allied with the Redeemed/Faithful, she tries to protect the being know as Enel, while battling damage done to her soul by the Omega, Eric Miller, who in turn defends the being know as Roch. What to say about this? She is lauded as a hero of Omni-Tek and yet she supports those who have become, by proxy, the enemies of Omni-Tek. Surely after such a stirring speech, the matter of Min-Ji's life and her allegiance should be commented upon?

These three matters are just the surface, there are many more secrets yet to unearth. Would it not be better for Omni-Tek to assuage the fears of their employees, and announce where they and their company stands?

To end, let me be clear as to my own affiliation: I support the Clans. I have been made bitter by the old conflict. I knew where I stood, but I feel that these new secrets revealed by the opening of the Shadowlands are so important that they may well change many perspectives on the Rubi-Kan conflict: Omni, Neutral and Clan. As such, these issues need to be brought out in the open as soon as possible. I have my own opinions on the motivations of both Omni-Tek's and the Clans' involvement in the Shadowlands, but in this article I leave them aside and call only for official comment on these most important events.

Letha "Vedanthi" Norn

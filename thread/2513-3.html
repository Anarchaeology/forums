FC had planned on A assassination attempt on Radiman. These are the 'transmissions' overheard in TAG. However, contrary to Funcom's plans, a player (Aberic) assassinated him first. Rather than contact Aberic and work him in as the assassin, they made up this story about a clone decoy. Oh, wait...TWO clone decoys; one for Aberic, and one for the 'real' assassin, because their predestined storyline had the 'real' assassin failing as well.

Since word of Aberic's attempt had already spread to far for them to squelch, FC came up with the speed-e-trial(tm) for Aberic, perhaps as a deterrent to other 'griefer' assassins. Aberic and Truwind were given slaps on their wrists, and Funcom continues on with it's predetermined storyline.

Funcom did not even consider that a player might attempt to 'interfere' with the storyline, thus Aberic took them completely by suprise. There is no indication (as far as I can see) that any actions commited by players to date will have any impact on the storyline.

I can understand how some say 'Funcom is telling a story', and I agree to a point; the story of Elaina, Radiman, and the Agent, Joshua, is a character-driven story, and it is their right to tell it as they see fit.

On the other hand, this is not the only story. What of Omni vs. Clan? We sit on the Funcom sanctioned Faction Leader Councils, arguing for or against the amnesty, and yet our voices make no difference.

If Funcom wants to tell the story of Elaina et al., let them do so, but against the background of OUR actions. Funcom's story should be flexible enough that, should WE take action, the story is only marginally effected. If a guild is brought up on charges by a Faction Council, have Radiman (or Ross) mention it in the movies during their chess match. We don't have to be the 'stars' of the movie, but toss us a frickin' bone here....

When I play pen & paper RPGs, I get off on making a difference in the game world. Sure, maybe the GM fudged a roll or two to make sure I could make it to the lowest level of the dungeon. OK, maybe he guided my decisions once or twice so it fit his vision of how the story should go. However, he always worked it so that, if my character died sacrificing himself for the village, his heroism would become legendary. My character's teammates would only have to mention his name and doors would open for them. His actions had impact...

anyway, just my 2 cents...

Massive interactive storylines
I have no problem with the storytelling model AO has chosen. Nor with the story they have chosen to tell in that model. As both a writer and a game designer I believe that trying to destroy the narrative you are participating in is the grossest form of disrespect.

So, for all those who went OOC in an RP situation (The Trial): do any of you think that you are able to do the job better than FC? Or do you think that by trying to destroy the system you are somehow improving matters? Or that by going OOC and screaming about "how much this game sucks" you are creating a better experience?

Seriously people - if we all pulled together and participated constructively the whole experience would benefit. But, I fear, there are far too many frustrated gamers too worried about being uber to actual show any respect for that FC is doing...

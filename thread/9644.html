My idea is for Funcom to generate a protocol to handle a particular task such as generating a dungeon layout. Your routine has certain inputs and generates a specific output. When you think it is ready, then you turn your code over to Funcom, they then use as much or as little as they want.

Another idea

How about letting players create quests. Players can already communicate through the game via code like the Shopbot does. What if Funcom published a protocol that let code control an NPC. You would have to get registered to do it and if there were problems Funcom could take your NPC away. Now players/guilds could have a persistent presence in the world.

For instance if you wanted to get in touch with a guild you could contact its NPC which could rebroadcast you tell on a guild channel. The NPCs could be set up to handle player created quests or guard a position. Imagine attacking a guild controlled NPC and then having that entire guild come to its defense. The possibilities are endless which is the point. You will not just have a team of three(?) people trying to add content but hundreds. The world would really seem alive with constantly changing NPCs offering up new quests, information or just plain atmosphere.

This is too cool not to do.

Player-run Shops
I have no idea why I'm posting this here, other than that Crin started it by posting his (interesting) suggestion So here goes:

I'm kinda partial to the idea of putting all those unoccupied buildings in town to good use by allowing players to set up shops in them to sell their wares. Since there are few buildings in relation to the number of potential sellers, you might even allow several sellers to occupy the same building, but put them all in their own virtual rooms in the same way missions are handled now. Then you have an NPC "barker" outside that constantly whispers (or says or shouts, but I like whispers) a one-line advertisement for each occupant that describes what he or she is selling. If someone wants to enter a shop, he or she just clicks on the barker (or tries to enter the building), at which point a menu of current occupants is offered up. The buyer chooses one and then enters the appropriate virtual room.

This accomplishes several things:

1) It gives the population some reason to move around the city more instead of always planting their butts in the same boring spots. I.e. it gives the rest of the city some reason for existing (and reduces frame rate drop at the same time!)

2) It allows players interested in trading as an occupation to set up shop in some predictable location that customers could always find.

3) Kinda related to #1, it would make city life seem more alive and exciting, as customers walked the streets and byways, listening to all the barkers to see if they could find what they're looking for, or maybe happen upon a deal.

4) It might reduce some of the shopping chat traffic, although I imagine vendors would still use that tool in addition to the barkers. If I had my way, I'd eliminate the ability to trade as we currently do altogether and implement this system exclusively. That way we'd be forced to go shopping at a store in town just as in real life, instead of unrealistically mind-beaming our buy and sell offers to everyone in the known universe. Making this system exclusive would also have the effect of creating and encouraging dedicated player merchants, whose occupation would be buying players' loot for resale to others (since presumably not all, or even most, players want to be bothered with the mechanics of trade; they just want to dump their stuff for a decent price and get on with the killing). This would solve the problem of people having to accept crap deals from NPC merchants if they don't want to bother with trade themselves.

5) It might be a decent system for forming teams. Players seeking a team or teams seeking members could set up shop and advertise what they were looking for. Anyone could then just enter their shop and negotiate or join up.

6) If anyone wanted to be an entertainer of some sort, this would allow him or her to set up a place to perform. It might even be possible to have the barker charge admission of those seeking to enter.

DOWNSIDE:

1) It could get extremely boring sitting in your one-room cave waiting for customers. But then again, there's always chat, and it's not that far different from sitting on the grass in the middle of town doing the same thing, so who knows?

Anyway, sorry for going on. It's just an idea I had. Thanks for listening.

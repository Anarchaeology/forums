Mr. Godager,


I feel that your article is rather vague for me to formulate an opinion on your proposal. Perhaps this was done for the sake of clarity.

For example, most weapons have more than one requirement to initially wield them. Yet, your article only addressed a single requirement in your example of the fictitious Rifle of Quality Level 25. For the sake of argument, lets assume it that rifle also had a 53 Aimed Shot requirement.

Will the overequipping calculation factor in a weapons secondary skill(s) or will it only check against the primary weapon skill?

Specifically, if a character had 75 rifle, and 53 aimed shot, would the OE Q ( 75 + 53 ) / ( 100 + 53 ) be a 16% OE Q? Or, will it check each one individualy and take the maximum OE Q and use that to determine the overall weapon reduction?

75 / 100 = 25% OE Q
53 / 53 = 0% OE Q

So you end up with a 25% OE Q.

Then there is the issue of weapons that have multiple primary weapon skills. Will you use the composite or will check each primary weapon skill to determine the weapons final OE Q?

Might I suggest that it might be better to use the existing skill damage curves. These are currently only used to increase damage based on the wielders current skill, to also reduce a weapon damage.

If the OE Q goes above a certain point, say 20% the skill based weapon damage modifer could actually start reducing a weapons damage. This means that there aren't the cutoff points that are present under your current proposal. I think it is better that there be a continual and gradual decrease in weapon damage potential as the OE Q increases.

Finally, what about weapon critical damage modifier? Is it also going to be reduced by 25% along with the min and max weapon damage?

In closing, I also feel that the OE Q should be listed on the Status window next to the attack skill number. This way we know exactly what the OE Q figure is. Having to open the wear screen is check if the weapon box is red or not is rather cumbersome and there is no way to know exactly what the OE Q figure is.

I am looking forward to further clarifications about your proposed solution. There are many unanswered questions that your article raised and at this point I am awaiting clarification prior to formulating a opinion about this proposal.

About the IP Wipe ...
I wish people would read Gaute's article thoroughly before going off on a rant about the IP wipe. For those who didn't before they ranted, here it is again but in the words of my NT character.

Basically, the IP wipe does two things ...

1. It allocates to your character (whatever it is) the IPs your character would have at its current level, without equipment, without implants, and without buffs.

Calculating IP points is trivial math because IPs are given at fixed values for a fixed number of levels. For example, Level 1 to 9 gets 1500 IPs per level; therefore, your level 10 character would receive (9 x 1500) + 4000 = 17500 IPs.

You should not lose any IPs if the math is done accurately.

But what this calculation does is nerf the extra points your character got by using "IP Rings". If you were one of the dupers who exploited IP Rings, you lose ... you are now just like all the rest of us.

If your character found three or four IP Rings and used them, your character (like my NT character) will lose 3000 to 4000 IPs from his or her IP pool. My NT character can live with that.

If you did not or could not use IP Rings, you get the same IP amount you had before, except the IPs are there in one big pool for you to assign to your character again.

If you were saving IPs, you will not lose them because they are part of the total IP pool you got originally.

2. It resets ALL your abilities and skills (except for Map Navigation) to their Level 1 values. Yes, that statement (or words to that effect) is in Gaute's post so go back and read it again!

What this does is let you rebuild your character the way you want it to be for the "new" game rules.

Resetting all abilities and skills back to Level 1 prevents some gamers from dumping IPs into dark blue skills so they can "cheat" later on.

The IP Wipe is not something to be feared; it is a good thing because you cannot lose any skills that you cannot rebuild, and you should have enough IPs to rebuild any special abilities or skills your character needs.

Hope this helps ...

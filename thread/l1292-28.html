Ah, imagine a system where not every class has equal combat abilities. This is AO. When a level 20 Soldier who is devoted to combat skills can whoop-ass on a level 60 engineer who didn't buy much combat skills, you will start to respect the melee classes more. Since weapons and armor are limited by skill usually, this means that only classes with good IP costs will be able to develop their combat skills high enough to use the really nice weapons/armor. I think you'll be happy as a fighting class when you can spend IP to get your own healing skill, or IP to raise your own engineering skill, NF skills, or whatnot. You will by no means be "just a fighter". You can learn other things too.

I've playing many pen and paper role-playing games that used a skill system like AO. In these systems the fighter-types kick major butt compared to everyone else. The rest of the group ends up trusting their lives to the massively dangerous and tough warriors. I think this will be the case in AO as well. Expect high level combat classes to be greatly respected for their prowess.

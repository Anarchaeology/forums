The etiquette of looting is a tough one. Do you open the chests so the folks without the BnE can have a crack or do you leave them closed so no mobs/bombs can surprise the team? When the team's in a headlong rush for exp do you pause to loot or let it rot to keep up?

It's not easy. I've been on all sides of this. My fixer can crack the chests AND catch up. If I open a couple chests, and the team is long gone, and I'm just leaving stuff there - and I KNOW we're all gridding out in the end cuz I'm the one that's going to be asked- feels like i'm wasting time and loot. So of course I'm going to start grabbing it all. I can offer 'anyone anything I find' but most people are too shy to take someone up on that and they'll just quietly resent the 'ninja looter'. Or I can demand we pause to sniff the boxes but then the headlong exp'ers get upset. "If you need the money that bad I'll pay you" some guy said recently. The guy doesn't realize I bought my first yalm a year before he bought his first metaplast. "Sure, you got 160 M for each of us? There might be some GA4 in here." Looting is an enjoyable part of the game to me, and yet ANOTHER hyper kid who wants to set speed records in the mish does not impress me... specially when they aren't watching the map.

So here's my take on the topics I've seen so far and a few that weren't mentioned (not sure they're all etiquette so much as methods/tactics/codes of conduct):
1) if its your turn in alpha loot the loot is yours, good or bad, that's that.
2) boxes and other items - there is NO rule - just be sensible and try to balance it with the team and the mish, ('need before greed' is what I hear from people who NEED all the uber loot, regardless of any credible prof issues - or even capacity to use, so that's always a flag to me that the person is a greedy hyprocrite, once asked someone why they 'needed' some stuff more than another guy who could actually use it, 'cuz i'm saving up to buy my HNT!' .... uhhh, yeah),
3) always help out the docs, grab the nanos, the armor, etc for them and hand it to them,
4) if you get alpha loot you don't want (ring quest stuff, armor), let the team know so someone can get back to it,
5) ALWAYS offer to roll the uber loot (NCU now, rare nanos, etc), even if you don't like your teamates
6) tanking - any situation that's being entered into should have only one tank, he/she should lead, no matter how good you are, the team will do better if the tank is clear, it's delicate (can alternate room to room) but it MUST be clear, teams die when tanks vie,
7) the tank is responsible for the team's attack success, if the tank runs into the middle of a new floor with his team and a bunch of you get cut down by adds then it's the TANK'S fault, if you've got some newb soldi/enf at the helm who doesn't understand how to adjust for situations (you PULL the mobs in a fresh floor (main room) unless you're 50 levels higher than them [or you've got a calmer who actually CAN calm adds in a timely fashion] ... and sometimes even then) then you either teach them or take over the lead or you kick them ('sorry dude, you're going to kill us'). The tank has got to watch the map and consider his team. My GA fixer can take a world of abuse and evac before he dies usually, this doesn't mean I haul into the center of a fresh floor and let it rip or charge into a cam/mob/turret combo room. So what if half the team dies? So it means I screwed up BIG TIME. I've seen too many players (up to and including guys that made 200 months ago) who judge the team's situation based solely on what they will survive. It's selfish, incompetent and most assuredly does not impress.
8) if you're not an HP/evade heavy (GA fixer or high MA) class then don't pretend to tank, you'll just burn out the doc and someone will die. HP/GA affords wiggle room. In its absence a single add and the doc is going to be forced to choose who's going to die when the tank doesn't possess that 'wiggle room' since nothing but dedicated healing will keep that 'tank lite' alive.
9) the doc decides the level of the mish if they want to - end of story. A good doc is going to gauge his past experience, look at his team, and judge what he thinks they can handle... if he cares. So if a doc states a preferred level for a mish this is not an issue for argument. Most docs suffer from the desire to please and to help in excess of what's good for a team. An adamant enf/soldi might bully them in to accepting a mission that will kill a few guys (usually not them). Doesn't work for me at all. I have tanks, I have docs: I have a clue. When I'm doccing I'll tell the wannabe tanker who INSISTS on running a mish 20 levels higher than I think we can handle to walk, just walk, "We're doing 140, you don't like it then walk". A lot of docs won't agitate on this and that's fine, they'll say 'whatever'. But if they DO say it, don't argue with them. If you win you'll poisoning the mission and probably kill a squishy prof. If you lose you'll be pissed and if the doc's me, he'll be inviting you to leave.

I tend to use one simple rule for all things involved with groups and teamwork concepts, I call it Rule One.

Rule One
All of your actions; good and bad, big and small, profound or mundane...will be remembered by someone. The greater the impact of those actions, the longer the memory of them will last and the more people will hear of them.


There are jerks out there, to be sure. But their actions quickly get them branded as jerks and word of mouth is a powerful tool. Every game has heroes and zeroes, it is up to the individual which end of that spectrum they wish to be on. Personally, I like to be well thought of and liked, so I play towards the hero side. Common sense and listening to more experienced players go a real long way in this game.

IMHO, YMMV.

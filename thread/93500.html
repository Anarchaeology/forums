Barriers to roleplaying and FC
I had a think about what I'd consider the major difficulties of roleplaying in AO (and by extension other mmorpgs). My background is with table top roleplaying games. People coming from LRP or the MUD/MUSH world may well have very different insights.

After each issue I've posed the question, "What can FunCom do to help?" I have not really come up with much, but its an open question for everyone to chip and throw in ideas.


1. You cannot affect the environment.
2. You cannot control the environment.
3. Your character is just one of many.
4. The language of online roleplaying.
5. The dynamics of online roleplaying.
6. Getting started.


1. You cannot affect the environment.
In p&p roleplaying, what the players do changes the world around them, whether it's just cleaning the hood from drug dealers of blowing up a planet. The p&p GM shapes the world around you. By contrast, there is very little you can do in AO that has any lasting effect on the world around you.

What can FC do to help?
FC has from time to time given out one off items (pioneer backs, Childhood Memories) and I think even a few titles to players. The posters in the Subway to give credit to players of groups of who have made exceptional contributions to the community. But its a tricky road. A lot of player are wary of any display of favouritism and any solution has to be cost-effective for the developers. We can't really have players blowing up Tir.

2. You cannot control the environment.
In p&p roleplaying the GM controls the environment completely. He can throw in monsters (mobs) and any point and in any number he wishes, cause earthquakes or just maybe just an simple ice-cream vendor to appear. He can skip over a boring journey in a second, take the players to the Bahamas one week and Venus the next. Obviously there are limits with what you can do with AO's game engine, but the real limiting factor is the number of story based GMs. FC cannot supply 1 GM for every 5-6 players, this is something we have to do for ourselves.

What can FC do about this?
Szentasha has time and again listed a number of tools and props that would help players run their own adventures as in special roleplaying mission settings. Naturally it would have to be abuse-proof.

3. Your character is just one of many.
In a p&p game, the players are very much the protagonists of the story. In a mmorpg, no matter how well known you character is, you are still just one face in the crowd. It's a difficult transition to make. Also, in a p&p game your enemies are all controlled by the GM, and as such a disposable. If you play your cards right, you will eventually be able to totally defeat your foes. In AO, your enemies are often another set of players (OT/Clan or police/criminals), who are also the protagonists of their own stories. How are you going to get any decisive victories or major turning points under these conditions?

What can FC do about this?
On rare occasion FC will show some measure of recognition of players or player storylines. Should they do this more often, bearing in mind this always opens them up to accusations of favouritism.

4. The language of online roleplaying.
The "language" (as in the tricks, techniques and conventions) of online roleplaying is still very young. When you start playing p&p games, it's usually with a group of more experienced players and you quickly learn the ropes just be watching them. The rulebook itself is likely to have a chapter about how to roleplay while off the shelf scenario packs will show you how to structure your adventure. Basically, though we may take it for granted, there is a lot in terms of established conventions, general know-how and traditions attached to p&p roleplaying which have grown and evolved since the 1970s. This "language" is not as well developed for mmorpgs yet. While it is easy enough even for a beginner roleplayer to grasp the concept of being in character, moving on to the next step and kicking off some player generate roleplaying storylines is much , much harder.

What can FC do?
In the time I've played AO, they have only published one article on roleplaying in AO, and that was mostly to tell us not to mess with the official storyline. I think they could do a lot more here. They could come up with a few example scenarios for players to try out, maybe Arks could offer one off coaching sessions for orgs interesting in trying these things.

5. The dynamics of roleplaying
The dynamics of roleplaying in an online game are very, very different from a p&p game. The environment in which a p&p game occurs is very structured. It usually involves a fixed set of players, usually real life friends, with similar level characters who meet up to play at regular times, say once week. Outside these times nothing much happens to your characters. You only play if the GM and most of the players are present. Most important of all, you know something is going to happen every single gaming session because the GM is there to make it so.

By contrast the roleplaying environment in a extremely fluid, not to say chaotic. Player turnover is a real killer. I've seen many player generated storylines die because one of the lead characters decided at that point to quit AO. I've even seen entire guilds collapse halfway through talks on joint events. Trying to get enough players in the right place at a set time is a major headache, you end up exhausted even before the event starts whereas if you try to keep the organisation of the roleplaying session fairly loose, you just end up with people logging in at random times and often missing each other. And even if you do pull off a good roleplaying session, as the average AO player logs on almost every day, the pressure is always on to come up with the next idea, the next twist in the story.

What can FC do?
For me this has always been the hardest part of keeping an, alive a vibrant roleplaying environment. I am not sure FC can do anything all about it. The only thing I can think off is persistent chat groups which could allow for easy cross guild communications (something people have developed bots for.)

6. Getting Started.
Time and time again players will say there is little if any roleplaying on AO, they don't know how to get started roleplaying and worse of accuse the existing roleplaying groups of being cliquish. As a result a number of roleplayers give up on AO before even getting started.

What can FC do? The most important thing has already been done: they saved our roleplaying forum.


Anyway, if you have other thoughts or suggestions, post away.

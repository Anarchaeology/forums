Obviously, a sample of 400 shots is enough to come up with a reliable average. 125 is not.

Why?

Not sure the exact stats on a QL 101 BBI, but I know a QL 63 BBI is 1-150 damage range. On my level 50 Advent, it has a max damage of ~180 on an average green mob.

This means that no sample under 150-180 shots would be a realiable sample, as it does not give the *opportunity* for every possible outcome to occur.

A test of 400 shots, however, DOES.

A QL 99 BBI in the database is showing as 1-220, so any test under ~225 with QL 101's would be a below-optimal set of data.

400 shots is 181% of the possible range, and a pretty good sample set. 200% would be ideal, as would 300% or higher. However, I would say that 400 shots is more than enough to warrant a decent sample set.

Critical, min, and max damage are not good comparisons, because they are always the same. I believe the issue here is if Funcom's multi-wielding routine lowers your AVERAGE damage range or not. Such a thing would have no affect on your potential min/max/crit over a large sample set, as those are always the same in any possible situation.

Now, Tetra has measured a ~4% increase in damage (or decrease, depending on how you want to look at it) when comparing average single/dual damage. Obviously, this puts dual-wielding at an advantage, as a ~20% increase in speed would put the total advantage at ~15%.

HOWEVER, this is at equal QL's. Since Funcom doesn't intend for people to be able to wield equal-QL pistols. If you have to equip QL 180 with a QL 200, you have gone and lost AT LEAST ~10% damage, perhaps more due to critical damage.

So, where does that put dual wielding with pistols? An advantage of, at most, ~5% in the Funcom-intended situation.

Now, you tell me... Does ~5% warrant the spent IP on multi-range, for one. (I would say "perhaps") The kicker question is, however, does it make up for the fact that pistols are substantially weaker than other 1-handed ranged weapons? (I would say, not at all.)

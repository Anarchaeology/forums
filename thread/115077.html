Thoughts...

When running a storyline in AO, I use a mix of in game events and posts on a message board ( our orgs private message board or Funcom's if appropriate). The in game events can be as simple as a meeting in which you discuss in-character the latest developments of the plot, or can be more complex, action orientated which might involve travel, fighitng or avoiding dagerous mobs, PvP (not that I ever resorted to PvP but its a possibility). The message boards posts are to cover those things that are difficult or dull to play out in game (like a huge space battle or just 36 hour stake-out). It is also very useful to write up a summary of past in game events so that those players who could not make it can still flow the story.

I take all sorts of liberties with the AO official setting, but mostly I tend to add bits where the official background does not cover rather than directly contradict anything. So, for instance in my previous guild, we decided that 'leet fighting' (sort of like dog fighting) was illegal in OT lands but that the World Leet Fighting Federation still ran clandestine fights. A few people turned around and told us the there was nothing illegal about leet fighting, but most roleplayers were willing to go play along.

One area where a lot of people play fast-and-loose with the rules is suppresssion gas and perma-death. Some people assume there are masks or drugs you can take to bypass the suppression gas, and perma-death weapons or fixers who can hack insurance have become a dime a dozen. I guess death or at least the threat of death is such a cornerstone of most traditional roleplaying scenarios, it gets very hard to do anything if you were to play this strictly by the rules. I personally think its bad form to abuse insurance; if one walks through evrey scenario with a "it doesn't matter what happens, I am insured" attitude, its not going to add a whole lot to the sense of drama. Talking about "insurance accidents" and longterm effects of repeteat ressurections can help in these occasions.

The lines between players and GM (as in a player running the story, not a FC GM) are lot more blurred. Moreover a lot of ropleplayers try to open their adventures to the roleplaying community at large. All this amounts to a more fluid sort of story than maybe you;d get in a table top rpg. All this suggests is keep your story flexible.

Finally, if you are roleplaying with strangers DO make ooc contact at some point. It may seem inelegant but it prevents so many misunderstandings.

As a very last though, check out the RP FAQ in this forum. I'll bump it for you.

An idea me and a few others did a year ago
When I first started playing AO I got started roleplaying and me and a few others had a great idea. Here is what we did...

6 of us were the main chars (we had 1 or 2 who couldn't be there every session, so they were npcs). We all roled new chars (untwinked) and RPed them all the time. Our chars were only used du;ring the sessions to insure that levels would remain about the same- the NPCS would level more or less depending on the situation. We didn't have a GM, so most of our RPing was through talking.

This was the greatest experience I have had on RK so far. I would love to do something like this again. Anyway, give me a tell in game on RK2 if you are interested in starting anything, or if you just want me as an NPC.

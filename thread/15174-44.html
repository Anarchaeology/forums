My Thoughts - Long and Detailed
I have thought about this issue a lot since I first heard about it. I am a serious overequipper. I had an enforcer with a 200 hammer on at some ungodly low level and who currently wields a 200 beam at 108, and a level 63 soldier with a QL 155 Nova Flow, and a MP who won't really be affected.

A 20% margin will give those that like to OE some margin to OE at higher levels. At low levels, it still might be advantageous with some weapons to OE up to 61% before the 50% damage reduction sets in, and take the 75% damage cut. I won't comment on engies pets, that pretty much sux for those of you used to getting mochams and wrangles for the big pet.

I guess my question is:

Why do we have steps? Why does the code have to look like

if (skill_have/skill_req)*100 < 80 then Damage=75%.

Why can't it just simply be a linear equation that your damage is reduced based on what your skill level is at.

Damage% = (skill_have*100/skill_req)*1.65-33.5.

The reason I say this is that my level 109 enforcer has about 790 or so in 2hb with my normal self buffing. The 200 beam takes 1007 2hb skill, which means I will have about 78.5% of the skill required.

The only thing I used to get that beam on was a 100 wrangle from a trader, and my own challenger buff. (It's not like I did a lot to OE) Now before a battle, in order to get the full potential out of my weapon, I will have to cast challenger before every battle, at the sacrafice of rage nanos, and mongo, meaning my team will suffer from me not being able to pull stuff. (I will probably just take the hit in damage, so I can be a proper tank)

Now some of you may say okay, stop whining, go get your 4 levels, and then you won't have any problem. I agree, and I will.
My point is that, I enjoy OE, I enjoy figuring out what is needed to get such and such on. I think the people who enjoy doing it could be appeased if it was still somewhat beneficial to OE, and you could see improvements in damage the closer you get to the requirements. (In other words, with each level, you hit harder and harder. I know that is a strange idea.)

This patch as I see it:

1) To improve PVP by limiting effects of OE
2) To add some longevity, so at level 100, most professions won't have the best weapon on for that profession. So what's next.
3) Funcom hopes that it will balance the professions a little more, and give some professions a purpose in teams (I.E. traders)

1) The patch will reduce the effectiveness of the level 6 twinks out there that are gods at PVP, but they will still be gods, because they have 100 NCU spots filled with a good essence, a crit buff, and maybe an AC buff, and will have great armor, and QL 50 implants. Plus I don't really care about the level 6 game, and most noobs don't PVP until they learn the game, and get a character up to level 50.

High Level PVP (75+) is unbalanced. We all know that, but I don't think any number of patches are ever going to fix it, and it is not unbalanced because of twinking. At the high level game, twinking is not much of an issue, it is the general makeup and abilities of the professions themselves which cause the unbalance.

But I don't care, I am of the mindset that PVP should not be balanced. I don't want a game that at any given time, I should be able to beat any other class, mono y mono. I believe in the rock, scissors, paper mindset of PVP. Rock beats scissors all the time, scissors beat paper, and paper beats rock. In RL as in AO, you must choose your battles carefully. Now some of you may come back with, well my profession loses to everyone, but damn I can run away fast., i.e. fixer. All I can say, is when did you ever see a mechanic out in the battlefield, beat a soldier.

Most of the population of Rubi-Ka do not PVP anyhow. (This is my belief, please don't respond to this message with 'I PVP...., and all my friends PVP. etc etc.)

In conclusion,

So what does all this mean for PVP. I don't think this patch will do anything, the low level twinks will still be better than the average joe, due to their NCU's, and implants, and the high level game will still be unbalanced.

For PVE, in short, you will reduce the effectiveness of those players who really know the game and choose with a bit of exercise to get the most out of their character. A primer in how the game you pay for works.
Ever wonder why a Wrangle is 132 and not 124 or 145? It provides almost exactly 1/3 of the 'magic' number in terms of skill increase with not going over.

quote:
I dont fully understand this 400 point increase you are referring to. Weapon's info display ??? WTF are you talking about? Are you saying if I increase my pistol skill by 400 then when I shift right click on the weapon its stats will have changed?
Perhaps the fact I have to explain this so often is one of the reasons we have so many newbs calling for Nerf! The newbs don't have any information on how the game works. Only after months of learning do they understand what matters and what doesn't. I've always in game and out continues to disseminate information to help others move up the ladder from newbdome to AO proficiency.

<searches for previous post in forum to refer to>

Please follow.

Since at least ONE person is interested in knowing how the game they play works, I'll take some more time for education of the ignorant masses. Here's a primer on how your attack rating affects damage output:

Fact: for every 400 your attack rating increases you improve your damage by an integer amount. What I mean by this:

- Hypothetical Gun does base damage (1-100)

- With attack rating of 1 you do 1-100 damage
- With attack rating of 400 you do 2-200 damage
- With attack rating of 800 you do 3-300 damage

A 132 Wrangle changes the damage to 1-133. Nice boost for a lvl1 character. But the better weapon he could wield with the Wrangle DWARFS the bonus damage from the Wrangle. <----- This is the game designer's fault NOT MINE.

The lvl 1 enforcer with his lvl200 beam does 1/3 the damage of the lvl120 enforcer with the same beam. Same weapon fractional damage. Many players are ignorant to this fact.

These are the rules of the game. People don't seem to understand them.

What the argument should not be is whether or not the lvl1 enforcer can get that qlvl200 beam into his hands within the rules of the game (exploiters make me sick). This process is a rich and uniquely AO game mechanic. If he can manage it through study, planning, credits, networking, whatever, then he proves himself to be a 'better' player than I could ever hope to be with respect to this particular issue.

The argument should be 'is that number 400 appropriate?'. I think it is not for several reasons.

- Attack rating is not enough of a factor in determining how effective you are with a weapon. More importance is placed on the quality level of the item. I sympathize with those who misguidedly think that over-equipping is the root of their problems rather than a contributing factor.

- Buffs are not as effective in 'live' gameplay as they could be. A +20 general weapon buff has some indirect value in increasing your damage by letting you use a higher quality weapon. However the value during combat is miniscule. 20/400 is a 5% integer increase in damage which works out to a 2.5% increase for the player with a weapon skill of '1'. It only goes downhill from there as the player's 'real' skill goes up and the benefit becomes essentially useless. If a +20 bonus is so meaningless, a trader's +74 wrangle can be seen as 'mediocre' at best.

- Debuffs are likewise ineffective for the exact points stated in the above paragraph.

- Buff/Debuffs to those using NanoFormulas can incredibly shift their power potential. +132 to a NT's MattCrea for 3 minutes can swing the battle for the team againt a tough MOB. Likewise, the Engineer can turn a difficult mission into easy with Mocham's and/or a Wrangle. They are more susceptible on BOTH ends in terms of having their power swing with respect to current skill level.

What we need is:

- First to play with that magic '400' number. For simplicity's sake let's say we change it to 200. Now your +20 general buff (and debuff) start at a base integer increase of 10% damage boost. Not overpowering, not neglectible. You would have to pay attention to your 'current' skill level and perhaps make some decisions regarding NCU complement. The Trader's Wrangles during 'live' play would have more impact.

Note that 'debuffs' would likewise become more usefull.

- Second we need to add some new straight high level debuffs to appropriate classes. A 200 point across-the-board attack skill debuff (without the Trader-like skill syphon) would have a real noticeable impact. Assuming a this debuff got landed on someone with only 200 in his attack skill, he would instantly see his damage cut in half. Not too shabby. Note that this example is probably a lvl 150 character landing his debuff on a lvl50 character.

Again we have more impact on the 'live' game playing experience without destroying the existing ruleset that myself and so many others get enjoyment out of while we're not mindlessly exp'ing at some camp or in a mission.

==============================================
When 'level' is embraced by the game's community almost to the exclusion of all other factors as the best way to determine a character's power, it is an indication of a simple community unable to grasp moderately complex concepts.

The simple-minded player wants to be at lvl100 and say 'oh, that guy's lvl110 better stay away' and 'oh, that guy's lvl90, I can kill him easy'. There's a place for that. It's called DAoC.

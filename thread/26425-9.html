Coldstrike
Supa Leet
One of the main problems we soldiers face is that people generally thing that our TMS is so much more powerful than it really is. Sure, it is good in certain situations, PvP being one of them.

If we look at tanking in general, any profession can obtain 72% reflect by getting RRF and having two 21% bracers for the particular damage type. I know that not everyone have a pair of 21% bracers for all damage types, but I will base this on the assumption that every involved character does.

Mobs that require good reflect to tank will generally take more than 1m20s to kill. This means that using the TMS is pointless, as it actually leaves the soldier even more vulnerable than others once the TMS wears off. So basically, if the healer can't keep up with the damage you sustain with 72% reflect, you pretty much know that you'll be dead when the TMS wears off, as this will leave you with only 42%.

So it is obvious that enforcers (with their extra hp) or any class with decent healing capabilities (to aid the other healers in keeping themself alive) will be able to tank better than a soldier, using our buffs.

Seeing that the adventurers have been promised a damage shield that also has 2% reflect on it, *self only*, just makes this even worse. This will mean that adventurers will have 74% permanent reflect, even more than soldiers. I'm not saying adventurers don't need some love, I just don't like to see that they are better than us in our defining feature.

The 3 ways I can see that will fix this is:
- Remove the nano in operative from TMS, and make it lock for instance MC for 2 minutes instead. This lets us slap on a new RRF when the TMS wears off, letting us relieve the healers with a TMS without being left more vulnerable than others in the in operative period that follows. This will also have the effect of letting us use taunts while in TMS.
- Add a nano line of small self only reflects that stack with the others we have. And I mean small both in a NCU cost and a reflect % sense. I only picture a line that maybe tops out at 4-5% in the highest version, just enough to ensure that a soldier will be best at their thing, if only by a small margin.
- Make the highest (25% or more for instance) reflects self only or soldier only. I don't really like this option tho, as it will be as much of a nerf on the population as removing crits was.

Hey all.
Gaute has just sent this up to me and here is the answer for:

How do you view the role of the soldier, where is it now and where it is going in the next 6 months?



The role of the Soldier:

The primary role of the Soldier is that of the tank; being able to take and deal damage. For this purpose the soldier has cheap body development yielding a high hit-point count, and nano programs that enable him to sustain more damage than anyone else (partial invulnerability shields). This gives him an obvious role in teams as the focal point of enemy attacks. Who hasn't wished for a soldier shield when clicking the boss-room icon in a team mission?

The Soldier is also the long-range weaponry expert, with the cheapest skills in distance combat weaponry compared to any other profession. This enables the use of high output assault rifles and special attacks with solid damage (burst, full auto). The combination of these enables the Soldier to be a steady source of damage and also to solo adequately. They are not the ones who are supposed to deal the highest amount of damage over short periods of time, nor the highest overall damage, but they should be among the best. They are, though, among the hardest to kill.

In PvP, the Soldier doesnt have a niche as the best at something specific - but there are few professions with an equally predictable ability to survive massive amounts of damage inflicted on them at critical times. Since they are also using long-range weapons, they are not as vulnerable to roots and run speed reduction nanos as i.e. the Enforcer.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
That's what was given as our answer. Tell me you don't see about twenty or so holes in the logic presented to us.

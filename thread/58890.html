Okay, well at least we're thinking of a solution instead of focusing on the problem. One of the last psots got me thinking:
quote:
It would be funny if you didn't have to kill this guy but rather perform some other task to get his loot. I can see some quest developer laughing hysterically as people try futiley to kill him.
Or maybe something else has to be triggered before the right hand can be killed?

Surprisingly, I think I may be on to something and this is only because I'm a total bookworm.

From the start, I thought that the name "general's right hand" sounded a bit too cheesy.....and also a bit too familiar at the same time. The name almost seems to beg the answer to us, does it not?

Tonight it struck me:

To fully understand what I'm talking about, please read the accounts of Flavius Josephus, a Jewish revolutionist from the first century A.D.

http://www.hillsdale.edu/academics/h...y-Josephus.htm

I think the devs are likening the dust brigade camp to Josephus' portrayal of the Roman army:

quote:
Nor can we find any examples where they have been conquered in battle, when they came to a close fight, either by the multitude of the enemies, or by their stratagems, or by the difficulties in the places they were in; no, nor by fortune neither, for their victories have been surer to them than fortune could have granted them
And if I'm wrong on this, it makes for one hell of a coincidence, doesn't it? Especially when you look at the pictures of all the dead clansmen.

But that part wasn't really the point I was trying to make.

quote:
Then does the crier stand at the general's right hand, and asks them thrice in their own tongue, whether they be now ready to go out to war or not? To which they reply as often, with a loud and cheerful voice, saying, 'We are ready'. And this they do almost before the question is asked them: they do this as filled with a kind of martial fury, and at the same time that they cry out, they lift up their right hands also
A "Harbinger" is (taken from dictionary.com) One that indicates or foreshadows what is to come; a forerunner

In other words, a messenger, or a 'crier'.

Just a couple of questions (ive only been to the dust brigade camp three times and got the taste slapped out of my mouth twice). Does the general, the right hand and the harbinger all spawn at the same spot? What makes each one spawn?

And how to defeat them. If Funcom likened the dust brigade to the Roman army, I first thought about the assassination of Julias Caesar, who was killed by his own men and how the dust brigade seemed prepared to fight each other at the slightest whim. But that theory detracts from Josephus' impressions of the Roman army being such a united force, and it also takes from a different period in history.

It's now 5pm us central time and I'm logging in to check out the db camp. If anyone who knows more about the general and lackeys care to show me where the general and harbinger spawn, pop me a tell in game

I like these new mobs, it's like a puzzle that begs to be figured out. I think the clue to how to destroy them is in the mob's name themselves, or it's just a fantastic coincidence. It's something to try anyway.

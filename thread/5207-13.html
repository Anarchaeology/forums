Coming in late, but hey, I have my top 10.

1) One thing I thing fits the Adventurer profession well would be a tame/charm/train animal ability. I don't know how Funcom has AO's creatures classified, but we should NOT be able to charm any Homo Breed (leave that to the Crats. For that matter, why can Crats charm just about anything? Seems like it should be limited to Homo breeds...), mutants (like eyes, shades, claws, etc.), or robots/cyborgs. But wildlife like Minibulls, RollerRats, Reets, Wolves, etc. are fair game. Maybe even the ability to just summon a pet. After all, Adventurers are supposed to spend their time away from the city with nature. It gets lonely when you're in the middle of nowhere with nobody to talk to, so we should have pets!

2) If I can turn into a reet I want to fly! Reets shouldn't switch to swim speed when hitting water. Does anyone else get the urge to dive bomb Yalms when they're in reet form? Kinda hard to do since we're not actually flying! What use are wings if you can't really fly?

3) Ability to be ridden on bigger morphs. Wouldn't it be kinda cool to be a wolf and have another player character ride you around? Or be a dragon and have someone (or mutiple someones) on your back? Seems like a good place to stick a Doc, NT, or Engineer (or any ranged attacker for that matter) A cool dragon attack might be the ability to pick up an attacker, fly as high as you can, then drop them on their sorry butts. Setup as an add on nano to the wolf and dragon morphs. Cats wouldn't tolerate a rider, and the fighting animations wouldn't make riding any fun anyway.

4) Able to renew morphs without terminating the morph program. If riding was implemented (yeah right, would be cool though) having to terminate the morph, rerun it, then reinvite your rider would be just annoying. Annoying as it is that they have to be terminated to be renewed.

5) A reason to have an Adventuring skill over 200. Only reason for a 200 Adventuring skill is large backpacks as far as I know.

6) Instead of Calm/Root/Scare...tranquilizers! Adventurer only weapons that take specal ammo to drug target up with tranquilizers. (so guns would take two kinds of ammo, and blades would just have a means of injecting the drug) Would be an added command like FlingShot, but depend on adventuring skill as deploying tranquilizers with a weapon is no different than attacking with that weapon. Low level tranquilizers will slow, higher level will sleep. Or maybe instead of weapons do tranquilizers as charged nano critters! Leaves your weapon choice free, new commands and new weapons do not have to be added. Profession lock them though.

7) Pistol/1HE buffs for Adventurers, Multi-Ranged/Multi-Melee Buffs. Less than wrangles, more than general nanos. (Yay! Coming!) But what about changing the damage type done by pistols? Would be interesting to have poison bullets, chemical bullets, or add parts to change the damage type, or add special attacks to weapons that do a different damage type and then have to recharge before using again.

8) Root breaker. Adventurers are too crafty to stay rooted/snared.

9 and 10: Covered with upcoming patches, so no need to post them. Maybe I'll come up with some other ideas down the line...

Adventurers are a super-hybrid class, with the theoretical potential to do everything. However, nobody can be good at everything, so divide their abilities four ways.

1) Physical Combat. Adventurers have pistols and Ihe, and the dual wield for both. Let them pick, a set of skills to enhance this.

2) Buffs (Including Morphs): These should also be exceptional. Perhaps not the best in the game, but second or third.

3) Medical: Again, this is another potential core-ability of the Adventurer. Making the above improvements would be very nice.

4) Nano-Combat: There is some potential here, but again, make sure they're well behind the NTs in this field, but they need to be much better.

Now, I would seem to be arguing to make the Adventurer the best class in the game, but here's my point: Adventurers should NOT have the IP necessary to do all of this. Far from it, they should be fairly restricted in their IP amounts, so that they can become the second-best in one area in the game and have a bit of dabbling in others, or be the third or fourth best in two related areas, and thus be able to function like a group of one.

When facing an Adventurer in PvP, the problem should be something like this: Mr. Agent comes over the hill and sees some guy turn into a tiger. Now, he didn't get a good look at the weapons. He's got 20 seconds, tops, to figure out whether to treat this guy as a melee fighter, a ranged fighter, a buffer, a medic, or a nuker, or he'll be finding out which it is in a most painful fashion.

As to how to accomplish this post-release: Not a clue.

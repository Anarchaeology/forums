A non Bug Letter to Funcom
Dear Funcom,

First I would like to state that in this letter I am assuming that all of the major bugs will be fixed and the game will be playable as intended. For this reason, I wont discuss lag, disconnects or many of the other problems plaguing the game. The problem is when I see the game completely fixed.

The picture I have about AO now is simple. Get in a group, grab 5 levels a day, do a mission or two and log off. Now this seems to be a Mecca for those sick of the slow leveling of EQ, however it has some inherent flaws. The main one is currently my main complaint with AO. AO has absolutely no community and no economy. It is like playing a single player game with many other people. Sure there is some interaction between players in PvP which has virtually no risks whatsoever if you go scan. Grouping is not helping the community either. It is hard to form friendships or do anything in a group that consists of following someone around a huge open area and looking for mobs. One thing that can be said for slow leveling, it induces cooperation and allows groups to stick together more. Further, the leveling situation in AO is killing any possibility of an economy or trade skills. When people are spread far and wide over a huge area, It is almost impossible to find buyers most of the time. People end up stockpiling items or just dumping them to machines. Why bother standing around for 2 hours to sell the gun you got in a mission when the buyers of the gun can go get it themselves and even if they cant, they can only use that gun for a day or so. The race is on, for items and weapons and levels. In AO it is most definitely a race with almost no purpose.

Another related issue is the leveling itself. As a genre we often tout the shoreline as the thing to meld this game together. The sad fact is that if you log in only for a couple hours, you wont be able to be involved in the story line unless your company is planning on employing a staff 24 hours a day to run events. With the leveling, it wont matter anyway. All of the power levelers will show up at the events and waste everything. The events will have to be increased in difficulty to match that and thus out of the reach of the average players. Further, if the power levelers can do level 200 in 8 weeks in a stable game, not far off by my math, then the non-power gamers will get there in 4 months. Then what ? Mission for items ? Do events, when they happen to be going on. Many governments throughout the world, such as the former Soviet Union, learned the hard way that humans do little or nothing without incentive. Most players wont find a level 200 life of no gain to be entertaining. Even getting just a little experience makes people stick around.

The next issue I wish to address is the nano formulas. Lets face it, most nano formulas are copies of others. There are a total of maybe 30 nano formulas in the game. Skill buffs, healing, lifetap, stat buffs, ac, lull, and a few more. The game as it is lacks the variety of spells seen in other games. The reality is that Major health funnel is really lossy health funnel upped a bit. The lack of variety induces extreme boredom. Especially when you can go to general formula shops and buy lesser potent versions of profession specific formulas.

The last issue is one of combat. Up to level 20, highest I've seen so far, all battles last under 30 seconds. They consist of either you overwhelming the mob or the mob overwhelming you. After running around in a group with others in Tir expanse the other day, I stopped even bothering to cast debuffs. We would either kill the mob in 15 seconds or it would kill us. Battles take no time. Further, with this model, what profession you are is pretty much irrelevant. Only relevant profession is the doctor. Everyone just get out your weapons and blast it. The interdependency of professions isn't there. Such lack introduces more anonymity and thus less social aspect.

So, what do we do about it. I have a few proposals and some of them are NOT going to be kindly received by some.

One: Drastically increase leveling difficulty at 100. Make it a polynomial progression if not geometric. Getting level 200 should be worth something, not just mean you played 3 months. Drastically slowing down leveling would fix many of the problems I illustrated.

Two: Implement static dungeons as soon as possible. Its all well and good running all over the landscape looking for mobs but it makes it a pain to talk, get to know people or form online friendships. Static dungeons would help with this as well as provide a sense of more than mundane that exists now. Don get me wrong, good outdoor zones and missions are good. Being forced to camp creatures is terrible. Not having that option isn't an answer either.

Three: Put a largest variety of spells in the game. Variety is in fact the spice of life.

Four: Please don count on the story for all of our playtime. Yes, we all want to be involved I the story, but we dot want the game to be mundane in between events.

Five: Keep increasing the level cap. As people start to get high level, increase it. Hopefully in an expansion of land and with dungeons as well.

Six: Put in some significant economic tools to deal with the reality of the diverese economic situation in AO. Vending machines that Trader class players can put items in would help. This is only one of many ideas that have been expressed here. Id be happy to dig the other ideas out of the mountain of trol garbage if you would like.

Seven: Make some other wayls to get exp then killign monters. Gettign it for opening locked chests and doors is ok, why not for trade skills. They are hard enough to do that they deserve some points. There are other things that could give exp too.

Eight: Give death lots more teeth. Most of the time I dont even care if I die. Only time i care is when Im out on a hunt and loose all the exp I gained but hat isreally no matter since I can hunt a half hour and get it all back. Ideas to make it harder include: Makeitems since last scan lost on death. Make credits since last scan lost. Dramaticically up the cost to scan. Dont make yourself auto scanned at leveling (this is cheesy at best anyway). Without risk, the game becomes a bore.

Now, if you will excuse me, I must put on my flame proof suit. I will close with the statement that I love the game and think you have all done a good job overall. I know you will fix the bugs. Its after that I am worried about. I am worried because I love the premise and potential of this game. EQ never reached its potential and I hope this game breaks that mold.

I agree with many of Derisor's points. I do enjoy AO, but I feel that many people will become bored with it for the reasons he has listed. I have some suggestions for a few of his points.

quote:
Originally posted by Derisor
AO has absolutely no community and no economy. It is like playing a single player game with many other people. Sure there is some interaction between players in PvP which has virtually no risks whatsoever if you go scan.


Community - we need to have the chat channels back. Having them available only in cities does not help 90% of the players. Having a few permanent chat channels really helps the social aspect of the game. I really liked the beta 4 model of getting a new channel every 10-20 levels or so. Perhaps when the chat server can handle the load we can go back to this model. Putting more monsters in zones will also help (the more players in a zone the less isolated you feel). I like the idea of more static dungeons, but they will have to be big enough and hard enough so that 3 groups cannot go in and camp xp.

Economy - this is a tough one. No matter what features FC adds, the economy will not pick up. Why? Because just about everything you need can be bought, and many items last you 10 levels or less (before you need to upgrade). Solutions? MORE MORE MORE items like the rings of knowledge that cannot be bought, are found randomly, and are incredibly sought after. Give us weaps, armor, implants that cannot be bought (but are still random), and you'll have trading and auctions going on in no time. Make trade-created items MUCH better than they currently are. They cost an arm and a leg to make, and are generally crappy compared to the store versions. Auction channels again will help both community and economy here.


quote:
Grouping is not helping the community either. It is hard to form friendships or do anything in a group that consists of following someone around a huge open area and looking for mobs. One thing that can be said for slow leveling, it induces cooperation and allows groups to stick together more.


I don't necessarily think that slower levels are the answer. Gaining levels and distributing IPs gives people a sense of accomplishment. I do think that gear should be more spread out (costing more, but requiring upgrades less often). This will make levels mean more, and enhance the player economy.

1. We need much more variety in nano formulas.

2. We need much more variety in professions.

Their basically are 30 different types of nanos. We need to have MANY more varieties added, and sufficiently different that each profession gets some distinctive qualities.

Currently, there are 4 classes in the game: Healers, Combats, Pets, and Agents.

Wait a sec, you say?!? My adventurer can morph into critters and my engineer can create trade items! Well, in combat, it don't mean diddly squat. The adventurer's leet form makes him a quasi-agent (the wolf form makes him a quasi-combat, the reet form makes him a quasi-fixer), and no one cares that the engy can make a crappy gun for around 150k creds. You are either attacking (with guns, fists, or swords), you are healing, you are sending your pet in to attack (and are generally useless otherwise), or you are stealthily pulling.

Agents are probably the most distinctive class right now. They have neat spells (shrinking), the ability to cast all prof spells (though it isn't overpowering, which is nice), and they can snipe and conceal.

Bureaucrats may become distinctive once the 101 problems with charm and lull are fixed.

Traders....<shudder>

Doctors - most loved.

The major combatant's are the real problem here (ma, sol, enf). Their acs are different, their styles are different, but man from where I'm standing they are identical.

Now, for example, if MA's had a feign death or similar skill, soldiers called in airstrikes, and enforcers could enlarge their size to induce fear into critters....Add in another 5-10 unique nanos/skills to each class and...well now we've got 3 different classes after all (just suggestions, and lame at that, but you get the idea).

Although I also agree basicly with all of solutions presented by Derisor, or at least acknowledge that they might be potential solutions, my biggest concern at the moment is that things will proceed to far before the people at Funcom starts looking into these important gameplay problems. I am certain that Funcom are working their @sses off at the moment (pardon my french) in the attempt at closing these technically very important bugs - and as most people with any programming experience knows, everything takes about 3.1417 times longer to fix than one expects, and every solution to a bug potentially opens up 10 new ones. I am certain that Funcom WILL get there eventually, and hopefully this will be soon, because there really are some gameplay issues, as mentioned in this very thread, which needs some attention.

But currently it seems as if some of the power-gamers are really getting ahead of schedule, and I'm a bit afraid the people behind AO really have other things to look into at the moment (ie. the technically important bugs). Hopefully they will get the job done in time to fix the powerleveling problem.

Another very real concern is, that although all of the ideas posted by Derisor (and others) are very valid and might work towards getting the perfect MMORPG game, the ideas which really matters, the ideas which will create the right amount of diversity in the game and will start making each profession unique, are not easily adapted into the gamplay and the game. As an example, creating new and interesting nanos which are super-specific for the professions is not enough - as mentioned by many, the general base of nanos which basicly all of the professions share, are simply too large in my opinion. In order to create more diversity and specialization, Funcom needs to remove a lot of the nanos, something which will make a lot of people slightly angered of being robbed of their abilities.

Hopefully Funcom will pull it off, and soon be able to focus on gameplay, so we dont end up with a whole army of level 200 characters, in one or two weeks, who has nothing to do except stand around and make war on each other, and pester lower level characters.

please...lets not turn this into EQ.

I have seen most of you compare this game to EQ. I think AC handled many things much better. (I am not trying to turn this into an AC vs EQ argument, but giving my thoughts on some of these issues)

levelling: ok in this game levelling does seem somewhat easy, and could possibly be made slightly harder. Do not turn this into eq though where you are working on a level for weeks.

one thing that made levelling better in AC was the idea of spending experience as you go. levelling did get much slower, but you didn't care much because you could still improve between levels. games like EQ and AO do not have this option, and so become boring since you are stuck with the same character and same stats for too long.

death: this is controversial. having died and lost almost all exp for my next level, i have seen how much death can hurt in this game. as for auto save on level.... hm.... exp-wise yes auto save is good. portable scanners would be good if they costed a LOT to use. death in eq meant running back to find your body and tons of exp lost and time wasted. death in asheron's call meant having a skill decrease penalty you had to work off (no permanent penalty, which was nice) and leaving some items on your body. leaving items on the body is good for avoiding the terminate after level problem. leaving money on the body is also a consideration. leaving everything since the last scan i think is stupid though (unless the portable scanners are an option) because it would encourage people to stay too close to the cities and keep people in missions instead of going out to group with others.

i felt a much better sense of community in beta4 with the global channels... and it was easy for newbies to ask for help using these channels. if the servers can handle it they would be a great thing to bring back.

as for the duration of fights....... hmmmmmmmmm.........
some of my fights in missions seem to last too long, though that may be because of lag. it is another 2-sided argument. long fights can be fun, but also boring. one thing i didn't like so much while hunting in a group was monsters would take a large piece of a person's life in 1 or 2 hits (no matter what class). this reminded me of diablo where a monster could kill you in 2 hits. this was, of course, killing stuff that was red to us. i don't have a solution, though the unpredictability of not knowing if you will die makes me opposed to high death penalties so i decided to point that out.

static dungeons....hmmmmmm......
as far as that is concerned i would just have to say avoid static spawns (especially with bosses) to avoid camping. it may be good to include these as long as better exp can be gotten elsewhere. in EQ players were basically forced into dungeons after a certain level. in AC the archers had more advantage outside where they could avoid being hit easier, with dungeons still being an option for all. how you can implement static dungeons without them being so advantageous some players feel left out by avoiding them i don't know, but that is good to take into consideration.


improvements are great, but it is a shame to see a good idea gone wrong.

A lot of excellent suggestions there, really. Would improve the game thousandfold if only Funcom would respond to some (most) of the ideas originally posted. Alas, as everyone probably realize it is too late. The train has already departed and is hammering across the prairie at 100 mph towards an unknown destination. In all software projects a point is reached where the specs are frozen, and AO is way past that point. Starting to implement any but the most minute changes to gameplay and mechanics at this point would not only be extremely demanding of the devs, as any change could/will have far-reaching and subtle consequences which are hard to foresee, triggering a series of other changes just to counter the effect on other areas of the game. It would also degrade the quality of the structure of the game as the patchwork of fixes severely affects the stability and visibility of the core code, making the whole system more 'locked' and unwieldy with each patch.

This form of development, where changes to the specifications are made AFTER implementation has begun, is frowned upon for a reason, as one would end up with a patched up mess of a product.

I believe Funcom would never consider making any of the changes suggested. Both for practical reasons, as stated above, and because frankly, I sense that at this point they are too exhausted economically and morally to have the energy to think beyond starting to get a return on their huge investment.

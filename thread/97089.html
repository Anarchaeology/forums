Suppression gas cycle in land control
We get a lot of feedback about the length of both the suppression gas cycle in land control areas. This feedback is about both the length of the "attack openings" (the 25% and 5% periods), and the time between them.

This poll is only about the time between the start of each attack opening.

We would like to know how often you want to be able to attack each base, and thus also how often you think you should need to defend your own base(s). Of course, an organization owning 5 bases will have to defend more often than others no matter what, unless they manage to get all running in perfect sync.

Let's know what you think about this, and feel free to post comments and suggestions in the thread.

My favorite idea isn't up as an option

I'd like to see it cycle once per day, but for an amount of time that's scaled to the controller QL.

Ie:
QL 1-50: 2 hours 25%
QL 51-100: 3 hours 25%
QL 101-150: 4 hours 25%
QL 150+ : 5 hours 25%

or:
QL 1-75: 2 hours 25%
QL 76-150: 3 hours 25%
QL 151-200: 4 hours 25%
QL 201+ : 5 horus 25%

This concept makes it more friendly and easier to defend lower QL bases (since they are weaker and take less time and people to conquer, overall), while still providing enough time to take over a large, high QL base.

It also gives guilds a bit of a choice - if they have a plot of land that straddles the QL ranges, they can choose between the additional org advantages from a higher QL tower, or a smaller window of vulnerability.

I also think 5% should just plain go away, unless and until an alliance system for friendly guilds can be implemented. Almost all 5% attacks are accidents; many of the rest are unguilded griefers who are taking advantage of the fact that the PvP-enabled flag doesn't allow same-sided people to attack them outside of 5% gas anyway.

There are pro's and con's on both side of this arguement.

1. A guild that has spent a great deal of time and money on their towers will want a window that is shorter and more infrequent than most. A guild without a base will want windows to be as large and frequent.

2. Certain bases are almost impossible to attack at the moment due to the current limitations of CC, the location of the base, the access routes used to reach the base, etc.

3. The number of towers within a base varies drastically, but this is not taken into account against either the amount of time at 25%/5% or the length between windows. This means that areas with a small base in can be used to gain a foothold into an area, whilst others are incredibly difficult to take, if not impossible.

The problem is that if you try an apply a cookie cutter timing system to a base, then you will get problems at the extreme ends of the scale.

Something more dynamic might be better.

quote:
People need to see the line between OOC and IC and not step between it.
((ALL OOC))
A simple concept to write down. Something I've said countless times. Also a rule that I and many others have broken countless times. It's not so easy to implement as it is to conceive.

I can't speak for others, and trying to rationalize what's going through their heads when they flame or grief someone OOC is an exercise in futility. I can only see what happens to me, and assume that others might be the same.

OOC, I'm already highly emotional. I immerse myself into my character, therefore "becoming" my character for periods of time, retaining the personality attributes that I want Tarryk to have. All too often, the worst part of those attributes leaks through, and when I'm ready to quit for the night, the IC occurances of the session may still be with me, as well as the emotion that was attached to them.

I think that's what creates most of the griefing and flaming from my own end. As I said, I can only assume that it's the same for others. I try to control it, and 98 percent of the time I do a very good job of it.

But now and again that button is pushed, and I go overboard without thinking about the consequences of my words.

Doesn't make me a bad person. Just a loose cannon in my own mind. I think what most of the flamers and griefers out there need to know is this:

Be ready to go back on your own words. When you realize that you've overstepped your boundaries, don't keep letting pride stand in your way of apologizing. Back off before you really do go too far, because that's when you earn your reputation for being a griefer and a flamer.

I'm admittedly a griefer, in my own way. I use different avenues, but it certainly doesn't make me any better than any other griefer. Just a little more humble...perhaps humiliated...because of it.

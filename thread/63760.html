Roleplaying Flavours of Rubi-Ka
When you talk to people in AO about roleplaying, you quickly find out that this term means a lot of different things to different people. So I just figured I'd list the prevalent styles I have encountered. Bear in mind that this styles are not mutually exclusive and most of us mix and match 2 or 3 of these flavours in our own games.

If you know of any genuinely distinct style of roleplaying I have not covered here, please feel free to add it below. I'd love to hear about it.

IC Mode: For some players all you need to do to roleplay is to stay in-character and just view the world from her character's perspective. It does not necessarily matter if you are just doing a mission or shopping for implants. Nor does it matter whether the players around are even aware that you are roleplaying. Of course the experience is most rewarding when other players do respond ic too, which is one reason why social spots such as Rompa Bar are so popular. Some players find it more practical to drop in and out of character depending on the circumstances, others find it more immersive to be ic all the time.

Unstructured roleplaying: Unstructured roleplaying is about semi-improvised stories. Typically the players will already in social, in-character situation when one of them, on impulse, will decide to throw a story hook into the conversation and, if he's lucky, the rest of the group play along with it. In these types of adventures no one player necessarily "owns" the story, but rather the players work together to progress the plot in ways they hope will be fun and will make sense in the end.

Unplotted Events: Unplotted events are in essence a way to stimulate in-character interaction by providing some sort of context for the occasion. The most common type of unplotted event is the party, but there have also been other types of unplotted events such as poetry readings, parades and rallies and organised leet fighting events just to name a few. A day out to explore some remote area also works well as long as its not so insanely dangerous you don't have time to roleplay. You don't have to be a roleplayer to enjoy these kind of events, though of course it helps.

Structured Roleplaying: Structured roleplaying resembles pen & paper roleplaying. One of the players in a defined group takes on the role of GM. She prepares a story in advance and sets time and date for the group to meet. Then by using alts to play NPCs and simple narrative to describe things that cannot be represented in AO (things like "You see a set of foot prints in the sand heading towards that hut..") she runs the adventure pretty much the same way as you'd run a D&D or Shadowrun game.

Massive Roleplaying: Massive roleplaying resemble live action roleplaying and its pretty much the model Funcom have used in their events. Typically there will be storyline that will involve a number of guilds, both OT and Clan, and often (though not necessarily) it will all end with a massive PvP battle. Unless you have a lead role in this event, you don't necessarily have to be a roleplayer to take part in this kind of event.

Message Board Roleplaying: A popular way of roleplaying is offline, using a message board to tell stories which involve your characters. Many of these stories turn out to be collaborations of various authors who, as with the Unstructured Roleplaying method,
keep adding bits to the plot without any one person controlling the overall direction of the story.

Official Storyline Roleplaying: This is not a method of roleplaying as such, but there are a significant number of player for whom the only roleplaying that matters is what which has been validated by Funcom, either because its an official event or because it involves a "greenie", and therefore is canonical.

Good post, Savoy:

My RP style changes depending on the situation and the people I am interacting with. In general:

Private chats never/rarely RP'd

/vicinities are often RP depending on who I am talking to.

guild chat is always RP, except when surrounded by (())

Dicussions with newbies in the newbie area are a mix of RP and not. I try and RP as much as possible to condition new Rubi-Ka arrivals, but recognize they might not 'get' it. Otherwise
in situations where RP is appropriate, I tend to want to do it strictly and become annoyed when people fall in and out of RP.

non-guild missions (ie: leveling) are not RP'd. Its just a fun time with friends and strangers.

RP events that I am involved in are typicaly semi-structured; where one or more endings must be reached, but the means to get there are fluid.

I'd like to point out, Savoy, that many of the player developed 'events' such as parties have little or no roleplay. With some, the major activity is to stand and talk about Real Life topics (ex: 'gee when is SWG gonna be out! I cant Wait!', yadda yadda yadda')

Forum posts are RP'd or not depending on the original poster. If I post a new thread, I might RP it depending on the forum.

Wasted IP points.... solutions...
Ok, I'd like everyone, including the AO teams working on improving the game to read this. This is about a very serious topic that has caused likely thousands of people to restart or completely give up on characters.

I'm talking about wasted IP points. When you start a character you put points in to skills that you think you want to use for your character's career. If you change your mind later on and go with something new you have points in these skills that are "wasted". You can't recover them and you have tens of thousands of IP points that you could have used to make your new skill better. Most people quit and restart their characters and go through these motions all over again.

I've done this numerous times with Martial Artists, Enforcers and Doctors. Now, everyone will say, "well you have to go with the green or light blue skills, etc." I agree to an extent. I also believe that this is a rare game in which I can be good at anything I want to be, it just might take a certain class a little longer than others. In Everquest, a cleric could NEVER use any edged or cutting weapons of any type. In Anarchy Online my doctor did quite well using dual hand axes. The doctor, however has light blue skills in Martial Arts and in Pistol. I for one, have totally given up on any ranged combat whatsoever and will most likely never play a class again that relies on rifles or SMGs or pistols as I can't stand running out of ammunition. The skills my doctor --should-- have been using were pistols or my fists. Before the 13.8 patch my fists were not a good choice as the damage output was low, but I didn't have to buy weapons. I went with dark blue melee skills because I could. Now, at level 43 I gave up on a Martial Artist because the damage output was terrible even though I had somewhere around 240 in Martial Arts. The recent patch of course, repaired this problem, but I was done playing the Martial Artist class for now.

My Doctor, now at 43 has started to use Martial Arts as a primary attack method. I have about 160 points in the skill but I also have close to 110 points in 1 hand edged, 90 points in Multiple Melee, 80 points in Fast Attack and 55 or so points in Sneak Attack. This is so I could equip these weapons. Now that I've switched to Martial Arts I have over 300 points in dark blue skills that I --could-- have applied to Martial Arts and Physical Initiative. I also have about 150 points in Melee Initiative. I'm already hitting for over double damage due to the recent repair of Martial Arts.

On to the problem. My doctor was about level 35 when the 13.8 patch was applied. A friend of mine restarted his level 48 Martial Artist to see if the damage in Martial Arts was really of any significance. We would both have to agree seeing as his new Martial Artist can hit so hard and kill enemies so fast that in just five hours of play he's already level 15. Another person he met made level 29 as a new Martial Artist in just nine hours of hard-core playing.

So these "wasted" IP points? These previous points were brought up to show what can happen to people that get tired of what they are using. So here's what I suggest be done to change that and allow people more flexibility when choosing how to develop ones' character as one progresses through the levels.

First of all, a "trading system" or "skill point migration" system or something should be added to help those people out who are tired of the skills they chose early one. I for one don't think that anyone should be penalized later in their careers for putting IP points in to a skill early that they don't use any more.

How will this system work? First of all, we'll use my doctor as an example but this can be used for any character of any class. I want to "migrate" my one hand edged points in to my Martial Arts skill. So the points would come from one and cause the other to rise. Simple enough, however these points would NOT go back in to the IP pool that you pull from when leveling. The second thing is that these points would not immediately move as this would cause some issues and exploits with item equipping, implants and with learning nanos. To fix that part only a certain amount of points would move at fixed intervals OR only a certain amount of points would move at level up. Points in skills would also NEVER be allowed to go below the minimum based on level and primary skills such as Strength, Agility, Sense, etc. This would stop people from leeching skills to make others better. For example, my doctor wants to move 100 one hand edged points in to the Martial Arts skill. If I moved all the points right away it would cause balance issues and be more of an exploit. So for instance I'm only allowed to move 1 skill point every 20 or 30 minutes. Another way to do it is by level. Using the same example I am only allowed to move 10 skill points per level and I MUST earmark them for "migration" before I level and then when my level goes up the points automatically move to the desired location. So to move 100 skill points from one ability to another it would take 10 levels to do so. If I don't earmark any points before I level, then I lose the chance for that level to move points. This should also not be allowed by anyone below level 15 or so. The other reason to only move points on a "skill point for skill point" basis is so that someone doesn't move 100 points from a dark blue skill and put them in to a green skill which would be worth far more than 100 points.

I've suggested this idea while in OOC and a lot of other people have thought that this is a terrific idea, simply because a lot of people don't like to have to redux an entire character because they put "one too many" IP points in to the wrong skill and later didn't want to use it.

The reason why I think this ability should be put in the game is simple: It's a bit more realistic to say that if one doesn't use a skill or stops practicing, then those abilities will not maintain their current level of use. I played piano for 15 years but I haven't taken any lessons for almost 10. Instead I started playing drums and have been practicing that on an almost constant basis for over 12 years. I'm quite a good drummer but I can barely play the piano at all like I could 10 years ago. I simply "migrated" my piano skill in to percussion instruments instead.

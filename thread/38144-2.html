Wrong assumptions on the original SW:G PvP system.

It was actually pretty good.

In the 'wild' zones you had unrestricted PvP in that you could attack anyone who was your 'enemy'. Rebels could attack Imperials could attack Hutts, etc.

You could also attack anybody who was _not_ your enemy. This meant you could attack your own faction or Neutrals. But, this is considered usually a 'bad' thing. This was where the 'outcasting' came in. If they chose to do so, they could 'outcast' you.

Once 'outcasted' you could never attack another _non-enemy_ again. <- please note 'non-enemy'.

This was unless of course you got 'forgiven' by that person. Basically, in order to get 'outcasted' you were either being a ganking ass, doing some RP (and the forgiveness would come as a result of roleplaying), or whacking a griefer delivering some justice (good luck getting 'forgiven').

But, there are player-run cities in SW:G. And you could be forgiven by the mayor of any city and have free reign to PvP anybody you want within the confines of the city limits.

You could _always_ attack enemys.

The system was pretty good. It went bye, bye.

Now, it has gotten even better. Basically, now you cannot attack a non-enemy. Ever.

Except. (heh, they really have a nice system)

Player run cities set their own rules. The mayor (elected by the citizens) can set the town PvP rules. He also can appoint Militia members who are able to attack anyone within the city limits. This can be for good as in the case of enforcing town 'No Begging' rules (not enforceable through game mechanics but easily enforceable by the Militia) or Evil as in Killing anyone who isn't on your guilds friend list.

What they did with this is to remove the whole idea of 'wild' lands and allow declared faction members to attack enemies _anywhere_ with the exception of certain strategic Truce zones (small areas where political talks can occur).

Now, declared faction groups can create 'bases' and these bases spawn what is known as 'battlegrounds'. Once a battleground spawns, the call goes out for defenders and attackers. Here we have group combat for victory regarding the specific battle objective. PvP combat directly influences which faction has control of an area. There are PvE battle grounds as well (you fight NPC's) , but they don't hold the same weight as the PvP battle grounds.

Faction membership comes with perks declared faction membership comes with additional perks. Example is only declared faction members can create military bases and have access to some vehicles that the faction sympathizers can only dream of.

In addition to the declared PvP'ers who are going to be battling for their side most of the time, PvE'ers who have declared for a side, but are too wimpy to expose themselves to attack 24/7 are able to join in the PvP battlegrounds if they are feeling their oats. Once they have been drop-shipped on the battlefield, there is no difference between them and fully declared faction members.

Temporary PvP is an option for the 'semi-carebares' and their influence on a battle may swing the control of a sector. These 'part-timers' gain a flag that takes time to wear off as a result of their direct combat with the other side. You have a chance to take them out for a while following their meddling in a battle.

Which faction (The Rebels, The Empire, The Hutts) controlls an area affects how player-run towns live day-t0-day. Example is if the Rebels control the area your town is in, then less items may be considered contraband and smuggling may be easier through the sector. They might charge no taxes.

If the Empire takes control, they may have frequent Stormtrooper NPC patrols checking your character for illegal goods. Shopkeepers may be charged an upkeep but may get a Stormtrooper patrolling the streets.

Now, speaking of contraband, just like AO, you can take various missions for your faction or neutral missions. The missions you take affect your faction standing. In fact you have to build a certain 'sympathy' for a faction before you are able to declare for them. But, back to my point. If you are charged with delivering a certain quantity of an item safely to a recipient, you must put up a personal bond equal to the value of the goods (prevents the 'ritual krys knife' type of deal).

If you fail to deliver the goods, the game puts out a 'bounty' on you with the reward being equal to your bond. You are now temporarily open to PvP until the statute of limitations runs out or a Bounty Hunter catches up to you and frags yer ass. It's not non-consentual PvP as you knew that if you failed the mission, you are probably toast next time you fail to look around as you have a drink in some cantina.

It's what all of you guys and I myself have been looking for. PvP with a meaning.

This is all real design material from Raph Koster the lead designer for the game. As much as I shy away from the 'Star Wars, I wanna be a Jedi like Luke' kiddy mentality, the game design itself is light-years ahead of AO.

I won't comment on the final product till I see it, but the actual game design 6 months before release is so much more mature and represents so much better a game than AO was 6 months before release. The tradeskills, the skill-tree, the Points of Interest (kinda like DynaCamps done right), the PvP system, bounty hunting, etc. It all represents a company that thought the game out before making it pretty.

Oh, and the graphics are mind-blowing if a tad bit 'cartoony'.

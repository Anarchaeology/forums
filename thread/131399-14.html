quote:
Originally posted by Wolfe
.

What makes the loot so important that you rely on a bot rather than try to meet the challenge with the rescources you have within your little and very special group of friends?
Well, if I have 30 friends, and only 3 are interested in raiding, we have a problem. I can 'force' the other 27 to help me, with neither party being satisfied (one guilty, the other just miserable), I can go try find another group of friends and abandon the ones who don't share my passion, or I can create a club that while not necessarily 'little and special' at least afford me the opportunity to engage in an activity I enjoy when I want to. On my own terms.

Guilds are not the end all and be all for a lot of people. I'd say for the vast majority of players, they are little more than a group of people who enjoy each other's company. That does not mean attacking or or defending towers. That does not mean sitting on 18 hr spawns. That does not mean anything at all, unless we are to take someone's definition as gospel.

'Notum Wars' destroyed a lot of guilds. People either felt guilty not going to battles, stopped logging in for fear of being called to a battle, or any of a number of other guild-breaking phenomenon. No longer were you together as a group of friends. The game now thrust 'duty' on to you ... unless you left your friends for some other org that didn't partake.

The same can be said for the static content around the planet (only mostly in reverse in this instance). So many players were in guilds that they enjoyed being in. Yet, for these players, it was important that their characters always achieved something or always were bettering themselves or had a new notch to put on the butt of their gun. If their guild didn't have the resources to satisfy their needs, they were often forced to abandon them for one of the well-known 'uber-guilds' in order to satisfy their 'professional needs'.

The same can be said for factions. There are so many sided players (most of which probably have since left the game) who were once Neutral and were 'forced' to 'pick a side' because of how the game works. Going against one's grain can be unsettling. Likewise, we have Clanners who would rather be Omni, but the faction they love doesn't offer the power for them to enjoy the aspects of the game that they like.

It all comes down to opportunity. If you are shut out of the opportunities to do what you enjoy because of your org, your faction, your timezone, your health, you either adapt or quit the game. Now, leaving your org or leaving your faction, while potentially addressing the symptoms, does not effect a cure. Even worse, it brings about nasty side effects in the form of guilt and a degree of self-loathing. If, in the isolated instance of raiding Rubi'Ka's 'unique' wildlife, you address your symptoms by an alternate means ('Hunting Clubs'), you don't have to leave your org, you don't have to leave your faction. You are happy and more fullfilled.

Why anyone would argue against a happier and more fullfilled general playerbase is beyond me. That's precisely why I recommend that FunCom look at the now larger group of happy players and somehow attempt to embrace what the players have achieved. They should run with it and enhance it to help provide playtime enjoyment for everyone in the game.

If they had any balls, they would implement a way for a group of 20 or 30 to zone into an outdoor-style playfield with a Merc Camp type feel to it, where they could enjoy what they like doing most without stepping on the toes of any other group of 20 or 30 who feel like getting jiggy with some uber mobs.

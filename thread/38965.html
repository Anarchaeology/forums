The South Paws
It's a fine, bright day in Rome Red's park, the perfect setting for the first day of the Rubi-Ka Little League softball season. Savoy regards the team's she's been coaching for the past few weeks, beaming with pride. The kids look great in their brand new, gold and black "South Paw" uniforms. Spirits are high.

The Galway Mini-Brontos take their positions on the field. By contrast they look a little nervous, Savoy thinks, but that's normal. The Paws are lucky to be able to play their first match at home.

Savoy nods to Yuri who walks up to the home plate, confidently swinging his bat.

"Strrrr-ike!" goes the referee.

Yuri looks apologetically at Savoy, she winks back reassuringly.

"Strrr-ike!"

"Strrr-ike!"

Six pitches latter the Paws are all out. And as the afternoon goes on, it just gets worse. Savoy has to contend between the lost, confused looks from her team and the icy stares of the parents. The final score, 14 - 0 for the Mini-Brontos, says it all.

The mostly Roman audience wanders off subdued. The Paws gather their equipment in dejected silence. Savoy feels a little tug on her sleeve and looks down.

"Ms Savoy-Coach?"

"Yes Raza?" replies Savoy to one of the younger Paws, a little girl of Asian ancestry.

Raza looks up at Savoy with her big, accusing eyes. "You stink." she says flatly.

"Thank you, Raza."

Savoy claps her hands twice to get everyone's attention. "Come on kids, I'll take you all to the Brontos Burger."

Watching form the stands
Watching from the bleachers, a wan smile formed on Ehrynns face. Yes, the South Paws were trounced; yes, the kids look dejected, albeit wonderful in their uniforms; yes, Savoy is handling it well, the bronto burgers will help.

Unfortunately, Ehrynn hears the grumbling of the parents. She knows that the sting of the lopsided score will wear off quickly for the children but it will take some doing for Savoy to settle down a few of the concerned parents. Ehrynn shakes her head again and grins as she anticipates the free coaching advice Savoy is about to receive.

She waved at Savoy who was coaxing the South Paws to keep their chins up. Savoy waved back at Ehrynn, Im taking the team for bronto burgersCan you come along Ehrynn?

Ehrynn turns to Gencop, who had been a good sport about going with her to the South Paw game.

Come with? Ill treat for the burgers. Besides the kids would be thrilled with a visit from a famous Omni-Pol general  It be almost as good as having one of those wild clanner DJs show up, she said teasingly. Ehrynn winked and did her best to smile sweetly as she waited for Gencops answer.

Gencop answered with a wry smile and a nod of the head.

Randa, visiting the park on her way to Trade sees the game unfolding. She notices Jill and waves, but Savoy seems oblivious anything but her players. One can obviously tell she's doing well by the way she grins and winks at her players, Randa thought to herself. Taking up position next to the bleachers, she watches trying to understand the game she's never seen before growing up on Crime.

It's a difficult task, made even more so by the rather strong accent of the person who appears to be wearing some weird sort of battle armor. Why does he keep insisting on telling Savoy's kids to steer right to get off the field? It's apparent to everyone else that's where you go after the first kid walks off in that direction. No need to repeat it three times!

The kids obviously feel bad about having being consistently told they have to walk in that direction so Randa decides to give the man in the heavy armor the evil stare for the rest of the match.

At the end of the game, Randa catches up with Jill and says "I understand it now. Thanks! And congratulations! You did much better than the other team! Not one of your players needed to use that bat to defend herself." Then before Savoy could answer, she runs off to Trade, looking for robot parts.

The South Paws Part II
Its the seventh inning and the South Paws are down 47 - 1 to Argentums Mini-miners. With two out, the Miners batter hits the ball high but not very hard. Savoy watches Maggie scrambles to catch it. "Good girl, good girl." she says softly to herself.

Maggie holds her mitten up, ready for the incoming ball. "That's right. Easy now, easy. Savoys eyes are fixed on the descending ball. She's got it, she's got it, she's got...and she's dropped it. Savoy breathes more easily now. Yup, just dropped the ball in true South Paw fashion."

The batter, who'd given up running when hed realised the ball was going to be an easy catch, springs back into action and rushes for the safety of first base. Maggie bends over to pick of the stray ball, but instead just manages to drop her glasses. The other Paws shout incoherently at Maggie to hurry. She gets on all four and blindly fumbles for her glasses. Her knee makes contact with glass with tale-tell crunch.

It is all too much for her. And as the Miner makes his triumphant home run, Maggie begins to cry. Her crying is loud, wholly unrestrained and uninhibited. The Cosmo has wronged little Maggie, and she is making sure its going to hear her lament.

Yuri picks up the twisted eyeglasses. He tries to straighten the frame a bit, but the glass is shattered. Sheepishly he hands the glasses back to his young teammate. Maggie just starts to cry even louder.

At last Maggie's mother, all red in the face, rushes out from the crowd to spirit her baby away. The onlookers, out of sympathy or sheer relief, give her a round of applause.

The ref looks at Savoy with a nasty "So, can we play ball now? expression, but Savoy just shrugs. By Paw standards, this was actually a comparatively good day.

The spawn time on the boss could be shorter... but... the boss should spawn at higher level if killed, and if not killed within a certain time frame, despawn and respawn at a lower level. So each camp has a level range that the MOBS vary within. The spawn time should be shorter, but it should also be random. There should be other suitable level MOBs nearby too.

Camps should have a small number of locations, and should every few hours, move to another of their locations at random.

Roaming boss MOBS are also a good idea.

The important aspect is that different areas need to be intended for different levels of party. All MOBS in that area (not a full zone, just an area of a zone) should be of a similar level range, with boss MOBs in the area a bit higher.

Hopefully, once there are more, this can be rectified. Risk should be involved, but the player should be able to judge the risk (though flinging the occasional curve ball is also good... lifebleeders at nw mines as an example).

Before I post this, let me make it clear. The only outdoor mob I hunt is the green one standing between me and the mission I pulled.

Reasons I hunt almost exclusively in missions, (and yes, with the degree I do missions, it's pretty much hunting. I've come to pretty much ignore the reward item if the mission is in a good location. The reward item is so rarely one I want anyway.

Reason one. I want tokens. I can get mission tokens in missions, but if there's a place a 53er can get them outside of missions, I don't know about it.

Reason two. I can ALWAYS find mobs of approximately my level in a mission. If the mission is in an area full of greys, or a friendly held city, I know I can kill what's inside for the coin it drops, and ocassionally a good piece of armor or instruction disk.

Reason three. There's 20 odd mobs in there. That's 20-odd mobs in the same general location. Maybe I should change my last statement from "hunting" in missions to "ranching" in missions.

Reason four. A nice 10-15 mobs worth coin reward at the end. You complete the mission, even so-so, and you get a good chunk of coin for your "trouble".

Reason five. Chests, and the loot, or exp, they represent. (Has anyone noticed how many locked chests are empty? I'm not complaining, I DID get exp for my effort.)

Ways to entice ME out of missions and into the great outdoors.

1. Cause a massive power-surge so I can't use my computer anymore and HAVE to go outside. Oh wait. IN GAME outdoors.

1. determine how many mobs a given level generally has to kill to get the 100 percent chance of token. Set this as X. Then make one in X mobs of that level, in selected hunting preserves, or selected "swarms", carry tokens.

2a. add a terminal that will point players to decent hunting grounds for their level/profession. (Level for difficulty, profession for loot-type.) This way I'd KNOW where to find good mobs to hunt.

2b. Add a chat channel to all the cities and shops that reports when a swarm has been sighted. Sort of a news-bulleten channel. "A herd of bronto's have been sighted at X,Y. Spotters report they are aggressive to All. Please use caution when traveling through this area.

3a. For preserves, make the spawn rate fast, and the number of mobs plentiful, so a person of a given level can expect to kill about as many as they'd kill in a mission, in about the same amount of time.

3b. For swarms, (randomly generated concentrations of mobs of a given type,) make the spawn rate fast, and the number of mobs plentiful, but make the fast-spawning time-out after some set duration, so maybe for 30 minutes you can not manage to kill the "last" of a given mob, but on the 31 minute, (if the timer was 30 minutes), they quit respawning at all.

4. In either case, make the mobs richer by 25-50 percent, to make up for the missing mission reward coin. Maybe do the same with experience.

5. In either "perserves" or "swarms" scatter some lootable items, or add some chest-database loot to the mobs, in addition to what they'd already be carrying.

If you did this, and the competition for the mobs wasn't too steep, (and I wasn't too likely to be trained like in EQ,) I might step out of the mission, (where I'm likely to get my pet stuck on the wrong side of a table anyway), and into the virtual sunlight.

Of course, it'd necessitate a new database. Associating MOBs with their group names. "Herd" of bronto, "Pack" of wolves, "Squadron?" of dragons, "Flock" of reet, etc.

The Great Indoors
Greetings.

Perhaps the appearance of the missions could be addressed?

The addition of an arboretum-style mission might afford for more of an open, outdoorsy feeling. High walls, simulated open-to-the-sky glass ceilings in certain rooms, trees, plants, streams, waterfalls, cave-style rooms to seperate mobs. I love the "natural" settings in the grey cave missions; some of the large rooms in there are absolutely beautiful, with wonderful ambient lighting pouring in from the cracks high up in the walls. There's dimension, height and texture in those rooms. Missions with high walls, vegetation, simulated sky ceilings--all within the context of being enclosed in an arboretum (or small terraformed "wildlife park", much like what the newbie hunting grounds look like) would add much variety to the current mission styles.

I really like what missions offer: experience, tokens, chests, cash, items, low lag, being able to hunt at one's desired pace, and all without the concern of anyone being encroached upon.

For many, missions are the most appropriate style of hunting for them, for whatever their reason(s) may be.

The addition of an arboretum-style mission would be very welcome--something which would be more open and "outdoorsy", something which FEELS like you were actually outdoors, while in a mission. That would be a wonderful, welcome addition to the various styles of missions currently available.

Be well.

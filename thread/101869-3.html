Animal Farm is not just a story, it's a political allegory based on the author's
observation of history. The moral of the story is that after a revolution, those who gain will start acting very much like those they replaced.

It is actually rather difficult to find an instance of a violent revolution the outcome clearly justifies the bloodshed and hardships caused by it. And I would not be so quick quoting the ancient American war of Independence as an example. I would argue that war was neither strictly necessary nor just. Allow me to explain.

1. While I agree that a colony having reached maturity has the right to self-rule, that in itself is no justification for war. Self-rule can and has been achieved by peaceful means. The United States would have become independent in time without anyone having to kill or be killed over it, just look at Canada.

2. The Americans were not an oppressed people. The standard of living and freedom enjoyed in colonial American was higher that in Britain, and Britain was doing a lot better than most other nations of the time. The taxation which sparked off the revolt was negligible. Again if you compare the quality of life in the post independence United States and Canada you do have to wonder if this conflict was necessary at all.

3. Of course there were a group of people who were being genuinely oppressed at the time, the slaves. When the constitution was drafted, the issue of slavery was tabled but in their wisdom the Fathers of the Nation decided to let it go.
This decision is directly responsible for the fact that slavery endured in the United States for another century and for the bloody Civil War the was to ensue. It is reasonable to assume that under British rule neither of these would have occurred. On the basis of the continued practice of slavery alone I would judge the American War of Independence a failure and the call "Give me liberty or give me death." rather hollow.

4. Having gained its independence, the former colonies went on a wild, aggressive expansionistic spree. They either bought out or killed anyone in their way of their way, including the Spanish, Mexicans and most notably the native American Indians. Even Canada had to defend itself from the young, aggressive Americans, not just once but twice. It is difficult to say whether under British rule the Americans would be been less aggressive, but its safe to say the United States
demonstrated the same kind of greed and ruthlessness as the undemocratic nations in the Old World. At this level, the Animal Farm parable still works for the American Revolution.

5. The actual differences between the limited American democracy and the parlimentary monarchy in Britain were really quite slight. It is also worth mentionig there have been a lot more AMerican presidents who were linked by blood to each other than British prime ministers.

All of which is to say that American War of Independence was neither a good or evil thing, just one more political struggle like many beofre and after. Most of the objectives of the revolution would have come to fruit without the thousands of deaths, withou the hardships suffered on both sides and without Loyalist families having to flee to Canada in fear of their lives.

The same can be said about the clan. With the Tir accords the clans had earned themselves the right to self-rule in most things. The final restrictions would go when Omni-Teks lease expired. Many people would say that was victory enough for the clans, but I guess human nature never changes. Everyone wants it all now, no one cares who gets hurt in the process.

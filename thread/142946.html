Trying to beat the cookie cutter
Most if not all professions seem to gravitate towards a cookie cutter model.

IE:
Trox Enforcer with a EQB
Soldier with a Div9 (or what ever the current killer weapon is)
Opifex Fixer with a Manex
And so on....

So I've been trying to think how to make it so that every profession and breed could be given a larger and more varied choice.

You could simply intorduce more weapons of equal power and stats. But FC have a habit of making the new weapons a bit better than the old ones, causing everyone to rush for them. Enforcers switching from 2 hand blunts to 2 hand edged. This then doesn't cure the problem, it merely moves it over a bit.

So... I've been thinking, wouldn't it be an idea if at the character selection screen you could pick as follows.
  • Select breed
  • Select size and shape
  • Select Profession
  • Select special skill
  • Select name


Not counting abilities or the first screen of skills really. But if you had a choice of any of the other skills to make Green, what would you choose?

The variation would need to be dynamic.
Kissysuzuki, that was a very interesting suggestion, although I am afraid that it doesn't overcome the cookie cutter problem. You see, what you've proposed is *static*, just like the other startup choices. In effect, you've just added one more crinkle to the cookie cutter.

To overcome the cookie cutter syndrome, you need to encourage *dynamic* variability, so that choices can be made at arbitrary times and then undone when required throughout a character's life. The cookie cutter disappears and your range of possibilities becomes controlled by the maximum extent of your envelope --- think amoeba now, not cookie cutter. :-)

To a very large extent, this is already the case in AO, but it's hidden deep inside each character, namely in your implants. To achieve the same kind of variability in visible items like armor and weapons requires those to be customizable dynamically too, either by their owner or by a tradeskill professional.

That wouldn't be hard, in concept. However, it would entail a pretty major revamp within an existing game.

quote:
Originally posted by Crysantimum


In reply to:

"To overcome the cookie cutter syndrome, you need to encourage *dynamic* variability, so that choices can be made at arbitrary times and then undone when required throughout a character's life."

My first reaction was: Why is that?
But perhaps I didn't understand it. Got another way of saying that?
Sure, I'll try. As Kissy described it, another selection made at startup creates one more characteristic attribute for a character --- it's what I called one more wrinkle, a feature that persist from day one through to eternity (hence invariant in time) and which always governs the same attribute of the character (hence invariant in feature space, one could say).

In contrast, choices made during the life of a character allow for attributes to change at arbitrary times and as many times as desired, so it's highly variant on the time axis. On the space axis, while a choice would persist for as long as it is not undone, the fact that a new and different choice can be made as often as desired means that there is the opportunity for a large amount of variability in feature space as well.

As a result, when you consider the character in its many guises over time and space, cookie cutter characters would be pretty unlikely to form.

An example would probably show the difference more clearly. Let's say that, as in Kissy's example, only one variant capability is allowed, which in her case she chose at character creation to specify that a particular ability would be green. Now let's retain that same limited set of choices but allow the selection to be made dynamically throughout the lifetime of a character. It is pretty obvious that already the cookie cutter begins to lose shape, since over the lifetime of the character a number of different selections of green ability could be made, and at any point in time you couldn't easily guess which would be in in operation. Then, for each new dynamic capability of this type that you add, the range of possible choices all interact to create a real explosion in the number of possibilites, all variant over time. Things become very flexible, and everyone doing the same thing becomes pretty unlikely.

Combine that with similar configurability in armor and weapon attributes and you *really* have an end to cookie cutters. :-)

quote:
Originally posted by Jynne
Too-often we as individuals hang our hats and our hopes on 'silver bullets:' fast, easy, and simple (often one-step) solutions to major and complicated situations and problems. Especially on AO. People in general tend to underestimate the time, effort, and other resources required to build an engine for impacting the storyline, and at the same time overestimate the impact they will have and the time-table according to which it will happen. People also tend to be ego-centric and presume that others will see that they're right, fall in line, and do the real work for them, if only they talk long and loudly enough.
I've enjoyed reading all the great ideas and observations in this thread but the lines from Jynne's post I quoted really grabbed my attention.

I can think of no bigger reason for RP failures, or one that occurs as frequently, as when the people planning it assume they should be listened to and respected just for having the idea for an event. They don't give proper attention to planning or to contingencies. They take a "just follow me and do what I say" attitude rather than telling participants what they're getting into and how the event will unfold.

Surprises are great, and there's a lot to be said for spontaneity. But in my experience if you want the event to work you have to sacrifice both surprises and spontaneity to pull it off. Nobody has to follow you, and the people you have along are more than capable of thinking for themselves - sooner or later they'll resent being used like cattle, to bolster the attendance figures and glorify the person who planned it.

It takes more than a creative idea to make an event. It takes work and lots of it. For a really large event you probably won't find the time to do anything else but coordinate it and plan how it's going to unfold. When I get a /tell from another guild saying something like "We're gonna go attack {x}, it's gonna be huge" I rarely ever say yes to that. Without a plan there's just chaos, and chaos has a bad effect on RP.

I love what Aktrez and Vixen Entertainment have done at the Rompa Bar (I'm not in there enough, is there a champagne room? ).

I'm no expert at events - in any of the ones I've worked on as much goes wrong as goes right. In the last one I made some stupid mistakes. But we've usually been able to get the job done and have a good time doing it. I guess what I'm trying to say is, if you want to have fun and impact the storyline like CoA has done, you've got to be prepared to work to do it. And it seems to me many folks don't think that they should have to work at something in a game.

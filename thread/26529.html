There's a solution Lazzz. It adds a lot to the game if a team decides to go with loot order for chests just like they can with moving monsters. Note that the loot order pertains only to taking the items out of the chest and not for opening it. Whoever is able to unlock the chest the easiest of course will be in charge of the Breaking and Entering:

http://forums.anarchy-online.com/sho...est+loot+alpha

My thoughts:

The chest should be treated just like any other monster. Successfully picking the lock should 'kill it' and experience should be shared and rewarded at this time. 'Looting' rights should go to the next in turn for chest loot.

The experience for picking the lock should be greatly increased and shared between the team. This should be done concurrently with a massive increase in lockpicking difficulty.

If my 129 MP with dark dark blue B&E is picking chests in lvl200 missions there is something wrong.

Those professions with an affinity for 'killing' chests should be wanted in high level groups because nobody else can even get the chests open.

Let's assume that a chest in a lvl200 mission required 900 B&E to open within the first five tries.

Let's also assume that said chest offered experience similar to a Veteran MOB.

Now assume that when the chest was opened, the experience was shared with the team just as if killing that Veteran MOB.

If their is a trap on the chest imagine that disarming it would give similar experience and be shared amongst the team as well. Picture the possibility of a 'Nerve Gas' trap that will Stun the affected meddler but not necessarily kill him. Is that enough of a penalty (and reward) to attempt to disarm the trap?

Then imagine that the abilility to get what was inside the chest was fairly passed from member to member just like monster loot is now.

If the rightful looter decides not to take what's inside, the chest ownership right decay just like they do now with a MOB.

Someone tell me that having a good lockpicker in you group would not be attractive in this scenario. Tell me that this method of dealing with things that need to be 'hacked' is unfair to either the hacker or the team.

To me, it sounds like a solution to many complaints. First the ability to gain experience without killing. And perhaps more importantly, the value of someone with high B&E to a team.

Megabio,

I reckon you'd be surprised at the damage a team can do if they're given the time to do it. With lots of crits a full team of weapons specialists carrying high critting weapons can do an incredible amount of damage in a very short time - but the damage over time on this style of play is nothing exceptional.

If you have battles that are very short, then the crit reductions will easily halve the damage in a team. However, with longer battles the good, steady damage over time specialists and debuff specialists begin to shine much more.

Debuffs that reduce AC will speed up the battle after 14.2, whereas now mobs drop so quickly that their effect is negligible. DoTs that reduce health and nano on mobs now only hit for one tick, but with the battle extended just a little bit longer they can do 6 to 10 times as much damage.

Pets and bots that rarely crit but hit steadily every few seconds, get the chance to hit more often (there's a delay between command and first hit) and players with weapons who don't hit for huge crits because their attack rating isn't as good with their secondary weapons (pet owners, NTs etc) yet still do good steady damage also become more important.

All nukes in the game will have a similar effect too. Most nukes hit steadily for near minimum damage (certainly in the low end). given the time in a battle nuking players will be able to make a much bigger difference to the outcome.

As a result, in an average team build you won't get mobs dropping like stones really quickly, but as the fights become twice as long, all these damage types do twice as much damage, which for some of them can add up to be a great deal of damage indeed.

To take my own profession as an example: if an average fight lasts 6 seconds, I probably have time to get my demon in for one hit and get a nuke off for a total of about 1600 damage. If it lasts 12 seconds I can get in more like 3 hits from the demon and minimum 3 nukes, for a total of more like 4800. Twice the length of battle would mean me doing three times the damage. If the battles last a little longer I have time to anima the pet too, which adds 250 to each hit and so on...

Then on top of that, as Crin points out, you get much more varied game play. In particular the defensive elements of battle become more important. Debuffs reducing damage and inits become more powerful, tactics and teamwork become more essential. The longer a mob stands up, the more chance he has of seriously damaging the team. High efficency single target healing from docs will become more important. Using first aid stims/heal pets to help out team mates in trouble will become more important. Using debuffs to reduce overall damage will be key.... as will managing the aggro by pulling aggro off struggling players and healers etc...

The differences become more obvious at higher levels, where it's common to hunt missions and mobs that are very easily killed indeed, but they will still be noticeable at lower levels too. Right now there are level 100 teams running around happily doing QL180s missions. If they don't manage the risk well by using all of the tools in the teams arsenal, they'll find themselves dropping the QL of missions that they do.

An important question as meligant says hinges around the rewards. As things stand now, speed of XP gained is essential as you move higher in levels. The amount of XP needed to ding rises so fast that many people feel the need to maintain similar increases in speed of gaining levels in order to make the game palettable. Many high level players I know still expect to ding about once a day... but when you need 40 million to ding, you have to kill a lot of critters very quickly.

At the same time, as mobs get harder and harder the overall rewards get less. While it may take a team twice as long to kill a high level mob, they will often find that they only get 50% more XP as reward. This is true at most levels. The level 100 teams doing QL 185 missions, would probably make considerably more XP over time if they chose to do QL140 missions and they'd have lower risk too. It's particularly high level teams that have big issues with the rewards structure though since for many of them missioning for XP is more a chore than a challenge.

Soloing Vs team play has been addressed to an extent. The swarm code that pulls mobs from 2 rooms away often building into massive add situations is far worse when a team is playing than when you are soloing (my worst so far has been 8 mobs at once in the entrance room... we hadn't even set foot in the main room yet! lol). Whereas a team of people seem to pull large numbers of mobs, the solo player can usually manage without pulling anywhere near as many adds.

Still, the rewards for soloing are still nowhere near as good as for teams, even though the risk is somewhat less since the swarm code was introduced.

I'm not entirely sure that raising the XP given for higher level mobs will necessarily work though. The XP/risk/time equation is at its worst at the highest levels and it may be that this is, at least in part, because the higher level players are simply bored with play (in particular missions). It's clear that at lower levels we choose right now to go for more risk at times when we could probably make more XP/time with less risk. As the team's level gets higher though the desire to do long, hard work for the XP becomes less. New content that stimulates the players might make the risk/reward equation less important.

It may turn out that radically new content will be the thing that really makes or breaks these changes.

Yes all of the debuffs in the MP range work.

The thing that you will hear quite often quoted is that the debuffs are 'useless' against mobs because mobs start with quite high skills, AC etc and as a result many players find them pointless in use. In reality they do make a difference. The doc init buffs are excellent. The damage debuffs definitely work. The AC siphons do in fact have an effect. The fact that the difference isn't huge doesn't mean it isn't there.

The other thing that often gets quoted is the taunt factor. Debuffs taunt mobs onto you quite a bit. The general feeling seems to be that using debuffs is pointless because they don't have a large enough effect to justify the risk associated with getting agg.

The best MP dmg/init debuff does -261 to all inits and -37 to all damage types. It's not a wildly good one because MPs have the nano debuffs as their strength. It makes little difference if the mob is only going to hit you a couple of times... but if the mob is going to stand longer, then the damage bit alone can be really very useful. The init reduction also reduces the speed a little.

Think about it, if the mob that hits for 400 stands for 10 hits, you'll probably save one hit in reduced speed and save 750 total in damage. If you put the best Doc init/dmg debuff and MP init/dmg debuff together... you get 2400 init reduction and 45 dmg reduction per hit.....

The thing about strategic team play is that there is no 'killer app' any more. The team that wants to do really well works together. While the mobs' present high skills and AC mean that very few single debuffs will make a huge difference, the combination of trader, Doc and MP debuffs can make a big difference. I've played in teams (particularly at lower levels) where the debuffers have got together and made a huge difference, so I know it works at least at low levels.

As to managing the taunt factor... well my personal opinion is that many professions are too worried about getting agg. I think that part of it comes from the lack of experimentation and lack of usage of the full range of tools. The rest of the team is there to support you if you try and if the team is working together as it should. The more the 'damage steamroller' team play of today turns into a tactical playstyle, the more people will work out the tactics that will work.

I'm a Nanomage MP that has always been nuking fast, debuffing and hitting with my creation weapons. I also have MA skills as my secondary damage... so I'm used to getting beat up on. I got a lot of agg over the levels and died seldom... even when my heal pet was stuck to somebody else! If you put the points into evades, imp for max health, take the HP and AC buffs etc then you can take some beating. And let's not forget that the tank and others are there to take agg back again and will often succeed. And the doc and others are there to heal you when your health starts dropping badly.

And keep in mind too... if you do get together with the other players to debuff, you all do it at the beginning of a battle, the tank puts the effort in to pull the agg back and everybody is now dealing with a slower hitting, weaker hitting, lower critting mob.

As far as the story goes...

I just left another game, Ultima Online, after two years of playing. One of the most often asked questions was: where's the story?

During those two years I saw a three month story-arc take place, but otherwise nada, zip...nothing. The players would have to log on to the official UO website and read "the fiction" surrounding the events, and then basically go kill spawn after spawn of monsters, with no word from anyone representing the Lord of the Land. In the three weeks I've been on Rubi-Ka, I have seen the slayerdroid malfunctioning incident, the explosion in Omni HQ, and the town meeting between Omni Representative Ridgefield and the citizens, where the former relayed to the latter the latest news on the cyborgs, and how the droids and explosion were linked to the cyborgs.

In these three weeks I have seen more interaction between the developers and the players of AO than I ever did in my two years on UO.

So, what happened in those other 21 months I played UO? The same that you, Kirrana, have described in your post. Mainly the players banned together and created their own stories, running parallel but outside of the story-arc provided by the developers. We planned our own events, fought our own wars, or generally "chatted" our time away. We often worked our characters to get them to greater achievements, or explored the countryside provided within the game world. During the three month story-arc we had a blast, running off to save the day at the latest spawn site, but still, there seemed to be something missing...

So, you folks have been waiting a while for things to happen. AO is still fairly new to the market, and the developers are working hard to fix bugs, add content, and make most everyone happy. UO is rapidly approaching its five year mark, and with the exception of the story-arc I've mentioned, and another that took place right before I began, they have done nothing but add expansion after expansion--each one supposedly telling a story--but there has been very little advancement in that area.

I left because of this, and other reasons, but mainly because I had grown bored with doing the same thing over and over. For me, UO was about the people in the game--us--and what we did together to make the game enjoyable for us. If we waited for the developers, all of whom are busy doing many different chores to keep the game playable, we would have waited a very long time. So, knowing this, we created our own stories, our own wars, and our own events to tide us over until the developers added their plot to our mix. It is, literally, the same thing you and countless others are doing in AO.

Thus, instead of questioning your achievements, be proud of what you and others have done to broaden the game for all of us, the players. We are the ones who make this game fun. We cannot wait for others to create the fun for us. Perhaps the developers have let the players down, but if you think this idea through would you really expect someone to provide all of the fun for you? FC has given us a world to explore, and the chance to meet other folks while doing so. They have left a lot of the small things unclear, perhaps to allow us to go our own way and tell our own stories, create our own adventures, and become our own heroes. Sure, it's be great to be at the right place, at the right time, and save Ross from a Clanner assassin (and then get a medal), but I also have a blast keeping my comrades healed while fighting off the bad guys while on a mission. Sure, that town meeting was a hoot, but the small gatherings of folks at Baboons has been quite fun (and a decent chance to role-play, as well).

In the end, this game is about us. FC has merely given us a backdrop on which we live and play, with a story to shape that backdrop as we move around on it. Keep up your good work, all of you, and remember, as writer Stephen King once said, "It is the tale, not he whom tells it."

Peace.

I go to bed thinking nothing of a post I made, other than what it stood for--my views on the AO story in the three weeks I've played--and awake to see that "it's people like [me] that prevent" the developers from admitting they're frauds. Hmm...my point was about the background story of AO, the current situation, and the backdrop which the developers have given us to flesh out with our own actions.

I did this in comparison to the only other MMORPG I've ever played, UO, and I found it interesting that no one disagreed with me...in fact, Amanite made my point for me. Furthermore, if he has played UO--at all--in the last several months, or even bothered to read their developer chats and the proceeding discussions on their boards, he would know that UO has been planning--for many months--a story-arc that could change the many shards in different ways depending on what "in-game" actions the players take during the arc. Sounds to me like UO is taking a page from AO in this respect. Unfortunately, after so many promises from UO, and absolutely nothing happening, I left that game.

I am sorry, but the in-game housing, libraries, and sitting around the West Brit Bank chatting did nothing for the game except become a status symbol for the privileged few who could actually afford housing on the most popular shards. The last thing I'd want is AO copying that prescription as a blueprint for how a game should be played by the players, or run by a company. The worst that could possibly happen to AO is housing for the players, outside of their apartments...you'd eventually have a ruined economy on Rubi-Ka, and perhaps even more players whining about how unfair the game has become, not to mention lag from hell, and the destruction of perhaps the prettiest landscaping I've ever seen in a MMORPG. So, you can keep your villa in Sosaria (Tram or Fel) for that matter, but leave Rubi-Ka alone.

Also, as a long, long time veteran of RPGs (I've been playing them since D&D was a game that came in a little white box, with three parchment like books with crude illustrations...think 1978, folks), while the role of the Dungeon Master was storyteller, it was his duty to create a world inwhich our players lived and breathed, and if we--the players--were at all interested to make the world seem like a "real" place, then by all means we explored, planned our own stories and acted them out under his/her supervision. It was our action, in the olden days, which sometimes propelled the story along. There were several times, when as a DM myself, I simply arrived to the session with a loose outline and let the players go wild with their imagination.

So, I draw a parallel to then and now with AO. If your only interest in AO has been to "level up" and do mission after mission, because Funcom will not spoon-feed you the story, then so be it. They have told us a wonderful story on their website, a well-crafted background--thus presenting us a backdrop--in which to allow our characters to run free and wild. Sure, I too level up, but not without taking time to chat with folks and role-play when given a chance. Sure, what I have just described could be attributed to UO, as well, but in-game and out of game are two totally different ball games. I will not rehash what I have already written to make my point.

There's a huge difference between pen and paper games and online RPGs; whereas I could plan events for one night or a series of sessions, and I could "go with the flow" when the players chose to go right at the intersection, when what I had written was for them going left, a game of AO's scale cannot be so easily manipulated. I am by no means a MMORPG developer, so I cannot fathom the amount of coding necessary to predict what possibly 1000s of players may do at any given time.

I did, however, learn a long time ago not to make promises I could not keep during my gaming sessions. I learned not to sit down at the head of the table and proclaim, "You folks are in for a treat tonight, after you see what I've planned..." Most often what I planned was thrown out the window when the players went right instead of left, or attacked an NPC (subsequently killing them) which was vital to the story...sure, I could and often did roll with the punches, and was able to think quickly on my feet. There's a big, a very big difference between playing with a group of five or more, compared to 100s of players and their randomness, I could not plan for that and I doubt any developer really could, for that matter. So, maybe then that is what really irks you folks...broken promises. Perhaps this is the first time in your life where you have been let-down? If so, welcome to the real world, where things don't always go as planned.

So, then, it comes down to "us" and what we are doing in the in-game world. You say there is no story here in AO, but I challenge you otherwise. During the town meeting where Ridgefield updated the citizens of Omni on the cyborg situation, he told us to keep our eyes and ears peeled for further information. Just last eve, whilst I slept, my fiancee comes across an NPC she has not noticed before in an outpost rarely visited. She iniated conversation with him, and lo and behold he gave her a mission based on "dismantl[ing] robots and other animated machinery found roaming without apparent supervison around the main cities of Rubi-Ka. Keep an eye out for anything unusual amongst their remains. If you manage to gather anything that looks suspicious enough" bring it back to said NPC. While this may, or may not, have anything to do with the cyborgs, it certainly was a shock to her to be asked to undertake this mission, and it adds to the intrigue already given to us by the current state of affairs on Rubi-Ka.

Nothing like this EVER happened in UO, though time and again the developers PROMISED us on-going content which would develop the world around us. In fact, due to a lawsuit, the in-game seers, the players who took time to create "one night stand alone" adventures for fellow players, were all let go and any on-going stories created by these seers were dropped. The three month story-arc they developed was nothing more than a chance to collect "semi-rares" or items needed to craft them. We never once met up with or even had a chance to catch a glimpse of the NPCs mentioned in their "story-arc fiction" (which, I might add, ANYONE with a sense of creativity could have written, as it was so one dimensional).

UOs latest attempt at an expansion, called Lord Blackthorn's Revenge, promised the very same kind of "world altering events" as promised by AO, but two months after the release I have yet to hear that any of this happened...and I am sure if it had begun I would have read about it on my old guild's website. Nearly five years after UO's release, and maybe a total of six to eight months of a story? Please, enough already. They did, and still do, promise ongoing content...which usually means "nerfing" character classes, changing character templates, or something equally non-involving but toally destructive to that which we worked so hard to create. They started the "Faction Wars" which were quite fun, but soon became tedious as it was not any kind of war, other than roaming gank squads with their white wyrms and dragons let loose upon the countryside. Sure, you could hold towns in Felucca with an invasion force, but the only ones in Fel were the factions, thus the impact upon the "world" as a whole was minimal, to say the very least. For some players this was a very good thing; for the majority, which was never effected one way or another by these wars, it was another let-down.

So, here I am, three weeks into AO. Perhaps I should not say anything about this so-called "lack of story" as I am so new to this world. However, I chose AO for its setting and gameplay. When I downloaded the game, for free I might add, I had no expectations except to have fun. I was unconcerned about the story-arc, as I was too busy learning the game's many different options and character templates. Yet, one night while minding my own business, I suddenly find myself in an area under invasion my malfunctioning slayerdroids, which is then capped off with an explosion in Omni HQ. Whoa. These events made me research the AO background story, and the subsequent short films, available on the website. Then the meeting with Ridgefield takes place, and I feel that I am part of a grander story than anything else I've played in two years (except stand-alone games like Baldur's Gate).

My original intention in replying to Kirrana's post was to comment that we cannot allow the world around us to become stagnant, that indeed it is up to each of us to contribute where we can within the world of Rubi-Ka. It was the same thing in UO, which a few of you so aptly pointed out; it was we, "the players" interacting with each other in the world provided to us by the developers. Instead of waiting for us to be told a story we created our own, hoping--but never receiving--that the UO developers would indeed keep their promises. After two years of broken promises, and becoming quite bored with rearranging the furniture in my UO home, I left. If you feel that owning property makes you a better player, fine. To me, owning a home in a world is a reward for knowing how to save my gold, having nothing to do with the story-arc or role-playing.

AO, again, is about US, not them. They have provided a rich, detailed world for our perusal and exploration. Instead of complaining that there's nothing to do in Rubi-Ka, here on these boards, why not take some time out to go do a little exploration? Create your own stories, or find a group that role-plays and have fun with them. We quit being spoon-fed around the age of two, so why would we expect the same to happen when we've reached a certain level of maturity? Last, if you're not having any fun here, while playing AO, and you haven't done anything on YOUR own to change that feeling, then for God's sake move on to another game. I'll tell you this much, though, you'll be damned lucky to find any other game out there that offers what AO offers.

All MMORPGs make promises. It's usually up to the player to fulfill them. Therein, folks, is your challenge. People like Kirrana have met that challenge, while the rest just complain. Until you folks become game developers, and realize what a huge undertaking a MMORPG must be, I would suggest you enjoy what has been offered to you.

I agree with Windchill in that the storyline should be a backdrop to our roleplaying. Even so it does matter. For instance, the end of the Amnesty was a small turning point for Savoy. She realised at that point she was not going back home anytime soon but rather this war was likely to drag on for years yet; probably long after her tour of duty was over. And though she still does "her bit" for OT, she fights with less conviction these days. This was genuine character growth based on an official event. What more can you ask for from a computer game?

For our day to day roleplaying however, we must organise it for ourselves, each one in their own respective roleplaying groups. It's hard, and in many ways the "grammar" of roleplaying in a mmorpg is still being developed. But consider how much time and work a table top GM puts into his or her campaigns?

Consider also this; good roleplaying is an intimate affair, it works best when the GM knows and understands the quirks and motivations of his players and can tailor events to suit them. By its very nature this cannot be played out on "massively multiplayer" scale. While the large scale events can be fun, roleplaying is an activity that thrives in smaller groups of people who know each other's player characters and have common interest; what in table top terms is referred to as "the party". Translating the dynamics of table top GM and party into your own AO group is I think the way forward. YMVY of course.

I've read the posts above, and although it saddens me, have to agree with most of them.

AO's potential to be an epic rollercoaster of a story is there, I even believe that the groundwork is so solid that if treated right, it could achieve cult status and spawn off a million sequels and side games, such is the potential for immersiveness.

All those out there who haven't read Prophet Without Honour, go buy it and read it now, it's fantastic. If all else fails, Ragnar, you're still one heck of a writer. I haven't enjoyed a book like that since the last Arthur.C.Clark book I read, and that's a big commendation. Finishing the book left me full of questions. Questions I wanted to game to answer in a way that I could take part, to actually be in this wonderful world that I had read about. Sadly, it doesn't look like this is going to happen.

I won't lie, the number 1 reason I came to Anarchy Online was for the storyline. I'm a sucker for emotion and I wanted to play a game that would make me happy, sad, angry, upset. AO looked perfect, beautiful graphics, a huge world and a complicated but immersive system sucked me in. Now however the storyline has died a pitiful death. The political scene in which I was so interested in is non-existant and so called events are few, far between, and generally nothing to do with a 'plot' whatsoever.

What disturbs me slightly is what I notice from working on AO-Basher. Since christmas, there has been maybe 2-3 news posts actually created by Funcom themselves. The rest, in their hundreds now, are from players. I know this because I get sent the same reports myself.

The Official site takes these reports as they are, with no changes and puts them on their site maybe 2-3 days later than they have been on the Fan sites. The reports on the AO site are all done at once as well, which means that the first posts disappear into the archives immediately and are not seen by 70-80% of the readers who don't have the time to troll through all the back posts. It's almost as if Funcom are trying to bury and storyline that the players are trying to create themselves.

I have to wonder whether priority is in the right place. First of all the official site seems to think that news is a chore, not a huge part of the game. Not even an acknowledgement or a statement about an event is created by the story team, even if it goes against that faction's beliefs!

Then there was the team play guide, which I honestly have to say was mostly useless to 99% of players. That time could have been spent on things which benefited all of us, not something that should have been in the original manual at launch.

Finally comes the lack of information from Funcom, we are hearing less and less official feedback every day. What has happened! Are the players no longer important enough to inform them what is happening! People would be much more tolerant of gaps without any physical activity if you informed them what was going on instead of leaving all of us in the complete dark and wondering if anyone is actually still at the Funcom Offices!

Well I'll stop there before this gets so long people stop reading it, but I urge everyone who reads this to add their views. It is unfortunately the only way in which we ever seem to get listened to!

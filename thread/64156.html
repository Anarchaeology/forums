Catharsis
I know this thread will most likely attract flames, but I don't honestly care...

This realization struck me while reading the Neocron forums and the arguments between some powerleveler/PKers and some RPers. It then struck me what almost the entire population of AO consists of....

MUNCHKINS

Those of you familiar to Pen and Paper RPG will know munchkins as those players who just crunched numbers and didn't understand what "in Character" meant. Munchkins thought the entire point of the game was gain levels, kill things, and get loot. RPers defined a character, got into character, and cared more about what fit in with the character's personality than what made the character more powerful.

It dawned on me that the concept of "nerf" is completely and totally irrelevent in a role playing game. The folks who scream nerf are treating the RPG the same as they would a game of space invaders. Try to get the highest score and the highest level fastest.

On the other hand, if someone were actually roleplaying their character, they would first off think of a backstory. They wouldn't choose their weapons and armor based on the effectiveness unless that itself fit in with their character. When you think along these lines, AO honestly isn't a bad game at all. Unfortunately the players have ruined it.

A roleplayer may decide to make a loner neutral, unconcerned with Omni-Tek, wishing only to be alone away from civilization. This character lives in deserts of Rubi-Ka, eeking out an existance while trying to find meaning in his existance in his solitude. As a desert dweller he wears Nomad armor. He would probably be an adventurer as that fits in with the "loner" concept. However, if this Adventurer didn't find a kind of symbiant relationship with the wildlife of Rubi-Ka, it wouldn't be in character to use any of the morphs adventurers get. As such, this roleplayer wouldn't bother using any morphs, even though they would give him more power. They would choose whatever weapon suited them, whether it was truly effective or not. Gaining levels wouldn't concern the player, they would be incidental increases in power that would merely unlock new areas for him to explore safely. If he doesn't do as much damage with the weapon he chooses, it doesn't matter since the point of the game for him has nothing to do with being able to do the most damage he possibly can.

Well, a character like that might very well be fun to play. Unfortunately, a loner is the only character type you CAN roleplay in AO because if you deal with any other players, you will never hear a single word that is actually in character.

To get back to the topic of "munchkins", in almost every campaign, munchkins completely ruined the sense of immersion and plot. As such, roleplayers took great delight when DMs smited the munchkin players at will. On the same note, and with that in mind, it would serve a greater good if Funcom nerfed every uber item in the game to complete and total uselessness to act as a lesson for the munchkins ruining the game for those who actually want to play as the game was meant to be played.

When I stop to look at the time taken to write all the item descriptions and the attention to detail in all the areas of Rubi-Ka, I realize that Funcom truly did wish for this to be a roleplaying game. All the settings were there for any player to roleplay to their hearts content; all they needed was a tiny bit of imagination. The players they got were mindless number crunching munchkins who ruined their vision and made a potentially great game into a 1980's arcade game.

You are dead on, Pip. Most MMOG's are filled with Munchkins and AO is no exception. Its kind of funny, I was recruited into a role playing guild. Yet, it took about two months of encouragement before anyone but the leader and myself would actually talk in character most of the time on guild chat. Before that, the primary topic was game issues(the munchkin variety) and the second was what the member did that day at work or school.

The nice thing is that people do change their play style if you encourage it. I use humor to draw people into developing a character. Travesty is a jokester, designed to pull people into 'in-character' discussions. She has a pet baby rollerrat named 'Sirsqueeksalot' and often updates the guild on its growth or latest evil deeds. By talking about it, the guild member has two choices: to ignore her play or to comment on the rrat. If they do the former, I choose, as a player, to ignore their next Munchkin chat('Im exhausted from my day at work'). If they choose the latter, I try to bring them into Trav's little make-believe world by laughing at their jokes, poking fun at them and cue them in on stupid stuff that she does. I think it has worked fairly well. I believe it has helped some non-roleplayers and casual roleplayers look at the game differently.

I think humor works best, because it is nonintrusive and doesnt have the nasty side effects that dramatic roleplaying can have. My first character, 'Shard', was a failure because she was so dark. The casual rp'ers couldnt relate to a melodramatic character that wasnt in a good mood all the time. She was designed to be an evil woman that was trying to turn a new leaf. I found that players in her old guild were actually frightened of her. They were shocked when I would make jokes or speak warmly out of character(in brackets of course). They just thought that I was a wicked b!tch. I don't think I converted a single player into a rp'er while playing Shard. She was too intimidating.

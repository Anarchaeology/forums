Here are a few tips I've picked up running adventures for my org. It's safe to say though that I am still learning.


* A table top GM has a lot more flexibility the online GM. He can control any number of NPCs, seamlessly skip from location to location or even skip ahead in time. To regain a bit of flexibility, don't try to tell the story in one big session but rather let the story unfold over a series of short in game sessions played over a period of time. That way if at the end of one session a player unexpectedly decides to go talk to the insurance investigators and see what they think about the accident, you have plenty of time to create the appropriate NPC and place him in a suitable location for the meeting before the next game session.

* Use email (or a message board) to supplement the in game sessions, especially for scenes which are too complicated (or to uninteresting) to play out in game.

* Don't be afraid of using GM narrative to describe things you cannot represent in game like a hut on fire or an angry mob coming towards you with torches and pitch-forks. I tend to use group chat for GM narrative and Say for in character speech.

* If I don't have an NPC in the current scene, I will use a character with a cloaking device to act at the GM's avatar.It helps to be able to see what the players are seeing.

* Some people are happy enough to re-use their "main" characters to play any old NPCs in an adventure. I was never comfortable with that. On the other hand if you were to create a proper character for each NPC you would soon run out of slots and waste vast amount of time levelling them up. In the end I've compromised and created a couple of "toons" called Generic01 and Generic02 to play all my NPC roles. Thus Generic02 in one session might be Dr Salvador Braun, in the next (after a quick wardrobe change) he may play Inspector John St John. The naming convention "Genericxx" serves to warn my players not to assume they recognise the NPC until I introduce him.

other ways
Savoys post is a really great explanation of how to get round some of the limitations of online game and thus run events in a table-top style... they sound great.

However being a live RP purist (where the GM only has NPC's if he can find people to play them) my take on it is that I prefer not to have narrative at all - if it cannot be done in AO then it can't happen in the story I'm telling.
The exception to this is to allow players to use emotes such as /me enters data crystal into pc control panel - or to send very simple ooc messages to individuals via direct comms like ((your Yalm is broken you are going to crash)

If I'm gonna have NPC's then I or someone else will role up an alt specific to that role. Or better still I'll liase with another guild and mesh some background of our guild members with something they are doing. (plans are for NPC's to run into other storylines later - but as the same character)

The downsides are - this takes a lot more time that Savoys style... and thus I run a lot fewer events. it can be logisitically exhausting sorting things out with other guilds - and there is more risk of too many players knowing what is likely to happen ((more people involved in planning))

the upsides - uses the strengths of Online RP to give a more immersive (I hope) event based around the virtual world which exists... not just tabletop on a pc

Dragon Coalition are a guild in the main new to RP (though some tabletoppers and live players amongst us) if you'd like to see how this kind of event evolves - come join us lilhymph (i think I'm right in thinking you have already met Symbo our head fixer IC) we're just starting heavily RP based events and smaller RP individual stories and it might be fun to be here and see how it goes.

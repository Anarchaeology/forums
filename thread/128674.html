Trade-once items as opposed to nodrops?
One thing I'd love to see are "trade-once" items. It wouldnt affect ninjalooting one bit, in fact - might even prevent it to an extent.

An item would be Trade-Once at first and when traded to someone it would become nodrop. While it is trade-once, it wont be possible to wield or use it, and to make it into the nodrop usable part you could have it either traded or make even more use of the ID-Data's you can make with the hologram camera+ID-extractor.

I dont really think I need to explain it further, but it would mean that anyone in the kill-team could loot an item from an "ubah boss" and then trade it to the person that wants it. If the right person loots it, all he has to do to use it is to attach an ID to it - should be simple enough in theory at least. I can imagine a number of things that could work like this actually but I wont make a list of them (mainly weapons) because people will try to bite my head off if I do.

Obvious benefits of trade-once:

Your guild drops a high level mob. Someone from your guild secures loot, loots everything then holds the raffle for it. Because the person who opened loot was trustworthy and because they can loot all the items instead of having to leave NODROPs on the corpse, they don't have to relinquish loot and risk its being ninjad. They can just give the items directly to raffle winners. Additionally, there is no risk of the corpse rotting with items still on it during the raffle.

Obvious drawback of trade-once:

Selling Gaily Painted Hood 800 mil!

The real question is: Do the benefits outweigh the drawbacks? I think so, but then, I'm just me

/rant on

NoDrop was the saddest attempt in making items rare. Making rare items simply drop off of unique creatures in ready-to-use form and then giving these rare items some morbid blessing that makes the first person to pick it up unable to trade it to anyone is, to be nice about it, illogical. Not to mention that a ninja'd NoDrop item can never be recovered.

Unique is another tag I really have problems with. Which mystical force of nature dictates that I may only have one copy of certain things? Is this tag a bad attempt at preventing loot whoring? I think it is. I think that the tag was implemented in order to try and stem loot whoring of certain items. As with NoDrop, it is logically backwards. The Unique tag is a band-aid. Loot whoring is a symptom, not the problem. The problem is the loot dispersion method. Loot whoring is only made possible when you put loot worth whoring on the drop table of a mob (whether that drop is guaranteed or if there's a chance to it).

TradeOnce will only add more complication and more problems to the game. It is simply another band-aid fix to a notorious symptom (ninja looting) of the problem (crap loot dispersion method); it doesn't cut straight to the problem itself (neither does the policy, another band-aid fix). The problem is purely in the fact that these rare items come in ready-to-use form and placed on the loot drop tables of unique mobs. When a unique mob is killed, there's nothing stopping someone on the kill team from going whacko and ninja'ing every piece of loot on the freshly killed mob and the ninja loot policy doesn't dictate the honorable return of the item to the rightful killers of the mob.

What I'd like to see happen:

1. NoDrop / Unique tags removed from the game.
2. Current loot tables of unique NPCs cleared. Nothing rare/uber should be a direct drop from an NPC.
3. NPCs who had their loot tables cleared should have their loot tables re-populated with common items and should give more XP...much more. Anything in the outdoors should give more XP, but that's for another thread...
4. All items previously on those loot tables be assigned a 'recipe' and should only be obtainable through tradeskills.

A very good example of what I'm thinking of can be found in the existance of the Virral Triumviate Egg. There is NO PLACE to camp Soul Frags. There is NO PLACE to camp ingots. There is a place to camp the Mantis Eggs, and that is the slight imperfection in this equation, but it is slight enough to overlook for the purposes of an example. You can't find a VTE dropping off of anything; it has to be made. If the whole purpose of NoDrop & Unique was to prevent rare item saturation, the VTE should be a glaring reminder of how ineffective a mechanism of prevention it is. I'm a level 199 NT; I've never worn or even possessed a VTE.

....and what was FC thinking when they made uber loot drop off of quest-related NPCs (read: Smug/Eel)?

/rant off

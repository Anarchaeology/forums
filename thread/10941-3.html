Which is really my primary point: communication between a company like FC and its customers is the single most important aspect of a service industry like this one. When "odd things" happen and disgruntled players are left to speculate, it quickly spirals out of control. quote:
Now my point is that he took one hell of a beating from some of these posters for something that caused no "$12.95 a month subscriber" any loss. So the bank analogy doesn't work here that's an example of theft and a nice ethical case example but unfortunately the world, as I previously mentioned, is not black and white and no loss was encurred to any paties envolved.
Actually, the reason the bank analogy is such a classic ethical example is because it too, on first blush, looks like a "victimless" crime. The very point of such an example, though, is to try to illustrate what might result if such actions were, in fact, allowed to be commonplace. You say that "no loss was incurred", but if that were really the case, why did some folks get so upset? Is it simply, as Paldorr suggests, just a case of "sour grapes"? Jealous players who want their handouts, too, and will whine if they don't get them?

I think it is slightly more than that...there is a principle involved, and allowing this sort of incident sets a very bad precedent. In order for a gaming universe such as AO to be judged as even and fair for all players, rules need to be adhered to. Sure, you can argue that in this case "no loss was incurred to any parties involved", but where does it end? If it's ok to have a GM move YOUR character from one server to the other, is it ok to have another GM buff MY character by +100 health and +2% crits for a big fight? What about +5000 health and +50% crits permanently? Is that still ok? Silly examples, I know, but they are just to prove a small point.

That point is that policies preventing GMs from providing services or showing favor to one player but not to others are, in fact, there for a very good reason: To keep the playing field level. In an online game designed around PvP and competition, such rules keep order and prevent a system which endorses company/player collusion and favoritism. Nobody wants a game universe like that, right?

I'll go one step further and elaborate on the idea presented here. Bear with me, as this is a complicated idea...

Right now CriticalIncrease is an interesting modifier. It means, in effect, "Increase the chance of a critical hit by XX%". This means that with a 24% crit buff, I'm guessing, the following algorithm happens:

1) Random number is generated between 1 and 100. If this number is <=24, automatically crit, goto #4, else, continue with #2.
2) else, calculate crit chance based upon evades vs. attack. Generate a random number to determine if the hit is a "normal" crit. If crit, goto #4, else, goto #3.
3) Calculate regular damage based upon attackers attack rating and inflict damage. END.
4) Calculate crit damage based upon attackers attack rating and inflict damage. END.

Why is this "wrong"? Because the critbuff unilaterally increases the crit chance. If you were deprived, plundered, divested, whatever back to the stone age, didn't have any buffs, etc, you'd still crit 24 % of the time with UVC (albeit the crit AMOUNT would be less, but....)

What about redoing CriticalIncrease as such: Let's say there is a "new UVC" buff with CriticalIncrease 200%. What this means that if you have a 5% chance of a crit based upon your ATTACK vs. the opponents EVADES (as I outlined in line #2 above), the 5% is increased by 200%, i.e. you now have a 15% chance to crit.

This means that having UVC or any other critbuff doesn't totally disregard others' evades.

It means that your attack rating is important in all cases. It means that a L125 player won't have the ability to crit a L200 player with maxed evades like mad (as is the case today, and will even be the case after 14.2).

So basically, by making CriticalIncrease increase the BASE CHANCE of a crit with attack vs. evades, as opposed to a unilateral % of your hits, regardless of what the opponents evades are, you actually take skill of the player into account (imagine that).

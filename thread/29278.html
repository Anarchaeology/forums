Why Anarchy Online has failed.
Why Anarchy Online Failed.

Anarchy Online was advertised as the be-all end-all to MMPORGS.
Leveling without killing
Viable trade skills
No camping
Changing game state
Huge story arc
The ability to solo play thru the entire level span
Player interaction and intervention into the story arc
Lots of game content

None of those happened. We can dismiss them from the discussion aside from camping and game content (which go hand in hand) that we�ll return to.

Each profession has at least one, usually more, game breaking complaints that have never been addressed.

Soldiers have ONE nanoline that actually matters or makes them unique. This line also comes with such a huge vulnerability (roots) as to make it almost unusable in PvP and VERY risky in PvM.

MAs don't really have a lot to complain about at the moment. Probably the least broken class. However, there are a couple of MA Specials that don't (and never have) work which most of them would greatly like fixed.

NTs lack the ability to OE their main damage line, and have comparatively weak top end nanos along with being highly limited in ammo.

MPs are almost entirely pet reliant, and pet pathing is so bad these days that many MPs are actually reducing their pet nanoline skill levels in order to equip better weapons. That being said, they are probably the third least broken class.

Fixers, well, where to start? The bad guns, the unreasonable nano requirements, and the class-balancing item that is unbelievably rare?

Agents are just vehicles by which to deliver a huge aimed shot. Such character-class focus on a single, hard to use, and devastating attack leaves them both horribly underpowered in PvM and unbelievably overpowered in PvP. They, at the same time, need fixing and nerfing (in many peoples opinion. I am not trying to restart that debate here.)

Engineers are entirely pet reliant, have almost no useful buffs, and in the end game, rarely even cast their pets due to the consistent problems with pet pathing. Their only real route to high-lvl play is Slayerdroid Transference.

Enforcers are overall well balanced, but horribly one-sided. The �hit Q and turn on the TV� quote almost entirely sums up the class. They are probably the second least broken class. Many people do actually enjoy playing enforcers, but they certainly could use some attention to their high-end game.

Docs are almost uniformly unable to solo past the entry levels. They are also ridiculously expensive to play without a higher-level player or guild to support them. In addition, their current power in PvP makes them hard to aid, as doing so would only unbalance PvP further.

Adventures, lol, um they need help and everyone knows it.

Traders have had their primary team skills removed in 14.2 and 14.4. They are capable of calming thru solo missions, but when 14.4 hits their heals, they will not be able to add much of anything to a team aside from randomly high damage (shotguns) which pulls agro from the tank.

Crats are incredibly powerful at low levels, yet once their charms stop working (around 90) they become very difficult to play well. While not badly broken, they need a better high-end game.

This is not intended to be a discussion of those problems. Rather, let us accept that every class has serious issues that FC has never resolved (aside from possibly MAs). That being said, and since FC is apparently unwilling to fix these issues, why do people still play this game?

There are several reasons:

� Character Twinking. AO is the game that most allows you to mold your character to the tiniest level of detail. You can take a lot of time and get them to be the absolutely best possible with the use of many, many resources. For lots of players, this was more fun than the actual game itself. This has been, for the most part, removed from the sub 120ish game by the 14.2 patch.
� Game content, such as it is. There are many people who play AO because they are able to enjoy the limited content that it has. However, most of the player base cannot. I will quote a post, in its entirety, that I feel strongly represents most of the player base: �
quote:
Originally posted by Bionitrous Gotta admit, it seems they really have their hearts set on making Midgard a good game. We haven't heard a peep out of the old-timers like Tommy Strand in ages. I think there were even some grumblings about developers having to be dragged off Midgard to keep AO alive. Almost everything you hear about Midgard is in response to what they did totally wrong with AO: no scripted story, ability to change the environment, proper skill system, tradeskills at the top of the list, etc., etc.

My opinion of many of the people in AO I would not like to put into words. The fact that the game mechanics bring out the absolutely most vile aspects of human nature in order to achieve 'success' in this game must be something that is both very sad and very enlightening at the same time for first-time MMORPG developers. As often as I call these guys 'inept', I probably should have used the word 'inexperienced' instead. I am almost certain that most of them never laid foot on Britannia or whatever Asherons's Call's continent is called.

The subject that this thread has moved towards (the total failure of the special content system rather than something as simple as 'spawn time') could rank as the ultimate nail in AO's coffin. People can live without Story (some won't, some will). Most people don't give a crap about PvP (I do, but won't indulge till it's fun). Most people will leave and leave at the next opportunity when it becomes increasingly apparent that there is absolutely nothing worth attempting in this game other than clicking on the mission terminal yet once more.

For most people (I guess some would call them 'undedicated') it would be a total and utter waste of time to attempt to enjoy any of the special content put into the game over the last half year. I try to speak for these people:

The people who are not being taken care of are the group of 6 lvl 50's who have absolutely no chance of getting Neleb's coat because there's some group of lvl200 Soldiers getting 40 for new guild inductees. They might keep their subscriptions longer if they could actually enjoy the dungeon when they log on. They decide to go on another mission.

The people who are not being taken care of are the group of 6 lvl80's who mezz and fight their way to the lab director's office only to find some level 200 Enforcer there sitting in an empty room. The Crat in the group might actually have enjoyed his day and put a smile on his friend's faces when he donned his suit for the first time. The Crat once again logs off in disgust. The others decide to do a mission.

The people who are not being taken care of are the group of 12 level 120's who decide to spend a whole day canvassing the Smuggler's Den in and effort to build comaraderie and guild unity only to find the dark side of human moral ability and 'dungeon' that more represents recess at middle school. They log off in disgust.

The people who are not being taken care of are the close-knit group of lvl 160 players who are getting burned out on doing missions and looking for some huge monster to try to kill. They are blocked from even trying by the fact that the monster is either not present or 20 level 200's quickly dispatch them time after time after time. They log off in disgust.

The people who are not being taken care of are those who wish to embark on a quest for the holy grail of their respective professional equipment. They gather their comrades and head out to the rumored location only to be spoiled by some pimply-faced societal reject who calls in his gang of lvl200 other-professions at the last minute. You log off in disgust.

These are the people I profess to be speaking for with my admitted rhetoric. I think there are more of them than the 'entitled' might realize. There are a ton of us out there. Every single day, week after week, it gets harder and harder to find a reason to log on to what could easily be an enjoyable game with a few minor changes.

If I'm wrong and the majority of people feel that it's a good thing that this content is set up in such a way that a tiny majority of the playerbase can enjoy it OVER and OVER and OVER again, then I apologize. To me, it just seems silly.

� People play because their friends do. As alternatives begin to appear on the horizon, we hear more and more players saying how they are only around until X game comes out. This will have a cascade effect. When RK1 is as empty as RK2 is now, will YOU still play?
� PvP players don�t have many options aside from AO at this time. Most PvP player are also �twinkers� and AO best allows them to demonstrate their skill by out-twinking other players. However, each successive patch re-breaks PvP in some new way, unbalancing it further or in an entirely new direction.


What could FunCom do to mitigate these problems? They are obvious to most reasonable people to one extent or another. Surely FunCom wants to keep AO going, at least long enough to get their next product out. To keep AO viable FunCom must quickly do the following:

1. Empower game arbiters. The current petition system is laughable. Players lose XP, money, and items even entire inventories and have no recourse what so ever. This is unacceptable to the player base and untenable to defend. It�s a database; it should be a simple matter to create a tool that checks yesterday�s database for item, XP, or cash discrepancies at player request. Everyone would be willing to understand that they have to wait a day to try and get their equipment (or whatever) refunded. What they do not understand and are unwilling to accept is that if a bug devalues their time spent obtaining something for their character, they have no recourse at all.
2. Attempt to balance PvP. There are many reasonable players who don�t just scream �nerf the last guy who killed me�. FC needs to use them as a resource to develop PvP game rules that allow for more fair play and general enjoyment. This cannot happen outside of the overall class fixing or you just empower a class for PvP and ruin them for PvM (ala agents). (Before you think that you don�t care about PvP, there really, REALLY is nothing else to do in the end-game but PvP, if you keep playing, you WILL care about it.)
3. Greatly, drastically, and immediately improve FunCom to player interaction. Not only should the devs responsible for game development post on these forums, they should be required to. Look at the excellent relationship Nizzo has with the test server player base. Information flows both ways and everyone feels like they are in the loop. All of this without any kind of in game support (outside of bug hunting). There are many people who play only on test. Why? Because they feel more a part of the game there than they do on any Live dimension. When the devs are going to nerf something, they should HAVE to run it by the player base, when they�re going to fix something, they should inform the player base. Take a page from the Customer Relationship Management book of EVERY company in the world: �I�m working on the problem, here�s what I�m doing to fix it, what do you think about that?� is ALWAYS a better response �I�ll fix it.� Customers will respond better to a fix attempt that fails than to no information at all. Every successful company in the world understands this, why does FunCom not seem to?
4. Game Content. FunCom needs more game content NOW. Not in Shadowlands, not by the fall, but now. SWG beta is about to start. FunCom cannot seriously expect players to pay for new content when the current content is basically unavailable to 75% of the player population. If FunCom did something to address the content/camping issue, I would be happy to buy ShadowLands, knowing that the content in it will be available to all. As it is now, since I am not and do not want to be a member of THE uber guild, why should I pay to not experience what ShadowLands adds to the game?
5. Rebalance PvM so that characters are able to advance at a slow but steady and VISIBLE rate, throughout the entire game, solo. I should be able to get a level in a weekend, playing on my own, all the way till at least 180. As it is, even I, as a pet player could not do this after 110. Perhaps the answer is to remove the huge HP buffs mobs got in 12.6 but lower the XP gained by team play even more. Leave it so that a full team can overall gain more XP faster than solo play, but not at the 5-to-1 ratio (roughly) that exists now. Obviously FunCom is concerned with players racing to 200 and quitting, but I feel they are in far greater danger of players quitting out of frustration long before they get to 200.

I hope that we can keep this thread constructive. As such, I ask that you leave your personal issues at the door. I�m sure some of you won�t and the usual suspects will be in here yelling at each other within in a page or two. Just remember when you read their comments; they�re so frustrated at each other because it�s almost the only enjoyment they have left in the game. TC and Storm LIKE to scream at each other, kill steal, block spawns, and gank each other whenever possible. Why? Because there�s nothing else for them to do. All of which, I feel, just proves my point.

I dont think AO failed
If you look at the current state of internet baqsed games, not just MMORPGs, you will see that not a single one has been everything that it has claimed to be. Each company uses ideas from previous games and incorporates them into a game that they hope will draw a strong customer base and provide many people with entertainment. This is normally accomplished in as fast a time as possible to make it under budget or they will infringe on the profit from those who work above them. That is just a matter of business.

No matter what you play, you will here people complain about something. EverQuest, people complain about customer service and class balencing. Dark Age of Camelot, complains about realm balencing and PvP issues. Asheron's Call, the lack of a true multi-player game and graphics. Most first person shooters, hacks and childish behaviors. Anarchy Online, look at these boards. All companies have problems and will continue to do so.

Now if we take a look at the positives, and leave the negatives behind, we will see that in reality, none of these companies failed. Because of the chances each company takes, we get improved graphics with each game or expansion. We get the ability to do just a little bit more. We get a larger game world with the ability to have more players online at a time.

It is not a matter of failure unless that is how you wish to view it. Me personally, as a gamer, I would like to say "Grats!" to the companies like Verant, Mythic, OWO, Cornered Rat, and yes, FunCom. How many of us can accomplish what they have? How many of us can say we effected the everyday lives of so many? All of this with a game. A game with issues, but a game that will play a role in the creation of each and every MMORPG in the future.

The question I have is "Why do people keep paying the monthly fee if they are not pleased with way it performs?"

Just my 2 credits and not directed toward any one post or person.

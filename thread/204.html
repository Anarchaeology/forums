Dungeon feedback, etc.
Since the old Test Server board is inactive, I'm posting the follow-up to "Of dungeon design, communication and other rants..." here. Basically, we've had the meeting I mentioned before and here are the actions decided upon:

- Reduce HPs of SoM's boss-types by 10-20%.
- Mind Shard is getting an extra punch.
- Make dungeon NODROPs sellable in shops. There's an issue with not having free data-flags that we'll need to get around first.
- Fix "deadly gray mobs" syndrome. Implementing a workaround of the con-code to better represent con-colors of especially empowered monsters.
- Suggest to coders that inhabitants' character levels should be considered when choosing what instance of the dungeon to put a new character in. There are other stats that take presedence (keeping team, keeping intended # of players), but hopefully this will be another modifier once the code is implemented. No promises on this one, the programmers have yet to convene on the issue.

Some stuff we decided NOT to do:
- Remove re-spawning of dead mobs. If there is no re-spawn a group would basically have to clear the whole dungeon for every specific type of loot they're looking for (e.g. Neleb's robe). The game designers don't want that. We considered slowing the spawn down to make a compromise, but we figure this is a case of either you go with a re-spawn system or you don't. Slow re-spawn would just weaken the model we've chosen.
- Proclaim different level-range for dungeon. For its high quotient of bosses and boss-loot, we're keeping SoM a dungeon that is intended to be best played when teams of the target loot-level work together. Future dungeons will be more geared towards 6-man teams though.
- Change loot-levels. See above point.

Can't give you exact ETAs on these things, but most is in the pipeline, and what isn't is being considered. I know that's not everything you asked for, but proposed medallions tend to have a backside.

Also gotta say I'm sorry to see that so many bugs snook into 13.0. The coders have basically changed the routines around how code is committed to patches, and 13.0 was the big turnaround job that was neccessary to be better prepared for the future. A lot of un-checked code had to be merged for them to change formats. The different coders' work will from now on be integrated in a much safer way, which should keep bugs from ressurrecting like they have.

As to why we let reported bugs slip into the patch, I can only assume there were other parts of the patch whose deadlines were overheating (locations for upcoming events that tie in with the story-line, for one). It doesn't mean we've ignored the 13.0 bug reports, they're still there, and we're acting on them one by one.

Thanks for playing,
Sverre

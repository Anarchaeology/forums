2 Posts in one: Why Fixers suck and how to fix them
Disclaimer: This starts as a rant but actually ends with some useful suggestions. If you want to avoid JAFR (Just Another Fixers Rantings) go directly to the End of Rant label.

FIXERS SUCK!!

I have to say that I am fast reaching this conclusion. I have tried hard to see the glass as half full. If you read my posts for the past month or so youll see that I have tried to be upbeat about the Fixer, stating that maybe We are just not playing them right.

Now I realize that we suck. Oh, did I already say that? Im so shell-shocked that I forgot that I had already mentioned that the Fixers suck when I said that the Fixers suck.

Sorry, my brain warped between reality and the grid for a while.

No matter what you say, this game comes down to either damage or healing. Either you are increasing your teams damage output or are helping your team live longer through heals or debuffing opponents. Fixers dont do any of that well in comparison to other players and no amount of speed, lock picking, disarming traps ability is going to make up for it, even if they were useful.

Those extra abilities will never be required, because that would mean that you would have to have a Fixer in your group. So while it might make your groups life a little easier to have a Fixer on some occasions, a group will almost always be better off if they fill the Fixers spot with someone who can fulfill one of the core roles.

Even if, and that is a big if, the Fixer becomes a desired member in a team there is another problem. Our abilities dont stack well with other Fixers. I mean how many Fixers does it take to pick a lock. I know that other professions will have problems with overlap too, but because most are also strong in either heals or damage, which do stack well, having more than one is still beneficial.

The lack of damage we have is the root of our problems. We have a ton of issues with our nano skills too, but Ill accept for now Funcoms statement that Fixers were not suppose to be good at nanos. Now our lack of damage was to be made up by the fact that we are fast. In fact this can directly be seen in our run buffs that increase speed but reduce damage. Apparently Funcom is afraid of kiting, and didnt want a character that was both fast and dealt decent damage. The problem is that our speed does not make up for the poor damage.

First, indoors speed is not really an issue during combat. Sure it will help you escape if things go bad, but it is not going to really help you beat a mob. Btw, our low damage is pretty much going to ensure that we will need that speed for a fast getaway. I can start to hear the low rumble of players saying that Fixers werent meant to stand toe to toe with mobs. Ok Ill give you that, but that brings me to my next point.

Second, our fights last too long. The only way for a Fixer to stand a chance is to draw out a fight by using our speed to go in and out of your opponents range, healing up in the mean time. I am sure this is what Funcom had in mind for the Fixer character. However, even though we might be able to take out a higher-level mob, it is not worth the time or effort required to do so. You would be much better off killing more low-level mobs. I know, I have taken on orange mobs and just gave up, not because I was dieing, but for the cost of the first-aid kits I was going through.

Third, running away from a fight really doesnt help your team. In a way the Fixer is spoiled. My chance of dieing in a Fight is probably the lowest of any profession. If things go wrong, I can usually outrun any danger, but how does that help my team. I use to think that pulling mobs was our niche, which it still is, but I have seen many other professions do it just about as well. Granted a fixer would probably be better, but not good enough to justify the teams loss in firepower. (I thank fighting the Borg in Mort for pointing out this little truism)

I used to say that Fixers were a good class, but only needed help with our identity. Now I realize that even if we get some cool Fixer things to do, we are going to still have problems. We need to be useful to a group that is hunting and no ammount of Black Market shopping is going to help with that. (Well maybe if you bribe them to let you join)

I dont want to play a soldier, but as the gameplay stands now, the only thing that is going to help the Fixer out is to increase our damage. (Increasing our HoTs would also work, but I don't see a Fixer as a healer.) I have seen just about ever other class out damage me. That even includes the Bureaucrat with a charmed pet. Im not sure about Adventurers but I hear they arent so bad if they are not trying to dual wield pistols.


*End of rant*
-----------------------


My solution is to allow the Fixer to set traps. These spray the damage dealing nanos used in our damage buffs and are area of effect DoTs. The traps couldnt be thrown, but would have to be set up on the ground. The nano bots are very fragile, so in order to protect them, when the trap is first set they are held in a dormant state. In order for the trap to detonate, the Fixer must be in range and arm it. Thereafter, for a brief period of time, any hostile character or mob will set it off if they get with in 10m.

It should be difficult to use while in a fight. If the Fixer gets attacked while setting a trap, it will go off damaging to Fixer. You can have different types of traps, some which act over a large area but have a smaller DoT, while others would affect a smaller area but do much more damage. You could even let the Fixer adjust the trap to change the area of effect with a trade skill. Of course the higher QL traps would do more overall damage. Now a Fixer puller is a real threat.

First the fixer lays a trap over his escape route. Then he goes in to snare-pull the mob so it is moving nice and slow. Finally he arms the trap and gets the mob to run over it, where it go boooom. All mobs take damage while in the area of effect, which will be a nice chunk of change because it is snared.

To prevent the Fixer from covering the ground with traps, put a long recharge timer on their ability to set a trap based on their Disarm Trap skill and have a maximum number of traps that any one Fixer can have set at a time.

This solution allows the Fixer to do more damage, while still relying on our traditional strengths.

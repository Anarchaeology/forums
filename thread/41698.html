Well first of all, thank you for mentioning the South Rubi-Ka Society in you article. It's always nice to get good press.

Personally I don't feel as if I am "going out of my way to pick up where Funcom left off" let alone trying to "fill in a void". Sure I do spend a lot of time working on events and adventures, but I only work on events which amuse me at some level. It's really no more than what I would do for a pen & paper roleplaying campaign.

I don't have strong feelings about official events either way. They are colourful enough, but in most instances I believe the best roleplaying is done on a small, personal scale, and that is something Funcom cannot do for us.

But hey, maybe that is just me.

My two credits
It maybe that I have low expectations about what a company can do to make my on-line gaming experience fun. For example, if Funcom would fix the lag in Baboons I would be thrilled because there would be another viable hotspot to visit and interact with a lot of players. It seems to me if you like to role-play and develop your character you dont need too much from Funcom if you have an imaginative group.

However, it would be nice if Funcom could deliver on its storyline promise, since it would add to the color of the Rubi-ka. There would be new events to discuss and spin adventures around. An occasional big event like the battle you described would certainly be nice.

The biggest disservice that Funcom has done, in my opinion, is to stifle the subplots that player groups developed to bring Rubi-ka to life. A little encouragement and a little support would have produced a very big payoff. In that regard, I think the players were attempting to provide life to the political and social landscape of Rubi-ka. Why Funcom did not make a small investment in these creative and clever endeavors is a mystery to me.

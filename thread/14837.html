Unique Items, Camping, Tradeskills, Overequiping and the Rest
This is a post about my observations as well as some new ideas.

UNIQUE ITEMS - CAMPING

First, the good -- Anarchy Online is not like EQ with regard to items. A character is not a "gimp" simply because he didn't camp a rare NPC for 6 hours to get the "only good sword" for his character. Unlike EQ, Anarchy Online permits characters to obtain "off the shelf" items that will permit their character to be of sufficient "strength". QL 200 items are abundant in missions. QL 200 armor is available without a "camp fest" (remember asking questions in EQ like - how long was it since "ultra rare" npc spawned last???).

Second, the bad -- Anarchy Online lacks distinguishing items. Many characters look alike. Many characters wear exactly the same items - have the same exact look. Obviously, I understand that there are certain limits to the number of graphics that the game engine can hold. But, by and large, there are certain weapons and armor that are the best - and that everyone tries or does wear.

Funcom has begun to implement Static Quests for unique items. Good, however, keep in mind that a BALANCE must still exist between "off the shelf" items and those "quest" items. We don't need an EQ camp fest.

That having been said -- we need more unique items and looks. The mass relocation robot is a start (permits modification to weapons) but, again, the implementation is not good. Bringing me to my next point.

Dungeons must have "loot" to encourage gamers to travel there for the time invested. Currently, it makes more sense to simply grab a couple of missions with friends and knock them off then put in the huge time commitments that a dungeon poses. What I argue is NOT that dungeons should be the focus of camping -- what I argue is that dungeons should have the proper rewards for the level of time it takes to complete them. Currrently, those rewards are lacking.

TRADESKILLS

Moving on from my last points -- tradeskills must be improved. The current implementation is too complex. Simplify the process. For example, clusters and implants are well implemented. Sure, shopping for clusters is boring (and a better system can be designed), but the process of creating implants is not overly complex. Other trade skills, however, are. As such, most people see no reason to bother with them.

Design trade skills to have a real purpose. Whether it is simply modifying a nova flow so that the "color" of the laser is different -- or that your Yamaha looks different -- implement something the players will use.

I personally like the idea of simplifying the weapon trade skills. Make it like clusters and implants. Add a certain item to a gun that has a slot -- bingo, the right skill combines it and you get a merged product. DIABLO II perhaps possesses the best weapon modification process that currently exists. There are slots on some weapons. Add the appropriate gem, walla, you have a unique item. Example: Nova flow with three empty slots. You decide to attach a "chemical processor" to make the beam shoot chemical damage. You decide to attach a larger clip. And, for your last addition, you add a "crit enhancer" that slows the weapon but adds to the critical damage. Your buddy may choose an entirely different route -- but -- again -- the trade skills are being used and creating unique items.

OVEREQUIPING

I know I will receive some flames about this - but - seriously, what is the big deal?

Overequiping is part of the game now, whether anyone likes it or not. There is an art to it as well. Simple guys, leave it in game. Balance the PVE elements (as Funcom has done) to account for overequiping. As for PVP, well, he who is stronger prevails. If you get the right buffs and have a great gun -- well, you should win the fight.

HOWEVER, note the following. The main arguments against overequiping stem from ridiculous overequipment by mostly traders; casters inability to overequip nanos; and, the uselessness of debuffs if overequiping continues. Let me address a couple of these briefly.

Trader overequiping -- Shotguns are out of hand. Everyone knows that. Why? The crits. Fix crits. Dont change the game simply because crits are broken. Fix the crits and the way they are calculated.

Caster's problem. Once uploaded, let a caster be permitted to cast a nano. Sounds reasonable. Just like the soldier that equips the high level gun, the caster can equip and use the high level nano.

Debuffs and Buffs. Of course, Funcom argues that debuffs then would be useless. Buffs would be useless. I agree, perhaps the CURRENT buffs and debuffs - at least some of them -- would be useless. SO -- fix them. Perhaps the focus should be on buffs that effect attack, attack speed, run speed, etc. and not a complete shut down of a character's abilities. Perhaps the trader's focus in battle should be the ability to debuff a character so his Initiatives are slower -- his attack rating slower, etc. Obviously, if the nano lands. Perhaps debuffs should be focused on reducing nano damage, gun fire damage, etc. However, a complete shutdown? Makes no sense. Change it. How hard is it?

Instead, Funcom wants to implement their overequipment change. They will make the process even more complex. If your skill is X% then the item works X% . . . etc. Why get so complicated and have the program crunching complex formulas when the whole process can remain simple, efficient and easy to use?

I know I have thrown a lot of ideas in here generally. If Funcom shows any interest in these ideas -- I will expand on them -- but I hope they do.

In any event -- thats my ten cents.

Unique looks and tradeskill improvements could solve each other's problems.

There are a ton of graphics for social clothes that go unused due the fact that you'd have to remove your over-equipped armor to put them on.

If they developed a system of armor crafting that allowed you to add protection to the currently "social only" clothes you could kill the problems of look-alikes and useless tradeskills with one stone. Instead of everyone using the graphics for a few sets of armor, you'd have a much wider variety of looks to choose from.

Players are loathe to seek out pc merchants due to the added hassle of time spent searching and haggling over price. Especially if they can get functional equipment from mobs, npc merchants and missions. But if they have the ability to look exactly like they want, and retain functionality, they'll take the time to find another player. And with a product finally in demand, more people will take up tradeskills.

The problem with the static dungeon system as currently set up (long camping, crappy reward) is easy to fix. As is, the system takes the worst of EQ without the benefit.

The solution is to go with what Turbine figured out for AC. short spawns in combination with unique/no drop items that are NOT necessarily better than loot items (just useful at a certain level). You can camp the dungeon all you want for xp, but you can only take 1 unique drop (leaving the rest for whoever else is there). Spawns in AC varied from 5 to 15 min. A whole team could get their desired items in an hour or 2 after fighting their way into the dungeon.

On Outdoor Hunting and Creature Mobs: What's Wrong & What Can Be Done
Well, seeing as the problem I outlined here has been dramatically exacerbated in Shadowlands, I wanted to bump this back up, first so people realize it's ALWAYS been like this, and second, so we can make some more suggestions.

We've been discussing at length in several different threads the difficulty of certain creatures, and how much more difficult their appearance in missions has made missions become. The two most often cited in these threads have been Mantises and Mechdogs, but to focus in on these creatures by themselves has not directly addressed the issues facing creature mobs.

First, I will define what I mean by creature mobs. A creature mob is defined here as any mob which is not a human, has no profession, is typically found only in the wild, and is not a unique. Using my sites system of classification (http://www.faunlore.org), this means everything found under the Terraformers, the Xenomorphs, and the Natives is a creature mob, while all of the Humanoids except the Humans is as well. The robots are counted as creature mobs for the purposes of this discusssion.

Now, turning to the history of creature mobs and Anarchy Online. When you look at AO's history, there have been four major gameplay issues not related to profession balancing that have been discussed ad nauseum. These are:

1.) Pet Pathing
2.) Mission Structure and Design
3.) Lack of high-level content
3.) The infeasibility of hunting

Of these, two have been addressed, and resoundingly well, I might add. The third is more difficult, but seems to get attention. The fourth, however, has been largely unaddressed. As we all probably have experienced, creature mobs are frequently much more powerful and dangerous than their equally leveled human mob counterparts. In terms of sheer strength, using a combination of factors such as HP, damage type, attack speeds, range, and so on, creatures outdistance humans in almost every category. Now, with the advent of the Shadowlands, virtually every problem that ever existed with creature mobs, from insane HP and NP, to very powerful attacks, to dreadful rewards and spawn times, has been increased tenfold.

As an example, I can give an Eremite.

Now, in terms of creature mobs, Eremites are slow. They have two melee attacks at 3.2s each. However, they are also imbued with an HP bar almost as large (if not larger) than a QL 160 human mob, they have higher ACs (as best as I can calculate), and have two attacks where most human mobs only have one. Clearly, then, an Eremite (which is typically level 130-140) should really be treated as a QL 160-170 mob, not as a 130. In other words, given their difficulty, they code yellow to a player at level 130, but good luck taking one down by yourself.

But let's say you're really good, and you take down that big, nasty Forest Eremite all by yourself in the middle of Central Artery Valley. Great work! But, what is your reward for taking down a creature whose strength is equivalent to that of a QL 170 human? Monster parts (if you're lucky) and XP worthy of a QL 130 mob. In other words, pound for pound, the QL 130 Eremite is harder and more dangerous than the QL 130 Scrap Pillager, but the XP is the same, and the Scrap Pillager has better odds for "useful" loot like armor or weapons. Essentially, then, the risk/reward ratios for creature mobs tilts heavily in favor of creatures being too risky for their reward... and so spelled the death of outdoor hunting and creature missions.

This was the state of affairs for a long time. Eventually though, two things changed that appeared to spell a renaissance for outdoor hunting and creature missions. The biggest of these was the Dynacamps, which essentially guaranteed creature spawns of a certain QL and the spawning of a boss that would have nice loot. Initially, Dynacamps were an interesting addition, but it was marred by three critical flaws from the start. The first is the problem with having lots of difficult creatures spawning in a camp nearly constantly, and this is a problem because most of the mobs are "regular" creatures, which means their risk/reward is horrible... the minions are a waste of time, and very risky to begin with. The second problem is the bosses themselves, and they are a problem because they are imbued with Boss Modifiers. For those not familiar with them, Boss Modifiers are items that are equipped to team mission and Dynacamp bosses to imbue them with special strengths (see Boss Modifiers section on my site for more details on the various kinds). So, now, you've created a very strong creature mob WITH additional boosts to its damage, ACs, health, and so on. In other words, Dynacamp bosses were significantly more dangerous than any other creature around them. The third problem is that the loot and XP off of these bosses, while an improvement over the regular "monster parts" variety, is still inadequate to the difficulty of the creature. In fact, in the case of Dynacamp bosses, the risk/reward ratio has gone up (meaning more risk, less reward). In the long run, Dynacamps have not addressed the issues with creature mobs.

The second major change was described by some as changing things for the better, when in the case of most creatures it was a disaster. The change I am referring to is the 10% increase in creature mob XP. Now, on the face this should be a good thing, revising the risk/reward on creature mobs downward (same risk/more reward). However, this is not what occurred, because at the same time as this was introduced, the change where mob melee attacks now did double damage (2 hits for the price of one attack) also appeared. This change was not overly noticed in human mobs, because the two melee professions for mobs are Enforcer and MA... and these were already a pain to fight anyway. However, with very few exceptions (Sylvabanes, Spiders, Omi, Riders, and Nanofreaks come quickly to mind), nearly every creature mob has at least one melee damage attack, and in some cases, all the damage they do is melee damage. I'd hazard a guess and say 80% of all creature mobs are all melee damage. This means that, for those mobs, they received a 100% increase in damage for a 10% increase in reward. Thus, on it's face, they actually became significantly harder, and the reward change did not keep pace with this... and thus the risk/reward was driven even even higher.

In the end, there was a third change to AO that only served to show what is possible for outdoor hunting if only there was attention paid to it. I'm talking about the Uniques (the Guardians, I call them) that were added to outdoor camps. While I dislike the fact that they are camped to hell, they are an example of what can be done when mobs are given rewards in keeping with their risk. Say what you like about Ian Warr and Co., how obscenely difficult they are to spawn and kill, even if you're in the uber-guilds that control them, and how annoying it is that they are mega-camped, the rewards of taking them on are clearly worth it. That's why people camp them, that's why they risk a lot to go out there and kill them. Regular creature mobs clearly do not deserve the rewards accorded to Ian Warr and the like... but they definitely deserve to have worth in fighting them.

Finally, the Shadowlands opened up, and essentially nerfed us all to hell, as the problems with mob damage and reward have been blown WAY out of proportion. In addition, the fleeing mob effect and wild camp aggroing has made SL hunting, which is virtually required for any progress, a virtual impossibility once mobs come to within 60% of your level.

What am I proposing as a solution to this? Well, first of all, leave RK mobs alone and don't remove them from missions or nerf them in any way. I feel that the problem is not necessarily with the strength of mobs, but it is with how that strength is portrayed to the player (improper level coding), and with the corresponding reward for dealing with such strength. The solutions to those problems could be varied, but I propose either that creatures gain a level boost, and the corresponding reward boost, that is more in keeping with their strength, or we accept their strength and they keep their level, but they gain significant reward boosts. It isn't too much to ask that a QL 130 creature mob that is as hard as a QL 170 human mob give the same XP as the QL 170 mob, and it isn't outside the realm of reason to find armor or some other special loot on a creature, especially ones that aggro on sight (it is conceivable that they've killed other settlers, no?). But, people must understand that a yellow creature mob is just as likely to kill you as a red human mob, and must be given the proper reward for accepting that risk. Do that, and I think you'll see a renaissance in outdoor hunting and fewer people screaming about how horrible it is to face down a Sand Mantis in a mission.

As for SL mobs, the first and most critical change would be a radical reduction in their attack strength, and a little more diversity in their attacks. From what I can tell in the DB, every mob, regardless of type, is granted the same blanket attack structure, rendering them devoid of ANY specialties whatsoever, but also making them all the same impossibly hard mobs with different skins. The way RK mobs were done was each was given a carefully thoughtout damage type and weapon, sometimes 2 or even 5, with carefully planned specials.

SL mobs are all over the map, doing every damage type at a crazy rate of damage. The first step is to give each of the existing SL mob weapons an immediate 50% drop in damage output, preferably by nerfing their weapons' damage. Then, begin phasing in appropriate damage types, immunities, and special attacks, the way many RK mobs were approached. Finally, the XP awarded and other rewards must be improved. I will not discuss SK here, as that has too much more to do with faction.

Finally, look at the way mobs run away in SL. I understand that they run away and occasionally summon help... but area aggroing of 30 mobs within a 50m radius, as has happened several times, is lethal. Maybe in RK I could handle up to 10 grey level 60 mobs, but in SL, this effect combined with the obscene damage results in near insta-death when aggroed mobs get much beyond 5 or 6.

I have written about this over and over, and been ignored. Many of us have the same sentiments. To the players, I implore you to understand: creature mobs are more powerful than human mobs in nearly every instance. That now grey SL Eremite may very well hand you a one way ticket to the Reclaim or Garden. If any of you wish there were more solutions than the same ol' human mob missions and the same ol' campin, make yourselves heard. We know that Funcom is very capable of coming through on the big issues, as they've shown in the past. While each of you may not agree with every word I have said, I'm certain we all agree that the situation regarding creature mobs is one of the most serious flaws in a game marked with great successes. To Funcom, this is a large and fundamental change people have been asking for since the beginning. Now, it's even worse than it ever was, and it's beginning to affect people's ability to play at all, which I'm sure inevitably costs customers.

I must say Krysta, excellent post. This has been a very sought-after issue, as making outdoor hunting viable would definately make Rubi-Ka a more enjoyable place to inhabit. My summary of the main issues are as follows:

a) In agreeance with Kyrsta's post the main problem is that on outdoor mobs (read: most mobs level 60+) not enough experience is given considering their actual difficulty.

b) Mob are too scattered both in number and in level across terrain. Scumbag put it best: "in these zones of a whole heap of Greys and Reds all herding right on top of eachother."

c) Spawn rate is too low considering mob tendency to roam. When hunting a particular type or level-range of mob, often hunting dies off after the first half-hour or so. Reason? Mobs are coming too sparsely...and often are scattered in random directions, making hunting an overly time-consuming task due to the amount of tracking that needs to be done. Not that I wouldn't enjoy tracking, so long as the xp reward were sufficient for the amount of effort expended.

d) None of the items which drop from outdoor mobs are compensation for even the cash reward of a single mission. However, I don't think the solution to this problem lies in adding more dyna-camps. (Dyna-camps encourage stagnant behavior similar to the mission-mentality. Camping a spawn of a single mob type isn't extremely fun if there is rote and routine. In the case of Lush Fields, because there are so many different types of social mutants, it actually makes pulling them a strategy and makes dealing with adds exciting.) I think the solution to the loot problem lies in making more outdoor-only items which can be a) sold for good amounts of money to an NPC requesting such items, something along the lines of the Tirbo Ratcatcher quest or, b) make outdoor-only tradeskill items which could potentially be high in demand...such as components for a new armor type. Imagine if every carb piece required one or two items dropped from mobs...as soon as a market formed, (similar to the notum market) people would be hunting like mad! These two loot solutions give adventurers and other potential hunting professions incentive to hunt because it could sustain some of their monetary issues if blitzing missions isn't their forte.

As an old-time player (since release), I can definately empathize with Halyera's positive nostalgic memory of outdoor hunting. I recall a time at release when hordes of optimistic Omni-Tek employees flooded O Forest and Greater O Forest with hunting parties. Some of the best times I've had in this game were while hunting in Rubi-Ka's great outdoors and I find it saddening that some people haven't had the chance to experience the rush of dealing with multiple social adds

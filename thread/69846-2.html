quote:
Originally posted by Nevver
It should be some sort of "painful" execution...
Public humiliation is always nice and painful . You could also dress the executioners up in cloaks/hoods, etc., or just keep them in full OP gear.

Thinking from an IC point-of-view, reclaim is apparently quite painful (I think...from what I've read and the after-effects of dying), so in a manner of speaking it is an effective punishment.

The only IC and OOC punishment that I can see as being an effective deterent is the imposition and levying of monetary fines. The amounts could vary based on the crime committed, and just for argument's sake, take the murder of Lupoh. If Thelonis were caught, he could be forced to pay an amount equal to 10,000 credits per level of Lupoh, i.e, if Lupoh was level 10, it would be 100,000 credits; level 100, 1,000,000 credits and so on. This system would take into account the fact that higher level players are of more value to society (whether for implant creation, buffing, knowledge and experience, etc.).

Similarly, and to use the example of Templus' kidnapping, if it could be proved that an entire organization orchestrated the commission of a crime, the org could be fined instead of the individuals.

As for non-payment, if the person does not have the money he can be forced to either take a mission, secure a loan or hunt, accompanied by officers of OP who would make sure the criminal didn't run away, until the fine was paid off. Similarly, if it takes, say, 3 days to accumulate the amount necessary to pay the fine, then for each day such person fails to pay he or she can either be publicly executed at the end of each day or accrue interest on the outstanding balance.

Note that, in no way, should a player be forced or compelled to sell his or her gear. How to pay the fine should be left entirely at the discretion of the criminal.

Also, I don't think imprisonment is a viable, universally-applicable punishment. Not everyone likes having alts (I have one and only one character, and he's enough as it is), and it also becomes a matter of game time. People have different schedules and routines, and a system that restricts that will inevitably break down. I can understand being held after arrest for questioning, etc., but held for anything longer than a couple of hours may be asking too much from people.

If we are going to try and establish "offenses" of criminal law, we'll need to develop something that everyone can agree is "the law".

Take a look at the Rubi-Ka Civil Code http://public.aci.on.ca/~robc/blp/rkcc.htm (and no this isn't a shameful advertisement - that's int he sig), specifically at Articles 5 and 6. I put those in the Code for discussion purposes, and they represent the types of offences that wouldn't contrave FC's policies (which is a big thing that has to be kept in mind), but that would be somewhat enforceable.

RP Suggestions
Here are some suggestions:

- develop your "personality". This includes what kind of person you character is. Roleplaying the 'job' part of a cop or robber type is only part of it.

- Are you a nice guy or burned out?
- Does your character have a sense of humor.
- Is your character hot headed or patient?

Yes obviously your RL personality will come through your characters some, but thats what RP is about, playing a character you are not. More details make your character more interesting. Consistency helps.

The main thing I wanted to suggest,

Use /tell for mainly OOC. Of course when you're in a /tell conversation its cool to say "checks his/her comm" or something
but perhaps Comm links can be hacked into.

Using Vicinity for the majority of RP also has an added benefit, it looks more interesting to non-PR people and attract more people. Some of my favorite times are when we're playing in a populated area and people are going WTF? It's all improve theater afterall.

GOOD guys Vs. BAD guys.

Like in real life, there are shades of gray. Omni-Pol has a history of corruption, and some Clans have the very best intentions, and vice versa. Where does your character fall in this?

Suppression Gas
If there was a way to "declare war" on a guild. I.E. all members of each guild are constantly in PVP with eachother. This would allow a coalition of RP groups to be in PVP to act out any RP they want without suppression gas interfering.

For example if Omni org 1, Omni org 2 and Clan org 1 all were 'at war' with eachother. Not in a RP sense, but as far as game mechanics, that would allow any combiation of RP situations not necessarily involving pvp to happen in 100% zones WITHOUT outside interferance.

If one of any of the players started killing randomly, they could be kicked from their org and no longer in PvP with the other orgs. Guild leaders would have to be active to keep the other coalition guilds trust and stay 'at war' with those guilds.

It would make entire orgs 'at war' with each other, effectively in a 0% to eachother throughout the entire game.

How this would effect RP:
Lets say 5 omni-pol take a criminal into custody. They may never fire, and the criminal goes along with them, but the threat would be "real".

Another example, an Omni-Pol character happens upon 5 criminals in the middle of a crime. They could warn him, get lost or we're gonna blaze you up cop. The omni-pol character would have to leave or face a "real" threat.

In my examples these players have a understanding, and respect eachother though they are on "opposite sides".

This would involve alot of trust. There would be a understanding between members not to simply use it to grief eachother or run in shooting unless agreed upon. It would open up the way for alot more flexability in what can be RP'ed.

The game code for this is already going into the game with Notum Wars. It would be a great option for the RP community to be able to turn it on and off without having to attack a tower. (if that doesnt make sense, read about the towers)

Greetings.

Forgive the following blasphemous reference to 'that other game', please. Everquest has a /duel command. One-On-One battle. Consensual and without interference from others.

As someone once said: 'Dueling is for roleplayers, PvP is for gamers'. One benefit of that /duel command--it was quite effective for resolving 'issues' between two people (or two people representing a faction/guild/goal/belief/etc).

In a Role Playing sense, it was integrated very well into scenarios--there was a bit more.... 'honor' and continuity involved in that process, than going to a PvP area and being subject to the whims of anyone that happened to be there at the time. The /duel command could be used almost anywhere, facilitating a better 'flow' to a roleplaying scenario at almost any location. However, guards did not take kindly to people dueling around them, so the location had to be choosen wisely. Dueling in Everquest was surprisingly undisruptive, all things considered. And, it was actually fairly entertaining to observe, if one chose to do so.

With Notum Wars just around the corner, and its heavy PvP focus, perhaps there can be a /duel command or the like, made available for folks...something which can be an appropriate addition to roleplaying (or just plain old dispute settling), within the framework of the current/upcoming Anarchy Online storyline.

Be well.

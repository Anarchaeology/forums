The Council of Ares was a fine experiment in seeing whether the general playerbase would accept this kind of player-empowerment. It was soundly and resolutely drubbed by the playerbase. When you have about ten thousand players who have Clan characters, the majority don't want to have a couple dozen players in some other guild making decisions for them. Unless they are your guild president or advisors most are unwilling to give up control over their destiny to some other guy who they feel has no more right than they to special treatment.

They are however, more than willing to deal with the consequences of what Radiman, Silverstone, and all the Clan leaders lead them into. This is why all the backlash during the 'dead period' when nothing happened at all with Ross, Radiman, or any of the players. During this period something was better than nothing. The fact that any of my Omni characters can stroll through Tir generally (damn, you Fizzban) unscathed is as remarkable as when the Amnesty was in effect.

It's only fair to the playerbase as a whole. When Radiman or Ross do something, it affects every single player in the game. There are no fingers to point at other paying players. Nobody wants to think that some other player sitting at a desk in his basement is getting preferential treatment vs. his 13 bucks a month.

What CAN be asked for is more direction from FunCom. If you want to roleplay your guild as a detachment of Omni-Tek's armed forces, you have the right as a paying customer to ask FunCom for some direction. 'Where do you want us to patrol?' , 'Is there a monster population you need thinned'. , 'Is there a target you need found and eliminated?'. We see people who feel they can't be 'heroes' if they can't be in control. The game seems 'useless' if they aren't in charge of the rules to a degree. They can touch their own arms and know they exist. Any game with a tiered level progression and significant differences in weapons effectiveness gives rise to a very unrealistic situation: People (characters) who can absorb repeated attacks that would quickly and surely kill any real person.

Real people don't have levels, or thousands or even hundreds of hitpoints. Real weapons don't have quality levels that make them more effective. In real life, applying a weapon to a human being will result in a crippled or dead human being, not a perfectly functional human being minus some hitpoints. It doesn't matter if it's a "one-hundred-year-old dirty knife" or a "shiny brand new ginsu knife" - if you stab someone with it, they're going to be seriously injured.

It would stand to reason that if we were to treat the game as a real world, any weapon regardless of game statistics (QL, actual effectiveness, etc) would have to be considered dangerous, and injuries would have to be treated as more than mere loss of hitpoints.

The reason people will laugh off a lower level character is because their own character is much more robust. They are survivable, while the lower level character is not. In real life you don't see that because in real life, effectively, everyone is "level 2."

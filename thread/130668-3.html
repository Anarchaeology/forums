Anyone who has had to study military strategy is familiar with the theories of F.W. Lanchester, a British mathmatician and military theorist who formulated a landmark mathematical "N-Square" law of governing attrition.

To explain it briefly, Lanchester differentiated melee combat versus ranged combat.

Numerical superiority in Melee gives a power advantage on a simple linear scale,

Basically, if Force A has 2000 soldiers, it is twice as powerful as Force B which has 1000 soldiers.

This is because no melee fighter is capable of attacking anyone but the enemy soldier directly in front of him.

But this linear power scale does not hold true for ranged combat. In ranged combat every soldier is theoretically capable of attacking *every* enemy soldier. This means, in mathematical theory, casualties climb on an exponential curve, not a linear one.

This means that Force A of 2000 ranged soldiers is four times more powerful that Force B of 1000 ranged soldiers of equal skill.

This point can be illustrated by imagining hypothetical exchanges between the two Forces.

Force A begins with a 2 to 1 advantage over Force B.

1) Force A fires 2 shots and kills 2 men in Force B

2) Force B fires 1 shot and kills 1 man in Force A

Force A now enjoys a 2.003 to 1 advantage over Force B.

As the exchanges continue, Force A's proportional superiority to Force B continues to increase until Force B is completely destroyed. Solving the equation, Force A is found to have lost only 268 men.

The principles of Lanchester's N-Square law is supported by the outcomes of real battles in history.

4 days into the 7 days trial...will I register? No! Because...
Greetings to the AO community.

I played the game as an adventurer until I reached the level 5. Then I balanced the pro & cons, and decided I wouldn't pay to continue playing. Don't get me wrong: I like some aspects of the game, but too much topics don't answer my expectations.

What I like:
- graphics, beautiful landscapes
- huge world
- decent lag
- helpful community
- only crashed once

What I dislike:
- the real goal is just levelling...
- looks like an adaptation of a fantasy RPG (a lot of characters are running with swords, nano=wizardry...)
- the concept is utterly un-original. It reminds me the AD&D mechanics. Level is the key...level determines the weapons you can use, the spells..uh...nano programs you can 'cast', the armor, the vehicles... Gee, nothing much changed since the eighties....
- fights are really porked: tactics don't matter. i.e. an enemy bearing a sniper rifle will be unhindered when you come close to him to attack with a melee weapon. Other example, I could hit repeatedly with a sword an enemy standing 15m away...Btw, why the heck has my character to wait a considerable amount of time between 2 bursts??? (As a good example of realistic gunfights, I'll mention Operation FlashPoint).

When will an online RPG simulate the need to sleep, the need to eat, the fear to die, the influence of appropriate tactics in a fight...?

I know the game is permanently evolving, and I won't discard it permanently. I'll continue to check this board regularly to see if the game mechanics evolve in another direction.

Thanks for all the help (and credits) a lot of players gave me during this trial. Although I don't like the game, the community is top-class!

- the real goal is just levelling...

If all you want to do is level, AO will suit your gameplay style.
If you want to explore, run a dungeon, hunt for a rare item for a guildmate or just go shopping.... AO will suit your gameplay style as well.

- looks like an adaptation of a fantasy RPG (a lot of characters are running with swords, nano=wizardry...)

Brilliant.... It's advertised as a Sci-Fi fantasy RPG.
It succeeds as a Sci-Fi fantasy RPG.

- the concept is utterly un-original. It reminds me the AD&D mechanics. Level is the key...level determines the weapons you can use, the spells..uh...nano programs you can 'cast', the armor, the vehicles... Gee, nothing much changed since the eighties....

It's a skill based system.
Skill, not level, determines what items you can use.
Skills can be raised by other means than levels.
The system is actually fairly innovative.

- fights are really porked: tactics don't matter

Of course they don't matter at level 5.
Fights at that level last about 20 seconds.

Go play EQ, DAoC, AC or any other MMOG. Get a couple of levels and let us know the tactics that are required at newbie levels.

.e. an enemy bearing a sniper rifle will be unhindered when you come close to him to attack with a melee weapon. Other example, I could hit repeatedly with a sword an enemy standing 15m away...Btw, why the heck has my character to wait a considerable amount of time between 2 bursts??? (As a good example of realistic gunfights, I'll mention Operation FlashPoint).

It's called game balance.
Melee weapons have a fairly generous range and weapons don't have an efficiency rating that varies with distance to the target.

You have to wait a length of time between special attacks because it would be overpowering if you could burst 10-15 times in one minute.

Every MMOG has to foraske quite a bit of realism in favor of game balance. If you're not comfortable with this, perhaps MMOGs are not for you.
OP is a very good game but it doesn't have the longevity, depth or social aspects of AO.

When will an online RPG simulate the need to sleep, the need to eat, the fear to die, the influence of appropriate tactics in a fight...?

Because people will not pay money to sleep. You can't accelerate time in a persistent game world. Why do you think sleeping in every computer RPG lasts roughly 5-15 seconds? Because people get bored staring at their screen, doing nothing.

Eating is an annoyance. Ask anyone who played EQ. Keeping stocked with food was a hassle.... a small hassle but still a hassle.

The fear to die? You wan't to play a MMOG where death is permanent? Interesting concept but it does not appeal to a large enough market segment to make a viable MMOG.
Penalties for death is a heavily discussed topic in MMOGs. The penalty in EQ was incredibly harsh. You could potentially lose weeks of gameplay XP with a death or two. MMOGs are moving away from harsh death penalties. People don't like them.

Tactics.... well as I said above, you can't understand if you have only gotten to level 5.




I have close to 300 levels between my top 3 characters.
I'm still learning new things about AO.

Just last night, I had more fun than I have had in months.... and I didn't even level once.... or get one decent item....

- I explored Newlands & surroundings
- I played around 10 missions, in Newlands backyard & Newlands City. I bought a key duplicator to accomplish these missions with a team, but I was able only twice to enlist other players. Most low-level character are only interested in hunting Leets and Rollerrats in the junkyard...Btw I bought a lockpick too, so I'm already aware of the 'joy' of lockpicking that one of you was mentioning (for me, it's just point & click until it works...really exciting)
- I hunted around Newlands.
- I explored Tir & Athens a bit.
- I tried to reach Tir by road (from Newlands), but an angry Ancient Gargantula put an unexpected end to my trip (I was around half-way). This is the part of the game I preferred actually.
- I traded a bit
- I hanged out in the Newlands Club (don't remember the name tho, it was near the Bronto burgers...)

Other critics that come to mind:

- At level 5, the other level 2 players aren't interesting to team with because they are too weak, while you won't interest players at level 8. At this early stage, it's really hard to form a team.

- Level 100 characters are powerful as demi-gods...Only interaction you'll have with them is receiving 1,000 creds or a free heal...that's nice, but otherwise they consider you as significant as an ant. Impossible to trade, team or travel with them...

- Why do you have to reach Map Reading at 40 (or something like that) to view the map of Newlands Desert?? Some will say 'game balance', I just view that as a (poor) trick to force people to play longer.

- Someone said it isn't a level-based game, but a skill-based one...right! Your hit points increase indefinitely with each level (AD&D??), while you can allocate IP to skills you've never ever used before. Nice skill-based system. Once again, it was original back in the 80's.

Other remarks:

- I certainly overlooked the fact that you had to save above level 6 in order to keep your exp points. I thought you only had to save before logging off, I didn't know unsaved exp points were lost at every death. So you have a point here.

- sleeping online is boring: of course! But I suppose most players don't stay online 72 hrs in a row. Why not consider the offline time as rest? But if you don't put your character in an adequate situation before logging off (motel room in a city, tent ot camp outside...), the recovery won't be as good and your stats will suffer some decay until next period of sleep.

-eating online is boring, having to carry food, to buy food, to hunt is boring...well, I thought it was a ROLE-PLAYING game...If the goal is just running around blasting up stuff, Quake comes to mind.

- Are all the missions about going into a special 'dungeon', opening doors, killing monsters and grabbing treasures? Or are they getting a bit different at higher level?

-...

Maybe everything I said above isn't valid anymore beyond level 20-30-40-..? I don't know. But I know I won't pay during 6 months for a game hoping it would get better when I reach a certain level.


I understand that a lot of people like the game, and I don't want to be disrespectful to them. I'm fond of a MMO simulation called 'Aces High', and although it isn't perfect (what is?), I spend most of my game time (and money) on it. I guess that some flaws of this sim will turn down some players while I can easily live with. Tastes...

Besides, if a friend wants to try a MMORG, I'll certainly advise him to try AO and make his own judgement.
The game is just not for me right now.

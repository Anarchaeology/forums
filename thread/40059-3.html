Halbert, as Mechanita sort of eluded to, if you want a closedor semi-closed community, the best option right now is, from what I understand, a game like NWN where you can invite only the people who you want or a single player game

Personally, I don't like the idea of exclusive clubs as it tends to make things even more "cliquy" than they already are. What you end up with is a few groups who do really great events and others who are barred from those events. So, rather than have a person like Aktrez get beat on in the club, she has to deal with it either through direct /tells(!!!) or outside of the club.

I don't have a good solution. But, I don't think creating a rift in the player base is a good idea as it only serves to create MORE ill feelings. ARK is there for when players act up - that is the mechanism that Funcom provided and, personally, I think that while it's not perfect, it's still pretty effective. So, it's not like Funcom hasn't done anything to try and help someone like Aktrez.

I was just reading through the thread titled "Social Issues" and there's a few well-intended but ultimately bad ideas in that thread. One is the "griefer board." Who is to decide who is a griefer? Who investigates accusations? How does one apply to get removed? What if the person who is put on the board is innocent and someone wanted them there due to a grudge? What if that same person who is innocent never heard of this board and is completely unaware of it yet is sent /tells from people he's never even heard of where they call him names and berate him?

The other idea was an rp server. Grief players get off on ruining other people's fun. What better way than to have all of the very people who you target in one, convenient area.

Games like this, like in rl, force you to deal with all sorts of people - it's called a society. We all have to live with each other and try to work things out. If a member of the society doesn't want to do that and starts breaking laws, you call a cop (ARK) and have them deal with it.

I really wish there was a solution that doesn't segregate the players or put babysitters in every corner of the world as a game like that is not something that would appeal to me.

Putting in prespective
[...Start OOC...]

Just to put a bit of perspective on things...griefers (or their type) aren't something that just came along with MMORPGs. Back in the 1980's we had dial-up BBS's instead of the Internet we had similar spoilers who liked to call in and disrupt chats and conversations by harrasing other callers, picking on them, sometimes following them from one BBS to another. After a while, what used to be a fun and welcoming place to hang out and chat with friends would become a warzone or arguing and abuse...or worse empty and dead.

Almost 20 years later..same kinda jerks doing the same kinda stuff (with newer technology of a MMORPG). Some system operators would try to ban them...but they would just come back under new IDs and do the same crap. Having a user validation system worked somewhat...but was a pain, and every BBS Sysop did things differently. The only thing that seemed to really work consistently was to completely ignore them, not even acknowledge that they existed or said anything. One creative soul that I know wrote a change to the BBS program so that it would take the text of anything the spoiler wrote and convert it to Jive or VallyGirl Speak or something. So at least if they were going to spew their garbage the rest of us could have a chuckle at their expense. After a while...when nobody would play with them anymore...they gave up. I don't think I ever saw an exception to this. As soon as they couldn't get their jollies anymore they left.

Anyways...I guess what I'm trying to say is I agee with Nevver above. Closed communities turn into cliques, preventing the inflow of the GOOD newblood that you need to keep any community healthy. The only thing that really works is the hardest thing to do. Ignore them and don't give them the satisfaction. And if most people tried to do this, then it would be a big first step.

Welp, that's my 2 cents.

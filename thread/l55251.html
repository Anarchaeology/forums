Effective role-play depends on more than the PC's, it needs an atmosphere generated by named NPC's - that is one thing sadly lacking in the game at the moment.

As a comparison, stand at the city gates of Qeynos in EQ and listen to the guards. Once you start talking to them too then you can get some great conversations going, one-sided in terms of the AI of course, but it draws other players in.

Role-playing is about a lot more that just asking someone "Would ye gimme a buff sor?" instead of "will ya give me a buff dude?". Its about creating a persona for your character and then sticking with it - another comparison with EQ I'm afraid, the trolls and ogres got their speech perfectly judged, whether wandering about solo or grouping they created a real persona.

My favourite role-player was in a text MUD, she played a mute. Never said a word, just used emotes a lot !

To sum up, any role-player needs to create a persona for his/her character and stick with it, but its easier and more convincing if it can be bounced off NPC's in places that have atmosphere that is driven by those NPC's behaving in a certain way themselves.

My Thoughts...
SO  how do you encourage roleplaying? I think one key is to have a truly engaging storyline. Conflict is an interesting part of roleplaying, but the "2 sides at war" aspect of the story only goes so far. Here's my suggestions:

1) Small sub-plots. Like Medusa attacking the outpost at X  need to have people go protect it. We've seen some of this in the tests, with giant monsters attacking Omni and Clan cities. These side events need to be ongoing, aside from the central, major Clan vs Omni conflict.

2) Side-quests, given out by NPCs controlled by Funcom employees roleplaying. In general, more of a presence from Funcom employees acting out the parts of the people in and around the world would greatly enhance the experience.

3) Random events, again perhaps involving Funcom controlling NPCs. It would be neat, for example, to be wandering along through Broken Shores and to encounter a Shopkeeper trying to get back to the Omni Outpost and being attacked by some nasty. You help him out, and he rewards you with some nice armor from his store.

A second key is to give the players deep and engaging characters that they can make their own and have a vested interest in developing and roleplaying. The problem here is that, in its current state, trade skills are defunct, and there is little to differentiate the classes. Another problem is that the missions and tasks tend to be "go there and kill this" missions as things now stand. Instead of this, there should be missions that allow the Agent to sneak in and "steal" plans for something, allowing the Agent to use concealment and to pick locks. Or there should be puzzles to be solved, allowing players to use brains instead of muscle. Perhaps the "puzzles" could depend on the main plot line, and on the player's knowledge of current events. For example, an NPC that was too powerful to kill, who, if interacted with one way, would just wipe out the character, but if interacted with in another way (and you'd know that way by keeping abreast of Omni-News), would allow the player to pass.

By the way, I agree with Loremaster that NPC interaction can be an important part of roleplaying, as it lends to the overall feeling that you're "in" the game-world.

Unfortunately, and this is a BIG problem, AO will face great difficulty, I fear, getting good roleplaying going at this point. I think that it was really fatal to wait to begin the storyline until several months after release. An essential part of getting players to roleplay involves encouraging it from the start. Now, players are used to the kill, kill, equipment, level format, and they've not even thought about who their character is or how he might fit into the AO plot. TO get people to entirely re-think their approach to the game at this point may well be impossible. Really, this question/thread should have existed in the limited beta.

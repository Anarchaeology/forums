Don't look at it as a balancing act. That's not important. Look at it as a player. Do you want to be good at only one thing, that being fighting, while other classes are just a bit worse than you at fighting, but they can also do a ton of other stuff besides fighting? I don't care about trade skills, because anyone can do them, and Soldiers aren't good at them anyways. The only thing Soldiers can do is fight. I'll repeat that. The only thing Soldiers can do is fight. Make sense? It's not fair that just because Soldiers are good fighters they get no USEFUL abilities. I'm sorry, but i'd sacrifice a bit of combat potential for a character who's still pretty good at combat, but can enter the Grid anywhere and transport the entire party around. It's about versatility. Soldiers can do one thing and only one thing. These other professions can add a TON of stuff to the group, including the ability to fight just a little worse than the Soldiers.

By the way, there's more to raising skills than just what the stats for it require. Skills are colour coded, based on their difficulty. Green is easy to raise, blue is hard to raise. Count on trade skills being blue for Soldiers.

Hm, let me put it to you this way

All characters will have their specialties, but have a lot of other options to them.

Soldiers specialty happens to be fighting.

Soldiers have a lot of potential to get quite a few useful abilities, the same potential that other classes have to get fighting ability. If you want other specialties, pick other classes. Funcom has no intention of making one class an UBER class that has the special and unique abilities of every other class.

I attended the conference session at E3, Building Online Communities. Tommy Strand was one of the panelists. The subject of player volunters and the recent events surronding them came up almost immediately. Basically all US companies are dropping volunteer support programs. This stems from a lawsuit filed by some AOL volunteers.

Tommy was pretty sure that player volunters would be in the game, since Funcom is a Norweign based company (this is also why UO still has volunteers for it's Japan shards).

There is bound to be a lot more legal stuff surrounding player volunteers. I would suggest to anyone who is thinking about becoming a volunteer to read the fine print and understand what they are getting themselves in to.

As far as powers granted them, I do not have any info.

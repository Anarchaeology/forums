Inocybe, you make a lot of really good points.

I would be interested to see what a current Advent weapon poll would look like, however...as compared with an older poll like conducted before. This would give a better picture as to the impact of the Pistol/1HE buffs as well as the release of better weapon options in that catageory (Sol Fire, River Seasons XP, and Electronicum) on the general weapon usage.

My guess is that, like you supposed, the inclusion of these better buffs and weapons has move the 1HE/Pistol population back to more the numbers I supposed rather than the 30% mark it was before.

While I can definitely see your points about being non-homogeneous, however...without specialties, the class becomes much weaker in practicality compared to other classes. Certainly, the inclusion of the Pistol/1HE buffs have helped the combat viability of the Advent tremendously.

The biggest problem with a "master of none" approach is that it simply doesn't mean anything in AO.

ANYONE can use ANYTHING later in the game. The only thing that seperates classes in buffs. That's the biggest issue.

So, what? Advents have a lot of Auqa's. That's really awesome in the early stages of the game for flexibility. However, later in the game when IP grows on trees, any class has parity in non-buffed skills. So what, it costs an extra 50,000 IP for a Fixer to max rifle compared to an Advent. What's an extra 50,000 at level 160+? 70,000 more IP for a Martial Artist to get our same value for 2HE at level 200? Not terribly impressive.

This, of course, is a flaw in Funcom's IP allocation system for the most part. Too much spare IP at the end of the game means any class can basically dump IP wherever they feel like with little consequence. (Especially with the IPRs now.)

This make a "Jack of all trades, master of none" class pretty much worthless overall.

An option? They could give us general buffs to raise many skills by a smaller margin. +75 buff for all primary abilities at level 195 or something. But that would probably draw the ire of other classes rather immeadiately.

So, the next option is to focus. Give us nice Pistol and 1HE powers, and make those two options both viable to allow for ranged/melee flexibility in a primary. A perfect option? Probably not. But it makes the most sense within AO's skill structure.

Interesting topic...

A couple of points though...

Adventurers have had their fix. Aside from the cyclic shields, I doubt we'll see any more love aside from weapons or adventurer specific items in the near future. Because the adventurer class has been made more easy to play, we'll see more adventurers in game over time, and funcom will listen to us, but I doubt we'll ever have the numbers of say fixers or MAs, with corresponding ability to influence game development.

Now....adventurers got weapons skill buffs, and the max is 100 as stated earlier. With the 87 buff available to a 145 adventurer, at title level 5 pistol skill maxes out around 920ish counting expertises. With that much pistol skill you won't be able to equip ALL pistols (or all swords with the 1HE line), but you are able to equip the "old" general pistols and swords for the most part, and as someone stated, even for the ADV only lines with the high requirements, a title level 5 ADV can equip a 190ish weapon, which does 95% of the damage of the QL 200 version. Wrangles are available if the QL 200 is a necessity.

So, weapon equiping...I think is a small point. Adventurers can self equip decent pistols and swords, maybe not always qL 200, but close enough.

Why aren't we given higher buffs....for the reason we are also given general damage buffs. CF Sabretooth adds 56 to the attack rating, and and Flurry and Blurr et al adds considerably more. At title level 6 an adventurer can boost his attack rating with self buffs over 200 if one is using edged or pistols....Adventurers are not meant to be the premiere damage dealers, and higher weapons skills buffs could possibly result in that. We still have the advantage of healing that few other classes posses remember and at some point damage output given has to be reconciled with ability to take damage.

Good points have been made on both sides but I fear adding to the offensive side of the equation would surely result in a lowering of our healing capabilities, which given the state of the mid game with not enough docs to go around would weaken the game experience for all classes.

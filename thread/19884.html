A New Approach: Breed/Profession Matching
Over on Beginner's Corner, Konril posted an incredibly useful guide to understanding the various armor types in Anarchy Online.

It got me thinking that there was an obvious correlation between two of the armor types and two of the breeds: Str/Stam obviously matches Atrox, and Int/Psy obviously matches Nanomage. Agil/Str more-or-less matches Opifex. Nobody gets Sense as a dark green ability, which surprised me when I realized it, which means that Sens/Psy armor makes no sense for anyone much. No wonder you don't see much Energized Armor running around. At best, if someone was determined to use it, it would only make sense (pun unintended) for a Solitus.

So then, much later, it occurred to me to sort the list of skills by the highest underlying attribute, and then combine them in the same combinations as the armor types. I wanted to then match combinations of skills to the most important (and most affordable) skills for each profession. That would give me "instant cookie cutter" characters - for each profession, there would be one or at most two obvious choices of breed, with one obvious choice of armor.

Some of these results surprised me. Until I tried playing a Trader, I never realized just how much you could make up for poor combat abilities (Nano) by using Ransack and Deprive (Nano) - so it almost makes as much sense to play a Nanomage Trader as the classic Opifex.

Nor had I consciously realized just how many of the weapon skills depend 70% to 100% on Agility, which Opifexes get in abundance. Sure, they don't get quite as many hit points as an Atrox - but they might actually make better soldiers, because they can buff higher and use higher QL weapons - assuming they were going to stay away from assault rifles and flame throwers. Don't laugh; there are some shotguns and SMGs with specials that neither a Trader nor a Fixer can afford to max out, and some of them do pretty impressive damage.

A similar argument applies to the idea of an Opifex Enforcer. On the face of it, that's a silly idea. But you know, that everybody thinks this is a silly idea may explain why there are so few edged-weapon Enforcers. Everybody assumes enforcers have to be Atroxes - to whom agility is dark blue.

Opifexes turn out to be more versatile than I thought they'd be. On the other hand, they'd be awful at one of the professions I somehow assumed they'd be good at:

I had not realized just how many of the Agent's most important skill depend on Sense, an attibute that (as I mentioned) nobody gets to raise at 5 per level. Somewhere I got the impression that Opifexes make good agents. Well, sure, they have good Agility - but Sense is a dark blue attribute for them.

Let me flame myself. I was browsing it one last time before going to bed, cross checking it against Konril's guide, when I realized that I'd completely misremembered the combination of attributes for Energized, etc. armor. Yes, I know it's not Sens/Psy. I was thinking of one of the more obscure armor pieces, one of the ICC cloaks. It's actually Psy/Stam.

That's actually even more useless. Psy is the primary attribute for almost nothing. Stam isn't the primary attribute for much of anything, either. Maxing Stam & Psy will give you great hitpoints and great nanopool. But it won't do much for any of your skills.

The main effect of this change in thinking is that I completely need to rethink the Agent profession again. But I don't feel bad. I put one evening into it, and got it wrong. How many man-hours has Funcom put into it, and they're just as wrong. More tomorrow.

You probably already noticed that some of your initial data was in error. One thing is, the sense of the Opifex was dark blue in the beta-test version, but it was made light-green in the live version. "Nobody gets sense as a light-green ability," is about 10 months out of date.

You are certainly on the right track, though. I doubt how few people realized how well atrox do at certain casting clases (Metaphysicist). Being an Opifex soldier sucks if you try to train her as a traditional soldier, but use shotguns instead of assault rifles, and she is suddenly very effective.

I believe it is a very good idea to plan your breed around your desired skillset instead of your desired profession. Style can become substance, when you plan correctly.

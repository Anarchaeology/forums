I'm an NT but my wife is an adventurer
I'm an NT but my wife is a high level adventurer. Here is my take on your profession.

One, your heals are slow but that can be made up with nano casting init. training. I don't feel they need to make the casting time faster on these but they do need to lower your recharge rates to atleast 2.5 or 3 seconds rather than the normal 4 seconds. Recharge is what is killing your heals. The initial casting delay can be trained off and should not be lowered.

Two, your pet morphs need some work. That reverse sitting bug has got to be annoying. Also, change the run speed for cats to +100 rather than -100. Fix parrot and parrot (other) so that when you fly into a building the NCU gets freed up. It shows the NCU free but it actually isn't. This is a bug. There are some instances with dragon form but I won't go into it. FC already knows about these.

Three, Adventurer's should have short duration roots. Yes, you should get some type of root to offset the chance you need to run but it should be a very short duration much like a stun. A good duration would be about 15 seconds.

Four, adventurer's should have a summon animal pet nano. The pet should be consistent with level, damage, AC, etc. per QL like other summon pets, but each animal pet ought to have an affinity with the adventurer much like a familiar would. I would suggest the following.....
......
........The summon pet should offer a portion of its health and AC to the adventurer while it's in effect. If the animal dies though, I think the adventurer should lose twice the health that was given from the affinity as a penalty to affinity shock etc. This would help make up the AC/health line and make pets a very very special thing for Adventurers. I think animal pets should mean something to them.

As for DoTs - NO.
As for DD nukes - NO

Adventurers I hope that FC gives you all some much needed love. Good luck and many blessings.

Maybe a special backpack that allows you to save your current location and any items inside the backpack, usable by anyone. The catch is you have to wear it to activate the save. It would require 300 Adventuring for non-Adv's and 200 for Adv's. With 300 skill, the Adv could save for all teammates that own one of the backpacks as long as it's in their inventory.

If killed, you would be transported to the saved location with the backpack and whatever items were placed inside, whether it was before the save or after, doesn't matter. Any item not placed in the backpack would be with the corpse at the location of death with, say, 5 minutes time to go and retrieve your items. After 5 minutes, anyone would be able to loot the body.

Lastly, the save for teammates would only take effect as long as the Adv is a member of the group. If the Adv leaves or gets kicked out, the save for the teammates who didn't wear their backpack and made their own save, would be deleted and revert back to their regular terminal save, resulting in more lost XP upon death.

This way there won't be teams who trick Adv's into joining the group just to save for them and then get booted.

Hmmm... I have only minimal experience with PvP. I've been shot at on the way to a grid terminal once, been rooted and ganked before getting to the same grid terminal another time, and engaged in one friendly duel once. Since that's it for me, this should be taken with a grain of salt; I'd really like to see knowledgable PvPers weigh in on the concealment issue, as I feel that PvP is central to AO. Also, I'm hypothesizing somewhat on dev motives, so please feel free to correct me if I'm off-base.

That third PvP experience made me understand why there's all the hubbub about "over-equipping". My opponent was slightly green to me when I was level 50 or so, and I was hitting him for 7,7,7,11,7,11,7.... I asked him how he did that, and he was wearing QL90 armor!

I know I'm not the first to suggest that PvP and PvM is hard, if not impossible, to balance, but the point was driven home by this encounter. If this guy could so thoroughly resist my guns in PvP, he could probably sail through 100% difficulty missions without breaking a sweat. This makes the green mobs with three times my hit points and unlimited nano make more sense. These ubermobs were an attempt to manage the leveling of the fastest levelers. The fastest levelers were equipped for PvP, and I was equipped for PvM -- meaning, much *less* equipped, or, more precisely, equipped for exploration -- I'm a Bartle EAS.

Nonetheless...

Precisely *because* I feel PvP is central to AO, I think that concealed should mean un-tabable. If your defining class-characteristic strength is hiding (like the Agents) or travelling (like Adventurers). then you need to be able to be *genuinely* hidden. This means invisible on screen, no name, untabbable, et cetera.

Invisibility would be balanced by the Perception rating checks of the other player, and by ideas like those suggested by Mharc and Psyphen. Also, this is more "natural": some people will be kickass by being armored, heavily armed, et cetera; others will be kickass by moving quickly and silently and thus choosing whether and when to fight. (For some literary archetypes, think of Obi-Wan making his way to the shield generator, or entire tribes of Fremen suddenly boiling from the sands.) Psyphen pointed out that attacking would break concealment, and I think attack is impossible with current animal forms anyway. Also, being in animal form would also require *acting* like that animal. If I see a leet standing at the mission terminal for 15 minutes, I'm pretty sure it's a fellow adventurer. :-)

And as always, un-tabable is essential for the agents... agents who can't vanish completely are missing out on something absolutely essential to their class. Even if you don't give this ability to anyone else, you *must* give it to the Agents.

Adventurers!
I have tried a few classes but always come back to my 77 adv. Adventurers could be the single most enjoyable class after the adv fix patch! Unfortunately, there are problems which I will not repeat since they are well known already.

So, on with my suggested improvements.

-1. Summon animal/pet: This would be the single best improvement. The pet would not have to be as powerful as an mp pet or an engy bot, but having an animal pet would truly distinguish the adventurer calss as an outdoor profession. Also, as stated somewhere else on this thread, an animal control nano would be great. We wouldn't have to totally control an animal but at least prevent it from attacking us.

Another nice option (Might be very hard to implement) would be the ability to actually ride a pet. While riding a pet the adv would never be aggroed by animals, much as wild animals IRL do not fear riders on horses as much as humans on foot.

2. Camoflauge Armor: This would be very cool. Why should agents have all the fun? Agents are supposed to be masters of urban environments... Let them conceal in the cities and let us adventurers use our camoflauge in the outdoors. Make the armor adventurer only. This would lend perfectly to my next suggestion, the scouting role.

3. Scouting abilities: With camo armor, we could spy on enemy encampments. The un-tab-able as animals suggestion was great also. Would it unbalance pvp? No! We arent supposed to be assassins! If we are found in a pvp zone, we would more than likely have to run rather than sneak up on someone using the un-tab-able ability!

4. Portable Tents: Heheh, these would be just plane fun to use. I would hardly ever go into the cities if I could set up my camp (long setup time as someone suggested) in the middle of the wilderness. Make it adv only, with the option for team members to save there.

I will post more later, but these simple improvements would cause me to play AO 10x more than I do now. People would not worry as much about fighting skills if we had coool and unique equipment and abilities like these!

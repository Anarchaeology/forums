implant organization and construction tool
Upgrading implants is a necessary, but dreaded exercise for most Rubi-Ka citizens, newbie and hardened veteran alike. The process is time-consuming, confusing, and expensive.

The expense, and the availablity of clusters (and higher level implants) is a gameplay issue, and not the topic of this note. Rather I want to focus on making the collection of clusters and implants orderly and correct.

By orderly, I mean well organized and simple. Currently, when I buy clusters and implants I try to organize them in backpacks, keeping the found clusters with the appropriate implant. Additionally, I have a list on my desk that I check off. Despite this, I have missed clusters, I have duplicated clusters, and on really bad days, whole implants have gone unbuilt.

A simple remedy to this haphazardness is to have a specialized backpack that has designated slots for each implant, and beside it designated slots for each of the faded, bright and shining clusters. When you find the cluster you want you put it in its slot. This way, you know at a glance which you have, and which you still need to get. Simple and organized.

By correct, I mean you get the clusters you intend to. I use Nano Nanny to determine what I want, and the list I check off, is the one I generate from that program. But any time you have to resort to external list-keeping, you know your application is missing something important. What is missing in Anarachy is something very similar to Nano Nanny - but it needs to be ingame, so external list-keeping is not required.

The obvious thing is to incorporate Nano Nanny into this specialized cluster-organizing backpack. Rather than generate a list, each of the cluster slots indicates exactly the cluster you want in it. Now you know exactdly which clusters you have and what you need to get. Additionally, at the side, there can be all the usual informaion regarding requirements for that cluster. And if you want to be helpful, you could add a summarizing note indicating maximal values.

The other nice thing about the backpack is that, once full, you now have a single item to pass to the nano programmer, who in turn has a well organized set of clusters to build.

But you can add further helpful features to this tool. One is to allow the owner to pre-check all collected clusters against the underlying specification. This way if there was an error in collection, the system will tell you before you give it to the nano programmer, and you have to rush out and repair it. A simple report tab would list a summary of the verification.

Another feature would be to have a build function on the backpack. Once the backpack is given to a nano programmer, he executes the build function, and all internal clusters are added to the implants automatically. All those that the nano-programmer has skill to build anyway. The programmer would receive xp for each of the clusters build off his skill. A summarizing report of those built would also appear in the report tab.

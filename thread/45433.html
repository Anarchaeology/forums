I have no doubt that people would use charm to attack high level mobs, to their certain death. That's the whole point of it. This isn't griefing if it is limited properly - like limiting the refresh time on the nano. But why is this a big no no? Isn't this the Crat equivalent of an agent's aimed shot or some other devastating attack? I still don't see what the problem is. I'm not talking about turning the other player into the Crat's pet - nothing of that duration. Just something long enough to get off an attack or two. If that were the case, people couldn't drag other players to the opposing sides territory.

As for mezzing - I agree that it should not work the same way as it does on a mob. That would mean you could keep another player mezzed indefinitely. Mezzing should calm a target, making it so he/she can't attack for a certain period of time. Against other players, it should only work once every minute or two and last for like 30 seconds. If Group A is in a pvp fight against Group B, I see no problem with the Crat from Group A mezzing 5 of the 6 people in Group B so that they cannot attack Group A (heck, even limit it to 3 mezzed mobs at one time). That clearly gives Group A the advantage for the limited time the mezzes are in effect. Does that make Crats uber? I don't think it makes them any more uber than a doc or any other support class. It just means Crats will all of a sudden be valuable in groups now instead of wasted spots.


I believe more in mezzes being allowed in pvp than I do charms. That said, I would still allow charms - Funcom would just have to be very careful as to how they are implemented. The closest comparison I have to what a mess charming *COULD* be is bard-burning in EQ. The details of how that was done differ somewhat from straight-up charming and sending the person to attack an uber mob, but is similiar enough to make the comparison. I think Funcom should think hard about how to implement both charms and mezzes in pvp. Done right, I bet it would be a blast.

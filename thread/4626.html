In a bizzarre combination of lack of forethought, and one of those pleasing twists of logic that make the universe such a interesting place to live in, only by never dying can we truly die.

Consider:

You are 35 years old, exactly.

You insure.

You run for half an hour, fight for half an hour.

You are now 35 years and one half of an hour old, and have learnt new things, (xp)

You are killed by a Leet.

You are reclaimed. Now; you have 'unlearned' the new things (Lost all xp since save) This implies one of two things. a) The memory engrams and suchlike are being deliberately removed from your body during some transport effect. A silly idea. Or b) You have died, and a carbon copy back-up is now 'you'. This new you has no knowledge of anything that happened after the save. This implies that it is a temporaly frozen 'snapshot', age: 35 exactly.

You (If that is even 'you' at all anymore, but I digress.) have effectively gone back in time half an hour.

It can be seen then, that to live forever, one must die often, and to truly die, one must never die at all...

There is a bit of a monkey wrench in the whole immortality business that some people are missing. Insurance scans naturally decay over time. You can't save at 35 years old, die of old age at 90, and be "resurrected" in the new body of a 35 year old. Since we are constantly experiencing new things, our soul and body is constantly changing. Resurrection shock partly occurs since our soul is forced suddenly into a new body that is missing experiences. It is simply not the same anymore. Since most of Rubi-Ka's fighters save at worst once a day, and often as little as once an hour apart, resurrection shock is minimal. If you go more than a few months without saving, the basic you becomes different enough from your scanned copy that the resurrection process will fail. Either the resurrection shock itself becomes severe enough to cause death all over again, or the system just isn't able to grab enough of your soul to keep it from slipping away irrevocably.

Now some of you may think you're immortal because you are dying on a daily (or hourly) basis, but this is actually not the case. Fact is, the soul itself suffers a nasty shock by the death and resurrection process. While this is certainly a lot more desirable than untimely death, a soul itself becomes wounded. Souls do heal, over time, however a "scar" is left behind, and if the death and resurrection cycle continues long enough, the soul simply can't be brought back.

Either way, you will eventually die of either old age, or shell-shock. Best we can do is try to push the date of the inevitable as far into the future as we can.

On the contrary
If you buy the boxed set, you get (among other things) an ad flier that Omni distributes on other planets in hopes of persuading people to move to Rubi-Ka. That flier makes it clear that it is only on Rubi-Ka that "Death Isn't Fatal! (tm)"

(Which, unless intellectual property law changed after the Long Winter, is a mistake. It should actually be "Death Isn't Fatal! (sm)" Products and company names get trademarked. Ad slogans can only be service marked. But maybe the rules have changed.)

The insurance terminal system only works here on Rubi-Ka because only here on Rubi-Ka does notum "naturally" exist. (In quotes, because in game, everybody assumes it's natural.) Elsewhere, notum is an expensive import that makes nanotech work much better than usual. Here there's so much notum in the airborn dust, let alone in the soil, that everybody's nanotech works almost as well as the Omegas' did.

The in-game and written documentation describes the Insurance Terminals as working something like this: You die. Your implanted NCU stops communicating with the planetary internet. It sends a signal back the insurance terminal you last saved at to reconstitute your body based on that save file. It does so. If it does so quickly enough, and the file is recent enough, your soul recognizes the body, more or less says to itself, "Hey, what am I doing over here when my body is over there?", and teleports "back" to the new body, awakening it. (Presumably it is this same process that cues the local nanotech at the corpse's side to disintegrate it.)

Now, notice that this automatically doesn't work for anybody too young or too poor to have an NCU.

So why doesn't it work for five-hundred-year-olds (or whatever the cutoff date for you ends up being)? They haven't said. It may be that after a certain number of centuries, your soul is bloody well looking for an excuse to leave.

Funcom's characters have said that during Black October, five years ago in game time, the original Dusters were a group who managed to shut down the entire resurrection system, planet-wide, before launching a major offensive. This may have been as "simple" as knocking the planetary communications network offline. Doing what they did meant that any of them that died in the attack would also be permanently dead - hence their motto, "Dust to dust." In other words, "I'm tired of killing people and having them come back, and I'm tired of having to keep coming back after I get killed. Let's just get this over with."
